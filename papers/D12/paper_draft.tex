\title{Kernel-Verified Detector and Readout Metrology:\\
From Photon Statistics to Composite Fidelity Ceilings}

\author{John Roehm}

\begin{abstract}
We report a machine-checked formalization, in the \Lean{} proof assistant with Mathlib and the
PhysLib physics library, of the floor-and-ceiling theory of physical readout chains --- the layer
beneath the channel- and device-level envelopes of quantum-network certification. Four layers are
covered. (i) \emph{Discrimination floors}: a distribution-free Le Cam/Bhattacharyya average-error
floor for Poisson discrimination, valid for every randomized count rule with no false-alarm
constraint and no likelihood-ratio assumption; the zero-false-alarm dark-baseline optimum; and a
sharp Gaussian separation-budget floor, uniform over threshold placement, with a two-sided
Chernoff/Mills/Birnbaum--Feller tail sandwich. (ii) \emph{Filtered-readout floors}: the
realizability bound $\mathrm{ENBW}\cdot T \ge 1/2$, proved sharp as a \emph{least} element and
characterized with equality \emph{iff} the filter agrees a.e.\ with its own DC average times the
boxcar --- a \emph{nonzero}, not necessarily positive, multiple; noise-equivalent-power
composition algebra; and matched-filter optimality as a \emph{greatest} element.
(iii) \emph{Electrothermal detector physics}: the linearized voltage-biased bolometer, with
stability as an exact dichotomy in the loop gain, and the Johnson noise-equivalent-power correction.
(iv) \emph{Two-level control}: the rotating-wave reduction as an explicit-remainder \emph{inequality}
at Bloch--Siegert scale --- never an equality --- and end-to-end assignment-fidelity ceilings. Every
theorem is kernel-pure with no project-local axioms, verified from axiom closures rather than by
spot check. A recurring deliverable is refutation: we prove that the folklore photon-counting miss
floor $e^{-N_{\rm diff}}$ fails in \emph{two} directions, being false-strict against a realizable
counter and exponentially fail-open as an average-error screen; that a magnitude-only electrothermal
stability criterion is unsound; and that the sign-free characterization of matched-filter saturation
is false. We are explicit about scope: our Chernoff bound is a factor-two sharpening of one already
in Mathlib, not a first, and two prior-art checks remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A single-shot measurement of a quantum system is the end of a physical chain: photons arrive and are
counted, a current is filtered, a thermal circuit responds, and a threshold is applied. Each stage
imposes a floor on the achievable error, and the floors compose. The engineering literature contains
these floors in abundance --- but often as folklore, with conventions left implicit and validity
conditions unstated, and occasionally in forms that are simply wrong.

This paper reports a formalization of that stack, machine-checked to the logical kernel. Our aim is
not to derive new detector physics but to establish an unambiguous reference layer: statements whose
hypotheses are explicit, whose conventions are pinned, and whose failure modes are exhibited rather
than assumed away.

\paragraph{The organizing discipline is two-layer honesty.} The mathematics --- Poisson sums,
exponential-series identities, tail inequalities, threshold algebra, linearized ODEs --- is verified
in the kernel. The \emph{identification} of any symbol with a physical instrument, of what counts as
an absorbed photon or which plane a power is referred to, is the consuming application's declared
hypothesis and never enters our statements. No theorem here is a claim about any particular device.

\paragraph{Refutation is a first-class deliverable.} Three of our results are negative, and they were
the most useful. Section~\ref{sec:poisson} shows the folklore photon-counting floor fails in two
distinct directions; Section~\ref{sec:etf} shows that two natural stability criteria for
electrothermal feedback are unsound; Section~\ref{sec:filter} shows that the obvious sign-free
characterization of matched-filter saturation is false. A floor that is quietly wrong is worse than
no floor, because it is trusted.

\paragraph{Scope, stated up front.} Section~\ref{sec:scope} sets out what we do \emph{not} claim.
The most important item: our Gaussian Chernoff bound is a factor-two sharpening of a sub-Gaussian
bound already in Mathlib, not a first kernel-checked Chernoff bound; and the quantum
hypothesis-testing and quantum-channel machinery this work sits beside is already formalized in three
proof assistants. Two prior-art checks remain open and are flagged as blocking.

\paragraph{Substrate summary.} Thirteen root-imported modules. Kernel purity was verified from the
project's extracted axiom closures for \emph{every} declaration in all thirteen modules --- not from
spot checks --- with no project-local axioms and the core axiom set exactly
$\{\texttt{propext},\allowbreak \texttt{Classical.choice},\allowbreak \texttt{Quot.sound}\}$. There
are no \texttt{sorry}, no \texttt{native\_decide}, and no \texttt{maxHeartbeats} directives. All
statements about library contents in this paper --- including every negative one in
Section~\ref{sec:scope} --- are scoped to Lean toolchain \texttt{v4.32.0}, Mathlib revision
\texttt{81a5d257} and PhysLib revision \texttt{c4843367}, and were established by direct inspection
of those checkouts rather than from documentation.

\paragraph{A scoping note on PhysLib.} We build against Mathlib and PhysLib, but we should be precise
about the second: PhysLib enters this development through the \emph{import closure} of the
composite-ceiling layer, not because any D12 theorem consumes a PhysLib result. The quantum
machinery our ceilings touch --- the POVM and Helstrom bounds of Section~\ref{sec:poisson} --- is the
project's own network-certification substrate. This matters because Section~\ref{sec:scope}
disclaims novelty against precisely the PhysLib results we do not use.

\section{Poisson discrimination floors}
\label{sec:poisson}

\subsection{The correct universal statement}

We model a possibly-randomized photon-counting decision as \thm{IsCountRule}: a function
$\delta : \mathbb{N} \to [0,1]$ giving the probability of declaring ``signal'' on $n$ counts. The two
error branches are \thm{falseAlarm} and \thm{missProb}, both as \texttt{tsum}s against the Poisson
measure. Working on the measure carrier rather than a deprecated density is deliberate: every
statement is phrased through \thm{hasSum\_poissonMeasureReal}.

The Bhattacharyya coefficient~\cite{Kailath1967} \thm{affinity} obeys a data-processing inequality
(\thm{affinity\_le\_binaryAffinity}): any randomized rule collapses the experiment to two outcomes,
and the affinity cannot decrease. Combining it with the two-outcome arithmetic-geometric bound
\thm{binaryAffinity\_sq\_le\_two\_mul\_add} yields the general Le Cam two-point floor~\cite{LeCam1986}
\thm{avgError\_ge\_affinity\_sq}. For the Poisson family the coefficient has a closed form,
\thm{poissonBhattacharyya\_hasSum}, needing no positivity hypothesis, and the two combine into the
headline bound
\begin{equation}
P_e \;\ge\; \tfrac{1}{4}\exp\!\big(-(\sqrt{N_a}-\sqrt{N_b})^2\big),
\label{eq:lecam}
\end{equation}
formalized as \thm{poisson\_avgError\_floor}. Its only hypothesis is that $\delta$ is a count rule.
There is no false-alarm constraint, no monotone-likelihood-ratio assumption, and no threshold
structure. The quantifier is non-vacuous: \thm{isCountRule\_thresholdRule} confirms every
deterministic threshold counter is admissible.

Two companions calibrate it. \thm{poisson\_avgError\_equalRates\_eq\_half} shows that at coincident
rates the average error is \emph{exactly} $1/2$ for every admissible rule, which measures the factor-2
slack in the Le Cam constant. And \thm{poisson\_darkBaseline\_miss\_floor} gives the one regime in
which a bare exponential is exact: against a dark baseline with zero false alarm, the miss
probability is at least $e^{-N_a}$, attained by the unit-threshold rule
(\thm{poisson\_darkBaseline\_miss\_optimum}). The zero-false-alarm hypothesis is not decorative ---
\thm{darkBaseline\_zeroFalseAlarm\_load\_bearing} exhibits a rule that beats the floor without it.

\subsection{The folklore floor fails in two directions}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/d12_fig1_poisson_floor_vs_folklore.png}
\caption{Both failure directions. \textbf{(A)} False-strict as a miss bound: the realizable
unit-threshold counter misses with probability $e^{-N_a}$, strictly \emph{below} the folklore value,
undershooting it by the factor $e^{N_b}$ --- certified at $148\times$ for $(N_b,N_a) = (5,10)$.
\textbf{(B)} Fail-open as an average-error screen: beyond the crossover at $N_a = 51.40$ the
folklore value falls \emph{below} the true Le Cam floor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lecam}, so a screen built
on it admits configurations the true floor forbids (shaded). Below that crossover the folklore form
is merely conservative, which is \emph{not} direction (A). \textbf{(C)} The exact exponent gap
$2\sqrt{N_b}(\sqrt{N_a}-\sqrt{N_b})$ of \thm{folkloreGap\_split}, with the $\log 4$ threshold and
the certified witness at $(N_b,N_a) = (50,60)$.}
\label{fig:folklore}
\end{figure*}

A widely repeated rule of thumb states the photon-counting miss floor as $e^{-N_{\rm diff}}$ with
$N_{\rm diff} = N_a - N_b$. We prove it wrong twice over, in senses that are genuinely distinct.

\paragraph{False-strict as a miss bound.} \thm{folklore\_miss\_floor\_false} states that for
\emph{every} bright baseline $N_b > 0$ and every $N_a$, the ideal unit-threshold counter misses
strictly less often than the folklore value. That counter is admissible, so this is not an artefact
of an exotic rule: it undershoots by the factor $e^{N_b}$. The quantitative form
\thm{folklore\_missFloor\_beaten\_148fold} certifies a factor of $148$ at the operating point
$(N_b,N_a) = (5,10)$, against the true $e^5 = 148.41\ldots$ The correct constrained statement is
\thm{poisson\_darkBaseline\_miss\_floor}, which the folklore form omits the hypothesis of.

\paragraph{Exponentially fail-open as an average-error screen.} The algebraic core is an identity,
\thm{folkloreGap\_split}:
\begin{equation}
(N_a - N_b) - (\sqrt{N_a}-\sqrt{N_b})^2 \;=\; 2\sqrt{N_b}\,(\sqrt{N_a}-\sqrt{N_b}),
\end{equation}
so the Le Cam exponent beats the folklore exponent by exactly twice the baseline amplitude times the
amplitude separation. \thm{folklore\_avgFloor\_unsound\_of\_bright} then characterizes the failure:
whenever that gap exceeds $\log 4$, the \emph{true} floor strictly \emph{exceeds} the folklore value,
so a screen built on the folklore form admits configurations the true floor forbids. At
$(N_b,N_a) = (50,60)$ the certified ratio is over $1000$
(\thm{folklore\_avgFloor\_unsound\_factor1000}), against a true ratio of $3491.96\ldots$; the
arithmetic core is \thm{brightGap\_5060}. Fig.~\ref{fig:folklore} plots both failures.

The two are not the same defect. The first says the folklore value is too large to be a miss bound;
the second says it is too small to be an average-error screen. Which one bites depends on which
quantity a practitioner thought they were bounding --- and the folklore statement does not say.

\subsection{Gaussian thresholds}

For a Gaussian readout we define the tail \thm{gaussianQ} project-locally, since Mathlib carries no
error function or Gaussian CDF at our pin, and the two branch errors \thm{thrErr0} and
\thm{thrErr1}. The headline is the sharp separation-budget floor
\thm{avgError\_ge\_gaussianQ\_sharp}: no threshold placement achieves an average error below
$Q\!\left(\Delta\mu/2\sigma\right)$. The sharp constant --- $Q$, not $Q/2$ --- comes from
\thm{gaussianQ\_two\_le\_add}, which says the centred window carries the most Gaussian mass, and the
optimum is attained at the midpoint (\thm{midpoint\_threshold\_symmetric}).

That ``probability'' is meant literally is itself a theorem: \thm{gaussianQ\_eq\_measure},
\thm{thrErr0\_eq\_measure} and \thm{thrErr1\_eq\_measure} identify these quantities with measures of
half-lines under Mathlib's own Gaussian, so the word is not merely a docstring gloss.

The tail is bracketed on both sides (Fig.~\ref{fig:enbw}, right). Above:
\thm{gaussianTail\_chernoff} for every $z \ge 0$, and \thm{gaussianTail\_mills}. Below: the sharp
Birnbaum--Feller bound \thm{gaussianTail\_birnbaum}, at \emph{every} real $z$ with no sign
hypothesis, and the parametric \thm{gaussianTail\_ge\_window}. Both directions rest on the shared
moment identity \thm{gaussianPDF\_moment\_Ioi}. Rational brackets \thm{gaussianQ\_two\_le\_rational}
and \thm{gaussianQ\_two\_ge\_rational} pin a worked value.

\subsection{The classical--quantum seam}

Shot noise connects the classical floor to the quantum discrimination bound.
\thm{diagonalState\_sqrtFidelity\_eq\_affinity} shows that the fidelity of two diagonal states is the
classical Bhattacharyya coefficient, resting on \thm{psdSqrt\_diagonal} --- the one piece of genuinely
new mathematics in the identification. \thm{povmAvgError\_diagonal\_eq\_avgAssignmentError} is then
hypothesis-free Born-rule algebra: the classical average assignment error \emph{is} a value of the
quantum error functional. The result is shipped as a sandwich,
\thm{poissonFloor\_le\_diagonalQuantumBound}, whose left conjunct is classical data processing and
whose right conjunct is the project's Helstrom/Fuchs--van~de~Graaf bound.

We are careful about what this does \emph{not} establish. The POVM layer consumed here is the
project's own network-certification substrate, not an external library, and no quantum
hypothesis-testing rate is consumed --- see Section~\ref{sec:scope}.

\label{sec:filter}

\subsection{A realizability floor, proved sharp}

With \thm{enbw} defined in the one-sided, DC-normalized convention, the central result is
\thm{enbw\_mul\_window\_ge\_half}: for every admissible filter with nonvanishing DC gain,
$\mathrm{ENBW}\cdot T \ge 1/2$. Three refinements make this more than an inequality. First,
\thm{enbw\_mul\_window\_isLeast} shows $1/2$ is the \emph{least} element of the realizable set, so no
better constant exists. Second, \thm{enbw\_eq\_half\_iff\_boxcar} characterizes equality: it holds
\emph{if and only if} the filter agrees almost everywhere with its own DC average times the boxcar.
The multiplier is therefore a \emph{nonzero} constant, not necessarily a positive one --- the only
constraint is that the DC gain not vanish, and $h = -\,\mathrm{boxcar}$ saturates the floor just as
$h = +\,\mathrm{boxcar}$ does, since ENBW is invariant under $h \mapsto c\,h$ for $c \neq 0$
(\thm{enbw\_const\_mul}). The two characterizations in this paper therefore differ in exactly this
respect, and the difference is easy to get backwards. Matched-filter saturation
(\thm{filteredSNR\_eq\_budget\_iff}, §\ref{sec:filter}) genuinely \emph{does} require $0 < c$,
because filtered SNR is a \emph{signed} quantity; ENBW does not, because it is a bandwidth and is
scale-invariant. Carrying the positivity across from the former to the latter would overstate the
ENBW characterization --- the $h = -\,\mathrm{boxcar}$ witness above refutes it directly. The
converse error, dropping the sign where it \emph{is} load-bearing, is what
\thm{unsigned\_saturation\_characterization\_false} refutes on the matched-filter side.
Third, \thm{enbw\_ramp\_gt\_half} exhibits a filter strictly above the floor, so the bound
discriminates.

Convention is load-bearing and we treat it as such. \thm{enbw\_oneSided\_ne\_twoSided} proves the two
normalizations disagree on the boxcar at every $T > 0$: a convention-ambiguous statement of this
bound is \emph{wrong}, not merely differently phrased. \thm{enbw\_const\_mul} confirms ENBW is a
bandwidth and not a gain. The hypothesis that DC gain is nonvanishing is non-droppable, witnessed by
\thm{enbw\_dcGain\_hypothesis\_load\_bearing}.

The definitional payoff is \thm{variance\_eq\_psd\_mul\_enbw}, and composing it with a
Johnson--Nyquist source gives the physical floor
\thm{johnsonNyquist\_filteredVariance\_floor}, whose prefactor is convention-pinned end to end.

\paragraph{Relation to the classical uncertainty product.} We cite Gabor's variance-based
time--bandwidth product~\cite{Gabor1946} as the classical antecedent, but our statement is a
different Cauchy--Schwarz consequence about a filter impulse response, not about the variances of a
signal and its transform. We located no canonical primary source for the $\mathrm{ENBW}\cdot T$ form,
so it is proved here self-contained.

\subsection{Noise-equivalent power and matched filtering}

The NEP layer fixes a unit contract as a theorem (\thm{nepOfPSD\_sq}) and composes it into the
operational chain \thm{sigma\_eq\_responsivity\_nep\_sqrt\_enbw}, with the shot and thermal
specializations \thm{shot\_nep\_formula} and \thm{nep\_thermal\_johnsonNyquist}, and the regime
boundary as an iff (\thm{shotLimited\_iff\_psd\_lt}) with an inhabiting witness.

Quadrature composition (\thm{nep\_quadrature\_two}) carries the \thm{IsUncorrelatedAt} hypothesis
explicitly, and that hypothesis is not free:
\thm{quadrature\_uncorrelated\_hypothesis\_load\_bearing} exhibits a fully-correlated pair whose
total power is twice what quadrature predicts, with the discrepancy quantified exactly as $\sqrt{2}$
by \thm{correlated\_pair\_sigma\_eq\_sqrt\_two\_mul}. Mixed conventions are likewise detectable
(\thm{sigma\_conventionMix\_ne}) while a \emph{consistent} convention switch is provably invisible
(\thm{sigma\_conventionPair\_consistent\_invariant}) --- so a falsifier aimed at the latter would be
vacuous.

The end-to-end ceiling is \thm{snrChain\_le\_window\_ceiling}, attained by the matched boxcar
(\thm{snrChain\_window\_ceiling\_attained}) and independent of responsivity
(\thm{snrChain\_independent\_of\_responsivity}).

Matched filtering is stated as an extremal property: \thm{filteredSNR\_le\_matchedBudget} bounds the
signed filtered SNR of every admissible filter by a \emph{template-only, filter-free} quantity
\thm{matchedBudget}, and \thm{matchedFilter\_isGreatest} makes it the greatest element.

\paragraph{A false characterization.} Saturation holds \emph{iff} the filter is a
\emph{strictly positive} multiple of the template (\thm{filteredSNR\_eq\_budget\_iff}), and the
positivity is not removable: \thm{filteredSNR\_neg\_matched\_eq\_neg\_budget} shows the negated
template saturates Cauchy--Schwarz in magnitude while sitting at \emph{minus} the budget. The
sign-free characterization is therefore false, formalized as
\thm{unsigned\_saturation\_characterization\_false}. We were careful to state that refutation
\emph{within} the admissible class, since refuting a universally quantified statement is not the same
as refuting its class-restricted form. In the power domain the sign does drop out --- but $c = 0$
does not, which is \thm{power\_unsigned\_characterization\_false}.

\section{Electrothermal detector physics}
\label{sec:etf}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/d12_fig3_etf_stability.png}
\caption{Left: the effective time constant $\tau_{\rm eff} = \tau/(1+\mathcal{L})$ across the
stability dichotomy \thm{etf\_stable\_iff}. On the unstable branch it is \emph{negative}; reading it
through an absolute value inverts the physics. Right: the Johnson noise-equivalent-power correction
$|1-\mathcal{L}|$, which is an equality rather than a direction --- the ETF-unaware budget

\label{fig:etf}

\subsection{The model and its dichotomy}

\thm{ETFModel} is a five-parameter structure --- heat capacity, thermal conductance, resistance, bias
voltage and the \emph{signed} temperature coefficient. Positivity is deliberately not a field, so
that degenerate branches can be discussed rather than excluded by construction.

The loop gain \thm{loopGain} is signed, and \thm{loopGain\_eq\_irwinHilton} proves it equals the
instrument-literature convention~\cite{IrwinHilton2005,Irwin1995}. That it is the right object is
\thm{linearizedSlope\_eq\_neg\_effectiveConductance}: the total slope of the heat-balance
right-hand side is exactly minus the effective conductance. The minus sign in the bias-power
linearization is \emph{derived} from the quotient rule over an arbitrary differentiable
resistance--temperature curve (\thm{biasPower\_hasDerivAt}), not posited.

The central result is an exact dichotomy, \thm{etf\_stable\_iff}: for a model with positive heat
capacity and positive thermal conductance --- both carried as explicit binders, not assumed away ---
every perturbation decays \emph{if and only if} $\mathcal{L} > -1$. Both directions carry content, and the trichotomy is
completed by \thm{etf\_diverges\_of\_loopGain\_lt\_neg\_one} and
\thm{etf\_marginal\_of\_loopGain\_eq\_neg\_one}, which shows the perturbation is frozen exactly at
the boundary. Quantifying over \emph{all} trajectories is legitimate because
\thm{solution\_unique} establishes uniqueness by an integrating factor. Speed-up and slow-down follow
from \thm{effectiveTimeConstant\_eq\_div}; on the unstable branch
\thm{effectiveTimeConstant\_neg\_of\_unstable} records that $\tau_{\rm eff}$ is negative
(Fig.~\ref{fig:etf}, left).

\paragraph{Two unsound criteria.} It is tempting to test stability on magnitudes.
\thm{magnitudeOnly\_criterion\_unsound} exhibits an unstable model that becomes stable when the
temperature coefficient is replaced by its absolute value, and
\thm{absLoopGain\_criterion\_unsound} exhibits two models with equal $|\mathcal{L}|$ on opposite
sides of the boundary. The sign of the temperature coefficient cannot be discarded.

\subsection{Responsivity and noise floors}

Both responsivities are genuine chain-rule derivatives of current with respect to absorbed power
(\thm{hasDerivAt\_responsivityETF}, \thm{hasDerivAt\_responsivityBare}), differing only in which
temperature-per-power map is composed. Their ratio is \thm{responsivity\_etf\_correction} --- but
only under $1 + \mathcal{L} \neq 0$: the unrestricted identity is \emph{false}, witnessed by
\thm{marginalWitness\_correction\_fails}. On the unstable branch the two even have opposite signs
(\thm{responsivity\_opposite\_sign\_of\_unstable}), so a magnitude-only treatment loses genuine
information (\thm{responsivity\_magnitudeOnly\_loses\_stability\_information}).

For noise, \thm{johnsonCurrentPSD\_eq\_johnsonNyquist} is a genuine fluctuation--dissipation citation
at the electrical conductance, and \thm{johnson\_transfer\_eq} \emph{derives} the transfer factor
from the coupled small-signal pair. The corrected result is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NEP}_J \;=\; |1 - \mathcal{L}|\cdot \mathrm{NEP}_J^{\rm naive},
\end{equation}
formalized as \thm{johnsonNEP\_eq\_abs\_one\_sub\_loopGain\_mul\_naive}. Three cautions attach, all
of them things we got wrong first. It is an \emph{equality}, not a direction: the naive budget
\emph{overstates} whenever $|1-\mathcal{L}| < 1$, and
\thm{johnsonNEPNaive\_lt\_johnsonNEP\_iff} states the boundary exactly, with the extreme case
\thm{johnsonNEP\_naive\_overstates\_at\_unit\_loopGain}. The factor is $|1-\mathcal{L}|$ and not
$|1+\mathcal{L}|$: an earlier treatment modelled Johnson noise as pure output noise and so omitted a
first-order electrothermal effect from the very layer whose subject is electrothermal feedback; the
two effects partially cancel, and at $\mathcal{L}=3$ an ETF-unaware budget is low by exactly two, not
four (\thm{speedupWitness\_johnsonNEP\_double}). And the factor is stability-blind:
\thm{johnsonNEP\_correction\_magnitude\_loses\_stability\_information} exhibits two models with the
same correction on opposite sides of \thm{etf\_stable\_iff}.

\paragraph{Provenance honesty on the phonon channel.} The phonon PSD prefactor in \thm{phononPSD} is
\emph{asserted}, not cited. An in-tree identity relating it to the Johnson--Nyquist form is an
arithmetic \emph{resemblance} only --- it constrains nothing, holding for any expression of the same
monomial shape, and is unit-incoherent as provenance. We label it as such rather than as a citation.
A $\gamma$-correction for radiative and ballistic regimes is not modelled;
\thm{phononPSD\_eq\_phononPSDGamma\_one} is the disclosure that our expression is the $\gamma = 1$
case.

\subsection{The composed floor}

The layers compose. \thm{bolometer\_nep\_floor} assembles the total input-referred noise from the
phonon and Johnson channels through the quadrature rule of Section~\ref{sec:filter}, with the
uncorrelatedness hypothesis intact. The capstone \thm{bolometer\_error\_floor} then runs an
electrothermal PSD through the matched-filter budget and out to the Gaussian separation floor of
Section~\ref{sec:poisson} --- three phases in one inequality.

Two structural results generalize it. \thm{phonon\_floor\_of\_psd\_ge} is channel-agnostic: any
readout whose composed white PSD dominates the phonon PSD obeys the phonon floor. And
\thm{phonon\_only\_error\_floor} states the layer's thesis as a theorem --- the Johnson channel
appears in the hypotheses and \emph{not} in the conclusion, so a Johnson-noise-free detector of this
class still cannot beat the phonon floor.

\section{Two-level control and composite ceilings}
\label{sec:control}

\subsection{The rotating-wave reduction as an inequality}

The rotating-wave approximation is usually presented as a replacement. We formalize it as a bound.
\thm{interactionHamiltonian\_decomp} splits the interaction-picture Hamiltonian exactly into a
co-rotating part and a counter-rotating remainder; \thm{bsAntiderivative} and its norm bound
\thm{bsAntiderivative\_norm\_le} control the remainder's integral at Bloch--Siegert
scale~\cite{BlochSiegert1940}; and \thm{rwa\_propagator\_difference\_bound\_physical} converts this
into a propagator-difference bound at an exact/co-rotating pair. The reduced dynamics is a genuine
solution of its own equation (\thm{rwaPropagator\_ode}, with uniqueness
\thm{rwaPropagator\_unique} via Picard--Lindel\"of), resting on the algebraic identity
\thm{rwaGenerator\_sq}.

That the bound is not vacuous is \thm{norm\_rwaPropagator\_quarter\_turn}, which computes an exact
propagator norm greater than one --- refuting the assumption that a unit Lipschitz constant suffices
in the naive Duhamel estimate. An applied instance with all admissibility conditions discharged is
\thm{diagonal\_drive\_propagator\_bound}.

Calibration algebra (\thm{calibrated\_duration\_transverse}, \thm{rwaAxisPhasor},
\thm{envelope\_phase\_alignment}) and Kramers degeneracy (\thm{kramers\_degeneracy}) complete the
layer. We note that \thm{trace\_blind\_to\_rotation\_direction} records a real limitation: the trace
does not distinguish rotation direction, so a trace-only calibration check cannot detect a sign
error.

\subsection{Assignment-fidelity ceilings}

A floor on the average assignment error is a \emph{ceiling} on \thm{assignmentFidelity}, via
\thm{assignmentFidelity\_le\_of\_floor} and the monotonicity \thm{avgAssignmentError\_mono}. Five
mechanism ceilings are shipped --- \thm{relaxation\_ceiling},
\thm{relaxation\_thermal\_ceiling}, \thm{photon\_budget\_ceiling} (consuming
Section~\ref{sec:poisson}), \thm{filtered\_readout\_ceiling} (Section~\ref{sec:filter}) and
\thm{detector\_chain\_ceiling} (Section~\ref{sec:etf}) --- each with a witness pair showing where it
does and does not bite.

Composition comes in two forms: the unconditionally sound \thm{combined\_floor\_max}, and the sharper
additive \thm{combined\_floor\_add}, which requires disjointness of the error events as an explicit
binder. That the sharper form is genuinely sharper is \thm{combined\_ceiling\_gap\_witness}, derived
by instantiating a measure rather than asserted. The first genuinely cross-layer composite is
\thm{relaxation\_photon\_ceiling}.

\paragraph{A disclosed weakness.} The biting witnesses for the filtered-readout and detector-chain
ceilings fire at zero matched budget --- a signal-free readout. That is a real operating point and
the witnesses are honest, but it is a \emph{degenerate} one, and we have not bracketed a
non-degenerate biting point for those two ceilings. We state this rather than let the witness pairs
imply more coverage than they have.

\section{Scope and prior art}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} The quantum-channel stack this work sits beside --- completely
positive trace-preserving maps, Kraus decompositions, Choi matrices and Choi's theorem, POVMs,
Stinespring dilation, trace distance, fidelity, data processing --- is already formalized, in
Lean/PhysLib~\cite{MeiburgLessaSoldati2025}, in Coq/CoqQ~\cite{coqq2022}, and in
Isabelle/HOL~\cite{UnruhCBO2025,EchenimMhalla2021}. We claim none of it.

\emph{Quantum} hypothesis testing is likewise already formalized: PhysLib's optimal
hypothesis-testing rate is a full development~\cite{MeiburgLessaSoldati2025}. Our work does not
consume it, and cannot: a Poisson experiment on $\mathbb{N}$ is not a finite type, and an asymmetric
Neyman--Pearson rate is a different object from the symmetric Bayes/Le Cam floor of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:lecam}. The POVM layer we do consume is our own project's network-certification
substrate.

Our Gaussian Chernoff bound is a \emph{sharpening}, not a first. Mathlib carries a sub-Gaussian
Chernoff bound at our own pin~\cite{Degenne2025Kernels}, whose constant at unit variance proxy is a
factor of two weaker than \thm{gaussianTail\_chernoff}. That is a genuine refinement and we present
it as one. Mathlib also carries Kullback--Leibler divergence; it does not carry a Hellinger
divergence, a Bhattacharyya coefficient, a Neyman--Pearson lemma, or any hypothesis test. Transform
methods and transfer-function algebra are formalized in HOL Light~\cite{RashidHasan2017}; the
stochastic noise-metrology layer above them is not. PhysLib formalizes operator uncertainty
relations, which are not bandwidth--duration bounds on a filter, and a thermoelectric \emph{material}
figure of merit, which is not a device thermal circuit.

\paragraph{What we do claim, hedged.} To our knowledge, and as of the pinned library versions, no
interactive theorem prover contains: detection-theoretic photon counting (miss, false alarm, dark
counts); Bhattacharyya/Le Cam average-error bounds for Poisson hypotheses; ENBW, NEP or
matched-filter optimality; an electrothermal \emph{device} model; or a rotating-wave approximation
with an explicit error bound. The strongest of these is the electrothermal device model, for which a
grep across the entire package tree returns no related identifier of any kind.

\paragraph{Two open checks, flagged as blocking.} First, an out-of-Mathlib Lean project on
information theory and hypothesis testing is under active development; a Bayes-binary-risk--divergence
lower bound there would be substantially our Le Cam floor, and it is the highest prior-art risk in
this work. Second, the Isabelle Archive of Formal Proofs and the Coq \texttt{infotheo} library are
unassessed. That is a gap in our evidence, not a finding of absence, and we do not assert absence in
those ecosystems. For HOL4, PVS, Mizar and Agda we say only that we are aware of no relevant
formalization. All negative claims are scoped to the pinned versions and should be re-checked before
publication.

\paragraph{Disclosed modelling hypotheses.} Eleven propositions carry modelling content that the
algebra cannot supply and that appear in the binder list of every consuming theorem. The two most
substantial are \thm{IsWhiteFilteredVariance}, in whose \emph{definition} the Parseval step and
spectral flatness live --- not in the theorem that consumes it --- and
\thm{IsShotFilteredMoments}, in whose definition Campbell's theorem lives. We flag these because a
reader could otherwise mistake the consuming theorems for derivations of the physics rather than
consequences of it.

\section{Conclusion}

The detector stack turns out to be a good subject for formalization precisely because so much of it
circulates as folklore. Three of our most useful results are refutations, and each closes a way of
being confidently wrong: the folklore photon-counting floor fails in two opposite directions
depending on which quantity one thought was bounded; a magnitude-only electrothermal stability
criterion inverts the physics on the unstable branch; and the sign-free characterization of
matched-filter saturation is false because the negated template saturates in magnitude while sitting
at minus the budget.

What the formalization adds beyond correctness is that conventions become checkable. The one- and
two-sided bandwidth normalizations provably disagree on the boxcar, so a convention-ambiguous
statement of $\mathrm{ENBW}\cdot T \ge 1/2$ is wrong rather than merely imprecise --- while a
consistently applied convention switch is provably invisible, so a falsifier aimed at it would be
vacuous. Both facts are theorems here, and neither is available to prose.

The floors compose, and that was the point: a single inequality carries an electrothermal power
spectral density through a matched-filter budget to a Gaussian separation floor and out to an
assignment-fidelity ceiling. We are equally clear about where the composition is currently thin ---
two of the five mechanism ceilings have only degenerate biting witnesses --- and about the two
prior-art checks that remain open.
