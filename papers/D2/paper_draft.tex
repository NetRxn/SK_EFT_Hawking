\title{Anomaly Constraints on Standard-Model Particle Content:\\
Two Constraints, Their Composition, and What a Proof Kernel Certifies}

\author{John Roehm}
\affiliation{Independent Researcher}
\email{jgroehm@gmail.com}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Two anomaly-theoretic constraints on Standard-Model fermion content are
audited inside a single Lean~4 library, and their composition is
computed. The first is a \emph{generation} constraint: under Wang's
proposal that the Standard Model's chiral content reduces to a $1{+}1$d
theory of chiral central charge $c_{-}$, framing-anomaly consistency
requires $24 \mid c_{-}$, and with the sixteen left-handed Weyl
components of a $\nu_R$-completed generation, $c_{-} = 8 N_f$ forces
$N_f \equiv 0 \pmod 3$. The second is a \emph{content} constraint: the
Garc\'ia-Etxebarria--Montero $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ Dai--Freed anomaly of a
$\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}$ structure vanishes exactly when the number
of Weyl components is a multiple of sixteen, which fixes the content of
one generation and places no condition at all on their number. These are
constraints on different quantities, and the superseded version of this
manuscript, which claimed the generation count was forced twice over, was
wrong. What they do jointly is exclude the $\nu_R$-free Standard Model:
at fifteen components per generation the framing constraint reads
$48 \mid 15 N_f$, equivalently $16 \mid N_f$, which is \emph{the same}
condition the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ anomaly imposes, and either one alone
already excludes the observed $N_f = 3$. The two conditions coincide
rather than compose, so the relevant modulus is sixteen and not the
$\operatorname{lcm}(16,3) = 48$ asserted previously. Wang treats this
case himself and supplies the missing $c_{-} = 3/2$ from an additional
sector rather than reporting a contradiction. Independently of all of
this, we give a kernel-complete, hypothesis-free proof that $8$ divides
the signature of every even unimodular integer form, with both classical
inputs --- a local--global statement for indefinite forms and level-one
theta modularity for definite ones --- discharged as theorems in the same
library, and we prove that the residual factor of two separating this
bound from Rokhlin's theorem cannot be supplied by the lattice Arf
invariant. The library builds clean with \axiomcount{} project-local
axioms and zero \texttt{sorry}; a closing section states, theorem by
theorem, which of this bundle's declared results carry the physics content
their names suggest and which do not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Standard Model has three generations of chiral fermions, and nothing
in its Lagrangian says why. Two lines of argument in the recent
anomaly literature bear on the question, and they are frequently quoted
together as though they answered it twice. They do not, and separating
them is the first task of this paper.

The first line is Wang's~\cite{Wang2024}. It proposes that the
Standard Model's chiral fermion content can be reduced, through a
sequence of sign-reversing mass domain walls, to a $1{+}1$d theory whose
total chiral central charge is $c_{-} = 8 N_f$ for $N_f$ generations of
sixteen Weyl components. Framing-anomaly consistency of that boundary
theory requires $c_{-} \equiv 0 \pmod{24}$, whence $3 \mid N_f$. This is
a constraint on the \emph{number} of generations, and it is conditional
on the proposal: Wang's own summary describes his constraints as
\emph{favouring} $N_f \in 3\mathbb{Z}$, not as forcing it, and the
reduction is a physical hypothesis about the Standard Model, not a
theorem about it.

The second line is Garc\'ia-Etxebarria and Montero's~\cite{GarciaEtxebarria2019}.
They identify a $\mathbb{Z}_4$ subgroup of a particular combination of
hypercharge and $B-L$ under which every Standard-Model Weyl fermion
carries charge $1 \bmod 4$, so that the theory can be placed on a
$\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}$ manifold; the Dai--Freed anomaly of that
structure lives in $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$, and it vanishes precisely when the
number of Weyl fermions is a multiple of sixteen. Their conclusion is
stated in their own words as a fact about content, not count:
\emph{``the number of fermions in the standard model must be a multiple
of sixteen for the $\mathbb{Z}_4$ symmetry to be anomaly-free. This is
precisely the number of fermions in a generation of the standard model,
once we include the right-handed neutrino.''} With a right-handed
neutrino, $16 N_f \equiv 0 \pmod{16}$ for \emph{every} $N_f$. The
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ argument places no condition whatever on the number of
generations.

\paragraph{The correction this draft makes.}
An earlier version of this manuscript asserted, in its abstract and
introduction, that the generation count is forced twice over --- once by
the modular argument and again, in the $\nu_R$-free case, by the
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ argument, along two algebraic chains it described as
entirely separate --- while its own \S2.4 stated the opposite.
Table~\ref{tab:branches} settles it. Neither chain forces $N_f = 3$
twice; the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ chain never yields $3\mathbb{Z}$ at all.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{The two constraints, by branch. The $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ condition
constrains content per generation; only in the $\nu_R$-free branch does it
bite on the count, and there the framing constraint gives the same
condition rather than an independent one.}
\label{tab:branches}
\begin{ruledtabular}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
branch & condition & solutions \\
\colrule
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$, with $\nu_R$ & $16 N_f \equiv 0 \;(16)$ & every $N_f$ \\
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$, no $\nu_R$ & $15 N_f \equiv 0 \;(16)$ & $N_f \in 16\mathbb{Z}$ \\
framing, with $\nu_R$ & $24 \mid 8 N_f$ & $N_f \in 3\mathbb{Z}$ \\
framing, no $\nu_R$ & $48 \mid 15 N_f$ & $N_f \in 16\mathbb{Z}$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{ruledtabular}
\end{table}

What the two lines do jointly is narrower and, we think, more
interesting. In the $\nu_R$-free branch they say the \emph{same thing}.
Fifteen Weyl components give $c_{-} = 15 N_f / 2$, so integrality
requires $N_f$ even and $24 \mid c_{-}$ becomes $48 \mid 15 N_f$;
since $\gcd(15,48) = 3$ this is $16 \mid 5 N_f$, and since $\gcd(5,16)=1$
it is $16 \mid N_f$ --- which is exactly the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ condition
$15 N_f \equiv 0 \pmod{16}$. Either constraint alone excludes the
observed $N_f = 3$. Section~\ref{sec:composition} works this out and
retracts the $\operatorname{lcm}(16,3) = 48$ composition the earlier draft
asserted, which was obtained by pairing the framing constraint evaluated
at \emph{sixteen} components with the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ residue evaluated
at \emph{fifteen}. Those are two different spectra and the arithmetic
does not compose across them.

The exclusion is also not ours to claim against Wang. He treats the
$\nu_R$-free case explicitly, notes the $45/15 = 3$ ratio in his own
title, obtains $c_{-} = 45/2$ and observes that the missing $c_{-} = 3/2$
suggests an additional Pfaffian-like non-abelian sector supplying it. He
does not report a contradiction, and he explicitly disowns any reliance
on the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ anomaly: \emph{``we requires no
$\mathbb{Z}_{4,X}$ symmetry's $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ nonperturbative anomaly
in 4d.''} The exclusion below is therefore \emph{our} composition of
his modular constraint with an obstruction he sets aside, and we state it
that way.

\paragraph{What is unconditional here.}
Section~\ref{sec:signatures} is independent of every physical hypothesis
above. It proves, complete inside the Lean~4 kernel and with no carried
hypothesis, that $8$ divides the signature of every even unimodular
integer form; isolates the residual factor of two that separates this
from Rokhlin's theorem for smooth spin $4$-manifolds; and proves that
this residual cannot be supplied by the Arf invariant of the lattice's
mod-two quadratic refinement. That section is the mathematical
contribution of the paper. It does not enter the generation argument and
would stand if Sections~\ref{sec:modular}--\ref{sec:composition} were
deleted.

\paragraph{Organisation.}
Section~\ref{sec:modular} gives the framing-constraint derivation and its
formal backing. Section~\ref{sec:z16-drinfeld} gives the
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ content constraint, including a reconciliation of the
$\mathbb{Z}_4$ generator's normalisation with the source's, which the
earlier draft omitted. Section~\ref{sec:composition} composes them.
Section~\ref{sec:signatures} is the signature theorem.
Section~\ref{sec:chirality-wall} audits the lattice chirality-wall
material. Section~\ref{sec:sixteens} collects the several distinct
occurrences of the integer sixteen and says which are related and which
merely coincide. Section~\ref{sec:ledger} states what the formalisation
does and does not certify, theorem by theorem, and
Section~\ref{sec:outlook} closes.

Throughout, ``machine-verified'' means the named Lean declaration's
\emph{statement} says what the surrounding prose says. Several
declarations in this bundle do not meet that bar, and
Section~\ref{sec:ledger} names them rather than quoting them as support.

\section{The framing constraint on the generation count}
\label{sec:modular}

\subsection{Central charge from Weyl content}

Under Wang's reduction~\cite{Wang2024}, each left-handed Weyl component
of the $3{+}1$d theory contributes one Majorana--Weyl mode to the
$1{+}1$d boundary theory, hence $1/2$ to its chiral central charge. The
project encodes this as $c_{-}(n) = n/2$ for $n$ Weyl components
(\texttt{WangBridge.weyl\_central\_charge}). Counting components in one
$\nu_R$-completed generation,
\begin{equation}
n_{\rm Weyl} = \underbrace{6}_{Q_L} + \underbrace{3}_{\bar u_R}
             + \underbrace{3}_{\bar d_R} + \underbrace{2}_{L}
             + \underbrace{1}_{\bar e_R} + \underbrace{1}_{\bar\nu_R}
             = 16 ,
\label{eq:sixteen-count}
\end{equation}
which is \texttt{SMFermionData.total\_components\_with\_nu\_R}, and
therefore
\begin{equation}
c_{-}^{(N_f)} = 8 N_f \qquad (\text{with } \nu_R).
\label{eq:c-from-Nf}
\end{equation}
The one-generation instance is
\texttt{WangBridge.fermion\_count\_gives\_central\_charge}, whose
statement is exactly
$c_{-}\bigl(\sum_f \mathrm{components}(f)\bigr) = 8$: the sum over the
six species is taken inside the theorem, so the theorem does depend on
the species table and not merely on the numeral $16$. Dropping $\nu_R$
leaves fifteen components and
\begin{equation}
c_{-}^{\text{no }\nu_R} = \tfrac{15}{2}\, N_f ,
\label{eq:c-no-nuR}
\end{equation}
which is \texttt{WangBridge.chiral\_charge\_without\_nu\_R} at
$N_f = 1$. That it is not an integer at $N_f=1$ is
\texttt{central\_charge\_fractional\_without\_nu\_R}, stated as
$\neg\,\exists\, n \in \mathbb{Z},\; c_{-}(15) = n$.

\subsection{Framing anomaly and $24 \mid c_{-}$}

A chiral $1{+}1$d theory's torus partition function acquires the phase
$Z(\tau+1) = e^{2\pi i c_{-}/24} Z(\tau)$ under the modular
$T$-transformation, the exponent being the framing
anomaly~\cite{diFrancesco1997}. Consistency on the torus, for a theory
that is to be a boundary of a framing-anomaly-free bulk, requires that
phase to be trivial. The Lean statement is an equivalence, not an
implication:
\begin{equation}
\forall c \in \mathbb{Z}, \quad
e^{2\pi i c/24} = 1 \iff 24 \mid c
\label{eq:framing}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{ModularInvarianceConstraint.framing\_anomaly\_constraint}), with
the underlying $T$-shift of the Dedekind-$\eta$ $q$-parameter,
$e^{2\pi i (z+1)/h} = e^{2\pi i /h}\, e^{2\pi i z/h}$, proved from
Mathlib's \texttt{Complex.exp\_add}
(\texttt{ModularInvarianceConstraint.qParam\_shift}). Both are
kernel-pure.

Combining \eqref{eq:c-from-Nf} with \eqref{eq:framing},
\begin{equation}
24 \mid 8 N_f \;\Longrightarrow\; 3 \mid N_f
\qquad (N_f > 0),
\label{eq:three-divides}
\end{equation}
which is \texttt{GenerationConstraint.generation\_mod3\_constraint},
closed by the Aristotle automated prover (run \texttt{a1dfcbde}) and
kernel-pure. Figure~\ref{fig:d2-generation-constraint} shows the
resulting spectrum of admissible central charges, and
Figure~\ref{fig:d2-modular-phase} shows the same statement on the unit
circle: the $T$-phase returns to $1$ exactly on $N_f \in 3\mathbb{Z}$.

\subsection{The status of the constraint}
\label{sec:modular-status}

Equation~\eqref{eq:three-divides} is a theorem of arithmetic. What is
\emph{not} a theorem is that the Standard Model satisfies its hypothesis.
Three inputs are physical:
\begin{enumerate}
\item that the $3{+}1$d chiral content reduces to a $1{+}1$d boundary
theory in the manner Wang describes;
\item that the resulting $c_{-}$ is the framing anomaly of a theory
required to be modular-invariant on the torus;
\item that the counting convention assigning $1/2$ per Weyl component is
the right one for that boundary theory.
\end{enumerate}
None is formalised, and none can be, in the sense that each is a
statement about a physical construction rather than about a
mathematical object in the library. They are registered in
\texttt{src/core/constants.py::HYPOTHESIS\_REGISTRY} under the keys
\texttt{modular\_invariance\_framing} and \texttt{c\_minus\_equals\_8Nf},
each with its own falsifier set. A referee who rejects Wang's
proposal loses \eqref{eq:three-divides} entirely, and nothing in this
paper argues otherwise.

We also record Wang's own hedge, because the difference matters for what
follows. His abstract writes that he \emph{derives} the family-number
constraint within his proposal; his closing discussion writes that
his constraints \emph{``still favor the $N_f \in 3\mathbb{Z}$ family,''}
and considers a fourth family not excluded by experiment if it acquires
mass by symmetric mass generation rather than the Higgs mechanism. The
constraint is a proposal's consequence, offered as a preferred solution
to the family puzzle.

\subsection{A structural reading: $c = 24$ holomorphic VOAs}
\label{sec:schellekens-chain}

The divisibility $24 \mid c_{-}$ can be read as more than a
Dedekind-$\eta$ identity. Niemeier's classification of the
twenty-four positive-definite even self-dual lattices of rank
$24$~\cite{Niemeier1973} is the algebraic spine of the $c = 24$
holomorphic-VOA classification; Schellekens' enumeration of $71$
meromorphic $c = 24$ theories~\cite{Schellekens1993} was completed at
the VOA level, in the strongly-rational weight-one-non-trivial subset of
$70$ algebras, by M\"oller and Scheithauer~\cite{MollerScheithauer2024}.
On that reading the integer central charges realised by a
strongly-rational holomorphic VOA in the classified family are multiples
of $24$, and $24 \mid c_{-}$ becomes a corollary of published
classification rather than a free consistency condition. The
Frenkel--Lepowsky--Meurman moonshine VOA, at $c = 24$ with trivial
weight-one space, lies outside the M\"oller--Scheithauer scope and is a
not-applicable witness rather than a counterexample.

We are careful about what the library carries here. The chain ships in
\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/Schellekens/} at the \emph{predicate} level:
\texttt{NiemeierLattice.lean} and \texttt{HolomorphicVOAc24.lean} encode
the two Mathlib-absent classification results as program-tracked
predicates, and the composed statement
\texttt{Schellekens.schellekensChain\_implies\_24\_divides\_c\_minus\_iff\_3\_divides\_N\_gen}
has the shape
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&\mathrm{IsSchellekensChainComposed} \;\to\; \\
&\qquad \bigl(\forall N_f,\; 24 \mid 8 N_f \iff 3 \mid N_f\bigr) ,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
whose antecedent is a predicate discharged by \texttt{trivial} and whose
consequent is the arithmetic equivalence already available from
\eqref{eq:three-labelled}. The scaffold therefore records the intended
structure of the argument; it does not verify the classifications, and
the load-bearing $3 \mid N_f$ result of \S\ref{sec:modular} does not
depend on it. We include the reading because it identifies where the
constraint's mathematical weight actually sits, and we flag the Lean
status because the theorem's name does not.

%% a stable anchor for the arithmetic equivalence quoted above
\begin{equation}
24 \mid 8N_f \iff 3 \mid N_f .
\label{eq:three-labelled}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The algebraic core: $\mathrm{Ext}_{A(1)}$ through degree five}
\label{sec:ext}

The bordism computation underlying \S\ref{sec:z16-drinfeld} runs, in the
Adams-spectral-sequence route, through the cohomology of the Steenrod
subalgebra $A(1)$. The relevant $E_2$-page input is
\begin{equation}
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}
\mathrm{Ext}^n_{A(1)}(\mathbb{F}_2,\mathbb{F}_2)
= 1,\,2,\,2,\,2,\,3,\,4
\qquad (n = 0,\dots,5),
\label{eq:ext-dims}
\end{equation}
and the library computes it. The construction has three layers.
\texttt{A1Ring} presents $A(1)$ as
$\mathbb{F}_2[Sq^1,Sq^2]$ modulo the Adem relations, with the Cartan
formula; \texttt{A1Resolution} builds an explicit minimal free
resolution, each connecting map a matrix over $A(1)$ with entries
polynomial in $\{Sq^1,Sq^2\}$, and verifies $d^2 = 0$ by
row-reduced-echelon kernel witnesses; \texttt{A1Ext} reads
\eqref{eq:ext-dims} off as homology.

Two qualifications belong on the page rather than in a footnote. First,
this is a computation at the resolution, chain-complex and rank-witness
level: it establishes the dimensions of the Ext groups, not the
multiplicative structure of the $E_2$-page, and it does not by itself
give the bordism group. The passage from \eqref{eq:ext-dims} to
$\Omega_5^{\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}}$ needs the Adams convergence
theorem (H3) and the Anderson--Brown--Peterson splitting (H4), both
tracked hypotheses. Second, the Adem-relation identities and the
$d^2 = 0$ witnesses are closed by \texttt{native\_decide}. That is a
compiler-evaluated decision procedure, not a kernel reduction, and it
places \texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool} in the axiom set of every declaration
downstream of it. Section~\ref{sec:signatures} makes a kernel-purity
claim; \eqref{eq:ext-dims} is outside it, and we do not extend the claim
to cover this computation.

\section{The $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ constraint on generation content}
\label{sec:z16-drinfeld}

\subsection{The $\mathbb{Z}_4$ generator, in two normalisations}
\label{sec:z4-generator}

Garc\'ia-Etxebarria and Montero observe that the Standard Model extended
by a right-handed neutrino admits a combination of hypercharge and $B-L$
under which every fermion carries the same charge modulo $4$. Their
eq.~(4.13) reads
\begin{equation}
X \;\equiv\; -2 Y_{\rm G} + 5 (B-L)_{\rm G} ,
\label{eq:gem-generator}
\end{equation}
where the subscript records that their $Y$ and $B-L$ are, in their own
words, \emph{``rescaled \dots such that all fields have integer
charges.''} Their Table~2 tabulates the resulting integers on the
CP-conjugate (all right-moving) multiplet.

The project's formalisation writes a different-looking generator. In
\texttt{SMFermionData}, hypercharge and $B-L$ carry their standard
rational values on the all-left-handed multiplet, and the $\mathbb{Z}_4$
charge is
\begin{equation}
X_{\rm ours} \;=\; 5(B-L) - 4Y
\label{eq:our-generator}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{SMFermionData.z4ChargeRaw}). The superseded draft printed
\eqref{eq:our-generator} and attributed it to
\cite{GarciaEtxebarria2019} with no reconciling sentence, so a referee
checking eq.~(4.13) found a different formula. We reconcile it here.

Reading Table~2 against the project's assignments species by species, the
source's integer charges are
\begin{equation}
Y_{\rm G} = 6 Y, \qquad (B-L)_{\rm G} = -3 (B-L) ,
\label{eq:rescaling}
\end{equation}
the relative sign being the conjugation between their multiplet and ours.
Substituting \eqref{eq:rescaling} into \eqref{eq:gem-generator},
\begin{equation}
X_{\rm GEM} \;=\; -12 Y - 15 (B-L)
\label{eq:gem-standard}
\end{equation}
in standard normalisation on the all-left-handed multiplet.
Table~\ref{tab:z4} evaluates \eqref{eq:gem-standard} and
\eqref{eq:our-generator} on all six species and reproduces every entry of
the source's Table~2.

Two facts follow, and only the second is what the argument needs.
First, $X_{\rm GEM} - X_{\rm ours} = -8Y - 20(B-L)$ takes the values in
the last column of Table~\ref{tab:z4}, every one a multiple of $4$; the
two generators therefore induce the \emph{same} homomorphism to
$\mathbb{Z}_4$, not merely generators of the same subgroup. Second, that
common assignment is nonzero and identical on all six species --- every
Standard-Model Weyl component carries odd $\mathbb{Z}_4$ charge. The
formal statement is
\begin{equation}
\forall f,\;\exists k \in \mathbb{Z},\quad X_{\rm ours}(f) = 2k+1
\end{equation}
(\texttt{SMFermionData.sm\_z4\_all\_odd}), with the six individual values
also pinned as separate theorems. That the charges are integral at all
in the rational normalisation is
\texttt{SMFermionData.z4\_charge\_is\_integer}.

\subsection{The anomaly per generation}

Because every fermion carries $\mathbb{Z}_4$ charge $\pm 1$, the theory
can be placed on a $\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}$ manifold, and its
Dai--Freed anomaly is valued in
$\Omega_5^{\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_{16}$. The
anomaly of a single charge-one Weyl fermion is a generator, so the
anomaly of a spectrum is its Weyl-component count modulo $16$. By
\eqref{eq:sixteen-count} a $\nu_R$-completed generation contributes
$16 \equiv 0$, and a generation without $\nu_R$ contributes
$15 \equiv -1$
(\texttt{SMFermionData.total\_components\_without\_nu\_R};
\texttt{Z16AnomalyComputation.sm\_anomaly\_without\_nu\_R}).
Figure~\ref{fig:d2-z16} is the component census.

\begin{figure}[t]

\caption{Weyl-component census of one Standard-Model generation against

\label{fig:d2-z16}
\end{figure}

The substantive consequence for the generation count is a single
theorem, and it is a two-way statement:
\begin{equation}
\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, \qquad
16 \mid 15 n \;\iff\; 16 \mid n
\label{eq:z16-count}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{Z16AnomalyComputation.generation\_anomaly\_period}), which holds
because $\gcd(15,16) = 1$. With $\nu_R$ the corresponding condition is
$16 \mid 16 N_f$, true for every $N_f$; the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ argument
imposes no constraint on the generation count in the physical branch. At
$N_f = 3$ without $\nu_R$ the residue is
$3 \times 15 = 45 \equiv 13 \equiv -3 \pmod{16}$
(\texttt{three\_gen\_total}, \texttt{three\_gen\_mod16},
\texttt{three\_gen\_is\_neg3}), so the spectrum is anomalous and a
cancelling contribution of $+3$ would be needed.

\subsection{The same sixteen from local anomaly cancellation}
\label{sec:local-anomaly}

It is worth recording how little of the above is new information about
the Standard Model, because the point is easy to lose and
\cite{GarciaEtxebarria2019} make it themselves. If the $\mathbb{Z}_4$ of
\S\ref{sec:z4-generator} is assumed to embed in the $U(1)$ generated by
the same combination \eqref{eq:gem-generator}, then the relevant bordism
group is $\Omega_5^{\mathrm{Spin}^c} = 0$ and the mod-sixteen condition
must already follow from ordinary local anomaly cancellation. It does,
by the following elementary argument, which we reproduce because it is
short and because it makes the strength of the constraint concrete.

Write the $U(1)$ charges as $q_i = 4k_i + 1$, which is available exactly
because every fermion carries $\mathbb{Z}_4$ charge $1$
(Table~\ref{tab:z4}), and set $p_l = \sum_i k_i^{\,l}$. Local anomaly
cancellation for a $U(1)$ factor, from
$\operatorname{Tr}(F R^2) = \operatorname{Tr}(F^3) = 0$, is
\begin{equation}
\sum_i q_i = \sum_i q_i^3 = 0 .
\label{eq:local-anomaly}
\end{equation}
The first condition expands to $4p_1 + n_F = 0$, so
\begin{equation}
n_F = -4 p_1 .
\label{eq:nf-p1}
\end{equation}
The second expands, using $(4k+1)^3 = 64k^3 + 48k^2 + 12k + 1$, to
$64 p_3 + 48 p_2 + 12 p_1 + n_F = 0$; substituting \eqref{eq:nf-p1} to
eliminate $p_1$ gives $-2 n_F + 48 p_2 + 64 p_3 = 0$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
n_F = 8\,(3 p_2 + 4 p_3) .
\label{eq:nf-eight}
\end{equation}
So $8 \mid n_F$, and by \eqref{eq:nf-p1} $p_1 = -2(3p_2 + 4p_3)$ is even.
But $k^2 \equiv k \pmod 2$ for every integer $k$, so $p_1$ and $p_2$ have
the same parity; hence $p_2$ is even, $3p_2 + 4p_3$ is even, and
\eqref{eq:nf-eight} gives
\begin{equation}
16 \mid n_F .
\end{equation}

The consequence for how this paper should be read is that the
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ obstruction is not independent evidence about the
Standard Model's content in the branch where the $\mathbb{Z}_4$ has an
anomaly-free $U(1)$ completion; there it is a repackaging of the
perturbative conditions. Its independent force appears where no such
completion is available --- that is, where the $\mathbb{Z}_4$ is a
genuinely discrete symmetry --- and that is the setting in which the
Dai--Freed classification is doing work the triangle diagrams do not do.
Under the assumption of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ grand unification the
$\mathbb{Z}_4$ is the centre of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ and the result
follows instead from $\Omega_5^{\mathrm{Spin}}(\mathrm{Spin}(10)) = 0$.

\paragraph{What the library does not establish here.}
The earlier draft attributed the hidden-sector conclusion to
\texttt{Z16AnomalyComputation.hidden\_sector\_required}. That theorem's
entire statement is
\begin{equation}
\forall a \in \mathbb{Z}/16,\; a \neq 0 \;\to\;
\exists b,\; b \neq 0 \wedge a + b = 0 ,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ every nonzero element of a cyclic group has a nonzero additive
inverse, witnessed by $b = -a$. It mentions no fermion, no generation,
no $\nu_R$ and no sector, and it is true of $\mathbb{Z}/n$ for every
$n > 1$. We cite it for the arithmetic remark and nothing else. The
physical statement --- that an internally consistent $\nu_R$-free
extension requires $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$-charged matter or a gravitational
route --- is Garc\'ia-Etxebarria and Montero's, and it rests on their
bordism computation, not on ours.

Likewise \texttt{Z16AnomalyComputation.dai\_freed\_spin\_z4}, which is
named for $\Omega_5^{\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_{16}$,
has the statement $\exists \varphi,\ \mathrm{Bijective}(\varphi)$ and is
witnessed by the identity equivalence. It is a placeholder pending
Mathlib cobordism infrastructure, it discharges trivially, and it backs
nothing. The isomorphism is an input to this paper, taken
from~\cite{GarciaEtxebarria2019}.

\section{Composing the two constraints}
\label{sec:composition}

\subsection{The $\nu_R$-free branch: one condition, not two}

The two constraints of Sections~\ref{sec:modular}
and~\ref{sec:z16-drinfeld} are constraints on different quantities, and
in the physical ($\nu_R$-completed) branch only one of them bites. It is
in the $\nu_R$-free branch that both bite, and there they say the same
thing.

At fifteen Weyl components per generation, \eqref{eq:c-no-nuR} gives
$c_{-} = 15 N_f/2$. The framing constraint requires this to be an integer
divisible by $24$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
48 \mid 15 N_f .
\label{eq:no-nuR-framing}
\end{equation}
Since $\gcd(15,48) = 3$, \eqref{eq:no-nuR-framing} is equivalent to
$16 \mid 5 N_f$, and since $\gcd(5,16) = 1$ it is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
16 \mid N_f .
\label{eq:sixteen-divides}
\end{equation}
This is precisely \eqref{eq:z16-count}, the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ condition.
The smallest positive solution is $N_f = 16$; the reader may check that
$N_f = 8$ gives $c_{-} = 60$ and $N_f = 24$ gives $c_{-} = 180$, neither
divisible by $24$, while $N_f = 16$ gives $c_{-} = 120 = 5 \times 24$.

So in the $\nu_R$-free branch the framing constraint and the
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ anomaly impose the identical condition
\eqref{eq:sixteen-divides}, and \emph{either one alone} already excludes
the observed $N_f = 3$. There is no composition to perform and no gain
from having two arguments; there is one condition, reached twice.

\subsection{Retraction of the $\operatorname{lcm}(16,3) = 48$ claim}

The superseded draft asserted that the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ condition
$16 \mid N_f$ and the framing condition $3 \mid N_f$ compose to
$N_f \in 48\mathbb{Z}$, ``in tension with the observed $N_f = 3$,'' and
cited \texttt{RokhlinBridge.constraints\_without\_nu\_R} as its
discharge. Two things were wrong.

First, the two conditions being composed belong to different spectra.
$3 \mid N_f$ comes from $c_{-} = 8 N_f$, which presupposes
\emph{sixteen} components per generation; $16 \mid N_f$ comes from the
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ residue at \emph{fifteen}. One cannot take
$\nu_R$ out of the anomaly and leave it in the central charge. Done
consistently at fifteen components, as above, the framing constraint
gives $16 \mid N_f$ and not $3 \mid N_f$, and the correct modulus is
sixteen, not forty-eight. The stronger true statement replaces the
weaker false one: the exclusion of $N_f = 3$ is available from either
constraint separately.

Second, \texttt{constraints\_without\_nu\_R} has the complete statement
$\operatorname{lcm}(16,3) = 48$. It is a fact about two numerals. It
forces nothing about $N_f$, and no theorem in the library does.

\subsection{What the exclusion is, and whose it is}

Stated carefully, the result is this. \emph{If} Wang's reduction and its
framing constraint are granted, and \emph{if} the
Garc\'ia-Etxebarria--Montero $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ obstruction is applied to
the same spectrum, then a Standard Model with three generations and no
right-handed neutrino is excluded --- by each of the two, independently,
and at modulus sixteen.

This is a composition we perform, not one either source endorses. Wang
explicitly declines to use the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ anomaly, and his
treatment of the $\nu_R$-free case is not a contradiction but a
prediction: he obtains $c_{-} = 45/2$ for three fifteen-component
generations, observes that $c_{-} = 3/2$ is missing, and proposes that a
Pfaffian-like non-abelian quantum Hall sector in the reduced bulk
supplies it, matching the topological sector of his Ultra Unification
picture. Read that way, the deficit is a specification of what an
additional sector must carry, not evidence against the $\nu_R$-free
Standard Model. Garc\'ia-Etxebarria and Montero, for their part, note
that the low-energy $\nu_R$ Majorana mass breaks $B-L$ and hence breaks
the $\mathbb{Z}_4$ explicitly, leaving fifteen massless fermions --- so
the obstruction is a statement about the ultraviolet completion.

A referee should therefore take from this section a conditional
exclusion with two named hypotheses and one honest deficit, not a
prediction of $N_f = 3$. The one number this paper does not derive is
three.

\section{The lattice chirality wall}
\label{sec:chirality-wall}

The constraints above are algebraic. Whether a lattice regularisation can
realise a chiral spectrum satisfying them is a separate question, and the
project formalises material bearing on it. This section reports that
material at the strength the Lean statements actually carry, which is
lower in places than the superseded draft claimed.

\subsection{The Golterman--Shamir conditions, and what evading them means}

Golterman and Shamir~\cite{GoltermanShamir2026} do not state a
stand-alone no-go theorem. Constructing a one-particle lattice
Hamiltonian for a fermion spectrum, they discuss the conditions for the
\emph{applicability} of the Nielsen--Ninomiya
theorem~\cite{Nielsen1981a,Nielsen1981b} to that Hamiltonian: if the
conditions hold, the massless spectrum must be vector-like. ``Evading
Golterman--Shamir'' throughout this paper therefore means exiting the
joint scope of those applicability conditions, and with it the
Nielsen--Ninomiya obstruction --- not contradicting a theorem. We take
this framing from their own abstract.

The conditions are formalised in
\texttt{GoltermanShamir} (\dtwoGoltermanShamirThms{} theorems) and
\texttt{LatticeHamiltonian} (\dtwoLatticeHamiltonianThms{} theorems) as
fields of a \texttt{GSConditionsBundle} record over a
\texttt{LatticeModel}: six explicit conditions C1--C6 (smooth Bloch
Hamiltonian, fermion-only Hilbert space, relativistic dispersion,
completeness of interpolating fields, absence of spontaneous symmetry
breaking, kinematical zeros) and three implicit ones I1--I3 (Hamiltonian
form, locality, finite-dimensional local Hilbert space). Two of the
nine, C4 and C5, are physics conjectures encoded as \texttt{Prop} fields
on the record rather than as \texttt{axiom} declarations; the project
declares \axiomcount{} project-local axioms and this is why.

\subsection{TPF evasion: three drivers, verified}

The Thorngren--Preskill--Fidkowski (TPF) construction~\cite{TPF2026}
--- the initials are the authors' --- exits the applicability scope. The
project verifies three drivers, each a genuine mathematical statement:
\begin{align}
&\neg\,\mathrm{FiniteDimensional}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl(\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})\bigr)
&&\text{(I3)} \label{eq:driver-I3}\\
&\neg\,\mathrm{Finite}(\mathbb{Z}) &&\text{(I1)} \\
&\neg\,\mathrm{ContinuousAt}\;\mathrm{round}\;(1/2) &&\text{(C1)}
\end{align}
The first is \texttt{TPFEvasion.violation\_I3}, re-exported from
\texttt{LatticeHamiltonian.rotor\_hilbert\_not\_finite\_dim} and closed
by Aristotle; the third is
\texttt{LatticeHamiltonian.round\_not\_continuous\_at\_half}. A fourth,
conditional, driver relates C3 to the rotor gap:
\texttt{tpf\_c3\_conditional} states that if the rotor modes are not
gapped at $\Delta$ then $\Delta \leq 0$, with the two boundary cases
\texttt{rotor\_modes\_gapped\_at\_positive} and
\texttt{rotor\_modes\_not\_gapped\_at\_zero}. The genuinely
model-independent statement is
\texttt{GoltermanShamir.tpf\_outside\_gs\_scope}, which quantifies over
any \texttt{TPFConstruction} and any \texttt{GSConditionsBundle} and
concludes that I3 fails on the construction's local space.

\paragraph{The master conjunction is weaker than its name.}
\texttt{TPFEvasion.tpf\_outside\_gs\_scope\_main} is a five-fold
conjunction. Three conjuncts are \eqref{eq:driver-I3} and its
companions. The remaining two are $4 + 1 = 5$ and $9 - 3 < 9$. These
are evidently intended to encode ``$4{+}1$ dimensions'' and ``six of the
nine conditions survive,'' but as Lean propositions they are
\texttt{decide}-true statements about numerals with no term-level
connection to the dimension of the construction or to the condition
record. Conjoining them weakens nothing and proves nothing, and it makes
a three-part result read as a five-part one. We name the three drivers
and do not represent the theorem as covering the condition count.

\subsection{The Gioia--Thorngren construction}

Gioia and Thorngren~\cite{GioiaThorngrenPRL2026} exhibit a BdG
Hamiltonian on $(\mathbb{Z}/L)^3$ realising an exact chiral symmetry. In
Brillouin-zone coordinates $p = (p_1,p_2,p_3)$,
\begin{align}
H_{\rm BdG}(p) &= \sin(p_1)\,\sigma_1 \otimes \mathbf{1}_2
 + \sin(p_2)\,\sigma_3 \otimes \mathbf{1}_2
 + \sigma_2 \otimes h_{\rm eff}(p),
\label{eq:gt-bdg}\\
h_{\rm eff}(p) &= r\,W(p)\,\mathbf{1}_2 + \sin(p_3)\,\tau_z
 + \bigl(1 - \cos(p_3)\bigr)\,\tau_x ,
\label{eq:gt-heff}
\end{align}
with Wilson mass $W(p) = 2 - \cos p_1 - \cos p_2$. The Wilson-mass zero
set is characterised exhaustively:
$W$ vanishes at the origin (\texttt{WilsonMass.wilson\_mass\_at\_zero})
and, in the three-dimensional form used in the library,
$M(k) = 0 \iff \cos k_x = \cos k_y = \cos k_z = 1$
(\texttt{WilsonMass.wilson\_mass\_zero\_iff\_cos\_eq\_one}), an
if-and-only-if rather than a one-way bound.

The central result is the exact commutation of the Hamiltonian with the
chiral-charge operator
$q_A(p) = \mathbf{1}_\sigma \otimes
\bigl[\tfrac{1+\cos p_3}{2}\tau_z + \tfrac{\sin p_3}{2}\tau_x\bigr]$:
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm BdG}(p)\,q_A(p) - q_A(p)\,H_{\rm BdG}(p) = 0
\qquad \forall\, r, p_1, p_2, p_3 \in \mathbb{R} .
\label{eq:gt-commutation}
\end{equation}
This is \texttt{GTCommutation.gt\_commutation\_4x4}, universally
quantified over all four real parameters and stated as an identity of
$4 \times 4$ complex matrices. Its proof reduces, after the correct
$\sigma \otimes \tau$ block expansion, to $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$; finding
that expansion was the substantive step, and it was discovered by the
Aristotle prover (run \texttt{18969de2}). Supporting Pauli-matrix
infrastructure (\dtwoPauliMatricesThms{} theorems in
\texttt{PauliMatrices}) and BdG infrastructure
(\dtwoBdgHamiltonianThms{} theorems in \texttt{BdGHamiltonian}) are
reusable.

Equation~\eqref{eq:gt-commutation} is the strongest machine-checked
statement in this section, and it is exactly what it says: the chiral
charge is conserved for all parameters, with no gap or locality
assumption entering.

\subsection{Onsager algebra and the Witten $\mathbb{Z}_2$}

The Onsager algebra~\cite{Onsager1944}, generated by $\{A_n\}$ subject to
the Dolan--Grady relation~\cite{DolanGrady1982}
$[A_0,[A_0,[A_0,A_1]]] = 16\,[A_0,A_1]$, supplies the algebraic context.
The library encodes the Davies presentation~\cite{Davies1990} as a
\emph{structure} \texttt{DaviesPresentation} whose fields are the
relations
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
[A_m,A_n] &= 4\,G_{m-n}, \\
[G_n,A_m] &= 2A_{m+n} - 2A_{m-n}, \\
[G_m,G_n] &= 0 ,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
carried as hypotheses of the structure rather than derived. On that
structure the In\"on\"u--Wigner rescaling~\cite{InonuWigner1953} is a
theorem:
\begin{equation}
[\varepsilon A_m,\, \varepsilon A_n] = \varepsilon^2\,[A_m,A_n]
= 4\varepsilon^2\, G_{m-n}
\label{eq:iw-contraction}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{OnsagerContraction.contraction\_rescaling} composed with the
structure's \texttt{AA\_comm} field), and the contracted $G$-sector
remains abelian (\texttt{contraction\_GG\_still\_zero}). The
$\varepsilon \to 0$ limit is the contraction to the Heisenberg--Onsager
loop algebra, with an emanant $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ on the
Gioia--Thorngren Weyl doublet (\texttt{GTWeylDoublet},
\dtwoGtWeylDoubletThms{} theorems). The fourth homotopy group
$\pi_4(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = \mathbb{Z}_2$ is the Witten
anomaly~\cite{Witten1982}, and it embeds in $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ at the
element $8$.

We are obliged to say what is not proved. The library does \emph{not}
contain the Davies isomorphism $\mathfrak{O} \cong L(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$;
it contains a structure whose fields are that presentation's relations,
together with consequences of those relations. The declaration named
\texttt{davies\_isomorphism\_statement} has the statement
$\mathrm{DG\_COEFF} = 4^2 \wedge 4 \neq 0 \wedge 2 \neq 0$, and
\texttt{davies\_roan\_classification} has the statement \texttt{True}.
Neither is cited above.

\paragraph{The ``bridge theorems'' are consistency records.}
The superseded draft described \texttt{ChiralityWallMaster} as containing
bridge theorems establishing, for instance, ``that the GT construction
violates exactly the GS conditions identified in Pillar 1, neither more
nor fewer.'' The module's \dtwoChiralityWallMasterThms{} declarations are
almost entirely arithmetic assertions about the fields of a record of
integers: \texttt{bridge\_gs\_gt\_evasion} is $2 \le 9$,
\texttt{pillar1\_nogo\_requires\_all} is $9 = 9$,
\texttt{pillar2\_non\_on\_site} is $1 > 0$, and
\texttt{pillar3\_witten\_in\_z16} is $8 \times 2 = 16$. The one
exception is \texttt{pillar2\_chiral\_symmetry\_exists}, which is
\eqref{eq:gt-commutation} restated. The module records that a set of
declared integers is mutually consistent; it does not prove a
correspondence between two constructions, and we do not present it as
doing so.

\subsection{Lower-dimensional evidence for the $4{+}1$d conjecture}

The chirality-wall program rests on one genuinely open hypothesis: that a
$4{+}1$d SPT slab admits a gapped interface trivialisation exactly when
its bulk anomaly cancels. In the library this is the tracked
\texttt{Prop} \texttt{TPFConjecture} (in \texttt{SPTClassification},
formerly \texttt{axiom gapped\_interface\_axiom}, converted to a tracked
\texttt{Prop} on 2026-05-19), consumed by downstream theorems as an

Supporting evidence exists in lower dimensions.
Fidkowski and Kitaev~\cite{FidkowskiKitaev2010} show that the free-fermion

$\hat T^2 = 1$ is broken to $\mathbb{Z}_8$ by interactions, and the
mechanism is that quartic interactions \emph{can} gap out the dangling
Majorana modes for eight parallel chains --- in their words, \emph{``it
turns out that we can use non-trivial quartic interactions to gap out the
dangling Majorana modes for the case of 8 Majorana chains.''} Where the
construction fails is at four chains, where the only available quartic
interaction leaves a doubly degenerate ground state.

We flag this because the superseded draft got both halves backwards. It
described the result as a reduction $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}_{16}$ and
asserted that ``eight Majorana wires cannot be gapped without breaking
time-reversal.'' The modulus is $8$, not $16$; the $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ of
this paper belongs to the $\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}$ five-bordism
computation and is a different object in a different dimension and
symmetry class; and eight chains are exactly the case that \emph{can} be
gapped. The bibliography entry was also for a different paper by the
same two authors, and is corrected below.

The library's model of the eight-Majorana system is a $16 \times 16$
integer matrix $W$ (\texttt{FKGappedInterface}) with genuinely computed
spectral data: $\operatorname{Tr} W = 0$ (\texttt{W\_trace}),
$\operatorname{Tr} W^2 = 224$ (\texttt{W\_frobenius}), symmetry
(\texttt{W\_symmetric}), commutation with the fermion-parity operator
(\texttt{W\_commutes\_parity}), the minimal polynomial
$W^3 + 12W^2 - 28W = 0$ (\texttt{W\_minimal\_poly}), and an explicit
ground-state eigenvector, $W v = -14 v$ with $v \neq 0$
(\texttt{eigenvalue\_ground}, \texttt{gs\_vec\_nonzero}). Together with
the multiplicity system $\{-14,0,+2\}$ at multiplicities $\{1,8,7\}$,
consistent with the trace, Frobenius norm and dimension
(\texttt{multiplicity\_system}), these determine the gap
$\Delta = 0 - (-14) = 14$. We cite these; we do not cite the declaration
named \texttt{FK.spectral\_gap}, whose complete statement is the integer
identity $0 - (-14) = 14$ and which is not a spectral theorem.

In $1{+}1$d the K-matrix invertible TQFT of the $(3450)$ model is
formalised in \texttt{VillainHamiltonian}, and the SPT stacking law

Fidkowski--Kitaev's $\mathbb{Z}_8$, and the two must not be conflated.

\section{Occurrences of sixteen}
\label{sec:sixteens}

The integer sixteen appears in this paper in several places, and it is
worth saying which appearances are related and which are not, because the
superseded draft enumerated four ``independent occurrences'' and let the
enumeration do argumentative work it cannot do.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Weyl count.} A $\nu_R$-completed generation has sixteen
left-handed Weyl components, eq.~\eqref{eq:sixteen-count}. This is a
fact about the Standard Model's representation content.

\item \textbf{The Dai--Freed index.} $\Omega_5^{\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}}
\cong \mathbb{Z}_{16}$, so a spectrum of charge-one Weyl fermions is
anomaly-free exactly when its count is a multiple of sixteen. This is
\cite{GarciaEtxebarria2019}'s computation, and it is an \emph{input}
here: the library's \texttt{dai\_freed\_spin\_z4} is a placeholder, and
no theorem of ours computes the bordism group.

Items 1 and 2 are genuinely related, and the relation is the content of
\S\ref{sec:z16-drinfeld}: the Standard Model's count saturates the
period. That is one coincidence, and it is the interesting one.

\item \textbf{Rokhlin's theorem.} The signature of a closed smooth spin
$4$-manifold is divisible by sixteen. Section~\ref{sec:signatures}
proves the algebraic half of this ($8 \mid \sigma$ for every even
unimodular form) unconditionally and isolates the residual factor of two
as an input requiring smoothness. Whether this sixteen has anything to
do with items 1--2 is not established here, and we do not assert it.
Connecting them would require the Smith homomorphism and the computed
identification $\Omega_4^{\mathrm{Pin}^+} \cong \mathbb{Z}_{16}$, neither
of which is in the library.

\item \textbf{The Kitaev sixteenfold way.} The chiral central charge of
the $\nu$-th phase in Kitaev's sixteenfold classification is
$c_{-}(\nu) = \nu/2$, and the sixteen phases are labelled by
$\nu \bmod 16$. The library formalises the algebraic core of this in
\texttt{KitaevSixteenFold}: the period statement
$c_{-}(\nu + 16) = c_{-}(\nu) + 8$
(\texttt{kitaevCentralCharge\_period16}) and, more to the point,
faithfulness --- for $\nu, \mu \in \{0,\dots,15\}$ the central charges
agree modulo $8$ if and only if $\nu = \mu$
(\texttt{kitaevCentralCharge\_faithful}), which is what pins the period
at exactly sixteen. Exactly eight of the sixteen classes have integral
central charge (\texttt{kitaev\_eight\_bosonic\_phases},
\texttt{kitaev\_integral\_charge\_iff\_even}), and the Standard Model's
sixteen components land on the trivial class
(\texttt{sm\_realizes\_trivial\_kitaev\_class}). All are kernel-pure.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A correction of attribution.}
The superseded draft attributed item 4 to ``Kitaev's DIII period-16
classification of free-fermion topological phases,'' citing the periodic
table~\cite{Kitaev2009}. We have read that paper's Tables~1 and~2
directly. Its subject is gapped phases of \emph{noninteracting}
fermions classified by Bott periodicity; the periodicity is \emph{eight}
in real $K$-theory and two in complex, the invariant groups running
$\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}_2, \mathbb{Z}_2, 0, \mathbb{Z}, 0, 0, 0$ over
$q \bmod 8$; class DIII in three dimensions carries a $\mathbb{Z}$
invariant, exemplified there by $^3$He-$B$. No sixteen appears anywhere
in that classification. The ``free-fermion'' half of the old phrase was
right and the ``period-16 DIII'' half was wrong.

\texttt{KitaevSixteenFold} formalises. We cite it accordingly and retain
\cite{Kitaev2009} only for the free-fermion periodic table itself.

\section{The SPT and categorical layer}
\label{sec:spt-layer}

Interpreting the stacking of $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$-classified phases
categorically requires a Drinfeld centre. The minimal layer needed is
\begin{equation}
Z(\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}) \cong D(\mathbb{Z}/2),
\end{equation}
the toric-code modular tensor category with four abelian anyons
$\{1,e,m,em\}$ and total quantum dimension $D^2 = 4$. The library
verifies the monoidal $\mathrm{Vec}_G$ instance for finite abelian $G$
(\texttt{VecGMonoidal}, \dtwoVecGMonoidalThms{} theorems), the braiding
structure (\texttt{ToricCodeCenter}, \dtwoToricCodeCenterThms{}
theorems) and the centre equivalence (\texttt{CenterEquivalenceZ2},
\dtwoCenterEquivalenceThms{} theorems). The non-trivial mutual braiding
$B(e,m) = -1$ (\texttt{ToricCodeCenter.braiding\_em\_is\_minus\_one}) is
a computed statement about the braiding phase function, and it is the
categorical avatar of the order-two element of $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$. The
generalisation to $Z(\mathrm{Vec}_{S_3})$ and to Drinfeld doubles $D(G)$
for arbitrary finite $G$ is deferred to a companion
paper~\cite{Roehm2026D4}; the argument here needs only the abelian
fragment.

\paragraph{A material exhibit.}
The noncentrosymmetric triplet superconductor
NbRe~\cite{Colangelo2025NbReTripletSpinValve}, recently established as an
intrinsic equal-spin-triplet superconductor by inverse-spin-valve
measurements on Py/NbRe/Py heterostructures, is a physical exhibit of
DIII-class structure: its crystal lacks inversion symmetry, enabling
antisymmetric spin--orbit coupling on the $\sim\!10\,\mathrm{meV}$ scale
at $T_c \approx 8.7\,\mathrm{K}$, and its pairing channel is triplet.
The library witnesses NbRe in the DIII class
(\texttt{NbReTripletSPT.nbRe\_is\_DIII\_topological}) against the
falsifier of elemental Nb, a centrosymmetric $s$-wave singlet
(\texttt{elementalNb\_not\_DIII\_topological}), and evaluates the
Fu--Kane TRIM-product Pfaffian $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant
\begin{equation}
\delta(\mathrm{sc}) = \prod_{k \in \mathrm{TRIMs}}
\operatorname{sgn}\bigl(\mathrm{Pf}\,w(\mathrm{sc},k)\bigr) \in \{\pm 1\}
\end{equation}
to $-1$ on any DIII-topological capsule
(\texttt{fuKaneInvariant\_eq\_neg\_one\_of\_DIII\_topological}).

The scope of that universal quantification must be stated precisely,
because the theorem's name overstates it. The universality is over the
parameterised \texttt{SCParameters} capsule under a chosen substrate
model --- a four-conjunct conditional on the sewing-matrix generators
encoding the standard inversion-sign and triplet-$d$-vector profile ---
and \emph{not} a derivation from a microscopic noncentrosymmetric BdG
Hamiltonian. The model reproduces the canonical Pfaffian sign count on
the two physically relevant quadrants; the other two quadrants are
disclosed boundary conditions of the substrate, pinned by
\texttt{fuKaneInvariant\_zero\_on\_degenerate} and
\texttt{pfaffianSign\_at\_gamma\_noncentrosymmetric\_singlet}. Note also
that this DIII class is the free-fermion one of~\cite{Kitaev2009}, whose
three-dimensional invariant is $\mathbb{Z}$; the connection to a sixteen
is not established by anything in this section.

\section{What the formalisation certifies}
\label{sec:ledger}

This bundle's declared closure is \bundleTheorems{} Lean theorems across
\bundleModules{} modules, built by \texttt{lake build} against Mathlib
\mathlibcommit{} on toolchain \leantoolchain{}, with \axiomcount{}
project-local axioms and zero \texttt{sorry}. Those numbers say that
everything type-checks. They do not say that every declaration's
statement means what its name suggests, and for a paper of this kind the
second question is the one a referee should ask. This section answers it
for the declarations this bundle registers.

\subsection{Statements that carry their prose}

The following are cited above as backing, and in each case the Lean
statement is the mathematical content claimed for it:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{ModularInvarianceConstraint.framing\_anomaly\_constraint}
--- $\forall c \in \mathbb{Z},\; e^{2\pi i c/24} = 1 \iff 24 \mid c$.

\subsection{Tracked hypotheses and open conjectures}

Four algebraic-topology results enter as tracked hypotheses. All are
established mathematics with standard published proofs, tracked only
because Mathlib lacks the corresponding infrastructure; their epistemic
status is entirely different from that of the genuinely open
\texttt{TPFConjecture}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1}: the ko-cohomology computation~\cite{Adams1974}.
\item \textbf{H2}: the Hom-tensor change-of-rings adjunction
$\mathrm{Hom}_A(A \otimes_{A(1)} M, N) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{A(1)}(M,N)$.
\textbf{Open}, as above.
\item \textbf{H3}: convergence of the Adams spectral sequence.
\item \textbf{H4}: the Anderson--Brown--Peterson splitting of
$\mathrm{MSpin}$ at the prime $2$~\cite{ABP1967}.

Separately, the $\mathrm{Ext}^n_{A(1)}(\mathbb{F}_2,\mathbb{F}_2)$

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

\subsection{What would falsify what}

The claims of this paper fail in different ways, and separating them is
the point of the preceding section.

The generation constraint $3 \mid N_f$ falls if Wang's reduction is
wrong, or if the boundary theory's framing anomaly is not $c_{-}$, or if
the per-Weyl-component counting convention is not $1/2$. It also has a
direct observational falsifier: an internally consistent Standard-Model
extension with $N_f \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$. Wang himself notes that a
fourth family acquiring mass by symmetric mass generation rather than the
Higgs mechanism is not excluded by present experiment, so this is not an
idle hypothetical.

The $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ content constraint falls if
$\Omega_5^{\mathrm{Spin}^{\mathbb{Z}_4}} \not\cong \mathbb{Z}_{16}$, or
if the $\mathbb{Z}_4$ of \S\ref{sec:z4-generator} is not a symmetry of
the ultraviolet completion --- and Garc\'ia-Etxebarria and Montero note
that the low-energy $\nu_R$ Majorana mass breaks it explicitly.

The exclusion of \S\ref{sec:composition} falls with either of the above,
since it is their composition; and it is weakened, without being
refuted, by any additional sector supplying the missing $c_{-} = 3/2$,
which is what Wang proposes.

The signature theorem of \S\ref{sec:signatures} does not fall with any of
these. It falls only if the Lean kernel is unsound or the statement is
misread, and the statement is displayed in full there for exactly that
reason.

\subsection{What would close the remaining gaps}

Three items would materially change the ledger of
\S\ref{sec:vacuity-ledger}. Formalising the $A(1) \to A$ adjunction as
an isomorphism, rather than a dimension identity, would discharge H2 and
is the smallest of the three. Mathlib cobordism infrastructure would
turn \texttt{dai\_freed\_spin\_z4} from a placeholder into a theorem and
would make item 2 of \S\ref{sec:sixteens} internal rather than imported.
The Smith homomorphism together with
$\Omega_4^{\mathrm{Pin}^+} \cong \mathbb{Z}_{16}$ would decide whether
items 3 and 4 of \S\ref{sec:sixteens} are related to items 1 and 2 at
all --- which is the question the ``sixteen convergence'' framing
presupposed an answer to.

\subsection{Cross-bundle scope}

Companion papers cover material presupposed or extended here. The
$Z(\mathrm{Vec}_{S_3})$ Drinfeld centre and the Hopf-algebra structure
supporting the qubit-encoding results are developed
in~\cite{Roehm2026D4}. A four-page Letter treatment of the framing
constraint is~\cite{Roehm2026L2}. The Lean library is described
in~\cite{Roehm2026I2} and the verification methodology
in~\cite{Roehm2026I1}.
