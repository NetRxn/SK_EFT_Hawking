\begin{abstract}
We adapt the Schwinger-Keldysh dissipative effective field theory (SK-EFT)
framework to predict analog Hawking radiation in graphene Dirac fluid flow.
The graphene Dirac fluid near the charge neutrality point is a natively
relativistic 2+1D system with sound speed $c_s = v_F/\sqrt{2} \approx
\SI{710}{\kilo\meter/\second}$ (monolayer) set by the conformal equation
of state $\varepsilon = 2p$. A sonic horizon forms where the drift velocity
reaches~$c_s$---a condition already achieved by the Dean group at Columbia
(Geurs~\textit{et~al.}, 2025) in a bilayer graphene de~Laval nozzle.

For the Dean bilayer nozzle, we predict $T_H \approx \SI{2.4}{\kelvin}$
(nine orders of magnitude above BEC), with adopted EFT correction estimates
(adiabaticity $D = 0.23$, dispersive correction $\delta_{\rm disp} = -2.8\%$).
The dissipative correction $\delta_{\rm diss} \approx +6.5\%$ is the larger
of the two and carries the opposite sign, so the corrections partially
cancel: the viscous damping rate is
$\Gamma_H \approx \SI{1.3e11}{\per\second}$ against a surface gravity
$\kappa = \SI{2e12}{\per\second}$. The Hawking
signal manifests as excess current noise $\Delta S_I \approx
\SI{e-26}{\ampere\squared/\hertz}$ across a \SIrange{0.5}{85}{\giga\hertz}
band---roughly 1\% of the thermal Johnson-Nyquist background---extractable
via broadband correlation analysis. The adopted noise response
$\Delta S_I(\omega) = 2\hbar\omega\,\sigma_Q\,\Gamma(\omega)\,n_H(\omega)$,
motivated by Keldysh FDT and Landauer-B\"uttiker/Bogoliubov analogies,
gives model statistical integration times under one second for $5\sigma$
under the assumed electrical coupling and readout. Target bilayer current
coupling, contact/channel normalization and microscopic route equivalence
remain to be derived; transmission, circuit transfer and background require
independent calibration. We adopt the BEC infrared scalar
$\Gamma_0=4c_Rv/(c_R+v)^2\approx0.99994$ at the supplied speed ratio;
its source does not derive bilayer transmission or finite-frequency
roll-off. The supplied quasi-1D envelope's $1.8\%$ illustration is not a
physical finite-width certificate. No achieved sensitivity or usable
physical band follows from these assumptions.

For the stated charged conformal assumptions, the first-order roster
contains 2~coefficients ($\eta$, $\sigma_Q$), matching the numerical
1+1D BEC count. The independent charged 2+1D second-order basis,
integration-by-parts quotient and applicable KMS/positivity constraints
remain to be constructed; an approximate count of nine is unestablished. The giant Wiedemann-Franz violation
($L/L_0 > 200\times$, Majumdar~\textit{et~al.}, 2025) arises from two-channel
transport: charge ($\sigma_Q$) and heat ($\kappa_{\rm th} \propto s^2/\sigma_Q$),
consistent with the SK-EFT two-channel structure. The adopted formulas
have scalar and algebraic Lean~4 checks (58~theorems, zero sorry), with
numerical illustrations from a Python computation pipeline.
No test cardinality or fresh test-run receipt is asserted here.
These checks do not derive the physical electrical response or establish
microscopic route equivalence or calibrated detection sensitivity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Analog gravity---the study of effective curved spacetimes in condensed matter
systems---has matured from a theoretical proposal~\cite{Unruh1981} to an
experimental science. Steinhauer's observation of thermal Hawking radiation
at $T_H \approx \SI{0.35}{\nano\kelvin}$ in a $^{87}$Rb Bose-Einstein
condensate (BEC)~\cite{deNova2019} confirmed the Planckian spectrum predicted
by Hawking, while polariton superfluids have demonstrated negative-energy
waves~\cite{Falque2025}. These systems share a common theoretical
infrastructure: the acoustic metric theorem of Unruh~\cite{Unruh1981} and
Visser~\cite{Visser1998}, which maps phonon propagation in a moving fluid to
massless scalar fields on a curved background.

The Schwinger-Keldysh (SK) effective field theory framework of
Crossley, Glorioso, and Liu~\cite{CGL2017a,CGL2017b} provides a systematic
derivative expansion for dissipative corrections to the acoustic Hawking
temperature. Papers~1--4 of this series applied this framework to 1+1D BEC
phonon hydrodynamics, establishing: (i)~a transport coefficient counting
formula ${\rm count}(N) = \lfloor(N{+}1)/2\rfloor + 1$; (ii)~CGL
fluctuation-dissipation relations (FDR) constraining noise from dissipation;
(iii)~adopted WKB/Bogoliubov and noise carriers with scalar corrections;
and (iv)~formal checks of stated algebraic hypotheses/consequences. These
do not complete physical mode/state or observable-error derivations.

In this paper, we extend the SK-EFT framework to the \emph{graphene Dirac
fluid}---a natively relativistic 2+1D system where electron-hole
quasiparticles propagate at the Fermi velocity $v_F \approx
\SI{e6}{\meter/\second}$. Three recent experimental developments motivate
this extension:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Geurs~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{Geurs2025} demonstrated \emph{supersonic
electron flow} through a de~Laval nozzle in bilayer graphene, creating the
first electronic sonic horizon ($v > c_s \approx \SI{440}{\kilo\meter/\second}$).
\item Majumdar~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{Majumdar2025} measured universal quantum
critical transport at the Dirac point: $\sigma_Q \approx 4\,e^2/h$, a
Wiedemann-Franz violation exceeding $200\times$, and $\eta/s$ within a factor
of~4 of the Kovtun-Son-Starinets (KSS) bound~\cite{KSS2005}.
\item Zhao~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{Zhao2023} directly measured the electronic
sound speed $c_s = v_F/\sqrt{2}$, confirming the conformal equation of state.
\end{enumerate}

The predicted Hawking temperature for the Dean nozzle, $T_H \approx
\SI{2.4}{\kelvin}$, is \emph{nine orders of magnitude} larger than in BEC.
A constant relation $c_s<v_F$ does not establish the sign of the
large-$k$ collective dispersion, nor eliminate microscopic sensitivity
of the Hawking spectrum. Subluminal dispersion can be studied as a
separate model; its actual bilayer validity, cutoff and controlled error
must be derived at the operating point.

\section{Acoustic Metric for the Dirac Fluid}
\label{sec:metric}

\subsection{Conformal equation of state}

The graphene Dirac fluid near the charge neutrality point (CNP) is described
by relativistic hydrodynamics with the conformal equation of state
$\varepsilon = 2p$ (tracelessness of $T^\mu{}_\mu$ in 2+1D). The speed of
sound follows from thermodynamics:
\begin{equation}
c_s^2 = \frac{\partial p}{\partial \varepsilon} = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad
c_s = \frac{v_F}{\sqrt{2}} \approx \SI{710}{\kilo\meter/\second}.
\label{eq:sound_speed}
\end{equation}
This was confirmed experimentally by Zhao~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{Zhao2023}
via hydrodynamic plasmon spectroscopy.

\subsection{The 3$\times$3 acoustic metric}

For quasi-1D flow along~$x$ with velocity $v(x)$ in the Dirac fluid, the
acoustic metric in lab coordinates $(t, x, y)$ is~\cite{Bilic1999}:
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu} = \Omega^2
\begin{pmatrix}
-(c_s^2 - v^2)/v_F^2 & -v/v_F^2 & 0 \\
-v/v_F^2 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega^2 = (n_0/(w_0 c_s))^2$ is the conformal factor.

The \emph{sonic horizon} forms where $v(x_H) = c_s = v_F/\sqrt{2}$: the
$g_{tt}$ component vanishes, and the flow transitions from subsonic to
supersonic. This is the analog of the event horizon in Schwarzschild
coordinates.

\subsection{Block-diagonal structure}

For quasi-1D flow (translational symmetry in~$y$), the metric
block-diagonalizes:
\begin{equation}
G^{3\times 3} = \begin{pmatrix}
G^{2\times 2}_{(t,x)} & 0 \\ 0 & g_{yy}
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $g_{yy} = \Omega^2$ decouples, and the $(t,x)$ block has the same
algebraic structure as the BEC acoustic metric with $c_s \to v_F/\sqrt{2}$.
This permits algebraic reuse on corresponding parameter records, not
transfer of the BEC physical PDE, EOS/transport, quantum state, boundaries,
normalized scattering or electrical observable. Actual bilayer matching
and reduction errors remain required; transverse transmission is one
part of that duty, not the only new physical effect.

A prospective quasi-1D reduction requires transverse hydrodynamics
($W\gg l_{\rm ee}$) and the actual flow/boundary problem; $v_y=0$ on
the symmetry axis alone does not establish it. For the supplied Dean
illustration ($W\sim\SI{1}{\micro\meter}$,
$l_{\rm ee}\sim\SI{50}{\nano\meter}$), $W/l_{\rm ee}\sim20$ is a
scale comparison, not an error bound. Finite width can affect the
inferred gradient, state and observable as well as transmission; the
block identity does not protect a physical leading temperature.

This block-diagonalization is formally proved in Lean~4
(\texttt{diracFluidMetric\_y\_offdiag\_zero},
\texttt{diracFluidMetric\_gyy}).

\subsection{Bilayer graphene: approximate conformality}
\label{sec:bilayer}

Our primary target---the Dean group bilayer nozzle---uses \emph{bilayer}
graphene, which has quadratic (not linear) band touching and is not
strictly conformal. A proposed hydrodynamic regime ($T\gg T_{\rm imp}$,
$\omega\ll\Gamma_{\rm ee}$) does not remove the band dependence of
its thermodynamics, inertia and current. Their actual collective
constitutive closure must be matched rather than assumed to be a
relativistic Dirac fluid. The
$c_s\approx\SI{440}{\kilo\meter/\second}$ reference in
Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025} is a source-calculated Fermi-liquid estimate
at a specified typical carrier density, not an independent measured
sound speed or a derivation from a conformal EOS. Its use here is
an adopted input; actual density, temperature and gate matching remain
required. The acoustic metric~\eqref{eq:metric} and Hawking temperature
formula~\eqref{eq:T_H} can be evaluated at that supplied $c_s$.
Their physical bilayer applicability requires actual constitutive,
inertia/current, gate/boundary and state matching, not only a speed
substitution.

The conformal assumption affects bulk viscosity and higher-order
transport. The capped QCD-inspired illustration
$\zeta/\eta\lesssim0.1$~\cite{Romatschke2010} is an adopted
fixed-input model estimate, not an established upper bound for
this bilayer device. It supplies no bound on the physical effect
of nonconformality on $T_H$, $\delta_{\rm disp}$ or the detector
error. The actual bilayer constitutive/inertia/current reduction,
including the heat/charge response and uncertainty, remains BUILD.
Bilayer-specific illustrations retain their supplied sound-speed
input rather than deriving it from a conformal EOS.

\subsection{Hawking temperature from surface gravity}

\begin{equation}
T_H = \frac{\hbar \kappa}{2\pi k_B}, \qquad
\kappa = \left|\frac{dv}{dx}\right|_{\rm horizon},
\label{eq:T_H}
\end{equation}
as the adopted acoustic expression under stationary mode/state and horizon
hypotheses. Its scalar form contains no embedding-dimension argument;
physical bilayer applicability still requires those actual hypotheses.
Bias variations on scales $\gtrsim\SI{1}{\micro\second}$ are slow
relative to the supplied $\tau_H=1/\kappa\sim\SI{1}{\pico\second}$,
a necessary scale comparison, not a complete stationarity/noise certificate.
For the smooth-nozzle illustration $\kappa\approx c_s/L$ is adopted;
the actual heated profile and gradient require independent determination.

\subsection{Dispersive correction}

We adopt the full phenomenological dispersive correction below,
including its nonzero quadratic leading term. With the model estimate
$l_{\rm ee}=\hbar v_F/(k_BT)$ and $D=\kappa l_{\rm ee}/c_s$,
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\rm disp} = -\frac{\pi}{6} D^2.
\label{eq:delta_disp}
\end{equation}
For the Dean nozzle at $T = \SI{150}{\kelvin}$: $l_{\rm ee} \approx
\SI{51}{\nano\meter}$, $D \approx 0.23$, $\delta_{\rm disp} \approx -2.8\%$.
This is an adopted-model parameter estimate, not a controlled physical-error certificate.

The correction is \emph{negative} (cooling): the effective temperature is
$T_{\rm eff} = T_H(1 + \delta_{\rm disp}) \approx \SI{2.36}{\kelvin}$.
This is formally proved positive for $D^2 < 6/\pi$ in Lean~4
(\texttt{graphene\_T\_eff\_positive}).

\subsection{Dissipative correction: comparable, and opposite in sign}

The dissipative correction from viscous damping at the horizon is
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\rm diss} = \frac{\Gamma_H}{\kappa}, \qquad
\Gamma_H = \nu \left(\frac{\kappa}{c_s}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where $\nu$ is an illustrative momentum-diffusion closure. Its
relativistic form below does not derive the quadratic-band bilayer
momentum susceptibility or viscosity; expressing it in measured $c_s$
does not supply that matching. Actual charge--energy transport remains
BUILD. Because the momentum
density of a relativistic fluid is $w/v_F^2$,
\begin{equation}
\nu = \frac{\eta\,v_F^2}{w} = \frac{\eta}{s T}\,v_F^2
    = 2\,\frac{\eta}{s T}\,c_s^2 ,
\end{equation}
the last equality holding for a conformal fluid ($c_s = v_F/\sqrt{2}$).
The $d = 2$ sound-attenuation bracket $[2(d-1)/d]\eta + \zeta$ is exactly
$1\cdot\eta$ with $\zeta = 0$, so no $O(1)$ factor survives; the familiar
$4/3$ is the $d = 3$ value. With $\eta/s \approx 4 \times \hbar/(4\pi k_B)$
(Majumdar~\textit{et~al.}) and $T \sim \SI{150}{\kelvin}$:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_H \approx \SI{1.3e11}{\per\second}, \qquad
\delta_{\rm diss} \approx +6.5\%.
\end{equation}
Writing $\eta/(sT)$, a \emph{time}, in place of $\nu$ omits the
velocity$^2$ and understates $\delta_{\rm diss}$ by eleven orders of
magnitude. Restored, the dissipative correction exceeds the dispersive
$-2.8\%$ and cancels part of it, leaving a net $+3.7\%$. These percent-scale model terms require physical bilayer transport
matching and uncertainty bounds before being interpreted as measurable corrections.

\section{Noise Spectrum and Detection Protocol}
\label{sec:detection}

\subsection{Current noise power spectrum}

We model a Hawking contribution to the electrical-noise response at frequencies
$\omega\sim k_BT_H/\hbar$, with cyclic
$f_H=\omega_H/(2\pi)\approx\SI{51}{\giga\hertz}$. The adopted
noise PSD is motivated by Keldysh FDT and Landauer-B\"uttiker/Bogoliubov
analogies. The physical target-current coupling, contact/channel normalization
and equivalence of microscopic derivations require construction; neither
analogy nor the scalar identity in Sec.~\ref{sec:lean} establishes them.

\textit{Keldysh analogy.}---The equilibrium Callen-Welton
FDT~\cite{CallenWelton1951} gives the symmetrized noise
$S_I(\omega) = \hbar\omega\coth(\hbar\omega/2k_BT)\,\mathrm{Re}\,G(\omega)$.
For the adopted response we model the pair contribution with the
mode-resolved distribution $F_u(\omega) = 1 + 2\Gamma(\omega)\,n_H(\omega)$
in place of the thermal $\coth$,
where $\Gamma(\omega) \leq 1$ is the greybody transmission
factor~\cite{AndersonBFP2014Graybody}. An equilibrium identity alone does not
establish this distribution or its electrical-current weight at the driven
nozzle. Its modeled \emph{excess} over the vacuum floor motivates the
response below.

\textit{Scattering analogy.}---B\"uttiker's multichannel noise
formula~\cite{Blanter2000} and Bogoliubov-mixing
channels~\cite{AnantramDatta1996} motivate the adopted response. For a 2D conductor we use
$N_{\rm eff} = \sigma_Q h/(2e^2)$ as an assumed effective channel
normalization. A physical bilayer scattering-current derivation, including
contacts, horizon state and circuit transfer, and its agreement with a
microscopic Keldysh calculation remain required. Under these assumptions,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Delta S_I(\omega) = 2\hbar\omega\,\sigma_Q\,\Gamma(\omega)\,
n_H(\omega)\;}
\label{eq:noise_psd}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_Q\approx4\,e^2/h$ is the supplied conductance reference
for the Dean device and $\Gamma(\omega)$ is an adopted transmission.
The leading occupation is $[e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT_H}-1]^{-1}$.
The plotted corrected numerical carrier instead uses
$T_{\rm eff}=T_H(1+q)$ with $q=\delta_{\rm disp}+\delta_{\rm diss}$,
then divides its bare Planck factor by $1-\delta_k$ and adds the
separate floor. It is distinct from the formal ratio/modified-unitarity
carrier in D1; physical current/state/normalization matching is required.

The Lean identity \texttt{GrapheneNoiseFormula.fdt\_consistency} substitutes
unit greybody factor into the defined product and simplifies it by ring
normalization. It does not derive a current observable, a physical
fluctuation-dissipation relation or equality of microscopic routes.

The \emph{dimensional consistency} of Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise_psd} is
$[\hbar\omega]\times[\sigma_Q] = \SI{}{\joule}\times\SI{}{\siemens} =
\SI{}{\ampere\squared\second} = \SI{}{\ampere\squared/\hertz}$, which
is correct.
An earlier version of this analysis used the prefactor $(2e^2/\pi)$
instead of $2\hbar$; this is dimensionally \emph{wrong} (units $C^2$,
not $J{\cdot}s$), off by a factor of $2e^2/h \approx \SI{77.5}{\micro\siemens}$
(twice the conductance quantum $G_0 = e^2/h$). The corrected formula changes the predicted
noise level by several orders of magnitude relative to that incorrect
expression.

The infrared scalar
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_0 \;=\; \frac{4\,c_R\,v}{(c_R + v)^2},
\label{eq:gamma0}
\end{equation}
is derived by Anderson \emph{et al.}~\cite{AndersonBFP2014Graybody},
Eq.~36, for the specified transversely frozen hydrodynamic BEC with

Its profile-independence statement is an infrared result within that
model, not a graphene transfer or finite-band theorem. Adopting
$v/c_R=0.985$ gives $157600/157609\approx0.99994$.
We separately adopt $\Gamma(\omega)=\Gamma_0[1-(\omega/\omega_{\max})^2]$,
clipped to $[0,1]$, and $\omega_{\max}=\sqrt{\kappa c_s/l_{ee}}$.
With $\omega_H=\kappa/(2\pi)$, the literal $51$~nm inputs give
$D=51/220$ and $(\omega_{\max}/\omega_H)^2=4\pi^2/D$, about
$13\,\omega_H$. Suppression at $4\omega_H$ is
$4D/\pi^2=51/(55\pi^2)\approx9.4\%$, not a few-percent flatness
bound. Rounded $D=0.23$ and the platform's derived length are separate

${\rm SNR}_{\rm cumul}^2=\sum_i{\rm SNR}_{{\rm bin},i}^2\Delta f\,t$,

$[0.5,4]\omega_H$. Full-grid model statistics must not be silently
assigned to either narrower band.

\section{Transport Coefficients in 2+1D}
\label{sec:transport}

\subsection{First-order classification}

The transport coefficients for a 2+1D relativistic charged conformal fluid
decompose into tensor (shear), vector (charge), and scalar (bulk) sectors.
Conformal symmetry ($\varepsilon = 2p$) forces $\zeta = 0$, leaving two
independent first-order dissipative coefficients:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Shear viscosity $\eta$ (tensor sector)
\item Charge conductivity $\sigma_Q$ (vector sector)
\end{enumerate}
This matches the 1+1D BEC count ${\rm count}(1) = 2$---a non-trivial
coincidence, since the 1+1D formula arises from a scalar sector while the
2+1D count involves tensor and vector sectors. The physical content is
entirely different: BEC has bulk damping ($\gamma_1$) and flow-dependent
damping ($\gamma_2$); graphene has shear viscosity and charge conductivity.

\subsection{Second-order basis: outstanding construction}

At second order, tensor, vector and scalar structures must be
classified in the actual charged 2+1D theory. The BRSSS
framework~\cite{BRSSS2008} motivates that construction, but does not
supply the charged 2+1D count used here. In particular, the
Haack--Yarom relation is a result for a restricted holographic class
in boundary spacetime dimension greater than three; in three
spacetime dimensions its shear-squared traceless tensor vanishes,
so its associated coefficient cannot be used to remove one member
of the present roster.\footnote{Haack--Yarom, arXiv:0811.1794v1,
Introduction, Eqs.~(6)--(9), especially the dimension restriction
following Eq.~(9).} An approximate count of nine is
therefore not established by that reduction. Constructing the
independent charged 2+1D basis, its integration-by-parts quotient
and applicable KMS/positivity constraints remains BUILD.

The scalar index formula ${\rm count}(N)=
\lfloor(N{+}1)/2\rfloor+1$ supplies no independent physical
2+1D classification here. The required charged tensor/vector/scalar
structures and their relations must be constructed order by order.

\section{Wiedemann-Franz Violation}
\label{sec:wf}

The reported large Wiedemann--Franz violation near the charge
neutrality point motivates a physical heat/charge response calculation.

\begin{equation}
L = \frac{\kappa_{\rm th}}{\sigma T} = \frac{v_F^2 s^2}{\sigma_Q^2 T}
\label{eq:lorenz}
\end{equation}
where the rightmost expression is supplied, rather than derived
from the device response matrix. At fixed positive $v_F,s,\sigma_Q,T$

Finiteness of $\sigma_Q$ alone therefore proves no neutrality
divergence or physical density regularization. Deriving the actual
Lorenz ratio requires charge/heat current normalization, momentum
relaxation, density/temperature/disorder dependence and contact

order---but does not predict the value of $\sigma_Q$, which is a microscopic

\section{Experimental Proposal}

We propose the following measurement protocol for detecting analog Hawking

($L \sim \SI{200}{\nano\meter}$), hBN-encapsulated, cooled to
$T_{\rm amb} \lesssim \SI{150}{\kelvin}$.

\item \textbf{Detection:} Measure current noise $S_I(\omega)$ across the
nozzle throat using Johnson noise thermometry
(\SIrange{0.5}{85}{\giga\hertz}).

\item \textbf{Analysis:} Calibrate and subtract the adopted white
background $S_{\rm thermal}=4k_BT\sigma_Q$, then fit the actual
raw-PSD template with transmission, $T_{\rm eff}$, multiplier and
floor. A Planck-occupation test additionally divides out the calibrated
$2\hbar\omega\sigma_Q\Gamma(\omega)$ response and treats the
separate floor and normalization explicitly. The leading
$T_H\approx\SI{2.4}{\kelvin}$ is not the corrected raw PSD's
Planck temperature.

\item \textbf{Integration:} Ideal model statistical estimate
$< \SI{1}{\second}$ for $5\sigma$ ($\Gamma \geq 0.01$), subject to the
Gaussian independent-bin estimator and adopted response assumptions above.
Practical integration time and the relative statistical/systematic limits
require actual transfer, background and calibration-error evidence.

\item \textbf{Verification:} Determine the actual sonic gradient
while changing bias or geometry and compare a calibrated spectrum.
$T_H\propto\kappa$ is the reference relation; $\kappa\propto1/L$
requires an extra fixed-profile scaling assumption, not bias alone.
\end{enumerate}

The Kim group's noise thermometer ($\SI{5.5}{\milli\kelvin}$ sensitivity)

\section{Conclusion}

The convergence of three developments---the Dean group's electronic sonic
horizon, Majumdar~\textit{et~al.}'s precision transport data, and the
maturity of the SK-EFT Lean framework---creates a concrete opportunity for
an observation of analog Hawking radiation in an electronic system.
The predicted temperature $T_H \approx \SI{2.4}{\kelvin}$ is nine orders
of magnitude above BEC, the adopted parameter is $D = 0.23$, and the
adopted noise response---motivated by Keldysh FDT and
Landauer-B\"uttiker/Bogoliubov analogies---gives a modeled signal roughly 1\%
of the thermal background. Its ideal radiometer estimates assume Gaussian
noise, independent frequency bins, the adopted electrical response and a
specified bandwidth; they supply no calibrated statistics-versus-systematics
boundary. Relative to BEC, the supplied graphene inputs give a much higher
illustrative $T_H$. Subluminal collective dispersion is a separate assumed
model whose physical bilayer validity and error regime remain to be derived.
The model also has a marginal dissipation ratio
($\omega_H/\Gamma_{\rm mr} \approx 1.6$) and
an adopted infrared scalar $\Gamma_0 \approx 0.99994$
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma0}] (Table~\ref{tab:platforms}); the
two platforms are complementary rather than competing. A physical bilayer
current/noise kernel, independently normalized contacts and circuit transfer,
the two microscopic derivations with their agreement, and calibrated
transmission/background remain required before these model statistics
can establish a usable detection band or sensitivity.

The adopted numerical models give dissipative $+6.5\%$ and dispersive
$-2.8\%$ terms at the stated inputs, leaving $+3.7\%$. They do not
establish dispersive dominance by eleven orders of magnitude;
such a comparison uses a dimensionally incorrect damping scale. The full
quadratic dispersive law and bilayer viscosity/current matching remain
constructive obligations. The charged 2+1D second-order basis
and its independence, integration-by-parts and KMS/positivity analysis
remain BUILD; no approximate nine-coefficient classification is
established here. The stated first-order roster and the reported
Wiedemann--Franz measurements do not establish the physical noise response.

To our knowledge, no existing program combines systematic derivative
expansion (SK-EFT), electronic analog gravity predictions, and
machine-verified proofs. Related approaches---geometric analog gravity
in strained graphene~\cite{Iorio2012}, holographic transport via
AdS/CFT~\cite{KSS2005}---provide complementary perspectives but do not
yield the device-specific noise spectrum predictions needed for
experimental design.
