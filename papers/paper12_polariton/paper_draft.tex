Reservoir-Corrected Predictions from SK-EFT}

\author{SK-EFT Hawking Research Program}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The measured Paris-LKB smooth horizon sets a nominal polariton
Hawking scale near 85 mK. We compute stimulated gain in an
adopted dispersive model and compare cavity decay with the
surface gravity. Under saturated greybody transmission, half gain
occurs at $\omega/\kappa_{\rm eff}=\ln(3)/(2\pi)$. This gives a
single-shot signal-to-noise ratio of five with 100 probe photons
per mode under ideal shot-noise scaling. A physical detection
prediction requires transmission, calibrated readout and background
noise over a usable band.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Analog Hawking radiation --- the quantum emission of phonon pairs at
acoustic horizons in flowing superfluids --- has been confirmed in
atomic BEC~\cite{Steinhauer2016,deNova2019} at temperatures
$T_H \approx 0.35~\text{nK}$. Exciton-polariton condensates offer a
${\sim}10^8$-fold temperature advantage, with predicted $T_H \sim 0.1{-}1~\text{K}$,
due to stronger interactions and sharper spatial gradients achievable
in semiconductor microcavities~\cite{Gerace2012,Nguyen2015}.

The 2025 demonstration of all-optically tunable acoustic horizons with
tailored curvature in a polariton quantum fluid at LKB
Paris~\cite{Falque2025,Giacobino2025} --- including the first observation of
negative-energy Bogoliubov modes in the supersonic (post-horizon) region ---
establishes the reported horizon and excitation profiles.
We evaluate a model for a stimulated measurement at that horizon.

Our analysis incorporates three corrections absent from prior
theoretical predictions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Reservoir correction to $c_s$}: The excitonic reservoir
absorbs 50--75\% of the polariton blueshift without contributing to
the dynamical speed of sound, reducing $c_s$ by a factor of
${\sim}2{-}3$ relative to naive Bogoliubov estimates
\cite{Estrecho2021,Stepanov2019,Claude2023}.

\item \textbf{Stimulated measurement}: CW pump-probe
spectroscopy~\cite{Grisins2016} motivates a stimulated gain measurement.
The measured gain and its uncertainty depend on transmission,
readout transfer and background noise.

\item \textbf{Formal checks}: \texttt{PolaritonTier1.lean}
proves properties of the adopted attenuation factor. The formal
checks cover the stated mathematical relationships.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stimulated Hawking Gain Spectrum}

\subsection{Bogoliubov scattering at the horizon}

A coherent probe at frequency $\omega$ incident on the analog horizon
undergoes anomalous Bogoliubov scattering with amplification
\begin{equation}
G(\omega) = \frac{\Gamma(\omega)}{e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa_{\text{eff}}} - 1}
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma(\omega)$ is the greybody factor.
For the model illustration we adopt
$\kappa_{\text{eff}} = \kappa(1 - c_1 D^2)$ with $c_1=\pi/6$.
At the smooth-horizon baseline $D \approx 0.60$, this gives a model
shift of about $-19\%$. At the steep horizon $D \approx 0.93$ the
same expression gives about $-46\%$, outside the small-correction
regime. A device-specific dispersion law and its error range remain
to be established. At unit transmission in the nondispersive limit,
$G(\omega) \to 1/(e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa} - 1)
= n_{\text{Planck}}(\omega,T_H)$.

For an input probe with $n_{\text{in}}$ quanta:
\begin{equation}
\langle N_\omega^{\text{out}} \rangle = |\alpha_\omega|^2 n_{\text{in}}
+ |\beta_\omega|^2 (n_{\text{in}} + 1)
\end{equation}
where the first term is ordinary scattering, the second is
spontaneous + stimulated Hawking emission with
$|\beta_\omega|^2 = G(\omega)$.

\subsection{Illustrative probe frequencies}

At unit greybody transmission the model gives
$G(0.1\kappa_{\rm eff}) \approx 1.14$,
$G(0.3\kappa_{\rm eff}) \approx 0.18$, and
$G(\kappa_{\rm eff}) \approx 0.002$.
Figure~\ref{fig:gain} uses an adopted upper working edge of
$0.3\kappa_{\rm eff}$. A physical error bound and a calibrated lower
edge remain to be established.

\subsection{Signal-to-noise ratio}

Under ideal shot-noise scaling,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SNR}(\omega) = \sqrt{N_{\rm shots}N_{\rm probe}}\,G(\omega).
\end{equation}
The illustrative case $\omega = 0.1\kappa_{\rm eff}$ with 100 probe
photons per mode gives a single-shot model SNR of about 11.
A detection prediction requires calibrated transmission, readout
transfer and background noise.

\section{Driven-Dissipative Considerations}

The polariton condensate is inherently driven-dissipative.
Key implications for Hawking physics:

\begin{itemize}
\item The acoustic metric description holds in the weak-loss,
long-wavelength limit~\cite{Amelio2020}. The ratio $\kappa\tau_{\text{pol}}$ compares the cavity
lifetime with the inverse surface gravity. A value above one
means the cavity lifetime exceeds that characteristic timescale.

\item The scale $m^* c_s^2/k_B\approx 0.81$ K is the mean-field
energy in temperature units. The stimulated measurement requires an
independent background-noise spectrum.

\item The cited quasinormal mode studies~\cite{Jacquet2023QNM,Burkhard2025}
motivate looking for resonant structure. Using a resonance to choose a
probe frequency requires a calibrated response and noise spectrum.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The measured smooth-horizon gradient and decay rate fix the nominal
temperature and cavity-loss scales used here. The gain curve is
conditional on the adopted dispersion and unit transmission. A
longer cavity addresses one requirement. The proposed experiment
also needs a measured transfer and noise budget over the working band.
