\title{GW170817 confines vestigial susceptibility to a finite interval
under an assumed graviton speed law}

\author{John G.\ Roehm}
\affiliation{NetRxn Foundation}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The conservative GW170817 cap $|\dccap|\le\tau=3\times10^{-15}$ confines $\chivest\ge0$ to $[(1-\tau)^2,(1+\tau)^2]$ under the assumed leading-order law $\cGW=c\sqrt{\chivest}$ and source-emission assumptions.
Here $c$ is the electromagnetic reference speed; the free susceptibility $\chivest$ models normalized dimensionless metric response.
The interval has width $4\tau$, contains $1$, and is over fourteen orders of magnitude narrower than adopted $[0.1,10]$.
Microscopic mode, normalization and range are not derived; only this kinematic model is constrained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spinor-gravity constructions use composite vielbeins built from
fermions~\cite{Diakonov2011,HebeckerWetterich2003}.
Volovik proposes a vestigial phase with metric order preceding a
nonzero tetrad expectation~\cite{Volovik2024Vestigial}.
His symmetry-breaking analysis distinguishes absorbed
Nambu--Goldstone modes from the Higgs-sector gravitational-wave
modes, whose counting also uses coordinate
constraints~\cite{Volovik2022Counting}.
Here we assume that a metric-channel excitation of such a substrate
can be identified with a spin-2 second-sound graviton.

\paragraph{Motivation and assumed speed law.}
Volovik applies Landau two-fluid hydrodynamics to a de~Sitter
state in~\cite{Volovik2026SecondSound}.
The underlying thermodynamic framework treats the gravitational
normal component as stiff matter~\cite{Volovik2024VacuumDecay}.
Within that de~Sitter model, the Landau formula and the stiff-component
thermodynamic relations give a second-sound speed $s_2=c$,
independently of the de~Sitter Hubble parameter $H$.
He discusses several possible graviton interpretations of this
collective mode and leaves its type open.
For the spin-2 substrate identification assumed here, we also assume
the susceptibility-dependent speed law:
\begin{equation}
  \cGW^2(\chivest) = c^2 \cdot \chivest, \qquad
  \cGW(\chivest) = c\sqrt{\chivest},
  \label{eq:cGW}
\end{equation}
where $\chivest$ is the dimensionless, normalized metric-channel
response of the assumed vestigial substrate.
It is a free parameter in this calculation.
The random phase approximation (RPA) resummation models it using
unevaluated bare susceptibility and coupling inputs, so that form
alone supplies no numerical value of $\chivest$.
The underlying
lattice-fermion framework is the one whose unitarity Vergeles
establishes~\cite{Vergeles2025}. Equation~\eqref{eq:cGW} is an
assumed kinematic speed law.
We constrain its free susceptibility without deriving a propagating
second-sound mode from a microscopic action, its propagator, or its
coupling to matter. This missing dynamical bridge is a limitation of
the present calculation, not an impossibility result.

The 2017 binary-neutron-star merger
GW170817~\cite{Abbott2017GW170817} yielded a tight propagation
constraint under the collaboration's conservative distance and
emission-time assumptions. Comparing the gravitational-wave arrival
time at LIGO/Virgo with the gamma-ray signal at Fermi/INTEGRAL gives
$-3 \times 10^{-15} \leq \dccap \leq +7 \times 10^{-16}$
under those assumptions. We use its conservative symmetric envelope,
$|\dccap| \leq \tau$ with $\tau = 3 \times 10^{-15}$.
This cap inherits the assumed source-emission window; alternative
emission histories can weaken the inferred propagation constraint.

Our assumed speed law satisfies the GW170817 bound only on a
sub-interval of our adopted range $\chivest \in [0.1, 10]$ narrower
than that range by more than 14 orders of magnitude. The
GW170817-compatible window $[(1-\tau)^2, (1+\tau)^2]$ has width
$4\tau$ in $\chivest$ and sits strictly interior to our adopted range
$[0.1, 10]$ about the anchor $\chivest = 1$.
Its relative width is $4\tau/9.9 \approx \input{tables/gw_relative_width.tex}\unskip$;
a probability mass additionally requires a specified prior. The result is a fine-tuning constraint on one specific
kinematic identification, not a refutation of emergent
gravity nor of the Akama--Diakonov--Wetterich (ADW) framework.
Three possible mechanisms are discussed below.

\paragraph{Relation to the 2017 constraint sweep.} Using GW170817 to
eliminate a gravity model is not new. Within months of the detection,
four Letters analyzed constraints on specified gravitational actions
and cosmological models. Baker \emph{et al.}~\cite{BakerBellini2017}
constrained scalar-tensor and vector-tensor theories and obtained a
conditional graviton-mass bound in bimetric gravity. Creminelli and
Vernizzi~\cite{CreminelliVernizzi2017} required luminal propagation
to remain robust on nearby cosmological backgrounds to avoid tuning,
reducing the three independent quartic and quintic beyond-Horndeski
functions to one. Sakstein and Jain~\cite{SaksteinJain2017} combined
the GW bound with cluster and stellar tests to constrain
beyond-Horndeski parameters; their separate combination of GW and M87
black-hole constraints made the covariant quartic galileon
cosmologically irrelevant for the order-unity matter couplings
considered. Ezquiaga and
Zumalac\'arregui~\cite{EzquiagaZumalacarregui2017} also discussed
constraints on GW-speed-modifying couplings in Einstein-aether,
some sectors of Ho\v{r}ava gravity, and Generalized Proca theories,
and the impact on TeVeS and MOND-like theories.

These analyses constrain specified actions, EFT operators and cosmological
models under assumptions about backgrounds and matter coupling. This Letter
instead constrains the susceptibility in the assumed
$\cGW=c\sqrt{\chivest}$ identification. It supplies no microscopic
Lagrangian for that identification and constrains no microscopic operator
coefficients. Its roughly one-part-in-$10^{15}$ width comparison within the
adopted order-unity window is neither a prediction of $\chivest$ nor a
prior-independent probability.

A microscopic realization must determine whether its operator-level
mechanisms preserve or alter the assumed speed law and its applicability.
The Discussion considers possible mechanisms; the finite-tolerance
constraint alone derives none of them.

\section{Formal setup and result}
\label{sec:result}

\paragraph{Adopted susceptibility comparison window.}
Our substrate models the vestigial-phase metric-channel susceptibility
by the RPA \emph{resummation}
$\chi_{\rm RPA} = \chi_0/(1 - \gamma_\star \chi_0)$ in the standard
Hertz--Millis convention. Here $\chi_0$ is the bubble integral;
$\chi_{\rm RPA}$ is the resummed response and $\gamma_\star$ the
effective coupling; physical normalization remains undetermined.
The formalized closed form establishes stability properties but
evaluates neither $\chi_0$ nor $\gamma_\star$, so it fixes no
numerical $\chivest$, which is why
$\chivest$ enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:cGW} as a free parameter. We adopt the comparison window $\chivest\in[0.1,10]$,
centered geometrically on the chosen exactly luminal anchor $\chivest=1$.
Neither the RPA identity nor dimensional analysis determines the physical
normalization, center or endpoints. Constructing the physical metric-channel
response, including its normalization and domain, and establishing the
susceptibility window from the microscopic theory remain required. We emphasize the
provenance: the window is a modeling choice, not a
published derivation: the Vergeles lattice
analysis~\cite{Vergeles2025} establishes lattice unitarity for
degree-of-freedom-preserving evolution under its stated function-space
assumptions. It does not compute $\chivest$ or assign it a range. The comparison window also occurs in the companion study of the
linearized Einstein equations~\cite{Roehm2026LinearizedEFE} for
$\alpha_{\rm ADW}$, its free dimensionless coefficient multiplying an
adopted induced-Newton baseline. We use that study's
$\alpha_{\rm ADW}\in[0.1,10]$ range as an order-of-magnitude comparison,
without identifying $\alpha_{\rm ADW}$ with $\chivest$ or deriving either range.

\paragraph{The leading-order $\cGW$.}
Under the assumed law~\eqref{eq:cGW}, a positive susceptibility
and physical reference speed $c>0$ give $\cGW>0$.
For every $c\ne0$, the relative speed deviation is
\[
  \dccap(\chivest)
  \equiv \frac{\cGW(\chivest)-c}{c}
  = \sqrt{\chivest}-1.
\]
On the nonnegative domain this deviation is strictly increasing
and vanishes if and only if $\chivest=1$. Thus unity is the exactly
luminal anchor; finite-tolerance compatibility requires an interval
rather than this single value.

\paragraph{Finite-tolerance compatibility.}
Fix $\tau=3\times10^{-15}$ for the conservative symmetric cap.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-tolerance compatibility]
  \label{thm:bicond}
  For $\chivest\geq0$,
  $|\dccap(\chivest)|\leq\tau
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \chivest\in[(1-\tau)^2,(1+\tau)^2]$.
\end{theorem}

Indeed, the cap is equivalent to
$1-\tau\leq\sqrt{\chivest}\leq1+\tau$.
Since $0<\tau<1$, both bounds are nonnegative, so squaring
gives the stated interval. Conversely, monotonicity of the
square root recovers the cap from these squared bounds.

Every compatible susceptibility therefore satisfies
\[
  0.1<(1-\tau)^2\leq\chivest\leq(1+\tau)^2<10.
\]
The compatible interval contains $\chivest=1$ and has nonzero width exactly
$(1+\tau)^2-(1-\tau)^2=4\tau=\input{tables/gw_compatible_width.tex}\unskip$.
The adopted range has width $9.9$, and the strict comparison is
\[
  10^{14}\bigl[(1+\tau)^2-(1-\tau)^2\bigr]<9.9.
\]
This is a comparison of interval widths within an adopted domain.
It supplies no probability without a prior: under a uniform prior
on $[0.1,10]$, the compatible interval has probability mass
$4\tau/9.9$.

\paragraph{Endpoint checks.}
The endpoints provide quantitative checks of the confinement.

\begin{theorem}[Lower endpoint]
  \label{thm:falsifier-lower}
  At $\chivest=0.1$, $\dccap=\sqrt{0.1}-1\approx-0.6838$,
  hence $|\dccap|/\tau\approx\input{tables/gw_lower_ratio_detailed.tex}\unskip$ and
  the conservative symmetric cap is violated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Upper endpoint]
  \label{thm:falsifier-upper}
  At $\chivest=10$, $\dccap=\sqrt{10}-1\approx+2.1623$,
  hence $|\dccap|/\tau\approx\input{tables/gw_upper_ratio_detailed.tex}\unskip$ and
  the conservative symmetric cap is violated.
\end{theorem}

The exclusions already follow from
$\sqrt{1/10}\leq\sqrt{1/4}=1/2$ and
$\sqrt{10}\geq\sqrt{4}=2$.
Both endpoints therefore fail simultaneously, while the finite
interval in Theorem~\ref{thm:bicond} remains compatible.
Figure~\ref{fig:cGW} shows this distinction.

\paragraph{Reference-speed independence of the endpoint constraints.}
The endpoint exclusion also applies directly to the relative
deviation of the speed itself:
\[
  \left|\frac{\cGW(\chivest)-c}{c}\right|>\tau,
  \qquad
  \chivest\in\{0.1,10\},\quad c\ne0.
\]
This includes the physical domain $c>0$ and follows from the
relative-deviation identity above. Changing $c$ therefore
cannot make either endpoint compatible under the assumed law.

\paragraph{Physical scope.}
This two-point exclusion leaves the compatible interior interval
intact. Neither it nor the $c$-free endpoint statement quantifies
over microscopic actions, propagators or matter couplings, so
neither establishes the absence or impossibility of a dynamical
derivation. A physical application still requires the response
normalization and domain, the physical state, a propagating
tensor mode relative to the same matter/light reference, and
controlled transport and frequency matching with units and
error bounds.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The conservative symmetric cap confines the assumed leading-order
identification to $\chivest\in[(1-\tau)^2,(1+\tau)^2]$.
Its endpoints $1\pm2\tau+\tau^2$ allow susceptibility offsets of
approximately $\pm6\times10^{-15}$ for a speed-deviation tolerance
of $\pm3\times10^{-15}$. Exact luminality anchors this finite
interval at $\chivest=1$; the measurement does not require an exact
fixed point or exclude generic ultraviolet theories.

A separate Kerr--Schild construction uses prescribed null directions
for analog backgrounds (\texttt{admitsKerrSchildForm\_all}). The
single-copy covector remains null for every scalar profile
(\texttt{isVortexLikeChargeDistribution\_all}). The polariton
predicate combines these algebraic properties
(\texttt{isConnectedToPolaritonRingdown\_polariton}). Relating this
construction to the speed test requires a dynamical ringdown model and
a common matter and light reference.

Three possible mechanisms illustrate the unresolved microscopic choices.
First, a deep-infrared symmetry, such as Lorentz invariance of the
emergent metric sector, could make unity an exact fixed point of the
renormalization-group flow. Such a mechanism would place $\chivest$
inside the compatible interval through symmetry; whether it occurs
in the ADW broken phase remains unproved. Second, a distinct
ultraviolet-input metric channel could replace the second-sound
identification and have dynamics that pin its susceptibility to unity.
This is a model-building proposal to be investigated through the
nonlinear emergent action. The missing dynamical bridge supplies no
reason to prefer it. The finite-tolerance result derives neither
mechanism.

Third, frequency dependence or a compensating correction in the LIGO
band could modify the assumed frequency-independent law~\eqref{eq:cGW}.
The product ansatz $\delta_{\rm disp}=\gamma\omega$ is algebraic,
not a derived microscopic derivative expansion. Its formal probe
$\omega=100$ requires a frequency convention and unit normalization
before it can represent $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
The proposed $\gamma=\Gamma_H/\cGW^2$ is unmatched: the same-symbol
horizon damping rate in the acoustic setting establishes no physical
identification with this metric-channel parameter.
The chosen placeholder $|\gamma|\leq10^{-30}$ bounds the correction
below the cap at that numerical probe; it is neither measured nor
derived, and the physical correction could be larger. This bounds
only $\delta_{\rm disp}$, not the total deviation.
In an additive model, an oppositely signed correction can restore
compatibility for offsets just outside the cap. The full additive
exclusion criterion, near-boundary restoration argument and supporting
checks are given in \hyperref[sec:support]{the appendix}.
Deriving the dissipative parameter and the normalization from microscopic
dynamics remains open.

\paragraph{Comparison with the induced Newton coupling.}
The in-preparation companion study~\cite{Roehm2026LinearizedEFE}
uses the emergent Newton-coupling parameterization
$G_{\rm N,emerg}=\alpha_{\rm ADW}\,12\pi/(N_f\Lambda_{\rm UV}^2)$,
where $N_f$ is a fermion-species-count parameter. In units $\hbar=c=1$,
the observed unreduced Planck mass is
$M_{\rm Pl}=(G_{\rm N,obs})^{-1/2}$, with $G_{\rm N,obs}$ the observed
Newton constant. At the adopted cutoff $\Lambda_{\rm UV}=M_{\rm Pl}$,
the exact algebraic match condition is
$\alpha_{\rm ADW}^{\star}=N_f/(12\pi)$.
For the companion's stated count choices, the reported rounded matching values span
$[0.40,1.27]$, inside its adopted $[0.1,10]$ range. Its kernel's Dirac-count
convention and these Weyl-count choices require reconciliation before this
comparison supplies a physical Standard Model normalization.

The companion proves the matching locus and properties of a candidate
coefficient function; it does not extract the microscopic coefficient that
selects the locus. That coefficient and $\chivest$ describe distinct response
normalizations. Our adopted interval contains unity but derives no value of
$\chivest$. The GW170817 cap confines this parameter to the much narrower
compatible interval without supplying a microscopic selection mechanism.

\paragraph{Physical implication.}
Under the assumed speed law and source-emission conditions, GW170817
selects a narrow finite interval of normalized susceptibility,
not a singleton. The adopted comparison range is no derived physical
prior, and its width ratio supplies no probability without a prior.
A physical realization still requires the actual response normalization
and domain, the physical state, and a propagating tensor mode relative
to the same matter/light reference, together with controlled frequency
and transport matching, units and error bounds.
The interval constrains the stated mathematical model while leaving
its microscopic realization open.

\section{Supporting formal results and reproducibility}
\label{sec:support}

This appendix gives the auxiliary predicate and frequency-correction
checks used to delimit the Letter's conditional interval result.
It preserves their domains and counterexamples; none supplies the
missing microscopic mode, response normalization or physical matching.
At the fixed $\tau=3\times10^{-15}$, write
$\verb|LigoSatisfied|(\delta)\equiv|\delta|\leq\tau$.

\subsection{Predicate audit and counterexamples}
The proposed identification imposes positivity (P1), compatibility
with the cap (P2), and sub-half-deviation (P3'):
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    &\verb|H_VestigialModeIsGraviton|(\chivest)\\
    &\quad\equiv
      \chivest>0
      \;\wedge\;\verb|LigoSatisfied|(\dccap(\chivest))\\
    &\qquad\wedge\;|\dccap(\chivest)|<1/2.
  \end{aligned}
\]
These three clauses are not independent.
At the fixed tolerance, P2 implies P3' because $\tau<1/2$.
On the nonnegative domain, Theorem~\ref{thm:bicond} also places
every P2-compatible susceptibility in a strictly positive interval,
so P1 holds there. Consequently, the complete compatible set for
these conditions is precisely that same finite interval, including
the anchor $\chivest=1$.

Four counterexamples distinguish compatibility from positivity
alone. The endpoints $0.1$ and $10$ refute the conjunction through
failure of P2. At zero, P1 fails; this point lies outside the
adopted range. At the interior point $\chivest=1/4$,
$\dccap=-1/2$: P1 holds, but P2 and the strict P3' inequality
both fail. The quarter-point example therefore shows that P1
alone does not imply P3'. These counterexamples do not establish
independence of the three clauses or isolate a uniquely failing
clause. The corresponding definitions and algebraic results are
recorded in \texttt{GravitationalWaves.lean}, Sections~1--4.


\subsection{Illustrative frequency correction and additive bound}
Equation~\eqref{eq:cGW} is
an assumed frequency-independent kinematic law.
A frequency-dependent susceptibility or a compensating correction
in the LIGO band is a possible phenomenological extension.
We consider the algebraic ansatz $\delta_{\rm disp}=\gamma\omega$,
without asserting that it is the leading term of a matched
microscopic derivative expansion.
The formal probe is the real number $\omega=100$.
Interpreting it as a $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$ band requires a physical frequency
convention and unit normalization. Identifying $\gamma=\Gamma_H/\cGW^2$
introduces a proposed, unmatched dissipative parameter $\Gamma_H$ in this
metric channel. The horizon damping rate denoted by the same symbol in the
acoustic setting does not establish that physical identification.
Under the assumed coefficient bound $|\gamma|\leq10^{-30}$,
$|\delta_{\rm disp}|$ at this numerical probe is below the
GW170817 tolerance.
The adopted $10^{-30}$ is a placeholder chosen to keep the
correction below the cap; it is neither derived nor measured.
In the further additive model
$\delta_{\rm tot}=\delta_0+\delta_{\rm disp}$, with
$\delta_0=\sqrt{\chivest}-1$, that assumed coefficient bound gives
$|\delta_{\rm disp}|\leq B=10^{-28}$ at $\omega=100$.
The reverse triangle inequality then excludes restoration of
$|\delta_{\rm tot}|\leq\tau$ whenever $|\delta_0|>\tau+B$.
Offsets just outside $\pm\tau$ can nevertheless become compatible
through an oppositely signed correction within this bound.
Larger corrections are not excluded by the placeholder theorem.
Deriving $\Gamma_H$ and the normalization of this ansatz from
microscopic dynamics remains open.

For a positive numerical probe $\omega$,
\texttt{dispersion\_within\_ligo\_iff} gives

GW170817 tolerance $\tau$, bounding the correction alone.
