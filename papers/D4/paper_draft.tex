\title{Machine-checked universality of the Fibonacci braid representation,\\
with a graded verification of the surrounding modular-tensor-category apparatus}

\author{John Roehm}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We give a machine-checked proof, in Lean~4 and with no unverified
hypotheses, that the three-strand Fibonacci braid representation
$\rho_{\mathrm{Fib}}\colon B_3 \to \mathrm{SU}(2)$ has dense image: for
every $U \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a braid
word whose image agrees with $U$ entrywise to within $\varepsilon$. The
representation is built from an explicit unitary pair satisfying the
braid relation, identified with the Fibonacci $F$- and $R$-symbols on
the literature's authority rather than derived from the fusion category
here; we are precise about that boundary. We then strengthen density to
efficiency, proving that the compilation of a target $U$ returns,
\emph{at one and the same algorithmic level}, both an approximation of
error at most $\varepsilon$ and a braid word of length at most
$C\,(\log 1/\varepsilon)^{c}$ with $c = \log 5 / \log(3/2)$: a
bundling that the existence-only statements of the Solovay--Kitaev
theorem do not provide. Around this result we develop the
modular-tensor-category apparatus it sits in. We give the Chevalley
commutator and Hopf structure of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ and
$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_3)$, with a rank-generic construction covering both;
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_k$ fusion with $S$-matrix unitarity and non-degeneracy
for $k \le 5$, and the Verlinde formula proved for every triple of
labels at $k = 3,4,5$; the Ising category's six hexagon and four ribbon
equations in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})$, with the closed-form invariants
$\langle \mathrm{trefoil}\rangle = -\sqrt{2}$ and
$\mathrm{RT}(4_1) = -1/\sqrt{2}$; and the Drinfeld double $D(G)$ of an
arbitrary finite group, with the eight-object non-abelian spectrum of
$D(S_3)$. For the entanglement entropy of a modular tensor category we
prove that Casini--Huerta saturation holds exactly when the category is
trivial, and that the deficit on a non-trivial category is the
topological entropy $\log\mathcal{D}$.
Because these layers were verified to different standards, we grade
them: each section states the strength of its own results, a summary
table appears in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, and a trust-base note records the
axiom set, the eighteen results discharged by compiler-level evaluation
rather than by the kernel, and the two hypotheses that remain assumed.
The layers are independent developments about a common subject rather
than a single derivation; we say where each stands and do not claim a
chain that the formalisation does not contain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Fibonacci anyon is the standard existence proof that topological
quantum computation is universal: braiding alone, with no measurement
and no additional gate, generates a dense subgroup of the unitary group
acting on the fusion space, so any target gate can be approximated to
arbitrary precision by a braid~\cite{FreedmanLarsenWang2002,
NayakSimonSternFreedmanDasSarma2008}. The argument is standard and it is
believed. What has not existed is a proof of it that a machine has
checked.

This paper supplies one. Our main theorem is that the three-strand
Fibonacci braid representation
$\rho_{\mathrm{Fib}} \colon B_3 \to \mathrm{SU}(2)$ has dense image, in
the concrete entrywise sense: for every $U$ in $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and every
$\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a braid $b \in B_3$ with
$\lVert \rho_{\mathrm{Fib}}(b)_{ij} - U_{ij}\rVert < \varepsilon$ for all
$i,j$. The statement is unconditional (it depends on no assumed
lemma, no tracked hypothesis and no numerical oracle) and it is
checked by the Lean~4 kernel from the axioms of the ambient type theory.
Section~\ref{sec:density} proves it.

Density is an existence statement, and on its own it is not yet a
computational one: it does not say how long the braid is. The
Solovay--Kitaev theorem supplies the missing bound, and the
Dawson--Nielsen analysis makes it
quantitative~\cite{KitaevShenVyalyi2002,DawsonNielsen2006}. What we
verify in Sec.~\ref{sec:sk} is stronger than either in one specific
respect: our compilation theorem returns the error bound and the length
bound \emph{about the same compiled object at the same recursion depth},
rather than asserting that a short word exists and separately that an
accurate word exists. Existence-only forms leave open, as a matter of
logical form rather than of mathematical fact, whether the short word
and the accurate word are the same word. Ours does not.

\paragraph{What this paper is, and how to read its claims.}
The Fibonacci result sits inside a larger Lean development covering
quantum groups, fusion categories, modular data, knot invariants and
categorical entropy, and we present that development here. But those
layers were built at different times and to different standards, and
some of them are considerably weaker than their names suggest. Rather
than present a uniform assertion of verification and let a reader
discover the variation, we grade every layer explicitly, in the section
that carries it, against the scale in Table~\ref{tab:grades}.

First, the Witten--Reshetikhin--Turaev partition functions we record in

\paragraph{The layers are independent, not a chain.}

exact standing.

\section{The Fibonacci braid representation and its density in $\mathrm{SU}(2)$}
\label{sec:density}

\emph{Grade: Theorem.} Kernel-checked, unconditional, no assumed
hypothesis.

Freedman, Larsen and Wang proved that the topological modular functor
of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Witten--Chern--Simons theory at a fifth root of unity
is universal for quantum computation, and identified the technical
core of that result as the density of the irreducible sectors of the
Jones representation~\cite{FreedmanLarsenWang2002}. Their setting is
$q = e^{2\pi i/r}$ with $r=5$, and the braid groups appearing there act
on $3k$ marked points in a disk. We verify the smallest non-trivial
instance of that density statement: three strands, acting on a
two-dimensional space.

\subsection{The representation}

Write $\varphi = (1+\sqrt5)/2$ for the golden ratio. The Fibonacci
braiding data consists of two $R$-symbols $R_1, R_\tau$ (roots of
unity attached to the two fusion channels of $\tau \times \tau$) and
an $F$-matrix $F$ built from $\varphi$. The braid generators are
\begin{equation}
\sigma_1 \;=\;
\begin{pmatrix} R_1 & 0 \\ 0 & R_\tau \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\sigma_2 \;=\; F\,\sigma_1\,F ,
\label{eq:fibgens}
\end{equation}
which is the standard presentation of the Fibonacci braid action on the
two-dimensional fusion space of three $\tau$ anyons with total charge
$\tau$~\cite{NayakSimonSternFreedmanDasSarma2008}.

Two points about \eqref{eq:fibgens} are worth stating explicitly,
because they bound what the theorem below asserts.

First, $\det \sigma_1 = R_1 R_\tau = e^{-i\pi/5} \neq 1$, so
\eqref{eq:fibgens} lands in $\mathrm{U}(2)$ rather than
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$. The formalisation carries an explicit determinant
normalisation by a twentieth root of unity $\omega = e^{i\pi/10}$, and
the object actually used is the normalised pair, assembled into a group
homomorphism
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{Fib}} \;\colon\; B_3 \longrightarrow \mathrm{SU}(2)
\]
by a construction that takes a pair of matrices together with a proof
that they satisfy the braid relation
$\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1 = \sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$ and returns the
induced homomorphism on the three-strand braid group. The Yang--Baxter
witness is discharged, not assumed.

Second (and this is the honest scope of the whole section) the
matrices in \eqref{eq:fibgens} are \emph{posited}. They are written down
with literal entries and are not derived, within this development, from
the fusion category of Sec.~\ref{sec:modular} by solving the pentagon
and hexagon equations. What we prove is a statement about the group
generated by two explicit unitaries that satisfy the braid relation. That
those two unitaries are the Fibonacci $R$ and $F$ data is a matter of
their agreeing with the standard presentation, which we assert on the
literature's authority and do not verify categorically here. A reader
who wants the categorical derivation should regard it as open; we list
it in Sec.~\ref{sec:open}.

\subsection{The density theorem}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Theorem (density).} For every $U \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$ and every
$\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a braid $b \in B_3$ such that
\[
\bigl\lVert \rho_{\mathrm{Fib}}(b)_{ij} - U_{ij} \bigr\rVert < \varepsilon
\qquad \text{for all } i,j \in \{1,2\}.
\]
\end{quote}

This is the statement as formalised, with the quantifiers in that order
and the approximation taken entrywise. Entrywise agreement on a fixed
finite-dimensional space is equivalent, up to a dimensional constant, to
agreement in any matrix norm, so nothing is lost by the concrete form;
we record it because it is what the machine checked.

The proof runs in two steps. The image
$\overline{\rho_{\mathrm{Fib}}(B_3)}$ is a closed subgroup of the compact
connected group $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, hence by Cartan's closed-subgroup
theorem an embedded Lie subgroup. A Lie subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is
either all of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, or contained in a maximal torus, or
finite. The torus branch is excluded because $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$
do not commute: $\sigma_2$ is a non-diagonal conjugate of $\sigma_1$
by $F$, and $F$ does not preserve the eigenbasis of $\sigma_1$. The
finite branch is excluded by a cardinality argument: the group generated
contains elements of unbounded order, so it cannot be finite. Only the
first alternative survives, and density follows.

The formalisation's project-side dependencies for this theorem touch the
braid-group construction, the explicit representation of
\eqref{eq:fibgens}, the one-parameter-subgroup analysis of
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and the bracket-closure argument. It consumes nothing
from the quantum-group presentation of Sec.~\ref{sec:qgroups}, the
modular data of Sec.~\ref{sec:modular}, or the Ising category of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ising}. As stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, these layers
are independent.

\section{Quantitative Solovay--Kitaev at the Fibonacci alphabet}
\label{sec:sk}

\emph{Grade: Theorem.} Kernel-checked, unconditional on the stated
precision range.

Density says a braid exists; it does not say how long it is. A universality
claim without a length bound is not yet a computational one, since the
approximating word could in principle grow exponentially in
$1/\varepsilon$. The Solovay--Kitaev theorem closes that gap, giving a
polylogarithmic bound~\cite{KitaevShenVyalyi2002}, and the standard
pedagogical treatment of the algorithm and its recursion is due to
Dawson and Nielsen~\cite{DawsonNielsen2006}.

\subsection{What is bundled, and why it matters}

The result we verify has a specific logical shape that we want to
isolate, because it is the respect in which it is stronger than the
usual statement.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Theorem (compilation).} There is a compilation procedure
$U, \varepsilon \mapsto w(U,\varepsilon)$ such that for every
$U \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$ and every $\varepsilon$ with
$0 < \varepsilon \le \varepsilon_0$, \emph{both} of the following hold of
that same compiled word:
\[
\bigl\lVert \rho_{\mathrm{Fib}}(w(U,\varepsilon)) - U \bigr\rVert \le \varepsilon,
\qquad
\mathrm{length}\bigl(w(U,\varepsilon)\bigr) \;\le\;
C \,\bigl(\log(1/\varepsilon)\bigr)^{c}.
\]
\end{quote}

The conjunction is the point. An existence-only formulation asserts that
an accurate word exists and, separately, that a short word exists; as a
matter of logical form it leaves open whether they are the same word.
Nothing about the mathematics makes them different, but a formalisation
that proves the two facts separately has not proved the conjunction, and
a machine-checked artefact is precisely where that distinction stops
being pedantic. Here the error bound and the length bound are properties
of one compiled object at one recursion depth.

The theorem holds on the precision range $0 < \varepsilon \le
\varepsilon_0$ with $\varepsilon_0 = 1/(8 K^2) = 2^{-23} \approx
1.19 \times 10^{-7}$, where $K = 1024$ is the composition constant fixed
below. A companion statement covers the complementary loose regime
$\varepsilon \ge 2\varepsilon_0$, where the base case of the recursion
already suffices and no descent is needed. The two together leave no gap
in $\varepsilon$ that is not covered by one branch or the other, which
is why we describe the result as unconditional rather than as holding on
a range.

\subsection{The exponent}

The length bound's exponent is
\[
c \;=\; \frac{\log 5}{\log(3/2)} \;\approx\; 3.9694 ,
\]
which is the value the formalisation defines and reasons with. Two
sentinel results bracket it without appeal to a decimal expansion:
$3 < c$ and $c < 4$. These are worth having explicitly, because the
exponent enters the length bound as a real power and a formalisation
that only ever manipulated a numeral would not have established that the
quantity it manipulates lies where the analysis needs it.

The value $\log 5/\log(3/2)$ is the exponent produced by the standard
balanced group-commutator recursion, in which each level replaces one
approximation by five words and improves the error exponent from
$\delta$ to $\delta^{3/2}$.

\subsection{The constant is a number, not a symbol}

Polylogarithmic bounds of this kind are often stated with an unquantified
constant. Ours is quantified, and the quantification is machine-checked:
the composition constant is fixed at $K = 1024$, and a numerical
inequality certifies that the constant chain assembled from the
recursion's own ingredients (the group-commutator stability estimate,
the order-two Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff cubic bound, and the
$\varepsilon_0$-net) is bounded above by it. Written out, the
certified inequality is
\[
\sqrt2\Bigl(12\sqrt2\,e\,\bigl(\tfrac{\pi\sqrt2}{4}\bigr)^{1/2}
 + 12\sqrt{2\varepsilon_0}\Bigr)
+ \sqrt2 \cdot 320 \cdot \bigl(\tfrac{\pi\sqrt2}{4}\bigr)^{3/2}
\;\le\; K .
\]
The margin is comfortable, which matters for a different reason than
tightness: it means the bound does not depend on the exact value of any
one of the contributing estimates, so improving any of them individually
does not disturb the theorem. The coefficient $320$ in the
Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff term is loose by the standards of the
published analysis, and we flag it as the place where a sharper constant
would most directly improve the bound.

\subsection{Scope: this paper owns the Fibonacci alphabet}

The result above is stated for one generating set, the Fibonacci braid
generators. The general theory (a quantitative Solovay--Kitaev
statement parameterised by an abstract generating set, lifted to
$\mathrm{SU}(d)$ for arbitrary $d$, and instantiated at Clifford$+T$,
Read--Rezayi $\mathrm{SU}(2)_5$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)_7$, trapped-ion
$\mathrm{SU}(4)$ and Clifford$+\mathrm{CCZ}$ $\mathrm{SU}(8)$) is the
subject of a companion paper~\cite{RoehmD8Compilation} and is not
developed here. The division is by subject and not by convenience: the
Fibonacci alphabet is the anyonic one, it is the alphabet whose density
this paper proves in Sec.~\ref{sec:density}, and compiling into it is the
step that turns the topological universality statement into a
computational one. The general theory neither subsumes nor is subsumed
by that; it answers a different question, namely what survives when the
alphabet is abstracted away.

\section{A dark state with a protected sign flip}
\label{sec:gate}

\emph{Grade: Theorem.} Kernel-checked, no assumed hypothesis.

Braiding is not the only route to a phase that is insensitive to the
details of how it was acquired. This section records an independent,
much smaller result of the same flavour: a two-site fermionic model with
a dark state whose sign is reversed by a half-circuit of a parameter
loop, with the dynamical contribution to the phase vanishing identically
along the relevant locus. It is included because it is a gate-level
result and it is proved, not because it depends on anything else in this
paper; it does not.

\subsection{The model and the dark state}

The model is a two-site Fermi--Hubbard dimer with hopping $t$ and a
pairing or detuning parameter $\Delta$, restricted to its singlet
sector, where the Hamiltonian $H_{\mathrm{s}}(t,\Delta)$ acts on a
three-dimensional space. Two facts fix the state of interest:
\begin{itemize}
\item the vector $v_{\mathrm{dark}}(t,\Delta)$ lies in the kernel of
  $H_{\mathrm{s}}(t,\Delta)$, for \emph{all} real $t$ and $\Delta$; and
\item it is non-zero whenever $(t,\Delta) \neq (0,0)$.
\end{itemize}
Both are proved. The second matters more than it looks: a kernel-membership
statement about a vector that might be zero is vacuous, and the
non-triviality lemma is what stops it from being so.

\subsection{The sign flip and the vanishing dynamical phase}

Parameterise the dark state by an angle $\theta$, writing
$\psi_{\mathrm{dark}}(\theta)$, and restrict attention to the locus
\begin{equation}
\Delta \sin\theta \;=\; 2t\cos\theta ,
\label{eq:darklocus}
\end{equation}
which is the condition under which $\psi_{\mathrm{dark}}(\theta)$ is the
dark state of $H_{\mathrm{s}}(t,\Delta)$. Note that
\eqref{eq:darklocus} is invariant under $\theta \mapsto \theta + \pi$,
since both sides change sign. On that locus we prove two things:
\begin{align}
\psi_{\mathrm{dark}}(\theta + \pi) &= -\,\psi_{\mathrm{dark}}(\theta),
\label{eq:signflip}\\
\bigl\langle \psi_{\mathrm{dark}}(\theta),\;
  H_{\mathrm{s}}(t,\Delta)\,\psi_{\mathrm{dark}}(\theta)\bigr\rangle &= 0 .
\label{eq:nodynamical}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:signflip} is the sign flip: the two ends of a
half-turn in $\theta$ carry states differing by exactly $-1$, not by an
arbitrary phase. Equation~\eqref{eq:nodynamical} says the expectation of
the Hamiltonian in the dark state vanishes, so the dynamical phase
accumulated along any path lying in the locus is zero. Together these
are the two ingredients from which a geometric phase of $-1$ is read
off: a sign difference between endpoints, and nothing dynamical to
account for it.

\subsection{What is not proved}

We are precise about the gap, because the gap is where the physics
statement would live. Equations~\eqref{eq:signflip}
and~\eqref{eq:nodynamical} are statements about the endpoints of a
half-turn and about an expectation value at a configuration. They are
not a transport theorem. To conclude that a physical protocol which
slowly varies $(t,\Delta)$ around the loop returns the system to
$-\psi_{\mathrm{dark}}$ requires an adiabatic theorem (a statement
that the evolution stays in the instantaneous kernel, with an error
controlled by the sweep rate and the spectral gap) and that is not
formalised here. What we have is the holonomy's would-be value together
with the vanishing of its competitor, not the holonomy. Closing that gap
is listed in Sec.~\ref{sec:open}.

\section{Quantum groups: the algebraic input}
\label{sec:qgroups}

The structure that encapsulates the braiding and fusion of non-Abelian
anyons is a modular tensor category: representations of the braid group
alone are not enough, because they must also be compatible with fusion,
and the resulting $F$ and $R$ matrices are then constrained by the
pentagon and hexagon
equations~\cite[Sec.~III.A.2]{NayakSimonSternFreedmanDasSarma2008}. What
this section reports is a formalisation of the algebras that stand
beside that categorical data, $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ and
$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_3)$, together with a rank-generic construction. We
prove statements about the algebras. We do not connect them to the
modular data of Sec.~\ref{sec:modular}, and we say below exactly where
that connection is absent.

\subsection{The presentation and the four defining relations}

$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is constructed as a quotient of the free
associative algebra on the four generators $E$, $F$, $K$, $K^{-1}$ over
the Laurent-polynomial ring $k[q,q^{-1}]$, for an arbitrary commutative
ring $k$. Four relations are verified on the quotient:
$KK^{-1} = K^{-1}K = 1$; $KE = q^{2}EK$ and $KF = q^{-2}FK$, which give
the conjugation form $KEK^{-1} = q^{2}E$ and $KFK^{-1} = q^{-2}F$; and
\begin{equation}
(q - q^{-1})\,(EF - FE) \;=\; K - K^{-1}.
\label{eq:chevalley}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:chevalley} is the Chevalley commutator with its
denominator cleared, which is what makes it meaningful over a base where
$q - q^{-1}$ is not invertible. It is not the quantum Serre relation:
that relation is cubic and couples generators attached to distinct
simple roots, so at rank one there is nothing for it to say.

One consequence of presenting the algebra by generators and relations is
worth recording for a reader who wants to reuse the development. The
carrier is a quotient, so unfolding a definition never reaches the
relations, and rewriting in Lean's simplification pipeline runs at a
transparency setting at which the quotient's algebra structure is
opaque. The relations above are therefore the working interface: every
later computation in this layer is a rewrite by one of them rather than
an appeal to the construction. \emph{Grade: Theorem}, kernel-checked,
over an arbitrary commutative ring.

\subsection{Hopf structure}

Comultiplication, counit and antipode are defined on generators and
shown to descend to the quotient. Coassociativity is proved as an
identity of algebra homomorphisms into the triple tensor product, and
the antipode axioms in convolution form,
$m \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta = \eta \circ \varepsilon$
and its mirror. The group-like identity $\Delta(K) = K \otimes K$ holds.
These discharge the obligations of Mathlib's own \texttt{HopfAlgebra}
class, so the Hopf-algebra structure on $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is a
registered instance rather than a bespoke record: it is available to any
library result stated for a Hopf algebra. We also prove
$S^{2}(x) = KxK^{-1}$ for \emph{every} element $x$, not merely on
generators. \emph{Grade: Theorem.}

\subsection{Rank two, where the Serre relations become non-empty}

For $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_3)$ the cubic relations are genuine and are
proved:
\begin{equation}
E_1^{2}E_2 + E_2E_1^{2} \;=\; (q + q^{-1})\,E_1E_2E_1 ,
\label{eq:serre3}
\end{equation}
with its counterpart for the lowering generators, alongside
$E_1F_2 = F_2E_1$ and $K_1K_2 = K_2K_1$. The Hopf structure is
established in the same convolution form as at rank one, again yielding
a registered \texttt{HopfAlgebra} instance. One scope difference is
worth stating rather than leaving to be discovered: the rank-two
antipode-squared result is proved for generators only, where its
rank-one analogue holds for all elements. \emph{Grade: Theorem}, with
that restriction on the last item.

\subsection{A rank-generic construction, and its scope}

Both concrete algebras are instances of a construction taking an integer
matrix $A$ of arbitrary rank as a parameter. Coassociativity and both
antipode axioms are proved there for every symmetric $A$ with
$A_{ii} = 2$, at every rank, so the Hopf structure is not a rank-by-rank
result. The quadratic-link Serre relation holds whenever $A_{ij} = -1$.

The symmetry hypothesis is a real restriction and we flag it rather than
let the word ``generic'' carry more than it should: it admits the
simply-laced types and excludes $B_2$, $C_2$ and $G_2$, whose Cartan
matrices are recorded in the development but to which these theorems do
not apply. Specialising $A$ to $[2]$ and to
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2 & -1\\ -1 & 2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
recovers the two concrete algebras as algebra equivalences, so the
generic construction is not merely parallel to them. \emph{Grade:
Theorem} for the generic Hopf and Serre results; \emph{Theorem
(evaluated)} for the two equivalences, whose relation-respecting
direction is discharged by \texttt{native\_decide}
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:nd}).

\subsection{The restricted quotient, and what is not proved about it}

Imposing $E^{\ell} = F^{\ell} = 0$ and $K^{\ell} = 1$ gives the
restricted algebra, and those three relations are verified on it. That
every Chevalley relation lies among the restricted relations is what
makes the passage from the generic algebra to the restricted one an
algebra homomorphism.

Two expected companion facts are \emph{not} proved: the dimension
$\ell^{3}$, and the number of simple modules. Nor is any bridge
established from this quotient to the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_k$ fusion data of
Sec.~\ref{sec:modular}. \emph{Grade: Theorem} for the three relations
and the homomorphism, and no grade at all for the dimension, the simple
count, or the bridge, because there is no statement to grade.

\section{Modular data: fusion, $S$-matrix unitarity, and the Verlinde formula}
\label{sec:modular}

This paper uses the phrase ``modular tensor category'' throughout, and
this is the section in which that phrase is discharged. Here the modular
data is exhibited as an explicit object and non-trivial properties are
proved of it: the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_k$ modular $S$-matrix is written down
for $k = 1,\dots,5$, it is proved unitary and non-degenerate at each of
those levels, and at $k = 3,4,5$ it is proved to diagonalise the fusion
rules. Each result is verified at a named finite level. No theorem in
this section is quantified over a general modular tensor category, so
the Verlinde content as a whole is graded \emph{Instance}; the
individual level statements are \emph{Theorem}.

\subsection{The $S$-matrices, unitarity and non-degeneracy}

At level $k$ the $k+1$ simple objects carry the Kac--Peterson matrix
$S_{ab} = \sqrt{2/(k+2)}\,\sin\bigl((a+1)(b+1)\pi/(k+2)\bigr)$. We do not
work with that trigonometric form directly. Factoring out the overall
scale $\lambda_k = \sqrt{2/(k+2)}\,\sin(\pi/(k+2))$ leaves
$S = \lambda_k M_k$ with $M_k$ algebraic: at $k = 3$ its entries are
$\pm 1$ and $\pm\varphi$, so that all downstream arithmetic happens in
$\mathbb{Q}(\varphi) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ and reduces under
$\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1$. The corresponding fields are
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ at $k = 2$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ at $k = 4$, and the
totally real cubic subfield $\mathbb{Q}(2\cos(\pi/7))$ of
$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)$ at $k = 5$, whose minimal polynomial is proved
rather than assumed. This is what makes the statements checkable by the
kernel without numerical evaluation. That the algebraic definition still
describes the intended matrix is proved separately: the quantum-dimension
row satisfies $S_{0b} = \sqrt{2/5}\,\sin\bigl((b+1)\pi/5\bigr)$ for every
$b$ at $k=3$, tying it back to its literal trigonometric values, with
analogous statements at $k = 4$ and $k = 5$.

Unitarity, $SS^{\mathsf T} = 1$, is proved at every level from $k = 1$ to
$k = 5$, and so is non-degeneracy, $\det S \neq 0$. Non-degeneracy is the
modularity condition, and it is what distinguishes a modular tensor
category from a merely pre-modular one. All ten are kernel-checked with
no assumed hypothesis and no compiled evaluation. \emph{Grade:
Theorem.} At $k = 5$ unitarity is additionally obtained by an
independent route, as an exact integer identity
$7\,SS^{\mathsf T} = 7\cdot\mathbb{1}$ between character sums over
$\mathbb{Z}$, which removes the transcendental content from the
statement altogether.

\subsection{The Verlinde formula}

The strongest result in this section is that the modular $S$-matrix
diagonalises the fusion rules, for every triple of labels rather than
for a chosen few. At $k = 3$ we prove
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{l=0}^{3} \frac{S_{il}\,S_{jl}\,S_{ml}}{S_{0l}}
  \;=\; N_{ij}{}^{m}
  \qquad \text{for all } i,j,m \in \{0,1,2,3\},
  \label{eq:verlinde-k3}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the truncated Clebsch--Gordan rule and $S$ is real
symmetric, so that conjugation acts trivially on its
entries~\cite{Verlinde1988}. That is $4^3 = 64$ independent identities,
discharged individually and then assembled. The same statement is proved
at $k = 4$ over $125$ triples and at $k = 5$ over $216$.

The route is a single reduction lemma. Every column quotient in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:verlinde-k3} carries exactly two powers of the overall
scale, so the sum collapses to $\tfrac{1}{10}(3-\varphi)$ times a
polynomial in the entries of $M_3$, with each column inverse
$1/(M_3)_{0l}$ replaced by its exact algebraic form, either $1$ or
$\varphi - 1$, using $\varphi(\varphi-1) = 1$. All $64$ identities route
through that lemma and then close by reduction in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$.
Kernel-checked, no compiled evaluation, no assumed hypothesis.

Two scope statements belong with this result. First, at $k = 1$ and
$k = 2$ only representative entries are verified, and those compare the
Verlinde sum to a numeral rather than to the fusion function; the tie to
the fusion rule itself exists at $k = 3,4,5$. Second, this development
defines an abstract Verlinde predicate on the finite data of a
pre-modular category, and no object in it discharges that predicate.
What exists is a family of level-specific theorems, each quantified over
its own label set. Nothing here proves the Verlinde formula for an
arbitrary modular tensor category, and the level statements should not
be read as instances of a general theorem proved elsewhere in this work.
\emph{Grade: Instance.}

\subsection{Modularity and the M\"uger centre}

One statement in this section is genuinely general: for the finite data
of any pre-modular category over an integral domain, $\det S \neq 0$
forces the only transparent index to be the vacuum. The mechanism is
that an invertible matrix admits no row proportional to another. It is
kernel-checked, and it quantifies over the data rather than over a fixed
level. Instantiated against separately proved non-degeneracy witnesses
it yields a trivial M\"uger centre for the Ising and Fibonacci data,
both kernel-checked.

The scope of the word ``transparent'' should be read carefully.
Transparency is defined here at the level of the data: index $i$ is
transparent when $S_{ij} = d_i S_{0j}$ for every $j$. Identifying that
condition with the categorical one,
$c_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y} = \mathrm{id}_{X\otimes Y}$, is a definition in
this development and not a derived result, so the theorem establishes
the linear-algebraic half of the correspondence and not the braiding
half. \emph{Grade: Theorem}, with transparency as just defined.

\subsection{Fusion rules and quantum dimensions}

The Ising rule $\sigma\times\sigma = 1 + \psi$ is recorded as three
separate decidable facts, and it is their conjunction that carries the
content: $N_{11}{}^{0} = 1$, $N_{11}{}^{2} = 1$, and the one that does
the work, $N_{11}{}^{1} = 0$. At $k = 3$ the analogous four facts give
$V_2 \times V_2 = V_0 + V_2$, and a further theorem identifies $V_3$ as
the charge-conjugation object, $V_3 \times V_j = V_{3-j}$. All are
kernel-checked.

Quantum dimensions are obtained as Frobenius--Perron data rather than
declared: the dimension vector is verified against the fusion-matrix
eigenvector equation, giving $d = (1,\varphi)$ for Fibonacci,
$(1,\sqrt2,1)$ for Ising, and $(1,\varphi,\varphi,1)$ for
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_3$. These are discharged by \texttt{native\_decide} over
the quadratic rings, so they are \emph{Theorem (evaluated)} and not
kernel-checked. Their statements are also narrower than the word
``eigenvector'' suggests: each is a single component identity with the
fusion-matrix entries written out as literals, so the matrix itself does
not appear in the statement being proved.

\subsection{Fibonacci as the even sector of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_3$}
\label{subsec:fibeven}

The Fibonacci fusion ring is the even sector of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_3$. We
prove that $\{V_0, V_2\}$ is closed under fusion, in the explicit form
that no odd label occurs in any of $V_0 \times V_0$, $V_0 \times V_2$,
$V_2 \times V_0$ or $V_2 \times V_2$; with the fusion facts above this
gives $V_2 \times V_2 = V_0 + V_2$, which is $\tau^2 = 1 + \tau$. Row
$0$ of the $k=3$ $S$-matrix is $\lambda_3(1,\varphi,\varphi,1)$, so
$d_\tau = S_{02}/S_{00} = \varphi$. The global dimension follows:
$\mathcal{D}^2 = 1 + \varphi^2 = (5+\sqrt5)/2$, a kernel-checked
identity over $\mathbb{R}$, and the Fibonacci $S$-matrix is proved
non-degenerate. The full $\mathrm{SU}(2)_3$ category has
$\mathcal{D}^2 = 5 + \sqrt5$, exactly twice the Fibonacci value, since
$\varphi$ occurs in two of its four rows.

\subsection{What connects this section to the density theorem, and what does not}
\label{subsec:posited}

One connective claim must be stated plainly, because how it resolves
governs the reading of Sec.~\ref{sec:density}. The $F$ and $R$ data from
which the three-strand Fibonacci braid representation is built there are
\emph{posited}, not derived from the fusion category described in this
section. The two generators are written down as literal matrices,
$\sigma_1 = \mathrm{diag}(e^{-4\pi i/5},\, e^{3\pi i/5})$ and
$\sigma_2 = F\sigma_1 F$ with
$F = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} \varphi^{-1} & \varphi^{-1/2}\\
\varphi^{-1/2} & -\varphi^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. What is then
proved about them, kernel-checked in each case, is that
$F^2 = \mathbb{1}$, that both generators are unitary, and that they
satisfy the braid relation
$\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1 = \sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$.

What is not proved is that they are the $F$- and $R$-symbols of the
Fibonacci category. No pentagon or hexagon equation is checked for this
pair. A Fibonacci pentagon equation \emph{is} proved elsewhere in this
development, exhaustively over the $2^9 = 512$ index assignments, but
for a different $F$-matrix, in a different gauge, over a different ring:
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix} \varphi^{-1} & \varphi^{-1}\\
1 & -\varphi^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$,
discharged by compiled evaluation. Nothing in the development relates
the two matrices, by gauge transformation or otherwise.

The density result of Sec.~\ref{sec:density} is therefore, as a formal
statement, a theorem about an explicit unitary pair of $2\times2$
matrices satisfying the braid relation. Its reading as a statement about
Fibonacci anyon braiding rests on the standard identification of that
pair with the Fibonacci $F$- and $R$-symbols, which this work takes as an
input and does not verify. Supplying that bridge is the single most
valuable thing that could be added to this development, and we list it
first in Sec.~\ref{sec:open}.

\section{The Ising category: braiding, knot invariants, and the trust boundary}
\label{sec:ising}

\emph{Grade: Theorem (evaluated)} for essentially everything in this
section: see Sec.~\ref{subsec:nd}, which is placed here because this
is where the issue first arises.

The Ising category has three simple objects $1, \sigma, \psi$ with
quantum dimensions $d_1 = d_\psi = 1$ and $d_\sigma = \sqrt2$, so that
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}^2 \;=\; \sum_a d_a^2 \;=\; 1 + 2 + 1 \;=\; 4 ,
\qquad \mathcal{D} = 2 .
\label{eq:isingD}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:isingD} is proved, in two independent
formulations: over the reals and inside the cyclotomic field below.
We state it prominently because $\sqrt2$ and $2$ are easy to interchange
here and only one of them is the total quantum dimension: $\sqrt2$ is
$d_\sigma$, the quantum dimension of the single non-abelian object, while
$\mathcal{D} = 2$ is the norm of the whole object list in the sense of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kp}. The Ising category and the toric code share
$\mathcal{D} = 2$, as they must, both having four sectors' worth of
squared dimension~\cite{KitaevPreskill2006}.

All braiding data is carried in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})$, the cyclotomic
field of sixteenth roots of unity, represented as an explicit
eight-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra with $\zeta_{16}^8 = -1$. Working
in an exact field rather than in floating point is what makes the
identities below decidable, and hence what makes them checkable at all.

\subsection{Hexagon and ribbon equations}

The braiding is specified by four $R$-symbols, $R^{\sigma\sigma}_{1}$,
$R^{\sigma\sigma}_{\psi}$, $R^{\sigma\psi}_{\sigma}$ and
$R^{\psi\psi}_{1}$, together with twists $\theta_\sigma$, $\theta_\psi$.
We verify:
\begin{itemize}
\item the six hexagon equations, in the form relating
  $\mathcal{D}^{-1}(1 \pm R^{\sigma\sigma}_\psi)$ to products of
  $R$-symbols, together with the three hexagons involving $\psi$;
\item the four ribbon equations, expressing each squared $R$-symbol
  through the twists: for instance
  $(R^{\sigma\psi}_\sigma)^2 = -1$ and $(R^{\psi\psi}_1)^2 = 1$;
\item the $R$-matrix squares
  $(R^{\sigma\sigma}_1)^2 = -\zeta_{16}^6$ and
  $(R^{\sigma\sigma}_\psi)^2 = +\zeta_{16}^6$; and
\item the Gauss sum $p_+ = 1 + \mathcal{D}^2\theta_\sigma/2 + \theta_\psi
  = 2\zeta_{16}$, which fixes the framing anomaly.
\end{itemize}
These are the coherence conditions that make the braiding well defined,
and verifying all of them (rather than a representative sample) is
the point: a braided structure that satisfies five of six hexagons is not
a braided structure.

\subsection{Knot and link invariants}

From the same data we obtain closed-form invariants.

The trefoil evaluates to
\begin{equation}
\bigl\langle \mathrm{trefoil} \bigr\rangle
 \;=\; \theta_\sigma^{-3}\bigl[(R^{\sigma\sigma}_1)^3 + (R^{\sigma\sigma}_\psi)^3\bigr]
 \;=\; -\sqrt2 ,
\label{eq:trefoil}
\end{equation}
an exact identity in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})$. The figure-eight knot
gives an unnormalised quantum trace of $-1$, and after dividing by
$\mathcal{D}/2 = \sqrt2$ (the normalisation appropriate to the
$\sigma$-coloured Reshetikhin--Turaev
invariant~\cite{ReshetikhinTuraev1991,Witten1989}) yields
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RT}(4_1, \sigma) \;=\; -\frac{1}{\sqrt2} .
\label{eq:fig8}
\end{equation}
Both \eqref{eq:trefoil} and \eqref{eq:fig8} are exact elements of the
cyclotomic field; \eqref{eq:fig8} is
$\tfrac12(\zeta_{16}^6 - \zeta_{16}^2)$ in the explicit basis, which is
$-1/\sqrt2$.

The Hopf link requires a more careful statement than it is usually given.
What we prove is
\begin{equation}
(R^{\sigma\sigma}_1)^2 + (R^{\sigma\sigma}_\psi)^2 \;=\; 0 ,
\label{eq:hopf}
\end{equation}
that is, the $\sigma\sigma$ quantum trace of the squared braiding
vanishes. The Ising modular $S$-matrix does have $S_{\sigma\sigma} = 0$,
so \eqref{eq:hopf} is consistent with the expected identification of the
Hopf-link invariant with an $S$-matrix entry, but \eqref{eq:hopf} is a
single-entry computation, and it is not a proof that the diagrammatic
invariant reproduces the $S$-matrix in general. No $S$-matrix appears in
the formalised statement. We say this because the natural way to report
\eqref{eq:hopf} is as an $S$-matrix match, and that report would claim
more than was verified.

\subsection{The trust boundary: \texttt{native\_decide}}
\label{subsec:nd}

Every identity in this section is discharged by \texttt{native\_decide}.
That tactic reduces a decidable proposition by compiling it to machine
code, executing it, and accepting the result; the resulting proof term
appeals to an axiom asserting that the compiled evaluation was faithful.
It is the reason these computations in an eight-dimensional cyclotomic
algebra complete in seconds rather than exhausting the kernel's reduction
budget.

The consequence for a reader is precise and worth stating once, clearly.
For these results the trust base is the Lean~4 kernel \emph{together
with} the Lean compiler and runtime, rather than the kernel alone. That
is a strictly larger base than the one underwriting
Secs.~\ref{sec:density}, \ref{sec:sk}, \ref{sec:gate}
and~\ref{sec:entropy}, whose results appeal to no axiom beyond the
ambient three. Of the results this paper puts forward as principal, 18
carry such an evaluation axiom, and all 18 are in this section and its
immediate neighbours. We grade them \emph{Theorem (evaluated)} rather
than \emph{Theorem} throughout, and we do not describe the development as
kernel-pure. Sec.~\ref{sec:trust} itemises them.

Nothing here suggests the identities are wrong; exact cyclotomic
arithmetic executed by a compiler is not a plausible source of error, and
the results agree with the published Ising data. The distinction is about
what a machine-checked claim means, and a paper whose contribution is
machine-checking owes its reader that distinction rather than a uniform
assurance.

\subsection{Witten--Reshetikhin--Turaev partition functions}

\emph{Grade: Encoding.}

The development records two WRT identities: the partition function on
$S^2 \times S^1$ equals the rank of the category, and the partition
function on $S^3$ equals $p_+/\mathcal{D}^2$. Both are true by unfolding
the definitions that name them: the first because the $S^2 \times S^1$
partition function is \emph{defined} as the rank, the second because the
$S^3$ partition function is \emph{defined} as that quotient. They fix the
Kirby-colour normalisation convention and establish nothing about it.

What a WRT section would need, and what is absent, is invariance under
the Kirby moves: the statement that the assigned number depends only
on the three-manifold and not on the surgery presentation used to
describe it. The development does contain genuine surrounding material:
Kirby-move equivalence on surgery presentations as a relation, with
handle-slide and stabilisation steps, the identification of zero-framed
surgery on the unknot with $S^2 \times S^1$, and exact lens-space
evaluations for the Ising category at sixteen framings. But the bridge
from those to invariance of the invariant is not built, and we do not
claim it. We also note, since the surrounding literature makes it
natural to reach for, that no form of the Lickorish--Wallace theorem is
formalised here.

\section{The Drinfeld double and a non-abelian anyon spectrum}
\label{sec:drinfeld}

The strongest result in this section holds for an arbitrary finite
group. For every finite group $G$ and every commutative ring $k$ we
construct the Drinfeld double $D(G)$ as the free $k$-module on
$G \times G$, carrying the twisted convolution
\[
  (f \cdot f')(a,h) \;=\; \sum_{g \in G} f(a,g)\,
  f'\bigl(g^{-1}ag,\; g^{-1}h\bigr),
\]
and prove that this product is associative and unital on both sides,
with unit $\sum_a \delta_a \otimes e$. On basis elements the product
evaluates to
\begin{equation}
  (\delta_a \otimes g_1)(\delta_b \otimes g_2) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \delta_a \otimes g_1 g_2, & a = g_1 b g_1^{-1},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:ddbasis}
\end{equation}
which is the multiplication rule Kitaev writes for the quantum double in
Sec.~5.1 of Ref.~\cite{KitaevAnyons2003}. The rank is $|G|^2$. All are
\emph{Theorem}-grade: kernel-checked, universally quantified over $G$
and $k$, and consuming no hypothesis.

What is \emph{not} formalised is the rest of the Hopf structure.
Kitaev's comultiplication, counit, antipode and $R$-matrix, which are
what make $D(G)$ quasi-triangular and therefore make its representation
category braided, have no counterpart here.

\subsection{The $S_3$ spectrum}

The $S_3$ spectrum is a weaker kind of object and we state its status
before its content. The eight anyons are introduced as an inductive type
with eight constructors and their quantum dimensions are assigned by a
table, so the statement $|\mathrm{Irr}\,D(S_3)| = 8$ is
\emph{Encoding}-grade: it unfolds to the declaration that names it and
derives nothing from $S_3$.

The identities built over that table are \emph{Instance}-grade, and they
are not empty, because a mis-assigned table would break them. The class
equation $1 + 3 + 2 = 6$ holds; the orbit--stabiliser relation
$|C_{S_3}(g)|\cdot|K_g| = 6$ holds for each of the three classes; the
quantum dimensions $(1,1,2,3,3,2,2,2)$ square-sum to the global
dimension $\mathcal{D}^2 = 36 = |S_3|^2$; the charge sector carries a
$d = 2$ object and the transposition-flux sector two objects of $d = 3$;
and the fusion-consistency check
$d_{A_3}^2 = d_{A_1} + d_{A_2} + d_{A_3}$, that is $4 = 1+1+2$, holds.
Six of the eight objects have $d > 1$, so the model is non-abelian in
the sense that matters for braiding: the protected space is
multi-dimensional and the braid group acts on it non-trivially.

The table itself is not our invention and can be checked against the
literature. Kitaev, Sec.~5.1 of Ref.~\cite{KitaevAnyons2003}, labels the
irreducibles of $D(G)$ by pairs $(C,\chi)$ with $C$ a conjugacy class
and $\chi$ an irreducible representation of the centraliser of any
element of $C$, and records for $G = S_3$ exactly the dimensionalities
$1,1,2$; $2,2,2$; $3,3$.

\subsection{Two declarations that are not used}

Two declarations in the development are named for Drinfeld-double
dimension counts but state something else. One unfolds to
$|G| \cdot |G| = |G|^2$ and the other to the implication from
$\sum_i n_i = \sum_i n_i$ to itself. Both are true of any natural
numbers whatever and neither constrains $D(G)$, so neither is used here.
The eight-object count is carried by the enumeration above instead.

\subsection{The abelian counterpoint}

The abelian counterpoint is the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ case, where the same
construction gives the toric code. Here the centre has four simple
objects $\{1, e, m, \varepsilon\}$, the fusion rules close on the Klein
four-group with every object its own antiparticle, all four quantum
dimensions are $1$, and the defining mutual statistic $R(e,m) = -1$
holds. These carry the same \emph{Instance} grade and the same caveat:
the anyon labels and the braiding phase are declared and then checked
for consistency, not derived from a lattice Hamiltonian.

The contrast with $S_3$ is the point. Passing from an abelian to a
non-abelian gauge group takes the global dimension from
$\mathcal{D}^2 = 4$ to $\mathcal{D}^2 = 36$ and introduces objects of
dimension $2$ and $3$, which is what a braid representation needs in
order to be more than a collection of phases. (The toric code shares its
$\mathcal{D}^2 = 4$ with the Ising category of Sec.~\ref{sec:ising},
which is a coincidence of the sum of squared dimensions and not a
relation between the two categories: the Ising value comes from
$1 + 2 + 1$ over three objects, the toric-code value from $1+1+1+1$ over
four.)

\subsection{Two limits}

First, the equivalence
$\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{S_3}) \simeq \mathrm{Rep}(D(S_3))$ organises
this section but is not proved in it. We verify the algebra $D(G)$ on
one side and enumerate the spectrum on the other; the functor between
them is not constructed.

Second, a non-abelian spectrum is not by itself a universal one.
Kitaev's braiding-based universality construction is built on $S_5$, and
he notes there that unsolvability of the group appears to be what the
argument needs (Sec.~7 of Ref.~\cite{KitaevAnyons2003}); $S_3$ is
solvable. The universality result of this paper is the Fibonacci one of
Sec.~\ref{sec:density}, which is proved from an explicit
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ representation and consumes nothing from this section.

\section{Entanglement entropy of a modular tensor category}
\label{sec:entropy}

\emph{Grade: Conditional} for the two biconditional results, which
consume a hypothesis package we state in full; \emph{Theorem} for the
Fibonacci evaluation and the negative result.

Kitaev and Preskill showed that the entanglement entropy of a
topologically ordered medium across a disk of boundary length $L$ has the
form $S = \alpha L - \gamma + \cdots$, with the non-universal coefficient
$\alpha$ depending on short-wavelength physics and the universal
subleading constant given by
\begin{equation}
\gamma = \log \mathcal{D},
\qquad
\mathcal{D} = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_a d_a^2} \;\ge\; 1 ,
\label{eq:kp}
\end{equation}
the sum running over superselection sectors~\cite{KitaevPreskill2006}.
The constant $\gamma$ vanishes exactly when $\mathcal{D} = 1$, that is,
when the category is trivial. This section formalises that dichotomy.

\subsection{Saturation holds exactly when the category is trivial}

Fix a hypothesis package $\mathrm{CH}(S_A, c_{LR}, c'_1)$ recording that
the entropy functional $S_A$ takes the Holzhey--Larsen--Wilczek /
Casini--Huerta form for a one-dimensional interval. Under that package we
prove, for every horizon category $H$ and all $0 < \varepsilon < L$:
\begin{equation}
S_A(H, L, \varepsilon)
 \;=\; \frac{c_{LR}}{3}\log\frac{L}{\varepsilon} + c'_1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal{D}^2(H) = 1 .
\label{eq:chsat}
\end{equation}
and, separately, that on any category with $\mathcal{D}^2(H) > 1$ the
inequality is strict:
\begin{equation}
S_A(H, L, \varepsilon)
 \;<\; \frac{c_{LR}}{3}\log\frac{L}{\varepsilon} + c'_1 .
\label{eq:chstrict}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:chsat} is the content of \eqref{eq:kp} in
biconditional form: the free-field logarithmic law is saturated precisely
by the trivial category, and any topological order produces a deficit.
Equation~\eqref{eq:chstrict} gives the sign of that deficit. Both are
kernel-checked. Both consume $\mathrm{CH}$, which is an assumption about
the form of the entropy functional and not a theorem of this
development; we grade them Conditional accordingly and state the
assumption rather than absorbing it into a definition.

\subsection{The deficit, evaluated}

For the Fibonacci category the two objects have quantum dimensions
$d_1 = 1$ and $d_\tau = \varphi$, so
$\mathcal{D}^2 = 1 + \varphi^2 = (5+\sqrt5)/2$, and we prove
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\mathrm{Fib}}
 \;=\; \tfrac12 \log\!\left(\frac{5+\sqrt5}{2}\right)
 \;\approx\; 0.6429 ,
\label{eq:gammafib}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Fib}} \approx 1.902$. This is a useful place
to check the arithmetic against the literature independently.
Kitaev and Preskill record, for $\mathrm{SU}(2)_k$ Chern--Simons,
$\mathcal{D}^{-1} = \sqrt{2/(k+2)}\,\sin\bigl(\pi/(k+2)\bigr)$, which at
$k=3$ gives $\mathcal{D}^2 \approx 7.236$~\cite{KitaevPreskill2006}. That
is exactly twice \eqref{eq:gammafib}'s argument, as it must be: the
Fibonacci category is the even sector of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_3$, containing
two of its four labels, and restricting to it halves the sum of squared
quantum dimensions.

\subsection{Topological entropy is not an area-law logarithm}

One negative result is worth stating on its own, because it rules out a
form that a reader might otherwise expect. For any horizon category $H$,
any $G_N > 0$, any $\alpha \neq 0$ and any constant $c$, it is
\emph{false} that
\[
\gamma(H) \;=\; \alpha \log\!\left(\frac{A}{4G_N}\right) + c
\qquad \text{for all } A > 0 .
\]
The reason is immediate once stated: $\gamma(H)$ depends only on the
category and not on the area at all, so it cannot equal a non-constant
function of the area. We record it because the logarithmic correction to
black-hole entropy and the topological entanglement entropy are both
logarithms and are easy to conflate, and this result says they are not
the same object.

\subsection{Scope: the error-correction layer}

The development also contains a layer relating horizon data to quantum
error correction, and we state its standing plainly rather than leaving
a reader to infer it.

That layer defines two real quantities on a horizon record (a
\emph{code distance}, defined as $\log d_{\max}$, and a \emph{scrambling
time bound}, defined as $\log \mathcal{D}^2$) and proves statements
about them. The statements are correct. But they are, without exception,
the elementary theory of the logarithm on $[1,\infty)$: the principal
one asserts that $\log d_{\max} > 0$ if and only if $d_{\max} > 1$,
together with the implication that positive code distance forces
positive scrambling-time bound. No error-correcting code is constructed,
no recovery map is defined, no encoding or decoding is exhibited, and no
scrambling dynamics is modelled. The vocabulary is an interpretation
placed on two logarithms, and the theorems are about the logarithms.

Two results in that layer do carry information and we keep them on that
basis. The horizon boundary condition is a five-field predicate
 (positivity of the entropy functional, a positive area-law
coefficient, monotonicity, modular invariance, and anomaly matching)
and a five-field predicate is worth nothing if nothing satisfies it. We
exhibit a witness: the Fibonacci horizon satisfies all five fields.
Symmetrically, the trivially abelian horizon has $d_{\max} = 1$ and hence
$\log d_{\max} = 0$, so it fails the positivity requirement. Together
these establish that the predicate is neither empty nor universal. That
is a real, if modest, thing to know about a definition, and it is what
this layer establishes.

\section{Conclusion and outlook}
\label{sec:open}

The result this paper is for is the one in Sec.~\ref{sec:density}: the
three-strand Fibonacci braid representation has dense image in
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and a machine has checked it. Section~\ref{sec:sk}
turns that existence statement into a computational one by bounding the
length of the approximating braid, with the error bound and the length
bound established of the same compiled word rather than separately. To
our knowledge no comparable universality-with-compilation statement has
previously been checked by a proof assistant in any language.

The rest of the paper is the apparatus that topological quantum
computation is usually presented through, formalised to standards that
vary, and reported at the standard each layer actually reaches. That
variation is the paper's second contribution and we would rather it be
read as one than discovered. Two of the strongest results in the
development are the ones we found by looking past names: the Verlinde
formula holds entry-by-entry at three levels
(Sec.~\ref{sec:modular}), and Casini--Huerta saturation is
biconditional with triviality of the category
(Sec.~\ref{sec:entropy}). Two of the weakest are also the ones with the
most confident names: the Witten--Reshetikhin--Turaev identities are
definitional, and the error-correction layer is the theory of the
logarithm on $[1,\infty)$ under coding vocabulary.

The single most consequential gap is not any of those. It is that the
matrix pair from which Sec.~\ref{sec:density}'s representation is built
is identified with the Fibonacci $F$- and $R$-symbols on the
literature's authority rather than by a checked pentagon or hexagon
equation, so the headline theorem is formally a statement about two
explicit unitaries. Closing that gap would make it a statement about
anyons.

\subsection{Open problems}

We list these in the order in which closing them would most improve the
development, which is not the order of the sections.

\paragraph{Connect the braid representation to the fusion category.}
This is the most valuable single addition, and Sec.~\ref{subsec:posited}
states the gap precisely: the unitary pair from which
Sec.~\ref{sec:density}'s representation is built is not proved to be the
$F$- and $R$-symbol data of the Fibonacci category described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:modular}. A pentagon equation is available for a different
$F$-matrix in a different gauge over a different ring, and no gauge
transformation relating the two is constructed. Building that bridge
would convert the density theorem from a statement about an explicit
matrix pair into a statement about Fibonacci anyons.

\paragraph{Kirby-move invariance.}
The Witten--Reshetikhin--Turaev material of Sec.~\ref{sec:ising} fixes a
normalisation and proves nothing about it. The surrounding
apparatus (Kirby-move equivalence as a relation on surgery
presentations, handle slides, stabilisation, and exact lens-space
evaluations) exists; what is missing is the theorem that the assigned
number is an invariant of the three-manifold rather than of the
presentation.

\paragraph{An adiabatic theorem for the dark state.}
Section~\ref{sec:gate} proves the endpoint sign flip and the vanishing
of the dynamical phase, which are the two ingredients of a $-1$ geometric
phase, but not the transport statement that would make it a holonomy.

\paragraph{Discharge the Casini--Huerta hypothesis package.}
The two biconditionals of Sec.~\ref{sec:entropy} are conditional on an
assumed form for the entropy functional. Deriving that form, rather than
assuming it, would upgrade them from \emph{Conditional} to
\emph{Theorem}.

\paragraph{Eliminate the evaluation axioms.}
The Ising identities of Sec.~\ref{sec:ising} are correct but
evaluation-checked. Replacing \texttt{native\_decide} with kernel
reduction (most plausibly by a better normal form for
$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})$ arithmetic) would put the entire paper on the
same trust base.

\paragraph{Complete the Drinfeld double.}
Section~\ref{sec:drinfeld} constructs the algebra $D(G)$ for arbitrary
finite $G$ but not its comultiplication, counit, antipode or
$R$-matrix, so the quasi-triangular structure that makes
$\mathrm{Rep}\,D(G)$ braided is absent, as is the functor realising
$\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_G) \simeq \mathrm{Rep}(D(G))$. Deriving the
$(C,\chi)$ spectrum from $G$, rather than declaring it, belongs here
too, as does the full $8\times8$ fusion table of $D(S_3)$.

\paragraph{Generalise the Verlinde formula.}
The development defines an abstract Verlinde predicate on pre-modular
data that no object discharges. Proving it for a general modular tensor
category, rather than level by level, would replace an \emph{Instance}
grade with a \emph{Theorem}.

\section*{Formalisation and trust base}
\label{sec:trust}

\paragraph{Ambient axioms.}
Results graded \emph{Theorem}, \emph{Conditional}, \emph{Instance} or
\emph{Encoding} in this paper appeal to no axiom beyond Lean~4's
ambient three: \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}. The development declares no axiom of its own: there
is no hand-written \texttt{axiom} in it.

\paragraph{Evaluation axioms.}
That last sentence is true and, taken alone, would mislead, so we state
the complement. Results graded \emph{Theorem (evaluated)} are discharged
by \texttt{native\_decide}, which compiles a decidable proposition to
machine code, runs it, and accepts the outcome via a generated
per-theorem axiom. Such an axiom is not hand-written and so does not
appear in a census of declared axioms, but it does extend the trust base
from the kernel to the kernel plus the compiler and runtime.

Of the results this paper puts forward as principal, \textbf{18} carry
such an axiom. They are the six hexagon equations, the four ribbon
equations, the two $R$-matrix squares, the $R$-matrix product, the Gauss
sum, the trefoil invariant, the Hopf-link trace, and the two
figure-eight identities: that is, the whole of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ising}'s braiding and knot content. Further results
elsewhere in the development also use \texttt{native\_decide}, including
the two algebra equivalences of Sec.~\ref{sec:qgroups}, the
Frobenius--Perron dimension identities of Sec.~\ref{sec:modular} and the
Fibonacci pentagon equation; the count of 18 is over this paper's
principal results and is not a census of the development.

The principal results of Secs.~\ref{sec:density}, \ref{sec:sk},
\ref{sec:gate} and \ref{sec:entropy} (including the density theorem
and the compilation theorem) carry no evaluation axiom.

\paragraph{Assumed hypotheses.}
Two hypotheses are assumed rather than proved, and both are stated where
they are used rather than absorbed into definitions. The horizon
boundary condition of Sec.~\ref{sec:entropy} is a five-field predicate
(positivity, positive area-law coefficient, monotonicity, modular
invariance, anomaly matching); it is exhibited as satisfiable by the
Fibonacci horizon and as failed by the trivially abelian one, so it is
neither empty nor universal. The Casini--Huerta hypothesis package
assumes that the entropy functional takes the
Holzhey--Larsen--Wilczek form on an interval. Results depending on
either are graded \emph{Conditional}.

\paragraph{Incomplete proofs.}
There are no \texttt{sorry} placeholders in the results reported here.

\paragraph{Declarations not used as backing.}
The development contains declarations whose names describe results
stronger than their statements: among them several that reduce to
\texttt{True}, to $a\cdot a = a^2$, to $6^2 = 36$, or to an implication
from a reflexivity hypothesis to itself. None is cited as backing
anywhere in this paper; where a section's argument would naturally have
reached for one, the section says so and routes around it
(Secs.~\ref{sec:qgroups}, \ref{sec:drinfeld}).

\paragraph{Toolchain.}
Lean~4 with Mathlib at the pinned revision recorded in the public
repository; every result is rebuilt from source by the repository's
continuous build.

\section*{Methods and tools disclosure}
The formal verification layer of this work was developed with
substantial machine assistance: Lean~4 proof drafting and iteration
used LLM-based agents (Anthropic Claude-family models) operating under
the project's staged validation pipeline. Every proof is ultimately
certified by the Lean~4 kernel via \texttt{lake build} on the public
repository, independently of any AI tool, with the explicit exception of
the \texttt{native\_decide} results itemised in
Sec.~\ref{sec:trust}. Manuscript prose was AI-assisted and
human-reviewed.
