\title{Two evaporation regimes for emergent-gravity black holes:\\
a machine-checked partition at the induced-gravity cutoff}

\author{John G.\ Roehm}
\affiliation{NetRxn Foundation}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
A black hole on a substrate whose Newton constant is induced by fermion
loops below an ultraviolet cutoff has two evaporation regimes, told apart
by the sign of $d\Thawk/dt$. Above a critical mass it heats as it
evaporates; below it, it cools toward extremality in infinite time. That
critical mass is the one whose emergent gravitational length reaches the
cutoff length. We formalize the partition in Lean~4: a decidable
classifier, strict monotonicity on each branch, a criterion theorem fixing
the sign of $d\Thawk/dt$ from a mass comparison, and falsifier theorems stating
what would refute it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A Schwarzschild black hole has negative heat capacity: it grows hotter as
it radiates. Its temperature $\Thawk = \hbar/(8\pi M)$ rises without bound
as the mass falls, and a hole of initial mass $M_0$ disappears after a time
of order $M_0^{3}$~\cite{Hawking1975CMP}. A sonic horizon in a flowing
fluid does the opposite. Balbinot, Fagnocchi and Fabbri solved the
linearized backreaction of the emitted phonons on the flow and found that
the horizon migrates, the supersonic region shrinks, and the emission
temperature falls linearly in time at leading order, with a tail
$t \sim \Thawk^{-3}$ that sends $\Thawk$ to zero only after infinite
time~\cite{Balbinot2005PRD}. They compare that end state to a near-extremal
Reissner--Nordstr\"om hole, and we use \emph{extremality} throughout in
that sense: a vanishing temperature that is approached and never attained.

Those are opposite signs of the same derivative. Each is derived on its own
background, and on a fixed background there is nothing for a criterion to
decide. The question becomes sharp on a substrate where the gravitational
coupling is itself induced by the matter it couples to. One system then
carries a geometric description at large mass and a fluid description at
small mass, and something has to separate them.

This Letter supplies that separation and machine-checks it. On a substrate
whose Newton constant is induced by $\Nf$ Dirac species below a cutoff
$\Ladw$, the sign of $d\Thawk/dt$ under evaporation changes at a single
mass,
\begin{equation}
  \Mc = \frac{\Nf\,\Ladw}{12\pi\,\aADW},
  \label{eq:Mc}
\end{equation}
where $\aADW$ measures the departure of the induced coupling from its bare
one-loop value. The scale is fixed once the substrate is, and it is not a
dimensional guess: Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mc} says exactly that the emergent
gravitational length of $\Mc$ equals the shortest length the substrate
resolves.

The formal development comprises \bundleTheorems{} theorems across
\bundleDecls{} declarations, in Lean~\leantoolchain{} against Mathlib
revision \mathlibrev{}. The kernel accepts them from \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound} alone, with no
substrate-specific axiom and no incomplete proof. Which hypotheses the
partition rests on is stated where each is used, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:falsifiers} gives the falsifier theorems whose content is what
would refute the construction.

\section{The critical mass}
\label{sec:Mc}

Sakharov observed that the one-loop effective action of matter fields in a
curved background already contains an Einstein--Hilbert term, so Newton's
constant need not be fundamental~\cite{Sakharov1968,Adler1982}. For $\Nf$
Dirac species regulated by a hard cutoff the induced coupling is
$G_N^{\rm Sak} = 12\pi/(\Nf\Ladw^{2})$. We write
$\GNem = \aADW\,G_N^{\rm Sak}$ for a substrate whose gravitational response
departs from that value, with $\aADW = 1$ the bare induced limit. The
numerical coefficient is regulator dependent; what survives regulator
choice is that the induced coupling is finite and of definite
sign~\cite{Visser2002}.

Substituting $G_N^{\rm Sak}$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mc} gives an identity:
\begin{equation}
  \GNem\,\Mc \;=\; \Ladw^{-1}.
  \label{eq:Mccutoff}
\end{equation}
$\Mc$ is the mass whose emergent gravitational length is the shortest
length the substrate resolves. A hole heavier than $\Mc$ has a horizon
large compared with the cutoff and admits a geometric description. A hole
lighter than $\Mc$ would need a horizon smaller than the substrate's own
resolution, and what responds is the fluid rather than the geometry. Read
this way, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mc} is the mass at which the two descriptions
exchange roles rather than a fitted scale. Equation~\eqref{eq:Mccutoff} is
also where the construction's one free choice sits: no derivation on this
substrate pins the crossover at $\GNem M = \Ladw^{-1}$ rather than at an
order-unity multiple of it, so the coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mc} is an
ansatz even though its parametric form is not.

A hole is described by its mass, its horizon area and its surface gravity
$\kappa$, and a substrate by $(\aADW, \Ladw, \Nf)$, each strictly positive;
$\Mc$ is then positive wherever it is defined
(\texttt{M\_c}, \texttt{M\_c\_pos}). Comparing $M$ against $\Mc$ sorts a
hole into one of three regimes, and the sorting is decidable and
reversible: each regime label holds if and only if the corresponding mass
inequality does (\texttt{classify}, \texttt{classify\_Schwarzschild\_iff},
\texttt{classify\_ADWExtremality\_iff}, \texttt{classify\_Boundary\_iff}).
A regime label and a mass comparison therefore carry the same information,
and neither can drift from the other.

\section{One substrate, both branches}
\label{sec:onesubstrate}

The strongest reason to want a criterion rather than an analogy is that a
single material realizes both signs.

A moving domain wall in a thin film of $^{3}$He-A obeys
$\Thawk(v) = \Thawk^{(0)}(1 - v^{2}/c_\perp^{2})$, with
$\Thawk^{(0)} = \hbar c_\perp/(2\pi d)$ fixed by the wall thickness
$d$~\cite{JacobsonKoike2002}. The temperature falls with the wall speed $v$
and stays finite as $v \to 0$. Radiation is fed by the kinetic energy of
the wall and slows it, so $\Thawk$ rises in time and the end temperature
does not vanish. The thin film sits on the heating branch, and Balbinot
\textit{et al.} draw exactly that contrast, noting that such a film appears
to show a non-vanishing end temperature of the evaporation
process~\cite{Balbinot2005PRD}.

A moving soliton in bulk $^{3}$He-A sits on the other branch. Its horizon
exists only for $v > c_\perp$, and its surface gravity carries a factor
$(1 - c_\perp^{2}/v^{2})^{1/2}$ that vanishes as backreaction slows the
soliton toward $c_\perp$; Jacobson and Volovik accordingly describe that
system as cooling toward an extremal
configuration~\cite{JacobsonVolovik1998}. Two textures in the same
superfluid, evaporating in opposite directions, is what a criterion has to
resolve.

\section{The two branches and the criterion}
\label{sec:branches}

Above $\Mc$ the profile is the textbook one, $\Thawk = 1/(8\pi M)$ in
natural units. It is positive and strictly decreasing in $M$
(\texttt{T\_H\_schwarzschild\_pos},
\texttt{T\_H\_schwarzschild\_strict\_decreasing}), so evaporation, which
drives $dM/dt < 0$, raises the temperature and the lifetime is
finite~\cite{Hawking1975CMP}.

Below $\Mc$ the profile is the sonic one. Writing $\kappa$ for the surface
gravity of the sonic horizon, $c$ for the sound speed, $A_0$ for the
amplitude of the quantum source driving the backreaction and $\epsilon$ for
the expansion parameter that switches it on, Balbinot \textit{et al.}
obtain to leading order
\begin{equation}
  \Thawk(t) = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi}\,\kappa \left[
    1 - \frac{563}{720\pi}\,\epsilon\,\kappa^{3}\,c\,A_{0}\,t
  \right],
  \label{eq:Tsonic}
\end{equation}
together with the extrapolation $t \sim \Thawk^{-3}$ that sends $\Thawk$ to
zero only as $t \to \infty$~\cite{Balbinot2005PRD}. The profile carried
through the development is instead the exponential
$\Thawk(t) = \Thawk^{(0)} e^{-t/\tau_{\rm cool}}$, positive and strictly
decreasing for positive $\Thawk^{(0)}$ and $\tau_{\rm cool}$, and equal to
$\Thawk^{(0)}$ at $t = 0$ (\texttt{T\_H\_acoustic\_evolution} and its
accompanying positivity, monotonicity and initial-value lemmas).

Substituting one for the other is a scope condition on everything proved of
the cooling branch, and it is worth stating twice rather than not at all.
The exponential agrees with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Tsonic} at $t = 0$ and shares the
two features the partition consumes, a temperature that falls throughout
the evaporation and reaches zero only in the infinite-time limit, but it is
a different function. What is proved is proved of a representative of the
cooling branch and not of an exact evaporation history. Equation
\eqref{eq:Tsonic} is itself a leading order and holds for a fluid of
constant sound speed, so a condensate, where $c \propto \rho^{1/2}$,
inherits the branch by the sign of $d\Thawk/dt$ rather than by that
formula.

The partition itself is a single theorem. A substrate satisfying positivity of
$\Mc$ and of the initial temperature, a positive evaporation rate above
$\Mc$, a negative one below, and the substrate response
$\dADW = (\aADW - 1)\Ladw$, has an evaporation rate whose sign is fixed by
a mass comparison alone: positive when $M > \Mc$, negative when $M < \Mc$
(\texttt{H\_RegimePartition}, \texttt{regime\_partition\_criterion}). The
two branch signs enter as hypotheses, each grounded in its own primary
source, Ref.~\cite{Hawking1975CMP} above $\Mc$ and
Ref.~\cite{Balbinot2005PRD} below it. What the criterion adds is that the
comparison against $\Mc$ decides between them, which is the statement
neither source makes.

Two of the four laws of black-hole mechanics enter the development, and
neither in full. The first-law slot fixes the positivity $0 < \GNem$ that
the coupling $1/(8\pi\GNem)$ requires, together with the response identity
above; the differential first law itself is not formalized. The second-law
slot records the Glorioso--Liu conclusion, that the entropy-current
divergence is non-negative, as a hypothesis rather than as a consequence,
because deriving it needs the Schwinger--Keldysh effective
action~\cite{GloriosoLiu2018,CrossleyGloriosoLiu2017,GloriosoCrossleyLiu2017}.
Constancy of $\kappa$ over the horizon, and any affine-time statement of
the third law, are absent for want of Killing-vector and affine-time
machinery. What does follow unconditionally is that a hole classified into
the cooling branch and satisfying those two slots has a strictly negative
evaporation rate (\texttt{FourLaws\_ADWExtremality},
\texttt{four\_laws\_consistent\_with\_acoustic\_regime}).

\section{What would refute this}
\label{sec:falsifiers}

A construction assembled from hypotheses is worth stating only if the
hypotheses can fail, so each load-bearing component is paired with a
theorem that derives a contradiction from its denial.

A single hole above $\Mc$ observed to cool as it evaporates refutes the
construction, with no appeal to how the cooling was modelled: the partition
hypotheses, a heating-branch classification and a non-positive rate are
together contradictory (\texttt{falsifier\_schwarzschild\_heating}).

On the cooling branch the refutable content is a comparison of two
measurements rather than a functional form. Any alternative history that
agrees with the profile at the start of an interval, and fails to decrease
across it, must disagree at the end
(\texttt{falsifier\_acoustic\_decay\_form}).

The third law enters through the time $\tau(T_\star)$ needed to reach
$\Thawk = T_\star$. Its strong form requires $\tau(T_\star)$ to exceed any
prescribed bound for all small enough $T_\star$~\cite{Israel1986}. Kehle
and Unger construct regular collapse data for the Einstein--Maxwell-charged
scalar field system that reach an exactly extremal horizon at finite
advanced time, which bounds $\tau$ uniformly on such a
tail~\cite{KehleUnger2022ThirdLaw}. The two are contradictory
(\texttt{falsifier\_third\_law\_form}), so a substrate must choose and the
third-law slot cannot be filled vacuously. The theorem selects neither
alternative.

The substrate response is constrained from both sides. It is strictly
positive whenever $\aADW > 1$, and nonzero exactly when $\aADW \neq 1$
(\texttt{falsifier\_alpha\_ADW\_dependence},
\texttt{delta\_ADW\_nonzero\_iff\_alpha\_ADW\_ne\_one}). A substrate with
$\aADW > 1$ and a non-positive response is excluded, and so is one measured
away from the bare induced limit with a vanishing response.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The result has two limits worth stating. No closed-form $\Thawk(M)$
interpolating the regimes is derived: $M = \Mc$ is the value at which the
classifier changes branch, not a demonstrated smooth crossover, and no
single analytic profile is shown to join the branches there. The
entropy-production statement is imposed within each regime separately, so
nothing here matches the entropy current across the boundary.

The partition is also independent of whether the substrate induces gravity,
and the two questions have different answers on the same examples. A film
of $^{3}$He-A satisfies Sakharov's conditions, while the acoustic
condensate of Finazzi--Liberati--Sindoni fails universal
coupling~\cite{FinazziLiberatiSindoni2012PRL}: its effective metric is
defined only for the phonon sector below the healing length, and the
depleted atoms occupying excited single-particle states carry no coupling
to that metric. That asymmetry is itself formalized, and a companion paper
on emergent gravity through black-hole thermodynamics carries the
classification in full~\cite{Roehm2026D3}
(\texttt{horizonCrooks\_substrate\_partition},
\texttt{volovikJannes\_vs\_flsBEC\_depletion\_asymmetry}). The cooling
branch survives the failure, because a negative $d\Thawk/dt$ constrains the
time evolution of the emission temperature on a given background and says
nothing about how the dynamics of that background arise. The two questions
meet only at the induced-gravity layer, where the Clausius relation
$\delta Q = T\,dS$ on every local Rindler horizon returns the Einstein
equation as an equation of state~\cite{JacobsonEFE1995}.

The entropy-production hypothesis is the natural target for replacement by
a derivation. Kirklin proves the generalized second law in perturbative
gravity to all orders beyond the semiclassical limit and without a UV
cutoff, at the cost of a free-energy term for the degrees of freedom that
locate the later horizon cut~\cite{Kirklin2024GSLAllOrders}, and Faulkner
and Speranza obtain the same law for arbitrary cuts of a Killing horizon
from crossed-product algebras~\cite{FaulknerSperanza2024}. Formalizing
either would turn the recorded hypothesis into a consequence. What settles
the partition, though, is measurement: a sonic horizon whose emission
temperature is observed to fall while it radiates lands in the cooling
branch, and the sign of $d\Thawk/dt$ is accessible in a fluid experiment or
a lattice simulation long before any end state is reached.
