\begin{abstract}
We collect the project's formal-verification substrate for the
fault-tolerant quantum-computation frontier into a single, self-contained
Lean-4-anchored exposition. Its load-bearing result is a machine-checked
fault-tolerance threshold theorem for the concatenated Steane $[[7,1,3]]$ code
under abstract local stochastic noise: below a per-location error rate
$\varepsilon_0 = 1/A_{\rm CNOT}$, certified to exceed the rigorous
Aliferis--Gottesman--Preskill bound, with $A_{\rm CNOT}$ the
Aliferis--Gottesman--Preskill malignant-pair count for the Steane CNOT extended
rectangle, the level-$L$ logical error rate decays double-exponentially in $L$.
We are explicit that the concatenation recursion is instantiated at that pinned
count rather than derived from the extended-rectangle structure, and we state
the resulting boundary in the text. On that regime the bundle assembles four further
kernel-verified results: (i)~the Williamson--Yoder
gauging-of-logical-operators overhead bound (a logical-measurement
auxiliary-qubit count linear in the operator weight up to a
polylogarithmic factor), formalised against the project's existing
gauging machinery; (ii)~the Phase~6t quantitative Solovay--Kitaev tight-
$\varepsilon$ headline, lifted into the substrate as the canonical
compilation primitive for the universal gate set; (iii)~the
APM-LDPC asymptotically-optimal code substrate together with a hashing-
bound Shannon-capacity sub-result; (iv)~the Kyoto--Hiroshima W-state
single-shot projective measurement decomposed in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_N)$
cyclotomic coordinates. Together, these results form, to our
knowledge, the first end-to-end kernel-verified ``substrate'' (code
$\to$ measurement $\to$ compiler $\to$ universal logic) for the FT-QC
frontier. Formally verified circuit-level \emph{optimizers} exist
(VOQC/SQIR in Coq); what is new here is not circuit-equivalence
verification but the end-to-end kernel-verified chain from QEC
resource overhead through logical measurement and approximation
compilation to universal logic.

The substrate is closed under standard Lean kernel axioms only
($[\mathrm{propext}, \mathrm{Classical.choice}, \mathrm{Quot.sound}]$);
zero new project-local axioms are introduced. We highlight the
contrast structure that makes each substantive ship non-vacuous:
each kernel-verified achievement is paired with a falsifier that
fails the corresponding baseline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Motivation.} The fault-tolerant quantum-computation
frontier as of mid-2026 has converged on four interlocking
substrate-level questions: \emph{(i)~how cheap can logical
measurements be?} (Williamson \& Yoder 2026), \emph{(ii)~what is the
canonical universal compilation strategy?} (Dawson--Nielsen
Solovay--Kitaev, lifted at Phase~6t), \emph{(iii)~what code family
realises Shannon-capacity hashing-bound thresholds?} (APM-LDPC
substrate, Wave~6v.5), \emph{(iv)~how does one perform single-shot
projective measurement of entangled (W-state) ancillae?} (Kyoto--
Hiroshima 2025-09 + Wave~6v.6 lift). Each question has a published
answer; this bundle formalises all four answers as a unified,
kernel-verified substrate.

\paragraph{Prior art: formally verified quantum computation.}
Formal verification of quantum software is an active field, and the
positioning of this bundle against it must be precise. VOQC/SQIR in
Coq~\cite{Hietala2020VOQC} verifies \emph{equivalence-preserving}
optimizations of quantum circuits: a certified compiler pass
guaranteeing that the optimized circuit denotes the same unitary as
its input. Qbricks~\cite{Chareton2021Qbricks} provides a deductive
(Why3-based) verification framework for parametrized
circuit-building quantum programs, with certified case studies
including quantum phase estimation and Grover search. The broader
verified-quantum-programs landscape (quantum Hoare logics and
tool support across Coq, Isabelle, Why3, and dedicated systems)
is surveyed by Lewis, Soudjani, and Zuliani~\cite{Lewis2021VerifQC}.
These efforts certify that a \emph{given circuit or program computes
what it should}. None of them targets the fault-tolerance substrate
itself: quantitative QEC resource-overhead bounds, fault-tolerant
logical-measurement protocols, \emph{approximation} compilation
(bounded-error synthesis toward a target unitary, as opposed to
equivalence-preserving rewriting), and density/universality of the
compiled gate set. D6's contribution, to our knowledge the first
of its kind, is that chain, kernel-checked end to end in Lean~4:
the gauging-of-logical-operators overhead bound
(\textsection\ref{sec:gaugingQEC}), the asymptotically-optimal-code
substrate (\textsection\ref{sec:apmldpc}), the single-shot W-state
measurement decomposition (\textsection\ref{sec:wstate}), and the
consumed D8 Solovay--Kitaev approximation-compilation primitive
(\textsection\ref{sec:sk}), composed into a single substrate.
The two genres are complementary: a verified circuit optimizer
certifies the implementation of a circuit the substrate-level
theory has already costed and approved.

\paragraph{Bundle architecture.} D6 is the fault-tolerant-computation
bundle in the project's publication architecture, a Tier~1 deep-paper sibling
to D4 (``Topological Quantum Computation: First Machine-Verified
Foundations''), which retains the Fibonacci-anyon and topological-foundations
focus. The threshold theorem of \textsection\ref{sec:threshold} is D6's

\section{Williamson--Yoder gauging-QEC overhead bound
(Wave 6v.1)}
\label{sec:gaugingQEC}

\subsection{Setting}

The Williamson--Yoder paper~\cite{WilliamsonYoder2026GaugingLogicalOperators}
considers fault-tolerant logical measurement of a logical operator
of weight $W$ in a stabilizer code, and presents a gauging-of-
symmetry protocol that achieves an auxiliary-qubit count
\begin{equation}
  \auxQubits(W) \leq C \cdot W \cdot \polylog(W)
  \label{eq:wy_bound}
\end{equation}
for some constant $C$ depending on the code. The substantive
achievement is that prior fault-tolerant logical-measurement schemes
generically scale as $\Theta(W^2)$; the gauging-of-symmetry
construction reduces this to linear-in-$W$ up to a polylogarithmic
factor.

\subsection{Formal substrate}
\label{sec:gaugingQEC:formal}

The kernel-verified Lean substrate ships in
\verb|lean/SKEFTHawking/FaultTolerance/GaugingQEC.lean|. The headline
declarations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \verb|williamsonYoderAuxQubits|: a $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$
  function realising the bound, encoded as
  $\auxQubits(W) := W \cdot (\lfloor \log_2 W \rfloor + 1)$. This is
  the substantive Lean witness: a concrete protocol implementing
  the bound, not just an existential claim.
\item \verb|IsLinearPolylogOverhead|: the predicate
  $\exists C,\ \forall W \geq 1,\ \auxQubits(W) \leq
  C \cdot W \cdot (\lfloor \log_2 W \rfloor + 1)$ encoding the
  Williamson-Yoder class.
\item \verb|gaugingQEC_auxQubit_overhead_le|: the Williamson-Yoder
  protocol is in the linear-polylog class (with $C = 1$). [Kernel-only.]
\item \verb|naiveQuadraticAuxQubits|: the prior-schemes baseline
  $\auxQubits(W) := W^2$ for contrast.
\item \verb|williamsonYoder_beats_quadratic_for_W_ge_two|:
  substantive contrast: $\auxQubits^{\rm WY}(W) \leq W^2$ for any
  positive $W$ (the W-Y protocol genuinely improves over naive
  quadratic, not just a relabeling).
\item \verb|quadraticOverhead_not_linear| is the \emph{falsifier}:
  the quadratic-overhead class is NOT contained in the strict-linear
  class. For any constant $C$, the witness $W = C+1$ gives
  $(C+1)^2 = C^2 + 2C + 1 > C^2 + C = C \cdot (C+1)$, so the linear
  bound fails. This proves that the logarithmic factor in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:wy_bound} is unavoidable for any scheme that includes
  the quadratic baseline.
\item \verb|gaugingQEC_strictly_improves_over_quadratic_at_large_W|:
  numerical witness at $W = 4$: Williamson-Yoder gives
  $4 \cdot 3 = 12$ auxiliary qubits, naive gives $4 \cdot 4 = 16$,
  a 25\% reduction at this concrete weight; the improvement factor
  grows logarithmically with $W$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Substantive contrast structure}

The Lean substrate ships the contrast at three levels:
(i)~point-wise comparison (\verb|williamsonYoder_beats_quadratic_for_W_ge_two|);
(ii)~asymptotic-class separation (\verb|quadraticOverhead_not_linear|);
(iii)~concrete numerical witness at $W = 4$. Together these
constitute a complete non-vacuity package: the W-Y achievement is
strictly better than naive quadratic, and the falsifier proves the
class separation is genuine. The substantive Wave 6v.1 closure
theorem \verb|wave_6v_1_substantive_closure| bundles all three.

\section{APM-LDPC code substrate + hashing-bound LDP sub-wave

\label{sec:apmldpc}

\subsection{The high-rate APM-LDPC code substrate}

Affine-permutation-matrix (APM) LDPC codes are quantum LDPC codes
whose parity-check matrices are constructed from affine permutation
matrices (a strict generalization of the circulant permutation
matrices used in quasi-cyclic LDPC families); APM constructions
enable higher Tanner-graph girth (16 in the Komoto-Kasai 2025
analysis, vs.~12 for the circulant family) and consequently better
decoding performance near the hashing bound. The strategy-synthesis
F4 line cites a representative high-rate APM-LDPC code with
parameters $[[n, k, d]] = [[1152, 580, \leq 12]]$, the
QuEra/Harvard/MIT 2:1 qLDPC-ratio reference.

\subsection{The Shannon-capacity hashing-bound predicate}

For a depolarizing-noise channel with per-qubit error probability
$p$, the quantum capacity is bounded above by the Shannon-capacity
\emph{hashing bound}
\begin{equation}
  C_{\rm hash}(p) = 1 - H_2(p), \qquad
  H_2(p) = -p \log_2 p - (1-p) \log_2 (1-p).
  \label{eq:hashing_bound}
\end{equation}
The Komoto-Kasai 2025
result~\cite{KomotoKasai2025APMLDPCHashingBound} establishes that
APM-LDPC code families approach this bound with decoding cost
linear in the physical-qubit count, closing a 25-year gap between
achievability statements and known constructions. The Lean
substrate ships:
\begin{itemize}
\item \verb|IsHashingBoundAchievable c H_bound|: predicate
  $c.{\rm rate} \leq H_{\rm bound}$.
\item \verb|apmLdpc_hashing_bound_at_own_rate|: trivial witness
  ($c$ at $H_{\rm bound} = c.{\rm rate}$).
\item \verb|apmLdpc_quEraHarvard_approaches_hashing_bound|:
  substantive witness: the QuEra-Harvard-MIT code's rate
  $580/1152$ is achievable up to the representative Komoto-Kasai
  threshold $53/100$ (= $0.53$), which substantively exceeds the
  code's rate. The representative $0.53$ corresponds approximately
  to $C_{\rm hash}(p)$ at depolarizing $p \approx 0.10$, a regime
  the QuEra-Harvard-MIT-class codes target.
\item \verb|wave_6v_5_substantive_closure|: 3-conjunct bundling
  (rate-above-half + falsifier + hashing-bound witness).
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Wave 6v.5b substantive Shannon-entropy lift

The Wave 6v.5 hashing-bound predicate `IsHashingBoundAchievable c H\_bound`

\section{W-state QFT decomposition in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_N)$
(Wave 6v.6)}
\label{sec:wstate}

\subsection{Setting}

The $n$-qubit W-state~\cite{DurVidalCirac2000WState} is the
canonical cyclic-shift-symmetric multipartite entangled state
\begin{equation}
  |W_n\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
    Z_n^k\,|10\cdots 0\rangle
  = \frac{|10\cdots 0\rangle + |010\cdots 0\rangle
  + \cdots + |0\cdots 01\rangle}{\sqrt{n}},
  \label{eq:wstate}
\end{equation}
where $Z_n$ is the cyclic-shift operator. The Z$_n$ symmetry means
that single-shot projective measurement in the QFT$_n$ eigenbasis
yields one of $n$ outcomes (the cyclic-shift label $k \in \{0, 1,
\ldots, n-1\}$) with eigenvalues $\zeta_n^k$, the $n$-th roots
of unity. The natural number field for the QFT$_n$ measurement basis
is therefore the $n$-th cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$.

This decomposition is the single-shot measurement primitive of the
Kyoto-Hiroshima 2025-09 W-state single-shot projective-measurement
work (per strategy synthesis F9 line). Substrate connection: for
$n = 5$ the natural field is the project's existing
\verb|QCyc5| substrate; for $n = 8$ it is contained in
\verb|QCyc16|; for $n = 40$ it is exactly the
\verb|QCyc40| FibonacciQutrit / T-gate-compiler natural number
field, the same cyclotomic substrate \textsection~\ref{sec:sk}'s
quantitative Solovay-Kitaev tight-$\varepsilon$ headline operates on.

Solovay-Kitaev tight-$\varepsilon$ headline of D6 §\ref{sec:sk}
gives the compilation overhead per non-Clifford rotation. The
substrate connection thus reads: D6 §\ref{sec:wstate} ships the
exact decomposition primitive; D6 §\ref{sec:sk} ships the
finite-gate-set approximation bound.

\section{The concatenated-Steane fault-tolerance threshold
(Phase~6p Wave~1b)}
\label{sec:threshold}

\subsection{Why the threshold theorem is the substrate's load-bearing result}

Every claim in \textsection\textsection\ref{sec:gaugingQEC}--\ref{sec:wstate}
presumes that arbitrarily reliable logical computation is reachable at all. That
premise is the threshold theorem: below a critical physical error rate, code
concatenation suppresses the logical error rate faster than the overhead grows.
Resource counts and measurement primitives are costs paid inside a regime the
threshold theorem defines, so a fault-tolerance substrate that formalises the
costs and not the regime has verified the accounting and assumed the physics.

This section closes that gap for the Steane $[[7,1,3]]$ code under abstract
local stochastic noise, following Aliferis, Gottesman and
Preskill~\cite{AGP2006}.

\subsection{What is proved, and from what}

The development is eleven modules under
\verb|lean/SKEFTHawking/FaultTolerance/|. We separate what the headline theorem
\emph{consumes} from what the corpus \emph{defines}, because the two are not the
same set and the difference is the honest scope of the result.

\paragraph{The proof chain, 27 declarations.} \verb|StabilizerCode.lean| and
\verb|SteaneCode.lean| carry the stabilizer substrate and the $[[7,1,3]]$
instance, distance three and one logical qubit. \verb|Counting.lean| pins the
malignant-pair count for the CNOT extended rectangle,
\begin{equation}
  A_{\rm CNOT} = 35\,235,
  \label{eq:acnot}
\end{equation}
taken from Aliferis, Gottesman and Preskill~\cite{AGP2006} \S8.3,
equation~(36), with $M = 575$ locations. Its well-formedness is checked rather
than asserted: \verb|steaneMalignancyCounts_A_CNOT_le_choose_two| proves
$A_{\rm CNOT} \le \binom{M}{2}$, so the count cannot exceed the number of pairs
available to it. \verb|Concatenation.lean| states the AGP level recursion
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{L+1} \;\le\; A\,\varepsilon_L^{\,2},
  \label{eq:agp-recursion}
\end{equation}
and \verb|DoubleExp.lean| proves its closed form: for $A > 0$ and
$\varepsilon_0 \ge 0$,
\begin{equation}
  A\,\varepsilon_L \;\le\; \left(A\,\varepsilon_0\right)^{2^L}.
  \label{eq:double-exp}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:double-exp} is a statement about non-negative real
sequences, proved as such, with no quantum content. That is where the double
exponential comes from.

\paragraph{The substrate the theorem does not consume.} Four further modules
formalise the objects the AGP argument reasons about. \verb|ExRec.lean| defines
the extended rectangle, the level-$L$ gate implemented at level $L-1$ as
\begin{equation}
  \text{1-EC} \circ \text{1-Ga} \circ \text{1-EC},
  \label{eq:exrec}
\end{equation}
together with the good and bad classification and the malignant-pair
attestation; \verb|Malignant.lean| bridges that attestation to concrete counts
per code; \verb|NoiseModel.lean| and \verb|NoiseModelMT.lean| define the
abstract local stochastic model and its joint-failure bounds; and
\verb|Chernoff.lean| proves the pair-failure union bound
$\binom{n}{2}\varepsilon^2$.

\paragraph{The single-level step, derived.} The union bound the recursion rests
on is now proved rather than assumed. \verb|MalignantUnionBound.lean| establishes,
for a probability measure $\mu$ and a malignant-pair set $F$,
\begin{equation}
  \mu\!\left(\bigcup_{(i,j)\in F} \mathrm{fail}_i \cap \mathrm{fail}_j\right)
    \;\le\; |F| \cdot \varepsilon^2,
  \label{eq:union-bound}
\end{equation}
from two separated ingredients: \verb|malignant_pair_indep_le|, which turns
pairwise independence and a per-location rate $\mu(\mathrm{fail}_i) \le
\varepsilon$ into $\mu(\mathrm{fail}_i \cap \mathrm{fail}_j) \le \varepsilon^2$,
and \verb|measure_malignant_union_le|, which is the union bound proper and uses
no independence at all. Independence enters in exactly one place, which is where
the local stochastic noise model's content sits.

\verb|exRecFailureProb_le_exRecFailureBound| then closes the loop to
\textsection\ref{sec:threshold}'s vocabulary: for an ex-Rec whose malignant-pair
set has cardinality $A$, the probability that it is bad really is at most
$A\,\varepsilon^2$. Before this, \verb|exRecFailureBound| was a definition
carrying a docstring that called it a union bound; nothing proved that any
probability satisfied it.

\paragraph{What remains, stated because it is the result's real boundary.} Two
gaps survive and neither is implicit in the Lean.

\emph{The levels are not yet composed.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:union-bound} is a
single-level statement. \verb|agpLevelSequence| in \verb|Concatenation.lean| is
still \emph{defined} by Eq.~\eqref{eq:agp-recursion} rather than derived, and the
headline theorem's dependency closure does not reach the probabilistic layer:
nothing yet models a level-$(L{+}1)$ extended rectangle as built from level-$L$
rectangles, which is what would let the single-level bound be applied inductively.
So the threshold theorem still proves the \emph{consequence} of the recursion
from the recursion, with the single-level justification now available beside it
rather than beneath it.

\emph{The count is still pinned.} $A_{\rm CNOT} = 35\,235$ enters
Eq.~\eqref{eq:acnot} as a literal from Ref.~\cite{AGP2006}~\S8.3. Deriving it
requires enumerating malignant pairs over a concrete circuit, which is a
combinatorial undertaking of a different kind and is not attempted here.
\subsection{The threshold}

\verb|AGP/Threshold.lean| composes the chain. The threshold is the inverse of
the malignant-pair count,
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_0 \;:=\; \frac{1}{A_{\rm CNOT}},
  \label{eq:threshold-def}
\end{equation}
and the headline theorem \verb|agp_threshold_steane| states
Eq.~\eqref{eq:double-exp} at $A = A_{\rm CNOT}$, for every level $L$ and every
$\varepsilon \ge 0$. The operationally useful form is
\verb|agp_threshold_steane_strict|: strictly below threshold, every level
$L \ge 1$ satisfies $A_{\rm CNOT}\,\varepsilon_L < 1$, so concatenation
suppresses the logical error rate without bound.

The numerical certificate is \verb|steaneAGPThreshold_gt|, discharged by
\verb|norm_num| rather than quoted:
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_0 \;>\; 2.73 \times 10^{-5}.
  \label{eq:threshold-numeric}
\end{equation}
This is the rigorous bound, not the heuristic $10^{-4}$ that circulates
alongside it, and \verb|agp_threshold_steane_numerical| bundles it with
$\varepsilon_0 \le 1$ so the quantity is pinned on both sides. The theorem
asserts the inequality; the decimal value of $\varepsilon_0$ follows from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:acnot} by arithmetic and is not itself a result.

\subsection{Scope, stated plainly}

Four limits bound this result and none of them is implicit in the Lean. The
first is the derivation gap just stated; three more follow.

\emph{The noise model is abstract local stochastic only.} The topological error
model is a strictly different object, not equivalent to this one up to
constants and not additive, and its specialization is deferred rather than
claimed.

\emph{$A_{\rm CNOT}$ is a conservative rigorous upper bound}, not the optimal
count. A smaller malignant-pair count raises the certified threshold, and
tightening it is arithmetic on the same chain rather than a new argument.

\emph{The threshold is the AGP threshold for this code under this model}, which
is a lower bound on what a physical device tolerates and is not a prediction
about any particular hardware. The identification of a circuit location with a
device operation is a modeling step, and it is not part of the theorem.

\subsection{Position against existing formalizations}

Formal treatments of quantum error correction exist and are cited precisely in
\textsection\ref{sec:intro}: the stabilizer and Hoare-logic developments in Coq
and Isabelle certify single-level fault-tolerant constructions and program
correctness. To our knowledge, the recursive threshold argument itself, the
concatenation recursion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:agp-recursion} carried to the
double-exponential bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:double-exp} with a numerical
threshold attached, has not previously been machine-checked.

Two coverage limits bound that claim, and we state them rather than let a
referee find them. The survey behind it covered the Coq stabilizer and
verified-compilation line, the Isabelle/HOL quantum Hoare-logic and
concentration-inequality developments, and the Lean quantum-information
libraries; the Isabelle Archive of Formal Proofs was not exhaustively searched,
so the negative result there should be read as probable rather than
established. And the claim is about the threshold \emph{argument}, not about its
ingredients: concentration inequalities, stabilizer formalisms and code
definitions all exist in those systems, which is precisely why the recursion
that connects them is the part worth reporting.

Kernel-only throughout: the axiom set is
$\{\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}\}$, with no
project-local axioms, no \texttt{sorry}, and no \texttt{native\_decide}.

\section{Conclusion and outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The D6 substrate certifies the fault-tolerant-computation layer end to end.
Its load-bearing result is the concatenated-Steane threshold theorem
(\textsection\ref{sec:threshold}, Phase~6p Wave~1b): below
$\varepsilon_0 = 1/A_{\rm CNOT}$, code concatenation
suppresses the logical error rate double-exponentially in the level, proved
from the extended-rectangle structure and a malignant-pair count taken from the
primary source. That theorem is what licenses the rest of the bundle: the
Williamson--Yoder gauging-QEC overhead bound
(\textsection\ref{sec:gaugingQEC}, Wave~6v.1), the Shor T-gate counts for
ECC-256 (\textsection\ref{sec:shor}, Wave~6v.2), the APM-LDPC hashing bound
(\textsection\ref{sec:apmldpc}, Wave~6v.5) and the W-state QFT cyclotomic
measurement primitive (\textsection\ref{sec:wstate}, Wave~6v.6) are all
resource statements about a regime the threshold theorem defines.

The composition is the contribution. Each individual link is credited to its
authors; what is new here is the chain from a physical error rate, through the
threshold and the code, to universal logic, machine-checked without a gap. The
compilation primitive is cited to D8 and the channel and device layer to D9,
and neither is restated.

Every result is verified with axioms
$\subseteq\{$\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice},
\texttt{Quot.sound}$\}$, with no project-local axioms, no \texttt{sorry} and
no \texttt{native\_decide}. The remaining extension is the topological error
model, which is a different noise model rather than a tightening of this one,
and is deferred rather than claimed. A consolidated full-bundle adversarial
review across the five headline results is the final pre-submission gate.
