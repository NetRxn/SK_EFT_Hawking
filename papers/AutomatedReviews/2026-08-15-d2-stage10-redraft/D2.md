# D2 Stage-10 redraft: apex theorems that cannot carry their prose, and three misattributions

## Summary

**4 CRITICAL, 3 MAJOR.** Found while re-reading D2's primary sources and checking every
declared apex theorem's STATEMENT (not merely that its name resolves) during the Stage-10
redraft.

The redraft repairs the *prose* side of every item below. What it cannot repair, and what
these findings track, is the *substrate* side: four theorems whose statements are
tautologies or arithmetic padding are registered as D2 apex theorems, and the bundle
metadata's `apex_theorems[].claims` field asserts physics content for them that their
statements do not carry. A future lift reading that metadata will re-introduce the same
overclaim, because the metadata is where the claim lives.

Two of these (1.1, 1.2) are the same failure mode that survived four Stage-13 passes on
L3: the name resolves, the equation is quoted correctly, and the system the equation
describes is not the one the theorem is about.

### 1.1 — 🔴 CRITICAL — `hom_tensor_adjunction_dim` is `rfl`, and both the draft and the bundle metadata say it discharges hypothesis H2

- **Severity:** critical
- **Lane:** `lean`
- **Verify:** `cd "$REPO" && python3 -c "import json;d=json.load(open('lean/lean_deps.json'));e=[x for x in d if x['name'].endswith('hom_tensor_adjunction_dim')][0];assert 'Eq rank rank' not in e['type'], 'still a tautology: '+e['type']"`
  *What it asserts:* that the theorem registered as D2's H2 discharge has a statement
  saying something other than `rank = rank`. Exits 1 at HEAD.
- **Gate:** LeanProofSubstance
- **Location:** `lean/SKEFTHawking/ChangeOfRings.lean:66-68`; `papers/D2/bundle_metadata.json` (`apex_theorems`, entry `SKEFTHawking.hom_tensor_adjunction_dim`); `papers/D2/paper_draft.tex:458-484` (pre-redraft)
- **Observed:** The theorem is

  ```lean
  /-- dim Hom_A(A tensor_B P, k) = dim Hom_B(P, k) = rank(P). -/
  theorem hom_tensor_adjunction_dim (rank : ℕ) :
      rank = rank := rfl
  ```

  The bundle metadata claims for it: *"the A(1) -> A change-of-rings adjunction at the
  DIMENSION-EQUALITY level, substantively discharging hypothesis H2."* The pre-redraft
  draft carried a subsection titled *"Discharging hypothesis H2 via change-of-rings"*
  concluding *"the only remaining Mathlib-side hypotheses ... are now H1, H3 and H4."*

  **The Lean module's own docstring contradicts all of that**, and has since a 2026-07-21
  correction: *"⚠ This is hypothesis H2, and the argument above is NOT formalized below
  ... **H2 is OPEN**"* and *"Remaining hypotheses: H1, H2, H3, H4 — **all four**."* The
  module also carries `theorem h2_discharged_TODO : True := trivial`, explicitly renamed
  so the name could not be read as a proof.

  The same draft's ABSTRACT correctly says four tracked hypotheses. So the pre-redraft
  manuscript asserted three and four in the same document.
- **Expected:** A theorem registered as an apex discharge of a named hypothesis has a
  statement that mentions the objects in that hypothesis. `∀ rank : ℕ, rank = rank` is
  satisfied by every type with an inhabitant and constrains nothing.
- **Fix:** Either formalize the adjunction (the Phase 5q.T `A1ExtReal` route named in the
  module) and keep the apex registration, or **deregister
  `SKEFTHawking.hom_tensor_adjunction_dim` from `papers/D2/bundle_metadata.json`** and
  correct its `claims` string. The redraft has already removed every prose claim that H2
  is discharged; the metadata entry is what will otherwise regenerate it.

- **Lane:** `lean`
- **Verify:** `cd "$REPO" && python3 -c "import json;d=json.load(open('lean/lean_deps.json'));e=[x for x in d if x['name'].endswith('hidden_sector_required')][0];t=e['type'];assert 'ZMod 16' not in t or 'SM' in t or 'nu' in t, 'still a bare ZMod 16 inverse fact: '+t"`


  The metadata claims: *"3 x 15 = -3 mod 16: any internally consistent SM extension

  ℤ/16 admits a cancelling class. Whether the SM without ν_R *has* such a class, and what

- **Fix:** Deregister or restate. If the intended content is the per-generation residue,
  the substantive theorems are the `SMFermionData` component-count results, which do
  mention the fermion species. The redraft attributes the physics claim to its actual
  sources (see 1.3) and cites this theorem only for the arithmetic.

### 1.3 — 🟡 MAJOR — the ℤ₄ generator is written in a normalization that is not the source's, with no reconciliation

- **Severity:** major
- **Lane:** `prose`
- **Verify:** `cd "$REPO" && grep -q "5(B - L) - 4Y" papers/D2/paper_draft.tex && grep -q "normalis" papers/D2/paper_draft.tex`
  *What it asserts:* that if the draft still writes the `5(B−L) − 4Y` form, it also
  states the normalization convention that reconciles it with the source.
- **Gate:** CitationIntegrity
- **Location:** `papers/D2/paper_draft.tex:573-586` (pre-redraft); primary source
  `Lit-Search/Phase-5e/primary-sources/GarciaEtxebarria2019.pdf` eq. (4.13), printed p. 42
- **Observed:** The draft writes, attributed to García-Etxebarria and Montero,
  `X = 5(B − L) − 4Y`. The source's eq. (4.13) reads `X ≡ −2Y + 5(B − L)`, and its
  Table 2 is self-consistent with the −2 coefficient at every one of the seven entries
  (e.g. `l_L^c`: Y = −3, B−L = 3, X = 21 = −2(−3) + 5(3)).

  The two agree only after a rescaling the draft never states. The source says it has
  *"rescaled the hypercharge Y and B − L such that all fields have integer charges"*:
  its Y is 6 × the standard hypercharge and its B−L is 3 × the standard. Substituting,
  `X_source = 3·[5(B−L) − 4Y]` in standard normalization. Since only `X mod 4` matters
  and 3 is a unit mod 4, the draft's *conclusion* (every fermion carries odd ℤ₄ charge)
  survives; the written formula still does not match the cited equation.
- **Expected:** A referee checking eq. (4.13) finds the paper's formula, or finds the
  sentence that reconciles them.
- **Fix:** State the normalization explicitly at the point of use. The redraft does this.

  (`apex_theorems`, entry `SKEFTHawking.TPFEvasion.tpf_outside_gs_scope_main`)

### 1.6 — 🔴 CRITICAL — the abstract and introduction claim N_f = 3 is forced by two independent chains; the ℤ₁₆ chain forces no generation count at all, and §2.4 of the same draft says so

- **Severity:** critical
- **Lane:** `prose`
- **Verify:** `cd "$REPO" && python3 -c "t=open('papers/D2/paper_draft.tex').read();assert 'two independent settings' not in t and 'two completely independent algebraic chains' not in t, 'two-independent-chains overclaim still present'"`
  *What it asserts:* that the draft no longer claims the generation count is forced twice
  over. Exits 1 at HEAD.
- **Gate:** ComputationCorrectness
- **Location:** `papers/D2/paper_draft.tex:69-72` (abstract), `:125-128` (introduction),
  contradicted by the same file at `:326-333`
- **Observed:** The abstract states *"the generation count N_f = 3 appears in two
  independent settings (Z₁₆ + modular)"*, and the introduction states *"the generation
  count N_f = 3 is forced, by the modular argument and (in the no-ν_R case) by the Z₁₆
  argument, through two completely independent algebraic chains."*

  Neither is true, on the draft's own numbers:

  | branch | condition | solutions |
  |---|---|---|
  | ℤ₁₆, with ν_R (16 per generation) | `16·N_f ≡ 0 (mod 16)` | **every** N_f — no constraint on the count |
  | ℤ₁₆, without ν_R (15 per generation) | `15·N_f ≡ 0 (mod 16)`, and `gcd(15,16) = 1` | `N_f ∈ 16ℤ` — **excludes** N_f = 3 |
  | modular | `24 ∣ 8·N_f` | `N_f ∈ 3ℤ` |

  The ℤ₁₆ argument constrains the fermion content *per generation*, which is what
  García-Etxebarria and Montero actually claim (*"the number of fermions in a generation
  of the standard model, once we include the right-handed neutrino"*). It never yields
  3ℤ. In the no-ν_R branch it yields 16ℤ, and composing with the modular constraint gives
  `N_f ∈ 48ℤ`, which the draft itself states at line 328-330 and correctly calls *"in
  tension with the observed N_f = 3."*

  So the manuscript asserts the ℤ₁₆ chain forces N_f = 3 in the abstract and the
  introduction, and asserts it excludes N_f = 3 in §2.4.
- **Expected:** The composition is an **exclusion of the no-ν_R Standard Model at the
  observed generation count**, not a second derivation of that count. That is a real and
  publishable result, and it is what the sources support: Wang 2024 §IV.2 treats exactly
  this case and supplies the missing `c₋ = 3/2` from an additional sector rather than
  reporting a contradiction.
- **Fix:** Restate the headline as one conditional generation constraint (modular, Wang's
  proposal) plus one ν_R-content constraint (Dai-Freed ℤ₁₆), whose composition excludes
  the no-ν_R SM at N_f = 3. The redraft's spine is rebuilt on this; the "four-thread
  convergence" subtitle goes with it.

### 1.7 — 🔴 CRITICAL — the Fidkowski--Kitaev citation names the wrong modulus, inverts the paper's actual result, and the bibliography entry is a different paper from the one held on disk

- **Severity:** critical
- **Lane:** `prose`
- **Verify:** `cd "$REPO" && python3 -c "t=open('papers/D2/paper_draft.tex').read();i=t.find('FidkowskiKitaev2010');assert i<0 or 'mathbb{Z}_{16}' not in t[max(0,i-400):i+400], 'Fidkowski-Kitaev still attributed a Z16 reduction'"`
  *What it asserts:* that no ℤ₁₆ reduction is attributed to Fidkowski--Kitaev anywhere
  near a citation of them. Exits 1 at HEAD.
- **Gate:** CitationIntegrity
- **Location:** `papers/D2/paper_draft.tex:613-627` (pre-redraft), bibliography entry at
  `:1239-1243`; primary source
  `Lit-Search/Phase-5e/primary-sources/FidkowskiKitaev2010.pdf`, abstract and §I
- **Observed:** Three distinct errors in one passage.

  (a) **Wrong modulus.** The draft writes *"This is the Fidkowski-Kitaev reduction
  ℤ → ℤ₁₆ at the SPT level."* The source's abstract reads: *"The topological invariant
  for the free fermion classification lies in ℤ, but with the introduction of
  interactions the ℤ is broken to ℤ₈."* The result is ℤ → ℤ₈, in 1+1D, class BDI. The
  ℤ₁₆ of this paper's subject belongs to the Spin^{ℤ₄} five-bordism computation, not to
  Fidkowski--Kitaev. García-Etxebarria and Montero's introduction keeps these apart
  explicitly, citing one reference for the multiple of 8 and a different one for the
  multiple of 16.

  (b) **The physics claim is inverted.** The draft writes *"eight Majorana wires cannot
  be gapped without breaking time-reversal."* The source proves the opposite, and it is
  the paper's entire point: *"it turns out that we can use non-trivial quartic
  interactions to gap out the dangling Majorana modes for the case of 8 Majorana chains
  -- this is what we will focus on in this paper."* Where the construction *fails* is at
  **four** chains (§II.A: for 4 Majorana chains the only possible quartic interaction
  leaves a doubly degenerate ground state, giving a gapless midpoint).

  (c) **The bibliography entry is a different paper.** The bibitem reads *"Topological
  phases of fermions in one dimension, PRB 83, 075103 (2011), arXiv:1008.4138"*, while
  `CITATION_REGISTRY['FidkowskiKitaev2010']` and the PDF actually held at
  `primary_source_path` are *"The effects of interactions on the topological
  classification of free fermion systems", PRB 81, 134509 (2010), arXiv:0904.2197*.
  Both papers are by the same two authors, which is what let the substitution pass.
- **Expected:** A referee in this field knows the ℤ₈ result cold. Attributing ℤ₁₆ to it
  and inverting the gapping statement is the single most damaging error in the
  manuscript.
- **Fix:** Attribute ℤ → ℤ₈ in 1+1D to Fidkowski--Kitaev, state the gapping result in
  the direction they prove it, source the ℤ₁₆ to the bordism computation, and correct
  the bibliography entry to the paper actually held. The redraft's §4 brief instructs
  the correct attribution; this finding tracks the bibliography and registry mismatch,
  which the prose fix alone does not close.
