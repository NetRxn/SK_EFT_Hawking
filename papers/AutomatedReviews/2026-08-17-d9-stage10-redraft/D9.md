## F1 — CRITICAL — the randomized-benchmarking "certificate" is directionally inverted

- **severity:** critical
- **lane:** claims
- **Gate:** `stage10_claims`
- **Location:** `papers/D9/paper_draft.tex` abstract ("a randomized-benchmarking-style two-sided
  certificate"), §5.1 ("$F_{\mathrm{avg}}$ controls and is controlled by the diamond distance to
  the identity"; "the two-sided bracket connecting an averaged benchmark number to worst-case
  channel error"; "these theorems certify the *fidelity-to-diamond conversion*"); §1 Contributions
  item 4; `papers/D9/bundle_metadata.json` apex entry for
  `SKEFTHawking.QuantumNetwork.avgGateFidelity_diamondDist_two_sided`
  ("the randomized-benchmarking-style two-sided certificate"); and the module docstring
  `lean/SKEFTHawking/QuantumNetwork/RBCertificate.lean` ("a measured average gate fidelity
  `F_avg` … **certifies a worst-case diamond distance** to the ideal channel").
- **Observed:** the corpus proves only a **lower** bound on the diamond distance in terms of
  $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$. `avgGateFidelity_diamondDist_bound` is
  `1 - sqrt(((n+1)·avgGateFidelity K - 1)/n) ≤ diamondDist K (idKrausPad m n)`.
  The theorem named `avgGateFidelity_diamondDist_two_sided` has three conjuncts, and the quantity it
  two-sides is **not** the diamond distance: conjuncts 1 and 2 bracket
  `traceDist (krausMap (tensorKraus K) (maxEntangled n)) (maxEntangled n)` — the trace distance
  between the Choi/maximally-entangled images — between `1 - sqrt(((n+1)F_avg - 1)/n)` and
  `sqrt(1 - ((n+1)F_avg - 1)/n)`; conjunct 3 is `traceDist … ≤ diamondDist K (idKrausPad m n)`.
  The diamond distance therefore appears exactly once, on the **greater** side of a `≤`. Its
  underlying lemma `entanglementDistinguishability_fvdg_bracket` is likewise a Fuchs–van de Graaf
  bracket on the maximally-entangled trace distance, not on the diamond distance.
- **Evidence:** statements read from `lean/lean_deps.json` (authority). Exactly two declarations in
  the whole `SKEFTHawking.QuantumNetwork` family mention both `avgGateFidelity` and `diamondDist`
  (`avgGateFidelity_diamondDist_bound`, `avgGateFidelity_diamondDist_two_sided`), and in neither
  does `diamondDist` ever occur as the bounded (left) argument of a `≤` — see Verify.
- **Why this is critical, not cosmetic:** a certificate must upper-bound the worst case. "Worst-case
  channel error is *at least* X" is not a certification statement; it cannot retire an error budget.
  Worse, the missing direction is not a gap that further work closes: $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$ is an
  average over input states and cannot upper-bound a supremum over input states in general
  (a channel can have arbitrarily small average infidelity and $O(1)$ diamond distance).
  The claim is therefore not merely unproven but false as stated, and the Lean module's own
  docstring repeats it, so the error is not confined to prose.
- **Expected:** the manuscript states the one-sided result it has (a *lower* bound on worst-case
  error from a benchmark number — a falsification tool, not a certificate), and lists the absent
  upper bound in §Documented walls with the reason it cannot be supplied from $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$
  alone.
- **Fix:** (a) manuscript: retitle §5.1's RB paragraph, state the two theorems by what they bound,
  add the wall. Landed in this redraft. (b) substrate follow-up: correct the `RBCertificate.lean`
  module docstring and consider renaming `avgGateFidelity_diamondDist_two_sided` to name the
  quantity it actually two-sides (`entanglementTraceDist`), since the current name is the source of
  the prose error. Not landed — substrate change, filed here.

## F3 — MAJOR — the erasure/PLOB "mutual consistency" compares two different channels

- **severity:** major
- **lane:** claims
- **Gate:** `stage10_claims`
- **Location:** §4.3 (`sec:network-capacity`): "the cross-link $1-p \le \mathrm{plob}(1-p)$
  (`erasureBound_le_plobBound`) proven: the two ceilings are mutually consistent, with the erasure
  form tighter on its domain."
- **Observed:** `plobBound η = -log₂(1-η)` is, per Pirandola–Laurenza–Ottaviani–Banchi (Nat. Commun.
  **8**, 15043 (2017), p. 1, col. 2), the two-way capacity **of the lossy bosonic channel** with
  transmissivity $\eta$. `erasureBound p = 1-p` is the two-way quantum capacity **of the qubit
  erasure channel** with erasure probability $p$ (Bennett–DiVincenzo–Smolin 1997; PLOB p. 1
  explicitly notes this is the one two-way capacity that was previously known). These are
  capacities of two *different* channels. Nothing about physics requires them to be ordered, and
  neither bounds the other. What the theorem actually proves, once the definitions are unfolded, is
  the real-analysis inequality $\eta \le -\log_2(1-\eta)$ on $[0,1)$ — an immediate consequence of
  the already-stated `linear_le_plobBound` ($\eta/\ln 2 \le \mathrm{plob}(\eta)$) and
  $\ln 2 < 1$. Presenting it as a cross-check that "the two ceilings are mutually consistent" gives
  a real inequality a physical warrant it does not have.
- **Evidence:** PLOB2017 p. 1 (held: `Lit-Search/Phase-1-and-Background/primary-sources/PLOB2017.pdf`),
  read at page level: "for the erasure channel we show that $K = 1-p$ … Finally, for the lossy
  channel, we show that $Q_2 = K = -\log_2(1-\eta)$ where $\eta$ is the transmissivity."
- **Expected:** the theorem is stated as what it is (a numerical ordering of the two rate-bound
  functions under the identification $\eta = 1-p$), with the channel mismatch stated, or the claim
  is dropped.
- **Fix:** restated in this redraft as a numerical ordering, with the channel identification named
  as a modelling step and no consistency claim.
- **Verify (executed, fails at HEAD):**
  ```bash
  grep -c "mutually consistent" papers/D9/paper_draft.tex
  ```
  Result at HEAD: `1` (expected `0`).

## F8 — MAJOR — the opening motivation cites evidence that argues against the contribution

- **severity:** major
- **lane:** narrative
- **Gate:** `stage10_prose`
- **Location:** §1 ¶1: "cross-validation studies have found quantitative disagreement between mature
  simulators on timing predictions, while fidelity predictions agree under matched error
  models~\cite{Chung2025}: **precisely the situation in which a machine-checked, model-independent
  envelope provides a deductive reference.**"
- **Observed:** the clause before the colon is faithful to the source. The clause after it is not
  supported by it, and is undercut by the paper's own §4.2, which states that
  "Handshake-inclusive link timing is model-dependent and deliberately not bounded: this is exactly
  the layer on which simulators disagree". D9's envelopes are **fidelity** envelopes. The cited
  study finds that simulators **agree** on fidelity and differ only on timing — by a constant factor
  the authors identify and attribute to the connection models, i.e. an explained and quantified
  difference, not an accuracy failure. So the one published cross-validation the paper leans on
  reports agreement exactly where D9 supplies a reference, and a resolved discrepancy exactly where
  D9 declines to supply one.
- **Evidence:** Chung *et al.*, arXiv:2504.01290, p. 1 abstract (held PDF), read at page level:
  "Our findings indicate that while the simulators differ in the time required to complete network
  tasks—a constant factor difference attributable to their respective connection models—they agree
  on the fidelity of the distributed resources under identical error parameters." Also p. 2 col. 1:
  "We find that the simulators disagree on the predicted time, differing by a constant factor. Using
  the differences in their designs, we identify and correctly quantify this offset. Furthermore, the
  simulators agree on the predicted fidelities of the final states, pointing to correct
  implementation of realistic error models in both simulators."
- **Expected:** the motivation stands on what a deductive reference is *for* — a claim that survives
  independently of any implementation, usable in an error budget — rather than on a simulator
  discrepancy the source resolved. Where Chung *et al.* is cited, the agreement finding is stated as
  agreement.
- **Fix:** §1 rewritten in this redraft: Chung *et al.* is cited for what it shows (independent
  implementations converge on fidelity under matched models, and the remaining difference is a
  design-attributable timing offset), and the warrant for the envelope layer is restated without
  leaning on a disagreement.

## Checked and found sound (recorded so a later pass does not re-open them)

These were attacked in the same way as the findings above and held up. Statements read from
`lean/lean_deps.json`; carriers unfolded to Mathlib or to an in-project construction.

- **`haarPauliZSqAverage_eq`** — the pattern to fear here (a constant *defined* as `1/3` and
  "proven" by `rfl`) is **not** present. `haarPauliZSqAverage` is a genuine iterated interval
  integral `(1/(4π))∫₀^{2π}∫₀^{π} ((pauliExpZ (blochKet θ φ)).re)^2 sin θ`, reduced to
  `haarPauliBlochAverage` through `pauliExpZ_blochKet` (an honest spinor computation) and evaluated
  by `cosSq_mul_sin_integral`, which is proved by `intervalIntegral.integral_eq_sub_of_hasDerivAt`
  against the antiderivative $-\cos^3\theta/3$. The manuscript's claim that the $1/3$ is "proven in
  the Bloch picture rather than assumed" is supported.
- **`diamondDist_eq_choiSDP` / `choiDualValue`** — `choiDualValue` is a real
  `sInf {r | ∃ W, W.PosSemidef ∧ (W - (J(Φ₁)-J(Φ₂))).PosSemidef ∧ r = ‖ptrace2 W‖}`, and the norm
  is `Matrix.instL2OpNormedRing.norm` (the spectral/L2-operator norm, opened as
  `Matrix.Norms.L2Operator`), which is the norm the Watrous program uses. Not an entrywise-sup
  stand-in. Kernel-pure with only `IsKrausChannel` hypotheses. Attainment on both sides
  (`exists_diamondDist_eq`, `exists_choiDualValue_eq`) is real.
- **`maxFlow_eq_minCut`** — genuine strong duality: `∃ f S, IsFeasibleFlow f ∧ IsCut S ∧
  flowValue f = cutCap S ∧ (∀ g feasible, flowValue g ≤ flowValue f) ∧ (∀ S' cut, cutCap S ≤ cutCap S')`.
  Both optimality conjuncts present.
- **`quantum_fannes_audenaert_sharp`** — the constant really is $\log(d-1)$: the RHS is
  `Real.qaryEntropy (Fintype.card ι) T` and Mathlib's `qaryEntropy q p = p·log(q-1) + binEntropy p`.
  The only hypotheses are `2 ≤ card ι` and the monotone-regime restriction `T ≤ 1 - 1/d`, both
  disclosed in the manuscript. (Notation caveat: F13.)
- **`mirsky_unconditional`** — no side condition beyond hermiticity; sorted-eigenvalue $\ell^1$
  distance bounded by the trace norm, as claimed.
- **`swapChain_fidelity_envelope`** — true and non-tautological (`endToEndFidelity F k =
  (1 + 3·wernerParam F ^ k)/4`, `wernerParam F = (4F-1)/3`), but weak: the proved interval
  $[\tfrac14,1]$ is the image of the hypothesis interval, so the theorem is a closure/invariance
  statement about the Werner parameterization rather than a bound that constrains a chain beyond
  its own parameter range. The manuscript's novelty claim ("to our knowledge, the first formally
  verified protocol-level fidelity bound for quantum networks") is not false, but I have restated
  what the envelope does and does not exclude rather than leaving it to carry §4.
- **`expNeg046_tight`** — `0.631 ≤ exp(-0.46) ≤ 0.632` is a real numeric enclosure, correct
  ($e^{-0.46} = 0.63128\ldots$).
- **`bbpsswRecurrence_gt`**, **`dejmps_single_step_can_decrease`** — the honesty witness is real:
  the corpus does ship a counterexample delimiting the monotone claim.
