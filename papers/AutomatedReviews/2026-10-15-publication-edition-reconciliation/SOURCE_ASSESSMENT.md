## D1 claim boundaries

The held Falque author preprint describes the smooth spectrum as quasi-thermal and quotes approximately 3 K in its own normalization. Applying the standard temperature convention to its measured gradients gives the manuscript's smooth approximately 85 mK scale and steep formal gradient scale approximately 134 mK. The discussion calculates Hawking flux and describes the predicted steep-horizon Hawking spectrum as nonthermal. The measured excitation dispersions and fluid profiles are separate from that emission prediction. D1 now identifies the latter as a scale and the 1.6-fold change as a measured gradient increase, without asserting hotter thermal emission. Its hierarchy caption distinguishes cavity decay validity ratios from perturbative fractional temperature shifts. These are interpretation changes; adopted device numbers are preserved.

The identity m*c_s squared/k_B≈0.81K expresses mean-field energy in temperature units. It supplies neither mode occupation nor an in-band detected PSD, and its ratio to T_H does not establish a spontaneous background-to-signal ratio. D1 now requires a measured in-band noise budget. Its stimulated factor is an adopted gain coefficient; converting it to detected counts requires greybody and transfer calibration, collection/detection efficiencies and measured reservoir, pump and detector fluctuations. The ideal shot-noise calculation remains conditional. Dispersion alone is not presented as a measurement of greybody transmission or a reason to assume a featureless noise floor.
