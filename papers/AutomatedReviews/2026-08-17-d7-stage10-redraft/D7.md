## D7-01 — CRITICAL — the KZM scaling exponent is the freeze-out-time exponent, documented as the defect-density exponent, and the cited paper's measured value lies outside the interval the module proves universal

- **severity**: CRITICAL
- **lane**: substrate
- **Gate**: `prose_theorem_reference_coverage`; also the docstring-claim surface with no gate
- **Location**: `lean/SKEFTHawking/KibbleZurekUnruh.lean:31-32, 97, 110-115, 128-138`
- **Observed**: `kzmScalingExponent e := (e.ν * e.z) / (1 + e.ν * e.z)` is documented in six
  places as the exponent of the **defect density**: "universal scaling exponent `μ`
  controlling the KZM defect density `n_def(τ_Q) ~ τ_Q^{-μ}`" (line 31-32), "Together they
  fix the defect-density scaling `n_def(τ_Q) ~ τ_Q^{-μ}` with `μ = νz / (1 + νz)`"
  (line 97). The standard Kibble--Zurek results are the freeze-out length
  `ξ̂ ~ τ_Q^{ν/(1+νz)}`, the freeze-out time `t̂ ~ τ_Q^{νz/(1+νz)}`, and the defect density

  **freeze-out-time** exponent. It coincides with the defect-density exponent only when
  `d = z`. The module's only concrete check, `kzmScalingExponent_1d_tfim_eq_one_half`, is
  the 1D TFIM where `d = ν = z = 1`, so the coincidence hides the misidentification at
  exactly the point that was meant to falsify it.

  ("the formal cross-check between the SK-EFT prediction ... and the KZM defect-density

  carrying the defect-density claim; or the defect-density claim is deleted from every
  docstring. In both cases the Tindall cross-check claim is deleted or replaced by a

- **Fix**: Correct the six docstring sites; add the dimension-carrying exponent as a new

## D7-05 — MAJOR — `bpFactorUpdate` sums over all complete assignments, not over the factor's scope

- **severity**: MAJOR
- **lane**: substrate
- **Gate**: none
- **Location**: `lean/SKEFTHawking/BeliefPropagation.lean:282-288`, and the modelling note
  at lines 259-265
- **Observed**: the sum ranges over
  `(Finset.univ : Finset (ν → X)).filter (fun y => y v = x)`, i.e. over complete assignments
  to **every** variable of the graph, while the Yedidia--Freeman--Weiss rule sums over
  assignments to `∂a` only. The product is correctly restricted to
  `otherVars G a v`. Two consequences follow. If `factorWeight a` depends on a variable
  outside `∂a`, then `m_{a→v}` depends on it, and the update is not local in the factor
  graph at all. If `factorWeight a` does factor through `∂a`, the sum overcounts the
  textbook sum by the constant `|X|^(|ν| - |∂a|)`, rescaling each factor message by a
  per-endpoint constant. Because `IsBPFixedPoint` is an exact equality of message bundles
  on unnormalized messages, that rescaling is not neutral, and no theorem relates the fixed
  points of the two rules.
- **Evidence**: the module's own docstring concedes the deviation and calls it "a sound
  modeling choice for finite factor graphs" without an argument. The sum's cardinality is
  `|X|^(|ν|-1)` regardless of `|∂a|`, which is also why the update is exponentially more
  expensive than the algorithm it models.
- **Expected**: either a `factorWeight` typed on `∂a` (a dependent `(∂a → X) → ℝ`, or a
  hypothesis `∀ y y', (∀ u ∈ ∂a, y u = y' u) → factorWeight a y = factorWeight a y'`), or a
  theorem that the fixed points of the two rules coincide under the scope hypothesis.
- **Fix**: add the scope hypothesis as a definition `FactorScoped G factorWeight` and a
  theorem `bpFactorUpdate_eq_scopedSum` relating the two under it; this is where the
  convergence theorem's physical interpretation is currently unbacked.
- **Verify** (fails at HEAD):

## D7-13 — MINOR — `bundle_sentence_length` counts a `verbatim` listing as one prose sentence

- **severity**: MINOR
- **lane**: infrastructure
- **Gate**: `bundle_sentence_length`
- **Location**: `scripts/validation/checks/bundles_readiness.py`, the `over_100_words`
  measurement (splitting at `re.split(r"(?<=[.!?])\s+", body)` over the whole body from
  `\begin{document}` to `\begin{thebibliography}`)
- **Observed**: the body is not stripped of `verbatim`, `lstlisting` or `align` content
  before splitting, and code carries no sentence-terminating punctuation followed by
  whitespace, so an entire listing is measured as a single sentence. A modest appendix of
  Lean statements registers as a 400 to 500 word sentence.
- **Evidence**: a probe of the check's own tokenizer on a synthetic body containing one
  20-line `verbatim` block returns a run of 165 tokens between two three-word prose
  sentences. The check's stated purpose is sentences "a reader must re-read to parse",
  which a code listing is not, and the incentive it creates is to shorten listings at
  exactly the venues where listings are the contribution.
- **Expected**: `verbatim`, `lstlisting` and displayed-math environments are removed before
  the split, alongside the existing comment stripping and macro blanking.
- **Fix**: extend the existing `_strip_tex_comments` preprocessing with an environment
  stripper for the code and display environments, then re-freeze the ratchet against the
  corrected measurement. The current ratchet was frozen against a measurement that includes
  listings, so it will need re-baselining rather than tightening.
- **Verify** (fails at HEAD):
