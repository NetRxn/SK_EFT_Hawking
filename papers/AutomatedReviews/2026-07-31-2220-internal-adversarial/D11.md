## QI Candidate

**A guard's gating constant must be derived by running the guard's own predicate over the live
corpus, and the derivation must be committed alongside it.**

Three of this round's findings (4.2, 4.5, and 5.1's fail-open paths) are the same defect: a
threshold, baseline or allow-list chosen from a *characterisation* of the data rather than from
the *quantity the guard computes*.

- `_PREFIX_FLOOR = 30` was set after measuring **label lengths**; the predicate compares **common
  prefixes**, whose corpus maximum is 18. The check cannot fire.
- `_LEDGER_DANGLING_BASELINE = 67` equals the count of ledger records whose id mentions
  stage9/stage10; the guard counts `review:`-scheme ids that resolve to no node, which is 66.
  Right ballpark, wrong population, and the justifying comment misdescribes 63 of the 66.
- `notebook_stored_outputs_current`'s MIME allow-list was written from the payload types the two
  current notebooks happen to emit, so it is correct today and blind the moment a renderer changes.

The author's own comment at `scripts/validate.py:3841` already names this as a six-time recurrence
this session and asserts the seventh is fixed. It is not. The recurrence is now structural enough
to deserve a rule rather than another comment:

> **Proposed rule.** No numeric threshold, baseline or allow-list may be introduced into a
> `validate.py` check without (a) a measurement of the *exact quantity the check compares*, taken
> over the live corpus, and (b) a test that plants a known positive and asserts the check fires.
> A guard that has never been observed to fire is unverified, not passing — the same standard
> already applied to guards that cannot import their dependencies.

The second half — plant a positive — is the part that would have caught all three. There is a
ready-made positive for the recurrence check in this repo: the "effective modulus" misnomer,
closed and re-raised across five rounds, and the `paper10_modular_generation:5.3` pair whose two
labels differ by one verb tense.
