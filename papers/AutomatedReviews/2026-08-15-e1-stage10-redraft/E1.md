# E1 — findings from the Stage-10 full redraft

## Summary

**2 BLOCKER, 3 REQUIRED, 3 RECOMMENDED.**

The two blockers came out of reading the primary source at page level,
which no check and no reviewer in this pipeline does. Both concern the
same paper the bundle takes every device parameter from, and both would
have been found by its own authors, who are the most likely referees.
Neither is a citation-formatting defect: one is a factor of $(2\pi)^2$
between the temperature this bundle prints and the temperature its source

as-built device's position, uses one dispersive coefficient throughout,
and drops three apex theorems whose statements do not carry their
claims. What remains filed is what lives in code, in figures, or in
another bundle, and which a prose redraft must not fix in passing.

`bundle_prose_em_dash_free` and `bundle_reader_facing_voice` are green
on E1; the latter two still fail corpus-wide on other bundles (F, D3,

### 1.1 — 🔴 BLOCKER — The primary source prints a Hawking temperature $(2\pi)^2$ above the one this bundle computes from the source's own $\kappa$

- **Severity:** blocker
- **Lane:** `research`
- **Verify:** `cd "$REPO" && grep -q 'lkb_T_H_falque_norm_K' papers/E1/paper_draft.tex && grep -q 'lkb_norm_ratio' papers/E1/paper_draft.tex`
  *What it asserts:* that the manuscript reconciles the two normalizations using **generated** values rather than a hand-typed aside. A cheap fix that merely added the sentence "conventions differ" with a typed number would not pass, because both scalars must be present and both are rendered from `papers/E1/tables.py`. Exits 1 at the pre-redraft baseline `a41d0ee3~1` (verified 2026-08-15).
- **Gate:** CitationIntegrity
- **Location:** `papers/E1/paper_draft.tex` (§II, the normalization paragraph);
  `Lit-Search/Phase-5w/primary-sources/Falque2025.pdf` p.6 §V and Eq. (9);
  `src/core/constants.py` (`POLARITON_PLATFORMS['Paris_standard']['kappa']`);
  propagated to `papers/D1` §6, which carries the same LKB device specs.
- **Observed:** The bundle computes $T_H = \SI{85}{\milli\kelvin}$ from
  $\kappa = 7\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ via $k_B T_H = \hbar\kappa/2\pi$.
  Falque et al., the source of that $\kappa$, write on p.6: "For the smoothest
  horizon (whose full spectrum is quasi-thermal) we obtain a Hawking
  'temperature' of $T^{\rm HR} = \hbar\kappa/k_B = 3$ K." The pre-redraft draft
  cited that paper for $\kappa$ nine times and never mentioned the discrepancy.
- **Evidence:** Read page-level from the held PDF. Their Eq. (9) is
  $I^{\rm HR}_\omega = 1/(e^{\omega/2\pi\kappa}-1)$. That exponent is
  $(2\pi)^2$ from the standard $2\pi\omega/\kappa$, and it is not a typo:
  it reproduces their own quoted values. With $f - f_{\rm pump} = 20$ GHz and
  $\kappa = 0.07\,\mathrm{ps^{-1}}$, $\omega/2\pi\kappa = 0.2857$ gives
  $I = 3.02$, and at $\kappa = 0.11\,\mathrm{ps^{-1}}$ gives $I = 5.01$;
  they report "an increase from $I^{\rm HR}_\omega = 3$ for smooth horizons
  to $I^{\rm HR}_\omega = 5$ for steep horizons." That exponent fixes
  $k_B T = h\kappa$, giving $\SI{3.36}{\kelvin}$, which is the 3 K they print.
  Their written formula $\hbar\kappa/k_B$ evaluates to $\SI{0.53}{\kelvin}$
  and matches neither. $\SI{85.09}{\milli\kelvin} \times (2\pi)^2 = \SI{3.36}{\kelvin}$
  exactly. Their $\kappa$ (their Eq. 8) is the ordinary-rate surface gravity,
  so no difference in the definition of $\kappa$ absorbs the factor.
- **Expected:** The manuscript uses $k_B T_H = \hbar\kappa/2\pi$, the
  convention of Unruh, Visser and Barceló et al., which the source itself
  cites as its refs [3-5], and it states in print that the source normalizes
  differently and by how much, so that a reader holding 3 K does not read
  85 mK as a disagreement about a measured quantity.
- **Fix:** Landed in the redraft §II. What remains is propagation: `papers/D1`
  §6 carries the same device specs and the same $T_H$ relation, and the
  bundle metadata records that E1 and D1 agree "character-for-character" on
  them, so D1 needs the same paragraph or an explicit pointer to E1's.
- **Cache:** local primary source (PDF), all 13 pages read.

## Not filed, and why

- **The as-built device failing the weak-dissipation criterion.** This is
  the redraft's headline result, not a defect: the platform record is
  correct, `tier1_valid = False` is computed correctly, and the figure
  plotted it correctly. The defect was that no manuscript sentence said so,
  which is 2.2.

- **`attenuation_ge_one` being elementary.** Its statement, $1 \le
  \exp(\Gamma L/v_g)$ from three sign hypotheses, is exactly what the
  redraft claims for it. What was wrong was the prior draft's assertion that
  it carried "the BEC Re $G \ge 0$ chain" to polaritons; the proof uses
  nothing from any BEC result. That claim is gone and the theorem is now
  cited for what it proves, so there is nothing left to file.

- **`hawking_temp_from_surface_gravity` being a `rfl`-discharge.** Already
  filed as `review:2026-05-11-1550-bundle-stage13:E1:5.2` and still open.
  Not re-filed. E1 no longer declares or cites it; E2 still does, and the
  existing finding is the right home.

- **`Paris_standard.Gamma_pol = 1.25e11` versus the source's $\hbar\gamma
  \approx \SI{80}{\micro\electronvolt}$ (which gives $1.215\times10^{11}$).**
  A 2.9% round-trip through the rounded $\tau = 8$ ps. It moves
  $\Gamma_{\rm pol}/\kappa$ from 1.74 to 1.79 and changes no verdict.

- **The 600-character abstract ceiling.** Recorded as `source_verified:
  false` in the metadata, and a scout pass could not reach journals.aps.org.
  The redraft's abstract is roughly 780 characters against the prior draft's
  ~2400. Whether it clears an unverified figure is not something this redraft
  can settle, and the metadata already carries the caveat.

- **Worktree-only gate failures.** `render_bundle_counts.py` triggers a full
  Mathlib clone in a worktree with no `.lake`. That is an environment
  condition, not a defect, and the counts were instead computed directly from
  `lean/lean_deps.json` using the same `render()` the script uses.
