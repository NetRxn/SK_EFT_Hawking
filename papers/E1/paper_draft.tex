\title{Cavity Lifetime Gates Analog Hawking Detection in
Exciton-Polariton Microcavities}

\author{J.~Roehm}
\affiliation{Independent Researcher}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Exciton-polariton fluids now support acoustic horizons of tailored
curvature with the surface gravity measured directly. At the
Paris-LKB platform's published smooth-horizon value
$\kappa = \input{tables/lkb_kappa_smooth.tex}\unskip\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$~\cite{Falque2025} the
standard analog-Hawking relation gives
$T_H = \input{tables/lkb_T_H_smooth_mK.tex}\unskip\,\si{\milli\kelvin}$, eight orders above the
atomic-BEC analogs, and a coherent probe crossing the horizon is
amplified by $G(\omega) = \Gamma(\omega)/(e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa_{\rm eff}}-1)$,
which reaches one half at
$\omega = \input{tables/lkb_gain_threshold.tex}\unskip\,\kappa_{\rm eff}$ for a
saturated greybody factor. The obstacle is not the horizon but the
cavity: the as-built \SI{\input{tables/lkb_tau_cav_ps_standard.tex}\unskip}{\pico\second}
device has $\Gamma_{\rm pol}/\kappa = \input{tables/lkb_ratio_standard.tex}\unskip$,
so the weak-dissipation expansion that licenses these numbers fails
by more than an order of magnitude and holds only for
$\tau_{\rm cav} \gtrsim \SI{\input{tables/lkb_tau_required_ps.tex}\unskip}{\pico\second}$.
We give the predicted spectrum, its falsifiable window
$\omega \lesssim 0.3\,\kappa$, and the two measurements that would
close it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Analog Hawking radiation has been observed in atomic Bose-Einstein
condensates at $T_H \approx \SI{0.35}{\nano\kelvin}$
\cite{Steinhauer2016,deNova2019}, a temperature so far below every
systematic in the apparatus that the signal must be recovered from
density-density correlations accumulated over $\sim 10^{4}$
realizations. Exciton-polariton fluids promise to remove that
constraint rather than to fight it. Their sound speed is two orders
of magnitude larger than in a dilute atomic gas and their horizons
can be written optically, so the velocity gradient that sets the
surface gravity is both large and adjustable, and predicted
temperatures lie in the \SI{0.1}{\kelvin} to \SI{1}{\kelvin} range
\cite{Gerace2012,Nguyen2015}.

Falque \emph{et al.}~\cite{Falque2025} have now built the horizon.
Their microcavity polariton fluid crosses from sub- to supersonic
flow along an optically imprinted velocity profile, the horizon
steepness is tuned all-optically between two measured values, and
negative-energy Bogoliubov modes are resolved in the supersonic
region, which is the kinematic signature that a horizon has formed
and that pair emission across it is possible. What the platform does
not yet have is a prediction sharp enough to falsify, and a
statement of the device conditions under which that prediction is
entitled to be believed.

This Letter supplies both. We evaluate the Schwinger-Keldysh
effective-field-theory (SK-EFT) analog-Hawking spectrum at the
published Paris-LKB parameters, and we ask, in the same expansion,
whether the polariton cavity is quiet enough for that spectrum to
be the leading behavior. The answer is that the horizon is already
good enough and the cavity is not. Cavity loss, not surface gravity,
is the gating parameter, and the gate closes at a lifetime we can
name.

\section{Device parameters and the predicted Hawking temperature}

We take the fluid parameters from Falque \emph{et al.}~\cite{Falque2025}
directly rather than re-deriving them. Their \S IV.1 reports a sound
speed $c_s \approx \input{tables/lkb_c_s_um_per_ps.tex}\unskip\,\si{\micro\meter/\pico\second}$
and a healing length $\xi \approx \input{tables/lkb_xi_um.tex}\unskip\,\si{\micro\meter}$
upstream (\SI{4.0}{\micro\meter} downstream), measured on a fluid whose
effective polariton mass is $m^* = \SI{7.0e-35}{\kilo\gram}$. Two
horizon steepnesses are reported: a smooth configuration at
$\kappa = \input{tables/lkb_kappa_smooth.tex}\unskip\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and a steep one at
$\kappa = \input{tables/lkb_kappa_steep.tex}\unskip\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.

One point of provenance is worth making explicit, because it is
easy to mistake for a correction we have applied. The sound speed
above is not a reservoir-free Bogoliubov estimate rescaled after the
fact; the dark excitonic reservoir is already inside it. Falque
\emph{et al.}'s Appendix~B carries the reservoir interaction energy
$g_r n_r \approx 1.84\,gn$ through the dispersion from which $c_s$
is extracted. Reservoir-free estimates for this class of device
reach $\sim \SI{1.0}{\micro\meter/\pico\second}$, and the factor of
roughly two-and-a-half between them and the measured value is the
reservoir, but that reduction is the primary source's result and not
ours~\cite{Estrecho2021,Stepanov2019,Claude2023}.

The dimensionless ratio $D = \xi\kappa/c_s$ compares the horizon's
gradient scale to the healing length and controls how far the
hydrodynamic description can be trusted. It is
$\input{tables/lkb_D_smooth.tex}\unskip$ at the smooth horizon and
$\input{tables/lkb_D_steep.tex}\unskip$ at the steep one. The leading dispersive
correction is
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\rm disp} = -\frac{\pi}{6}\,D^2 ,
\label{eq:delta_disp}
\end{equation}
giving $\input{tables/lkb_delta_disp_smooth.tex}\unskip$ and
$\input{tables/lkb_delta_disp_steep.tex}\unskip$ respectively. Equation~\eqref{eq:delta_disp}
is the manuscript's only dispersive coefficient, and \S IV uses the
same expression rather than an independent one.

The Hawking temperature follows from the surface gravity alone,
\begin{equation}
k_B T_H = \frac{\hbar \kappa}{2\pi} ,
\label{eq:T_H}
\end{equation}
which at the smooth horizon gives
$T_H = \input{tables/lkb_T_H_smooth_mK.tex}\unskip\,\si{\milli\kelvin}$.

Two qualifications attach to that number, and both come from the
primary source rather than from the theory.

The first is a normalization. Falque \emph{et al.} quote a Hawking
temperature of \SI{3}{\kelvin} for their smoothest horizon, and
their Eq.~(9) writes the emitted flux as
$1/(e^{\omega/2\pi\kappa}-1)$. That exponent corresponds to
$k_B T = h\kappa$, which at the same
$\kappa = \input{tables/lkb_kappa_smooth.tex}\unskip\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ evaluates to
$\input{tables/lkb_T_H_falque_norm_K.tex}\unskip\,\si{\kelvin}$ and reproduces the
flux values they report. It exceeds Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H} by
$(2\pi)^2 \approx \input{tables/lkb_norm_ratio.tex}\unskip$. We use
Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H}, the convention of the analog-gravity literature
the same paper cites~\cite{Visser1998,Unruh1981,Barcelo2011}, and we
flag the factor here so that a reader holding \SI{3}{\kelvin} in
mind does not read \SI{85}{\milli\kelvin} as a disagreement about
$\kappa$. The two papers agree on every measured quantity; they
normalize $T_H$ differently.

The second is that Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H} is only an observable where
the spectrum is thermal, and at the steep horizon it is not. Falque
\emph{et al.} state that the steep-horizon spectrum is dominated by
nonlinear frequency dispersion and is therefore not thermal, which
$D = \input{tables/lkb_D_steep.tex}\unskip$ anticipates: the dispersive correction
Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta_disp} is then near one half and the expansion
that produced it has stopped converging. We therefore quote a
Hawking temperature at the smooth horizon only, and treat the steep
configuration as what the source shows it to be, a demonstration
that $\kappa$ is tunable, not a hotter black hole.

\section{The cavity lifetime the measurement requires}

Polaritons leak. A quantum written into the fluid at the horizon
decays at the cavity rate $\Gamma_{\rm pol} = 1/\tau_{\rm cav}$
while it propagates to where it is read out, so the occupation the
apparatus records is smaller than the occupation the horizon
emitted by
\begin{equation}
A = \exp\!\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\rm pol} L}{v_g}\right),
\label{eq:attenuation}
\end{equation}
with $L$ the propagation distance and $v_g$ the group velocity.
Recovering the intrinsic spectrum means dividing by $A$. Three
elementary properties of Eq.~\eqref{eq:attenuation} are checked in
Lean~4 and hold for every parameter choice rather than for a fitted
one: $A \geq 1$ always, so the recovery never deflates an inferred
occupation (\texttt{attenuation\_ge\_one}); $A$ is monotone in
$\Gamma_{\rm pol}$ at fixed geometry
(\texttt{attenuation\_mono\_Gamma}); and $A = 1$ exactly when
$\Gamma_{\rm pol} = 0$, which is the closed-system limit
(\texttt{attenuation\_eq\_one\_at\_zero\_decay}).

The question is not whether $A$ can be divided out. It is whether
$\Gamma_{\rm pol}$ is small enough that the emission being corrected
is still the emission Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H} describes. Dissipation
enters the SK-EFT as an expansion in $\Gamma_{\rm pol}/\kappa$, and
we take
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm pol} < \input{tables/lkb_weak_dissipation_ratio.tex}\unskip\,\kappa
\label{eq:weak_dissipation}
\end{equation}
as the condition under which the leading term is the answer. The
cut in Eq.~\eqref{eq:weak_dissipation} is a declared convention, not
a derived boundary; what is not conventional is the size of the gap
between it and the device.

Falque \emph{et al.} measure a polariton decay rate
$\hbar\gamma \approx \SI{80}{\micro\electronvolt}$, or
$\tau_{\rm cav} \approx \SI{\input{tables/lkb_tau_cav_ps_standard.tex}\unskip}{\pico\second}$.
Against $\kappa = \input{tables/lkb_kappa_smooth.tex}\unskip\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ this gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Gamma_{\rm pol}}{\kappa} = \input{tables/lkb_ratio_standard.tex}\unskip ,
\label{eq:ratio_asbuilt}
\end{equation}
about eighteen times the cut. The as-built cavity is not weakly
dissipative; over one healing length of propagation the recovery
factor $A$ is already
$\input{tables/lkb_atten_per_xi_standard.tex}\unskip$, because the exponent in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:attenuation} per healing length is exactly the product
$(\Gamma_{\rm pol}/\kappa)\,D$ of the two dimensionless parameters
of \S II.

This is a cavity specification, and it is the one axis of the
platform that is a fabrication choice rather than a physical limit.
Holding the optically written horizon fixed and varying only the
mirror stack, a \SI{100}{\pico\second} cavity gives
$\Gamma_{\rm pol}/\kappa = \input{tables/lkb_ratio_long.tex}\unskip$ and a
\SI{300}{\pico\second} cavity gives
$\input{tables/lkb_ratio_ultralong.tex}\unskip$. Eq.~\eqref{eq:weak_dissipation}
is met from
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\tau_{\rm cav} \gtrsim
  \SI{\input{tables/lkb_tau_required_ps.tex}\unskip}{\pico\second}\;}
\label{eq:tau_required}
\end{equation}
onward, where the per-healing-length recovery factor has fallen to
$\input{tables/lkb_atten_per_xi_required.tex}\unskip$. Long-lifetime microcavities in
this range exist; the Paris-LKB horizon does not need to change to
use one.

Figure~\ref{fig:regime} places the device on that axis. The same
criterion applied outside the GaAs family closes the other end of
the range: the UPenn MoSe$_2$ nanocavity of Wang \emph{et
al.}~\cite{WangKimBZHe2026PennTMDPolariton}, whose lower-polariton
linewidth $\gamma_{\rm LP} = \SI{1.8}{\milli\electronvolt}$ implies
$\Gamma_{\rm LP} \approx \SI{2.7e12}{\per\second}$, sits at
$\Gamma_{\rm LP}/\kappa = \input{tables/penn_ratio_vs_steep.tex}\unskip$ even when
paired with the most generous surface gravity the polariton family
has demonstrated, so no SK-EFT statement in this Letter extends to
ultrafast TMD nanocavities. That the ratio there is at least
$\input{tables/lkb_weak_dissipation_ratio.tex}\unskip$ is itself kernel-checked
(\texttt{polariton\_tier1\_fails\_tmds}); the value
$\input{tables/penn_ratio_vs_steep.tex}\unskip$ is arithmetic on the same
encoded parameters.

Two consequences separate cleanly, and the rest of this Letter
depends on the separation. What does \emph{not} depend on the
cavity is anything fixed by $\kappa$ alone: the horizon exists, its
steepness is measured, and Eqs.~\eqref{eq:T_H} and~\eqref{eq:gain}
set the temperature and the shape of the gain spectrum, including
the frequency at which the gain crosses any given threshold. What
\emph{does} depend on the cavity is the claim that those leading-order
expressions are the whole answer, and with it the quoted gain
magnitudes, the recovery of an intrinsic occupation through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:attenuation}, and the error bars on the window of
\S V. The numbers in \S IV and \S V are therefore stated for a
device satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:tau_required}; at the as-built
cavity they are the correct target of the measurement, not a
prediction of what it will return.

\section{The stimulated-Hawking gain spectrum}

%% Numbers consumed from src/core/formulas.py :: stimulated_hawking_gain
%% and :: stimulated_hawking_snr via papers/E1/tables.py. The argument is
%% omega/kappa_eff, which is what the exponent contains.

Waiting for spontaneous emission is what makes the atomic-BEC
measurements slow. Driving the horizon instead removes that cost.
A coherent probe injected at frequency $\omega$ scatters
anomalously at the horizon, and the amplitude returned in the
partner channel is set by the same Bogoliubov mixing that produces
the spontaneous pairs, so a classical mean-field measurement reads
out a quantum-gravitational analog coefficient~\cite{Grisins2016}.
The gain is
\begin{equation}
G(\omega) = \frac{\Gamma(\omega)}{e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa_{\rm eff}} - 1},
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
with $\Gamma(\omega)$ the greybody transmission factor and
$\kappa_{\rm eff} = \kappa\,(1 + \delta_{\rm disp})$ the surface
gravity carrying the dispersive correction of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta_disp}. At the smooth horizon
$\kappa_{\rm eff}/\kappa = \input{tables/lkb_kappa_eff_ratio_smooth.tex}\unskip$, a
19\% reduction, and this is the only place a dispersive coefficient
enters; the Letter does not introduce a second one.

Every number below assumes the greybody-saturation limit
$\Gamma(\omega) \equiv 1$, and the statements that follow are
universal in $\kappa$ only within that limit. The assumption is
defensible in the low-frequency window
$\omega \lesssim 0.3\,\kappa$ of interest here, where greybody
corrections enter at $\mathcal{O}(\omega^2/\kappa^2)$ in the
hydrodynamic regime and leave the plateau
flat~\cite{Finazzi2012,Jacquet2023QNM}; it is not defensible outside
it, and a measured $\Gamma(\omega)$ profile is one of the things the
experiment would deliver.

Under it the gain falls monotonically from a low-frequency
divergence: $G = \input{tables/lkb_gain_at_0p1.tex}\unskip$ at
$\omega = 0.1\,\kappa_{\rm eff}$,
$\input{tables/lkb_gain_at_0p3.tex}\unskip$ at $0.3\,\kappa_{\rm eff}$, and
$\input{tables/lkb_gain_at_1.tex}\unskip$ at $\kappa_{\rm eff}$. Setting
$G = 1/2$ inverts Eq.~\eqref{eq:gain} exactly, with no device
parameter surviving:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\omega_{1/2}}{\kappa_{\rm eff}} = \frac{\ln 3}{2\pi}
  = \input{tables/lkb_gain_threshold.tex}\unskip .
\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:threshold} is the sharpest prediction in this
Letter, because it is a pure number. Expressed in the surface
gravity the experiment measures rather than in $\kappa_{\rm eff}$,
it sits at $\omega_{1/2} = \input{tables/lkb_gain_threshold_absolute.tex}\unskip\,\kappa$
at the smooth horizon, and the difference between
$\input{tables/lkb_gain_threshold.tex}\unskip$ and
$\input{tables/lkb_gain_threshold_absolute.tex}\unskip$ is exactly the dispersive
correction. A measurement that locates the half-gain frequency
therefore measures $\delta_{\rm disp}$, and through it $D$, without
needing an absolute calibration of the probe.

For a shot-noise-limited readout,
$\mathrm{SNR} = \sqrt{N_{\rm shots} N_{\rm probe}}\;G(\omega)$, so
$G = 1/2$ with $N_{\rm probe} = 100$ probe photons per mode is a
single-shot $5\sigma$ detection. At
$\omega = 0.1\,\kappa_{\rm eff}$ the same 100 photons give
$\mathrm{SNR} = \input{tables/lkb_snr_at_0p1.tex}\unskip$. The comparison that
matters is not with a better spontaneous measurement but with the
scaling: a spontaneous correlation signal at the
$5\times 10^{-5}$ level needs of order $10^9$ repetitions to
resolve~\cite{Grisins2016,Jacquet2022}, and a stimulated
measurement replaces that repetition count with probe power.
Quasinormal modes of the driven-dissipative
geometry~\cite{Jacquet2023QNM,Burkhard2025} concentrate the response
at particular frequencies and offer additional windows, which the
Paris-LKB apparatus has already resolved.

\section{Noise floor and the falsifiable window}

The polariton fluid is not cold. Its mean-field energy scale
corresponds to
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm noise} = \frac{m^* c_s^2}{k_B} = \input{tables/lkb_T_noise_K.tex}\unskip\,\si{\kelvin},
\end{equation}
an order of magnitude above $T_H$, and in a spontaneous measurement
that ratio is the whole problem: the Hawking occupation would have
to be dug out of a background ten times larger.

The stimulated measurement does not face that ratio, and the reason
is spectral and phase structure rather than magnitude. The
driven-dissipative background is broadband and phase-incoherent
with the probe, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:gain} describes a response at
the probe frequency, phase-locked to it. A narrowband
phase-referenced readout therefore rejects the background by the
ratio of its bandwidth to the detection bandwidth, and this is not
a modification of the Paris-LKB apparatus but a description of it:
the existing coherent probe spectroscopy already modulates the
probe at \SI{5}{\mega\hertz} and detects in a \SI{300}{\hertz}
bandwidth around that modulation~\cite{Falque2025}. What survives
in the detection band is the shot noise of the probe itself, which
is what the SNR above assumes. The background enters the
uncertainty only through its contribution to the condensate's
amplitude fluctuations, which is spectrally flat across the
window~\cite{Amelio2020} and so cannot mimic the Planckian shape of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gain}.

That shape is what makes the prediction falsifiable rather than
merely quantitative. We give the window
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\omega/\kappa \lesssim 0.30\;}
\label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
at the smooth horizon. The upper edge is derived: above it $D$ is
no longer small against the frequencies being probed, the
$\mathcal{O}(\omega^2/\kappa^2)$ greybody corrections that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gain} neglects stop being negligible, and
$G$ has fallen to $\input{tables/lkb_gain_at_0p3.tex}\unskip$, near the point where
the returned signal is no longer a large fraction of the probe. The
lower edge is set by the apparatus, not by the theory: $G$ grows
without bound as $\omega \to 0$, and what stops the window there is
the frequency resolution of the probe scan and the finite spatial
extent of the fluid. We do not fix it here, because fixing it
requires the resolution figures for the tailored-profile
configuration rather than for the one already
published.

Inside the window the prediction is over-determined, which is what
gives it teeth. The three device quantities $(c_s, \xi, \kappa)$
enter $G(\omega)$ only through $\kappa_{\rm eff}$, a single
combination, so a measured gain profile is a one-parameter fit to a
three-parameter set that has been independently measured. Agreement
is a nontrivial check; disagreement in the shape of $G(\omega)$,
as opposed to its overall scale, cannot be absorbed into the
greybody factor and falsifies the framework.
