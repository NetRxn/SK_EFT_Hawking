\title{Kernel-Verified Quantum-Network and Device-Characterization
Certification Substrate}

\author{John Roehm}

\begin{abstract}
We report a machine-checked chain, formalized in Lean~4 on Mathlib, that
carries stated device and link parameters to a certified interval on an
achievable quantum-network or device quantity and terminates in rational
arithmetic the proof kernel performs, with no floating-point
transcendental and no external oracle. Attacking every link of that chain
produces the second result: one link that a large experimental practice
relies on cannot exist. An averaged benchmark fidelity is an average over
input states and the diamond distance is a supremum over them, and the
two are separated by a square root already for coherent rotations, so
randomized-benchmarking data can \emph{refute} a worst-case channel
budget and cannot \emph{certify} one. The formalization proves the
refutation direction, and we state the certification direction as a
negative result rather than shipping a theorem named for a certificate.
The third result is a classification of where such chains break: at
identification steps, which literature closes; at library gaps, which
work closes; at genuine impossibilities, which nothing closes; and at
statements that pass the kernel and carry no information, which is the
failure mode internal to formalization and the one a proof assistant
cannot detect. A worked certification carries published transmon readout
parameters through the chain and finds the proven floor violated at one
of the two reported operating points, which localizes the failure in the
identification rather than in the mathematics and is what a falsifiable
certificate is for. Supporting this, the corpus supplies the
finite-dimensional distinguishability calculus (both Fuchs--van de Graaf
inequalities, the two-sided Choi sandwich, the Watrous semidefinite
program with attainment of both optima, and exact diamond distances for
the named channel families a benchmark targets), the sharp Audenaert
continuity constant $\ln(d-1)$ in unconditional form, a constructive
derivation of the average-gate-fidelity identity, and max-flow/min-cut
strong duality built in project.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Certifying a quantum device or network means producing a statement of the
form: given these measured parameters and this stated model, the
achievable value of this quantity lies in this interval. This paper
reports that chain, built and machine-checked end to end, and three
results that came out of attacking it.

\emph{First}, the chain exists and terminates. Stated device and link
parameters ($T_1$, $T_2$, temperature, frequency, window length,
transmissivity, per-link fidelity) reach a certified interval through
named theorems, and the final evaluation is rational arithmetic performed
by the proof kernel: no floating-point transcendental, no interval
oracle, nothing outside the assistant. Section~\ref{sec:worked} carries
one published parameter set through it.

\emph{Second}, one link of that chain cannot exist, and it is a link a
large experimental practice assumes. Randomized benchmarking measures an
average gate fidelity $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$; an error budget wants a
worst-case channel error $\ddist$. The first is an average over input
states and the second is a supremum over them, and for coherent errors
they are separated by a square root: a unitary over-rotation by angle
$\theta$ has $1-F_{\mathrm{avg}} = \Theta(\theta^2)$ while
$\ddist = \Theta(\theta)$. No bound linear in the average infidelity
exists, and the corpus proves no bound of any shape in that direction.
What it does prove is the other direction, a lower bound on worst-case
error from a benchmark number, which makes benchmark data a
\emph{falsifier} of a claimed diamond-norm budget rather than a
certificate for one (Sec.~\ref{sec:rb}).

\emph{Third}, the chain's failure points are enumerable, and the
enumeration is the transferable content. Sections~\ref{sec:params}
to~\ref{sec:owned} build the chain; Sec.~\ref{sec:breaks} classifies
where it breaks. Three of the four classes are familiar: an
\emph{identification} that literature closes, a \emph{library gap} that
work closes, an \emph{impossibility} that nothing closes. The fourth is
internal to formalization and is the one this project had to learn to
see: a statement that passes the kernel and carries no information, a
bracket whose upper endpoint is the universal range bound, an inequality
whose conclusion is its hypothesis with a constant added. A proof
assistant cannot detect these, because they are true.

\paragraph*{The rule that makes the failure points visible.} Every result
in this corpus lives on one of two layers, and the layers are never
written in the same voice. The \emph{formula layer} is a kernel-checked
theorem about explicitly defined mathematical objects, and nothing in its
statement refers to a device. The \emph{identification layer} is the
reading of a mathematical object as a physical quantity; it is a
modelling step, it enters as an explicit named hypothesis of the theorem
that uses it, and it is never absorbed into the verified statement. What
makes this a method rather than a virtue is what it forbids. It forbids
calling a rate-bound function a converse. It forbids the word
\emph{certificate} for a lower bound. It forbids absorbing a cited bound
into a proof. The amplifier floor is the sharpest case, and showing it is
worth more than stating the rule again:

\begin{theorem}[\lean{fdt\_noise\_floor\_amplifier}]
\label{thm:caves}
Let $\hbar\omega > 0$ and let
$h_{\mathrm{Caves}} : \hbar\omega/2 \le A$. Then
$S_{\mathrm{JN}} < S_{\mathrm{JN}} + \hbar\omega/2 \le S_{\mathrm{JN}}+A$,
where $S_{\mathrm{JN}}$ is the Johnson--Nyquist spectral density.
\end{theorem}

That is the whole theorem. The physics, the Caves half-quantum bound on
phase-insensitive amplifier added noise~\cite{Caves1982}, is entirely in
the binder $h_{\mathrm{Caves}}$, and what remains is arithmetic. We
report it as a \emph{disclosure} and not as a result: it certifies that
the corpus never assumes the bound silently and never carries it as an
axiom, and it certifies nothing about an amplifier. Every floor and
ceiling in Sec.~\ref{sec:params} has this shape, with more mathematics
attached and the same rule applied.

\paragraph*{Scope of the survey claims.} Two claims in this paper are of
the form ``to our knowledge, the first''. The scope searched is the
Lean~4 and Mathlib ecosystem including its quantum-information
developments (Sec.~\ref{sec:related}), the Coq/Rocq quantum verification
line, and Isabelle/HOL quantum formalizations, by library index and by
publication title. Unpublished repositories were not searched.

\paragraph*{Verification status in one sentence.} Every theorem named
here is verified to the Lean~4 kernel with the axiom set
$\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},\,\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$,
with no project-local axioms, no \texttt{sorry} placeholders and no
compiled-evaluation (\texttt{native\_decide}) trust escapes; the
dependency closure of the results reported here comprises
\bundleDecls{} declarations, \bundleTheorems{} of them theorems, across
\bundleModules{} modules (App.~\ref{app:verification}).

Two features of the figure carry the argument. The first is that there is
exactly one identification layer, at the top. Once a model channel is
fixed, every subsequent step is a theorem about mathematics, so a
disagreement about a certified interval is either a disagreement about
the top arrow or an arithmetic error, and those are very different
conversations to have. Section~\ref{sec:worked} is a worked instance of
that separation paying off. The second feature is the absent arrow.
Certification pressure runs upward, from a measured aggregate to a
worst-case guarantee, and Sec.~\ref{sec:rb} is about why that arrow is
not merely unbuilt.

\section{From device parameters to channel error}
\label{sec:params}

This section is the top arrow of Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}: model channels
built from universally stated device data, their exact channel-level
quantities, and the identification each one requires. Every
identification is displayed, so a reader can count them.

\subsection{Gate error from coherence times}

The bench-data bridge is the average-gate/entanglement-fidelity identity.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{avgGateFidelity\_eq}]
\label{thm:avgfid}
For a Kraus channel $\Phi$ on a $d$-dimensional system,
$F_{\mathrm{avg}}(\Phi) = \big(d\,F_e(\Phi)+1\big)/(d+1)$.
\end{theorem}

% TODO: Nielsen, Phys. Lett. A 303, 249 (2002) is cited from its standard
% statement; the full text is not held in this repository's source corpus.
% Acquire before the attribution sweep clears this claim.
The identity~\cite{Horodecki1999,Nielsen2002} is proven constructively
rather than imported, and the construction is one of the results the
formalization owns (Sec.~\ref{sec:owned}). For the standard $T_1/T_2$
model channel of a duration-$t$ gate, amplitude damping with
$\gamma = 1-e^{-t/T_1}$ composed after dephasing with
$p = (1-e^{-t/T_2})/2$, it closes to a formula:
\[
F_{\mathrm{avg}}(\Phi_t) \;=\;
\tfrac12 + \tfrac16 e^{-t/T_1} + \tfrac13 e^{-t/(2T_1)-t/T_2}
\]
(\lean{avgGateFidelity\_coherenceChannel}, with
\lean{entanglementFidelity\_coherenceChannel}), monotone decreasing in
the gate duration (\lean{avgGateFidelity\_coherenceChannel\_antitone})
with exact endpoints $1$ at $t=0$ and floor $\tfrac12$
(\lean{avgGateFidelity\_coherenceChannel\_ge\_half},
\lean{avgGateFidelity\_coherenceChannel\_le\_one}). Each of those is an
exact statement about $\Phi_t$, which is a matrix-valued function of $t$,
$T_1$ and $T_2$ and nothing else.

\begin{hypothesis}[coherence-limited noise]
\label{id:coh}
The realized gate channel is at least as noisy as $\Phi_t$.
\end{hypothesis}

Identification~\ref{id:coh} is what turns the formula into a ceiling on a
physical gate, and it is the only thing that does. Composition with a
control-error budget gives the composed-gate bound
(\lean{diamondDist\_coherence\_after\_control\_le}) with its fidelity
reading (\lean{sqrtFidelity\_coherence\_after\_control\_ge}) and both
binding regimes identified
(\lean{diamondDist\_coherence\_after\_control\_le\_coherence\_binds},
\lean{diamondDist\_coherence\_after\_control\_le\_control\_binds}), so a
budget can be apportioned between decoherence and control without leaving
the verified layer.

\subsection{Readout error: one channel where no conversion is lost}

State-preparation-and-measurement characterization is the one place in
this paper where a measured number is a worst-case channel number with no
loss. The classical readout-error channel
$\Phi_q(\rho)=(1-q)\rho + qX\rho X$ has
$\ddist(\Phi_q,\mathrm{id}) = q$ exactly
(\lean{diamondDist\_spamBitFlip\_eq}), the readout error probability
itself, and the process fidelity carries the same identity as the gate
case (\lean{processFidelity},
\lean{avgGateFidelity\_eq\_processFidelity}). The contrast with
Sec.~\ref{sec:rb} is the point: a readout bit-flip probability is already
a worst-case quantity, and a benchmark fidelity is not.

\subsection{Readout floors from the window, $T_1$, and temperature}
\label{sec:readout}

The relaxation envelope rests on the excited-state decay probability
$p_{\mathrm{decay}}(t,T_1) = 1 - e^{-t/T_1}$ (\lean{readoutDecayProb}),
with range $0 \le p < 1$ (\lean{readoutDecayProb\_nonneg},
\lean{readoutDecayProb\_lt\_one}), strict monotonicity in the window
(\lean{readoutDecayProb\_strictMono\_time}), strict antitonicity in $T_1$
(\lean{readoutDecayProb\_strictAnti\_relax}) and endpoints
(\lean{readoutDecayProb\_zero\_time},
\lean{readoutDecayProb\_tendsto\_one}). The thermal envelope rests on the
excited-state occupancy $p_{\mathrm{th}}(x) = 1/(1+e^{x})$ with
$x = \beta\hbar\omega$ (\lean{thermalExcitedPop}), and it is derived
rather than defined into existence: the two-state canonical ensemble of
the community library~\cite{Meiburg2025PhysLib} supplies a closed-form
probability in $\tanh$, which the bridge identity
$\tfrac12(1-\tanh u) = 1/(1+e^{2u})$ (\lean{half\_one\_sub\_tanh})
converts into the Boltzmann-occupancy theorem
\lean{twoState\_excited\_probability}. The occupancy carries strict
antitonicity in $x$ (\lean{thermalExcitedPop\_strictAnti}), strict
monotonicity in temperature and strict antitonicity in frequency through
a temperature-$\beta$ bridge
(\lean{thermalExcitedPop\_strictMono\_temperature},
\lean{thermalExcitedPop\_strictAnti\_frequency}), and endpoints
($\tfrac12$ at $x=0$, tending to $0$ as $x\to\infty$).

Two identifications convert these into floors, one per mechanism.

\begin{hypothesis}[relaxation misassignment]
\label{id:relax}
A relaxation event inside the measurement window flips the recorded
outcome, so the excited-branch error satisfies
$p_{\mathrm{decay}}(t,T_1) \le e_1$~\cite{Gambetta2007,Walter2017,Geerlings2013}.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}[thermal misassignment]
\label{id:therm}
Residual excited-state population misassigns at least at its own rate, so
$p_{\mathrm{th}}(x) \le e_1$~\cite{Jin2015,Geerlings2013}.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[combined readout floor,
\lean{avgAssignmentError\_combined\_floor}]
\label{thm:combined}
Let $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{avg}}(e_0,e_1) = (e_0+e_1)/2$ be the
uniform-prior averaged assignment error. If $0 \le e_0$ and $e_1$
satisfies Identifications~\ref{id:relax} and~\ref{id:therm}, then
\[
\frac{\max\big(p_{\mathrm{decay}}(t,T_1),\, p_{\mathrm{th}}(x)\big)}{2}
\;\le\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{avg}}(e_0,e_1).
\]
\end{theorem}

A readout subject both to relaxation during the window and to thermal
population is floored by the worse of the two mechanisms, and the
prefactor $\tfrac12$ is exactly the uniform prior. The single-mechanism
specializations are \lean{avgAssignmentError\_relaxation\_floor} and
\lean{avgAssignmentError\_thermal\_floor}. Section~\ref{sec:worked}
evaluates Theorem~\ref{thm:combined} at published parameters and finds
one of the two identifications false there, which is what the displayed
form is for: when a certificate fails, the reader can see which line to
doubt.

\section{What benchmark data cannot certify}
\label{sec:rb}

Randomized benchmarking~\cite{Magesan2011} produces an average gate
fidelity. An error budget wants a worst-case channel error. This section
is about the relation between them, because it is the one arrow of
Fig.~\ref{fig:chain} that runs the wrong way and the one place where the
mathematics refuses.

The corpus proves one direction.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{avgGateFidelity\_diamondDist\_bound}]
\label{thm:rblower}
For a Kraus channel $K$ on an $n$-dimensional system,
\[
1 - \sqrt{\frac{(n+1)F_{\mathrm{avg}}(K) - 1}{n}}
\;\le\; \ddist(K,\mathrm{id}).
\]
\end{theorem}

A companion result (\lean{avgGateFidelity\_diamondDist\_two\_sided})
brackets on both sides the trace distance between the Choi images of $K$
and of the identity, using the Fuchs--van de Graaf bracket on those
states (\lean{entanglementDistinguishability\_fvdg\_bracket}), and
records that this trace distance is at most $\ddist(K,\mathrm{id})$. The
bracket is two-sided on the entanglement distinguishability. It is not
two-sided on the diamond distance, and no theorem in the corpus is: the
diamond distance occurs in exactly two statements involving
$F_{\mathrm{avg}}$, and in both it stands on the greater side of the
inequality.

\begin{nonresult}
\label{nr:rb}
No bound of the form
$\ddist(K,\mathrm{id}) \le c\,\big(1-F_{\mathrm{avg}}(K)\big)$ holds for
any constant $c$, in any dimension.
\end{nonresult}

The witness is the coherent error, and it is elementary. Take $K$ to be
the qubit unitary channel of an over-rotation by angle $\theta$, with
eigenvalues $e^{\pm i\theta/2}$. Its entanglement fidelity is
$F_e = |\operatorname{tr}U|^2/4 = \cos^2(\theta/2)$, so by
Theorem~\ref{thm:avgfid}
$1 - F_{\mathrm{avg}} = \tfrac23\sin^2(\theta/2) = \Theta(\theta^2)$,
while the diamond distance to the identity is $\Theta(\theta)$ as
$\theta \to 0$. The ratio $\ddist/(1-F_{\mathrm{avg}})$ therefore
diverges, and any admissible bound must degrade at least as
$\sqrt{1-F_{\mathrm{avg}}}$. We state this as an argument, not as a
formalized theorem. The corpus supplies both halves of the arithmetic,
Theorem~\ref{thm:avgfid} and the unitary conversion
\lean{diamondDist\_unitaryKraus\_le}, but does not assemble them into a
kernel-checked counterexample family, and by this paper's own rule an
unformalized argument is not reported as verified content.

\paragraph*{What follows for practice.} A measured $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$
near $1$ does not certify that worst-case channel error is small. What
Theorem~\ref{thm:rblower} provides is a \emph{falsifier}: a floor on
worst-case error that a claimed diamond-norm budget must clear, so
benchmark data can refute a budget. That is a real and correctly oriented
use, and it is the use we claim. Certifying a budget requires worst-case
data, and the corpus supplies two routes to it: the exact diamond
distances of Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol} for the named channel families, and
the semidefinite program of Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp} evaluated on a
reconstructed Choi matrix, whose dual side is attained so that a single
witness certifies the bound.

Separately from the direction of the inequality, the identification of an
experimental benchmark decay constant with $F_{\mathrm{avg}}$ of a fixed
channel, which is what gate-independence of noise and Markovianity buy,
is the cited model hypothesis of Ref.~\cite{Magesan2011} and not a
theorem of this corpus. The two failures compound: the benchmark number
is the wrong kind of quantity for the job, and it is attached to a fixed
channel only under assumptions.

\section{From channel error to protocol guarantees}
\label{sec:protocol}

This section is the middle arrow of Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}, and it is
deduction throughout. It runs as one composition argument: a per-stage
channel error becomes an end-to-end budget, an end-to-end budget becomes
a protocol-level number, and a protocol-level number becomes a rate.

\subsection{The tools: distinguishability at full strength}

The calculus is built concretely on
$\mathrm{Matrix}\;(\mathrm{Fin}\,n)\;(\mathrm{Fin}\,n)\;\mathbb{C}$ with
no Stinespring dilation and no abstract $C^*$-algebra detour.
Throughout, $\ddist$ is the halved diamond norm,
$\ddist(\Phi_1,\Phi_2) = \tfrac12\dnorm{\Phi_1-\Phi_2}$, so that it is
$[0,1]$-valued on channel pairs. Density operators are
\lean{IsDensityOperator} (positive-semidefinite, unit trace), the
Schatten-1 norm is \lean{traceNorm}, and the trace distance is
$D(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\trnorm{\rho-\sigma}$. The linchpin
\lean{traceNorm\_posSemidef}, that $\trnorm{A} = \operatorname{tr} A$ for
positive semidefinite $A$, is proven without a
continuous-functional-calculus instance by a characteristic-polynomial
root-multiset argument; the general non-Hermitian triangle inequality
follows through the Hermitian dilation, making the trace distance a
genuine metric (\lean{traceDist\_triangle}) with range $[0,1]$ on density
operators (\lean{traceDist\_mem\_Icc}). The Uhlmann
fidelity~\cite{Uhlmann1976} enters as the root fidelity
$\sqrt F(\rho,\sigma) = \trnorm{\sqrt\sigma\sqrt\rho}$
(\lean{sqrtFidelity}), proven equal to the literal Uhlmann trace
expression, with $F \le 1$ (\lean{sqrtFidelity\_le\_one}).

\begin{theorem}[Fuchs--van de Graaf, both directions]
\label{thm:fvdg}
For density operators $\rho,\sigma$,
$1 - \sqrt F(\rho,\sigma) \le D(\rho,\sigma) \le
\sqrt{1 - F(\rho,\sigma)}$.
\end{theorem}

Both are kernel-checked~\cite{FuchsvandeGraaf1999}: the lower bound
(\lean{one\_sub\_sqrtFidelity\_le\_traceDist}) by a Powers--St\o
rmer-type argument with the non-commuting Loewner step removed rather
than assumed, and the upper bound
(\lean{traceDist\_le\_sqrt\_one\_sub\_sqrtFidelity\_sq})
purification-free via Holevo--Helstrom~\cite{Helstrom1976} plus the
classical inequality, powered by a sharp Schatten-2 Cauchy--Schwarz
$\trnorm{AB} \le \lVert A\rVert_F\,\lVert B\rVert_F$
(\lean{traceNorm\_mul\_le}) built with no singular-value decomposition.
Both directions are consumed downstream: the lower one by
Theorem~\ref{thm:rblower}, the upper one wherever a fidelity budget must
become a distance budget.

Channels enter as Kraus families. For trace-preserving
$\Phi(\rho)=\sum_k K_k\rho K_k^{\dagger}$ the corpus proves trace-norm
contractivity, trace-distance data processing
(\lean{traceDist\_krausMap\_le}), density-operator preservation and Choi
positivity~\cite{Choi1975} (\lean{choiMatrix\_krausMap\_posSemidef}).
Data processing lifts to the fidelity domain in full generality via the
Alberti form~\cite{Alberti1983}:
\lean{sqrtFidelity\_krausMap\_ge\_psd\_inputs} gives Uhlmann monotonicity
for all positive-semidefinite inputs and any trace-preserving Kraus
channel, with no full-rank assumption, together with joint concavity
(\lean{sqrtFidelity\_jointly\_concave}) and Loewner monotonicity
(\lean{sqrtFidelity\_mono\_left}).

The diamond distance~\cite{Kitaev1997,Watrous2018} is the supremum of
output trace distances of $\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}$ over input density
operators on the doubled space. The stabilized channel is proven CPTP
(\lean{isKrausChannel\_tensorKraus}), the supremum is well-defined from
boundedness, the result is a $[0,1]$-valued metric
(\lean{diamondDist\_triangle}), and the supremum is attained by an
optimal input (\lean{exists\_diamondDist\_eq}) via trace-norm continuity
(\lean{continuous\_traceNorm}, a Lipschitz bound that sidesteps the
discontinuity of individual eigenvalues) and compactness. Two results
make it computable.

\begin{theorem}[two-sided Choi sandwich]
\label{thm:sandwich}
With $J(\Phi)$ the Choi matrix on an $n$-dimensional system and
$\opnorm{\cdot}$ the spectral norm,
\[
\tfrac{1}{2n}\,\trnorm{J(\Phi_1)-J(\Phi_2)} \;\le\;
\ddist(\Phi_1,\Phi_2) \;\le\;
n\,\opnorm{J(\Phi_1)-J(\Phi_2)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Watrous semidefinite program,
\lean{diamondDist\_eq\_choiSDP}]
\label{thm:sdp}
For Kraus channels $\Phi_1,\Phi_2$ on an $n$-dimensional system,
\[
\ddist(\Phi_1,\Phi_2) \;=\;
\inf\{\opnorm{\mathrm{Tr}_2 W} :
W \succeq 0,\; W \succeq J(\Phi_1)-J(\Phi_2)\},
\]
and the infimum is attained.
\end{theorem}

The sandwich's lower bound comes from the maximally entangled input
(\lean{diamondDist\_ge\_maxEntangled},
\lean{diamondDist\_ge\_choi\_traceNorm}) and its upper bound
(\lean{diamondDist\_le\_choi\_opNorm}) from a vectorization identity that
moves the Helstrom pairing onto the Choi matrix, which is the
formalizable content of the Watrous Choi characterization with no conic
duality required. The program itself is proven unconditionally, via
geometric Hahn--Banach separation over a self-adjoint-matrix
inner-product carrier whose Riesz vector is the optimal dual witness;
weak duality (\lean{diamondDist\_le\_dual\_witness}) is elementary and
attainment of the dual infimum is \lean{exists\_choiDualValue\_eq}, so
both sides are genuine optima and either can be certified by exhibiting a
witness. The norm throughout is the spectral norm, matching the
convention of Ref.~\cite{Watrous2018}, not an entrywise surrogate. That
attainment is what makes Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp} usable as the worst-case
route promised in Sec.~\ref{sec:rb}.

For the channel families a benchmark actually targets, the dual witness
closes the sandwich to exact values. Dephasing and depolarizing, both
covariant, give $\ddist(\Phi_\gamma,\mathrm{id}) = \gamma$
(\lean{diamondDist\_dephasing\_eq}) and
$\ddist(\Delta_p,\mathrm{id}) = p$
(\lean{diamondDist\_depolarizing\_eq}) from explicit optimal witnesses
with no twirling machinery. The Pauli channel gives exactly its total
error weight, $\ddist(\Phi_{\mathbf p},\mathrm{id}) = 1-p_0$
(\lean{diamondDist\_pauliKraus\_eq}), and this generalizes to any unitary
error basis, a family $\{U_i\}$ with $U_0 = 1$ and Hilbert--Schmidt
orthogonality (\lean{diamondDist\_errorBasisKraus\_eq}), with the
two-qubit Pauli channel (\lean{diamondDist\_twoQubitPauliKraus\_eq}) and
the qutrit Weyl--Heisenberg channel (\lean{diamondDist\_weylKraus\_eq})
as instances; the pure cyclic-shift leakage channel has diamond distance
exactly its leakage probability (\lean{diamondDist\_shiftLeakage\_eq}).

Where the channel is not covariant the corpus reports bounds rather than
exact values, and the two available cases are not of the same quality.
Amplitude damping has a genuine two-sided bracket,
$\gamma/2 \le \ddist(\mathcal A_\gamma,\mathrm{id}) \le
\gamma + 1 - \sqrt{1-\gamma}$ (\lean{diamondDist\_ampDamp\_ge},
\lean{diamondDist\_ampDamp\_le}), both endpoints depending on $\gamma$.
Generalized amplitude damping toward a bath of excited population $N$ has
a \emph{lower} bound only, $(1-N)\gamma \le
\ddist(\Phi_{\gamma,N},\mathrm{id})$, achievable and recovering $\gamma$
at $N=0$ (\lean{diamondDist\_genAmpDamp\_bracket}). The upper endpoint
proven alongside it in that declaration is the universal range bound
$\ddist \le 1$, which every channel pair satisfies and which therefore
says nothing about this family. That is an instance of the fourth failure
class of Sec.~\ref{sec:breaks} and we report it as one.

Composition then turns per-stage distances into end-to-end budgets:
$\ddist$ is subadditive under channel composition
(\lean{diamondDist\_composeKraus\_le}), with a worked two-stage instance
showing a depolarizing-then-dephasing pipeline within $\gamma+p$ of the
identity (\lean{diamondDist\_dephasing\_after\_depolarizing\_le}),
discharged from the exact per-stage values above. The unitary-channel
conversion $\ddist(\Phi_U,\Phi_V) \le \lVert U-V\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$
(\lean{diamondDist\_unitaryKraus\_le}) is the entry point for a compiled
gate, and its composition with a verified compiler into an end-to-end
compiled-gate certificate is reported in
Ref.~\cite{CompanionCompilation}, which consumes this layer.

\subsection{Protocol-level quantities}

For a chain of $k$ entanglement swaps of Werner links~\cite{WernerState1989}
with per-link fidelity $F$, the Werner-parameter representation
$w = (4F-1)/3$ makes the swap multiplicative (\lean{wernerSwapFidelity}),
which is the Briegel--D\"ur--Cirac--Zoller composition
law~\cite{BDCZ1998}, with the closed form
$F_{\mathrm{e2e}}(F,k) = (1+3w^k)/4$ (\lean{endToEndFidelity}). The
closed form pins $F_{\mathrm{e2e}}$ exactly, and the substantive
constraints on a chain are its monotonicity in $F$ and antitonicity in
$k$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:swapchain}). The accompanying envelope
(\lean{swapChain\_fidelity\_envelope}), that $\tfrac14 \le F \le 1$
implies $\tfrac14 \le F_{\mathrm{e2e}}(F,k) \le 1$ for every $k$, is a
closure statement: no number of swaps of links in the classically
correlated-to-perfect range drives the end-to-end state outside that
range, so a computed $F_{\mathrm{e2e}}$ outside $[\tfrac14,1]$ from links
inside it is a defect in the computation rather than a physical
prediction. It is a consistency check on an implementation and we do not
present it as more. The memory-decay generalization
(\lean{memoryDegradedFidelity},
\lean{decayInclusive\_fidelity\_envelope}) carries the same statement
through the depolarizing memory model
$F(t) = F e^{-2t/\tau} + (1-e^{-2t/\tau})/4$ for all $t \ge 0$ and
$\tau > 0$; the Klein-four convolution map handles general Bell-diagonal
swaps (\lean{bellDiagSwapA\_werner}); and the nesting-doubling identity
(\lean{endToEndFidelity\_nest\_double}) gives the repeater recursion,
with the chain-level teleportation threshold
$F_{\mathrm{e2e}} > \tfrac12$ if and only if $w^k > \tfrac13$
(\lean{endToEnd\_teleportation\_useful}). For arbitrary states, the
data-processing chains \lean{traceDist\_applyChain\_le} and
\lean{sqrtFidelity\_applyChain\_ge\_psd} remove the uniform-Werner
restriction in both monotone directions.

Distillation, teleportation and key rates follow. Purification strictly
increases fidelity on $F\in(\tfrac12,1)$
(\lean{bbpsswRecurrence\_gt})~\cite{BBPSSW1996}, with the whole statement
reducing to one cubic inequality; the corrected-pairing DEJMPS
map~\cite{DEJMPS1996} increases monotonically on the phase-flip-only
sub-basin (\lean{dejmps\_increase\_phaseFlipOnly}); and a verified
counterexample (\lean{dejmps\_single\_step\_can\_decrease}) shows that
$\lambda_{00} > \tfrac12$ does \emph{not} guarantee a single-step
increase, which delimits the claim. The full asymptotic convergence basin
rests on Macchiavello's non-monotone argument~\cite{Macchiavello1998} and
is cited, not formalized.

\begin{theorem}[Horodecki teleportation fidelity, unconditional,
\lean{teleportAvgFidelity\_horodecki\_unconditional}]
\label{thm:teleport}
$f_{\mathrm{avg}}(F) = (2F+1)/3$, with the Haar--Pauli quadratic average
supplied as a proven value rather than a hypothesis.
\end{theorem}

The single analytic input,
$\int_{S^2}\langle\psi|\sigma_z|\psi\rangle^2\,d\mu = \tfrac13$, is
proven rather than assumed, and how is a result the formalization owns
(Sec.~\ref{sec:owned}). The classical threshold
$f_{\mathrm{avg}} > \tfrac23$ if and only if $F > \tfrac12$ then holds
with no hypothesis
(\lean{teleport\_beats\_classical\_iff\_unconditional}) and composes over
chains (\lean{teleport\_useful\_over\_chain\_unconditional}), which is
where the swap-chain closed form re-enters.

For key rates, the BB84 asymptotic rate is $r(e) = 1 - 2h_2(e)$
(\lean{bb84KeyRate}, with the bits-normalized binary
entropy)~\cite{BB84,ShorPreskill2000}, strictly decreasing
(\lean{bb84KeyRate\_strictAntiOn}), positive exactly when
$h_2(e) < \tfrac12$ (\lean{bb84KeyRate\_pos\_iff\_binEntropy\_lt}), with
a genuine crossover $e^* \in (0,\tfrac12)$ satisfying $r(e^*) = 0$
produced by the intermediate-value theorem
(\lean{bb84\_crossover\_exists}; Fig.~\ref{fig:bb84}). The conventional
threshold near $11\%$ is recovered as the implicit root of
$h_2(e) = \tfrac12$ and no decimal is asserted in the formal layer.
Composition with the end-to-end quantum bit error rate gives the fidelity
threshold for positive key over a chain
(\lean{bb84\_positiveKey\_fidelity\_threshold}), which takes the
crossover as a parameter satisfying $r(e^*) = 0$ on $[0,\tfrac12]$, where
strict antitonicity makes it unique.

Multipartite rates complete the protocol layer. The Fortescue--Lo
random-pair yield after $D$ rounds is $D/(D+1)$, surpassing the
single-copy specified-pair value $\tfrac23$ for $D \ge 3$
(\lean{fortescueLoYield\_gt\_two\_thirds}) and tending to $1$
(\lean{fortescueLoYield\_tendsto\_one}; Fig.~\ref{fig:wghz}) without
attaining it (\lean{fortescueLoYield\_lt\_one}). The comparison that
makes this interesting is against the three-party
Greenberger--Horne--Zeilinger state, whose corresponding random-pair rate
is the demonstrated $0.64$ rather than $1$~\cite{FortescueLo2007}; the
value $1$ for that state is its specified-pair single-copy figure of
merit, a different quantity. The strictly positive randomization
advantage is \lean{w3\_beats\_ghz\_randomization\_advantage}, and the
asymptotic optimality conjecture of Ref.~\cite{FortescueLo2007} is not
claimed.

\subsection{Rates, capacity, and where the converse is cited}
\label{sec:capacity}

Two repeaterless rate-bound functions ship in the two-layer posture. The
bound of Pirandola--Laurenza--Ottaviani--Banchi~\cite{PLOB2017} enters as
the function $\mathrm{plob}(\eta) = -\log_2(1-\eta)$ (\lean{plobBound})
with its rigorous loss-penalty properties: zero at total loss, strictly
increasing on transmissivity (\lean{plobBound\_strictMonoOn}), bounded
below by the linear penalty $\eta/\ln 2$
(\lean{linear\_le\_plobBound}), and decaying to zero along an
exponentially lossy fiber
(\lean{plobBound\_tendsto\_zero\_of\_exp\_decay}). That this function is
the two-way assisted capacity of the lossy bosonic channel, so that no
two-way-assisted protocol exceeds it, is the cited theorem of
Ref.~\cite{PLOB2017}, resting on relative-entropy-of-entanglement bounds
and teleportation stretching that no current formal library provides. The
quantum erasure channel's two-way quantum capacity
$1-p$~\cite{BennettDiVincenzoSmolin1997} enters the same way
(\lean{erasureBound}). The two are capacities of two different channels
and we draw no relation between them.

Multipath distribution requires flow theory, and Mathlib at our pin has
none, so the corpus builds it.

\begin{theorem}[strong duality, \lean{FlowNetwork.maxFlow\_eq\_minCut}]
\label{thm:maxflow}
For a finite real-capacity network with distinct source and sink there
exist a feasible flow $f$ and a cut $S$ with
$\mathrm{val}(f) = \mathrm{cap}(S)$, such that $f$ maximizes
$\mathrm{val}$ over feasible flows and $S$ minimizes $\mathrm{cap}$ over
cuts.
\end{theorem}

Both optimality conjuncts are part of the statement, so this is the
Ford--Fulkerson duality~\cite{FordFulkerson1956} and not the weak half.
It is assembled from weak duality (\lean{flowValue\_le\_cutCap}),
witnessed optimality (\lean{maxFlow\_minCut\_of\_saturating}), existence
of a maximal flow by compactness (\lean{maxFlow\_exists}) and the
augmenting-path theorem. Worked instances connect flows to repeater
chains: the two-edge path network has capacity exactly the bottleneck
(\lean{path3\_capacity\_eq\_bottleneck}), and instantiating the
capacities with the rate-bound function at two fiber segments certifies
the repeaterless series bottleneck
(\lean{path3\_repeaterless\_bottleneck}). The physical-layer anchors that
feed it are the decibel-to-neper attenuation identity
(\lean{fiberTransmission\_eq\_exp\_neg\_attenuationNp}), the
linear-optics Bell-measurement bound of success at most $\tfrac12$
(\lean{bsmSuccessProb\_le\_half\_of\_linearOptics})~\cite{CalsamigliaLutkenhaus2001},
and the elementary-link rate $\tau = L/(c\,p_{\mathrm{link}})$
(\lean{linkRate}) with the expected-attempts factor derived as a
geometric-distribution mean
(\lean{geometric\_expected\_attempts})~\cite{Sangouard2011}.
Handshake-inclusive link timing is model-dependent and deliberately not
bounded.

\section{A worked certification}
\label{sec:worked}

The claim of the preceding sections is that a device number reaches a
certified interval through named theorems. This section carries a
published parameter set through, names every step including the
identifications, and reports what happens.

\subsection{Terminating in rational arithmetic}

The bottom arrow of Fig.~\ref{fig:chain} is a single bracket, introduced
here because this is where it is first needed.

\begin{theorem}[Bernoulli bracket, \lean{expNeg\_enclosure}]
\label{thm:bernoulli}
For $r \ge 0$,
$\;1 - r \,\le\, e^{-r} \,\le\, \dfrac{1}{1+r}$.
\end{theorem}

Both endpoints are rational at rational $r$, so every decay factor in the
corpus (fiber loss, memory decoherence, readout decay, and thermal
occupancy through \lean{thermalExcitedPop\_expNeg\_form}) admits a
machine-checked rational bracket with no floating-point transcendental
and no external interval-arithmetic oracle. Rearranged for the decay
probability, Theorem~\ref{thm:bernoulli} gives
\[
\frac{t/T_1}{1+t/T_1} \;\le\; p_{\mathrm{decay}}(t,T_1)
\;\le\; \frac{t}{T_1}
\]
(\lean{readoutDecayProb\_enclosure}; Fig.~\ref{fig:readout}, left), which
proves the short-window rule $p \lesssim t/T_1$ as an exact inequality on
the full domain rather than as a small-parameter approximation. The
thermal side has the two-sided rational form
$(1-x)/(2-x) \le p_{\mathrm{th}}(x) \le 1/(2+x)$
(\lean{thermalExcitedPop\_rational\_lower},
\lean{thermalExcitedPop\_rational\_upper}; Fig.~\ref{fig:readout},
right), the lower endpoint proven on $0 \le x < 2$ and sharp at $x=0$,
with positivity (\lean{thermalExcitedPop\_pos}) as the floor elsewhere.
When more precision is needed, a degree-$n$ Taylor squeeze on Mathlib's
exponential remainder bound tightens the bracket at kernel-checked cost:
a representative fiber-loss operating point, $e^{-0.46}$ at $0.2$\,dB/km
over $10$\,km through the decibel-to-neper identity, is bracketed loosely
by the Bernoulli form (\lean{expNeg046\_enclosure}) and to three
decimals, $0.631 \le e^{-0.46} \le 0.632$, by the degree-5 squeeze
(\lean{expNeg046\_tight}).

\subsection{The parameter set}

We take the dispersive readout of Walter \emph{et
al.}~\cite{Walter2017}, which reports, for a transmon of transition
frequency $\omega_q/2\pi = 6.316$\,GHz and lifetime $T_1 = 7.6\,\mu$s at
a sample temperature of $10$\,mK, two operating points: a readout
fidelity of $98.25\%$ at an integration window of $48$\,ns, and $99.2\%$
at $88$\,ns, the latter stated to be limited predominantly by the qubit
lifetime. Their fidelity is defined as $F = 1 - P(e|g) - P(g|e)$, so the
uniform-prior averaged assignment error of Theorem~\ref{thm:combined} is
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{avg}} = (1-F)/2$, giving $8.75\times10^{-3}$ at
$48$\,ns and $4.0\times10^{-3}$ at $88$\,ns.

The thermal branch is inactive at these parameters. At $10$\,mK and
$6.316$\,GHz the dimensionless ratio is
$x = \hbar\omega/k_BT \approx 30$, so
$p_{\mathrm{th}} \le 1/(2+x) < 1/32$ by
\lean{thermalExcitedPop\_rational\_upper} and in fact far smaller, so the
relaxation branch dominates the maximum in Theorem~\ref{thm:combined} and
we track it.

\subsection{The chain, evaluated}

At the $88$\,ns point, $r = t/T_1 = 0.088/7.6$, a rational number. Every
step below is exact rational arithmetic.

\emph{Formula layer.} By \lean{readoutDecayProb\_enclosure},
$r/(1+r) \le p_{\mathrm{decay}} \le r$, that is
$1.1446\times10^{-2} \le p_{\mathrm{decay}} \le 1.1579\times10^{-2}$. No
exponential is evaluated numerically anywhere.

\emph{Identification layer.} Identification~\ref{id:relax} is asserted
for this protocol; Identification~\ref{id:therm} is asserted and
inactive.

\emph{Formula layer.} Theorem~\ref{thm:combined} then gives
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{avg}} \ge p_{\mathrm{decay}}/2 \ge
5.72\times10^{-3}$.

\emph{The reported value is below the floor.} The measured
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{avg}} = 4.0\times10^{-3}$ is smaller than the
proven floor $5.72\times10^{-3}$. At the $48$\,ns point the same
computation gives a floor of $3.14\times10^{-3}$ against a measured
$8.75\times10^{-3}$, comfortably satisfied. The certificate holds at the
short window and fails at the long one.

\subsection{Reading the failure}

The failure is informative precisely because the chain has exactly one
identification layer. The formula layer is arithmetic on rationals and
Theorem~\ref{thm:combined} is a two-line consequence of its hypotheses,
so nothing in the deduction can be wrong. Therefore
Identification~\ref{id:relax} is false for this protocol, and the way it
is false is legible: it assumes that any relaxation event inside the
window flips the recorded outcome, whereas Walter \emph{et al.}
discriminate on a weighted integral of the measured quadrature over the
window, so a qubit that decays late still contributes a mostly-excited
trajectory and is still assigned correctly. The all-or-nothing
identification is therefore too pessimistic, and it grows more
pessimistic as the window-to-lifetime ratio grows, which is exactly the
observed pattern: satisfied at $t/T_1 = 6.3\times10^{-3}$, violated at
$1.16\times10^{-2}$.

This is what a falsifiable certificate is for, and it is why the
two-layer posture is a method rather than bookkeeping. A formalization
that had absorbed Identification~\ref{id:relax} into a proof would have
produced a kernel-checked theorem contradicted by a published
measurement, with no line to point at. Here the contradiction localizes
in one displayed sentence, and the repair has a clear shape: replace the
all-or-nothing model with a weighted-integration model whose
misassignment probability depends on the filter, leaving the formula
layer above it unchanged. The weighted-integration reading is a physical
explanation offered here and is not a claim of the verified corpus.

\section{Results the formalization owns}
\label{sec:owned}

Almost every theorem in this corpus formalizes known mathematics. Four
places are exceptions, in the sense that the formal development itself
had an obstruction to clear, and we give each its obstruction and its
route.

\subsection{The sharp continuity constant, unconditionally}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{quantum\_fannes\_audenaert\_sharp}]
\label{thm:audenaert}
Let $\rho,\sigma$ be density operators on a space of dimension $d\ge2$
and $T = \tfrac12\trnorm{\rho-\sigma}$. If $T \le 1 - 1/d$ then
$|S(\rho)-S(\sigma)| \le T\ln(d-1) + H(T)$, with $H$ the binary entropy
in nats.
\end{theorem}

% TODO: Audenaert, J. Phys. A 40, 8127 (2007) is cited from its standard
% statement; the full text is not held in this repository's source corpus.
% Acquire before the attribution sweep clears this claim.
The obstruction is not the classical inequality but the transfer. The
sharp bound~\cite{Audenaert2007} lives on probability distributions; the
quantum statement needs it on the eigenvalue sequences of $\rho$ and
$\sigma$, and the natural transfer inequality, Mirsky's, is usually
stated with a sorting or alignment condition that is exactly what a
formalization cannot leave implicit. The corpus therefore proves Mirsky
unconditionally.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{mirsky\_unconditional}]
\label{thm:mirsky}
For Hermitian $A,B$ with eigenvalues sorted in a common order,
$\sum_k |\lambda_k(A)-\lambda_k(B)| \le \trnorm{A-B}$, with no side
condition beyond hermiticity.
\end{theorem}

Its supporting tower is the Schur--Horn-type step that diagonal entries
of a Hermitian matrix in any orthonormal frame are a doubly stochastic
mixture of eigenvalues
(\lean{eigenvalue\_eq\_doublyStochastic\_combination}), the Ky Fan-type
partial-sum subadditivity (\lean{sum\_top\_subadditive}) and the
Weyl/minimax perturbation bounds with their Lipschitz consequence
(\lean{weyl\_lipschitz})~\cite{Bhatia1997}. With
Theorem~\ref{thm:mirsky} in hand the classical sharp bound, established
on the eigenvalue distributions by a maximal-coupling and spreading
argument, transfers with nothing left over, and the residual hypothesis
that a sorting-dependent Mirsky would have forced into the statement is
absent. Both terms of Theorem~\ref{thm:audenaert} are in nats, matching
the formal statement; Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol} uses the bits-normalized
$h_2$ for key rates, and the two are not interchangeable in a numerical
evaluation. Within the survey scope of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} this is, to
our knowledge, the first machine-checked discharge of the sharp
Audenaert constant.

\subsection{The average-gate-fidelity identity, constructively}

Theorem~\ref{thm:avgfid} is usually obtained by invoking the
unitary-2-design property of the Haar measure. A formalization cannot
invoke it, because Mathlib at our pin has no unitary-2-design
development and adding one is a larger project than the identity
requires. The corpus instead computes the required degree-$(2,2)$
complex-sphere moment directly, along a Gaussian-to-sphere route:
one-dimensional Gaussian moments, a Wick/Isserlis tensor for the
higher-order contractions, polar reduction to separate radial from
angular parts, and a realification step to move between the complex
sphere and the real one. The 2-design second moment is then a theorem
rather than an import, and the identity follows. The route is
general-purpose and is the component of this corpus most likely to be
reusable elsewhere.

\subsection{The Haar--Pauli quadratic integral}

Theorem~\ref{thm:teleport} rests on
$\int_{S^2}\langle\psi|\sigma_z|\psi\rangle^2\,d\mu = \tfrac13$, which
earlier development carried as a tracked hypothesis and which is now
proven. In the Bloch parameterization $|\psi(\theta,\varphi)\rangle =
(\cos(\theta/2), e^{i\varphi}\sin(\theta/2))$ the spinor expectation
reduces to the Bloch coordinate,
$\langle\psi|\sigma_z|\psi\rangle = \cos\theta$
(\lean{pauliExpZ\_blochKet}), and the normalized solid-angle integral
$(1/4\pi)\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi}\cos^2\theta\,\sin\theta\,
d\theta\,d\varphi$ evaluates to $\tfrac13$ against the antiderivative
$-\cos^3\theta/3$ by the fundamental theorem of calculus
(\lean{cosSq\_mul\_sin\_integral}, \lean{haarPauliZSqAverage\_eq}). The
obstruction here was discipline rather than difficulty: the tempting
shortcut is to define the constant as $\tfrac13$ and prove the identity
by reflexivity, which would leave Theorem~\ref{thm:teleport}
kernel-checked and vacuous. The definition is the integral.

\subsection{Max-flow/min-cut in Lean}

Theorem~\ref{thm:maxflow} is a library gap rather than new mathematics,
and we report it because the gap is real and the development is
reusable: Mathlib at our pin has no network-flow theory at all, so
augmenting paths, the residual network and the compactness argument for
existence of a maximal flow were all built here. The trace-norm
multiplicativity $\trnorm{A\otimes B} = \trnorm{A}\trnorm{B}$
(\lean{traceNorm\_kronecker}), the unconditional Mirsky inequality of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mirsky} and the sphere-moment toolchain above fall in
the same category.

\section{Where certification chains break}
\label{sec:breaks}

Building the chain and then attacking every link produced a taxonomy of
four classes, and the fourth is the one that matters for anyone doing
this work.

\paragraph*{Class 1: identification. Closed by literature.} A
mathematical object must be read as a physical one, and the reading is
citable but not provable. Identifications~\ref{id:coh},~\ref{id:relax}
and~\ref{id:therm} are of this class, and so is
Theorem~\ref{thm:caves}: the Caves half-quantum bound requires the
bosonic ladder algebra $[a,a^{\dagger}]=1$, absent from the libraries at
our pin, and is carried as a named binder rather than as an axiom. The
cost of this class is bounded and visible, because the reader can count
the hypotheses, and Sec.~\ref{sec:worked} shows the payoff: when a
certificate fails, the failure has an address.

\paragraph*{Class 2: library gap. Closed by work.} A step is provable
but the substrate for it does not exist yet. Max-flow/min-cut was of this
class and is now closed. LOCC is of this class and is open: the corpus
has no LOCC development, so its entanglement monotonicity results, the
one-shot distillation no-go (\lean{no\_local\_distillation\_to\_bellPair},
that a state with $\trnorm{\rho^{\Gamma}} < 2$ cannot be mapped to a Bell
pair) and the $n$-copy rate bound
(\lean{logNegB\_ncopy\_localKraus\_le}, giving
$E_N\big((\Lambda\otimes\mathbf 1)(\rho^{\otimes n})\big) \le n E_N(\rho)$)
both quantify over Kraus channels acting on one party alone. Those are
the one-sided-local specializations of the monotonicity underlying
$E_D \le E_N$, and the standard LOCC statement of
Ref.~\cite{VidalWerner2002} is cited, not formalized. The
continuous-variable converse behind the rate-bound functions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:capacity} is likewise of this class, as are the DEJMPS
convergence basin~\cite{Macchiavello1998} and an exact diamond distance
for the non-covariant thermal channel.

\paragraph*{Class 3: impossibility. Closed by nothing.} Negative
result~\ref{nr:rb}. The arrow from an averaged benchmark to a worst-case
guarantee is not unbuilt; it cannot be built, because it asks an average
over inputs to bound a supremum over inputs. This class is small and
worth hunting for, because a project that does not distinguish it from
Class 2 will spend indefinite effort on it and, worse, will ship a
theorem named for the thing it wanted.

\paragraph*{Class 4: kernel-checked and empty. Invisible to the
assistant.} A statement can be true, unconditional, kernel-pure and
carry no information, and no proof assistant will say so, because
checking is about truth and this is about content. Three shapes of it
appear in our own corpus. A \emph{trivial endpoint}: the generalized
amplitude-damping declaration proves a bracket whose upper end is the
universal range bound $\ddist \le 1$, so half of it says nothing about
the channel family it names, and reporting it as two-sided overstates by
exactly that half. A \emph{hypothesis wearing a conclusion}:
Theorem~\ref{thm:caves}, whose entire physical content is its binder and
whose deduction is addition. A \emph{closure statement read as a bound}:
the swap-chain envelope, whose proved interval is the image of its
hypothesis interval, so that it constrains an implementation and not the
physics.

The defense we have found effective is not a checker but a question asked
of each statement before it is written: what would have to be true of the
world for this to be false? A statement for which the answer is
``nothing'' belongs to Class 4, whatever its axiom set. That question is
the one thing here we would ask another formalization project to adopt.

\section{Related formalizations and scope}
\label{sec:related}

The closest work is the Lean quantum-information library of Meiburg,
Lessa and Soldati~\cite{Meiburg2025PhysLib}, published under the name
Lean-QuantumInfo and since incorporated into the community physics
library under which we depend on it. Its target is the generalized
quantum Stein's lemma, and the substrate it built to reach that target,
completely-positive trace-preserving maps as bundled objects, a Hermitian
matrix tower, mixed states, trace norm, von Neumann and R\'enyi
entropies, the data-processing inequality and strong subadditivity,
overlaps ours on the entropy side and not at all on the device or network
side. Our relation to it is deliberate and one-directional: we adopt it
where it is authoritative rather than re-proving, through a faithful
bridge (\lean{toMState}, \lean{toCPTPMap}, \lean{toCPTPMap\_apply\_m})
identifying our Kraus-family channels with its bundled channels, so that
its sandwiched R\'enyi data-processing inequality transfers to our
channels (\lean{krausMap\_sandwichedRenyi\_DPI}) and its strong
subadditivity and subadditivity read back as statements about our density
operators (\lean{repo\_strong\_subadditivity},
\lean{repo\_subadditivity}). The bridge is an identification of
representations, not a Choi reconstruction, and its kernel purity is
verified by axiom audit; the derived quantum fluctuation--dissipation
floor and the derived thermal occupancy of Sec.~\ref{sec:params} are
worked demonstrations that bridging beats re-deriving. Where we do
re-prove on our own concrete carrier the reason is the same each time:
the Klein inequality (\lean{relativeEntropy\_nonneg}), subadditivity
(\lean{vonNeumannEntropy\_subadditive}), concavity
(\lean{vonNeumannEntropy\_concave}) and the Gibbs variational bound
(\lean{cross\_sub\_entropy\_eq\_relativeEntropy},
\lean{vonNeumannEntropy\_le\_cross}, \lean{cross\_self\_eq\_entropy})
are used at matrix level throughout Secs.~\ref{sec:protocol}
and~\ref{sec:owned}, where crossing the bridge for each use would cost
more than the proofs do.

Within the survey scope of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} we find no formalization,
in any assistant, of a protocol-level quantum-network fidelity statement,
of the sharp Audenaert constant, or of a device-parameter-to-interval
certification chain of the kind reported here. We claim novelty for no
individual inequality: the diamond-norm calculus, the entropy
inequalities and the entanglement measures are standard, and the
contribution is that they are machine-checked at full strength and wired
together with their identification steps exposed.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The substrate does what the title says: a device or network claim traces
through named theorems from stated parameters to a certified interval,
and the interval is rational and computed by the kernel. What we did not
expect at the outset is that the interesting content would be the
failures. Three of the four classes in Sec.~\ref{sec:breaks} are ordinary
engineering; the third and fourth are not. Negative result~\ref{nr:rb}
says that a standard experimental practice cannot supply the guarantee it
is often read as supplying, and Class 4 says that a proof assistant,
which is the tool one reaches for to eliminate this kind of error, is
structurally unable to detect it.

Both conclusions argue for the same discipline. Separating the formula
layer from the identification layer is what let us notice that
Theorem~\ref{thm:caves} was a binder and that half of a bracket was a
range bound, and it is what made the failed certificate of
Sec.~\ref{sec:worked} diagnostic rather than merely wrong. The rule costs
one hypothesis per physical claim and a certain flatness of tone. What it
buys is that a reader can audit the boundary of the deductive content
without reading Lean.

What this paper does not contain: gate-synthesis and compilation theory,
which is the subject of Ref.~\cite{CompanionCompilation} and which
consumes Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol} for its channel-level certificate,
including the Kronecker-with-identity operator-norm bound
$\opnorm{A\otimes\mathbf 1} \le \opnorm{A}$
(\lean{l2\_opNorm\_kronecker\_one\_le}) that its ancilla-register
argument needs and that it reports as an upstream candidate; the logical
layer of fault tolerance, Ref.~\cite{CompanionFaultTolerance}, to which
the abstract code-distance suppression results
(\lean{logicalErrorBound\_antitone\_distance},
\lean{logicalErrorBound\_tendsto\_zero}) are our interface; the
large-deviation foundations of Ref.~\cite{CompanionStochastic}, whose
compatibility with our linear-response rate function is
\lean{linearResponseRateFunctionCentered\_is\_ldp\_certified} and whose
tail bound away from the fluctuation-dissipation-pinned mean is
\lean{fdt\_rare\_event\_tail\_is\_ldp\_certified}; and runtime tooling,
vendor-specific parameter fits, or experimental data analysis. The corpus
is a mathematical substrate, and its numerical companion pipeline is part
of the public repository's automated validation suite.

\section*{Methods and tools disclosure}
The formal verification layer of this work was developed with substantial
machine assistance: Lean~4 proof drafting and iteration used LLM-based
agents (Anthropic Claude-family models) operating under the project's
staged validation pipeline. Every proof is ultimately certified by the
Lean~4 kernel via \texttt{lake build} on the public repository,
independently of any AI tool. Manuscript prose was AI-assisted and
human-reviewed. The repository's public validation suite reports, for
every draft, any numerical value written inline in prose rather than
sourced from a generated table; that report is the authority on this
manuscript's numerical provenance.

\section{Verification status}
\label{app:verification}

Every theorem named in this paper is verified to the Lean~4 kernel with
axiom set $\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},\,
\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$ and no project-local axioms. The parent
repository's validation suite enforces \axiomcount{} project-local axioms
and \sorrycount{} \texttt{sorry} placeholders project-wide, across
\totaltheorems{} theorems in \leanmodules{} Lean modules. The population
relevant to this paper is the dependency closure of its stated results,
comprising \bundleDecls{} declarations, \bundleTheorems{} of them
theorems, across \bundleModules{} modules; both figures are generated
from that closure rather than typed, and the closure walk stops at
private declarations, so they are lower bounds. The corpus contains no
\texttt{native\_decide} compiled-evaluation escapes and no heartbeat
overrides.

Conditional statements are kernel-checked exactly as stated with their
hypotheses in the statement: the amplifier binder of
Theorem~\ref{thm:caves}, and the identification hypotheses of every floor
and ceiling in Sec.~\ref{sec:params}. Two statements in this paper are
\emph{not} formalized and are marked as such where they appear: the
coherent-error witness for Negative result~\ref{nr:rb}, an elementary
argument assembled from two formalized ingredients, and the
weighted-integration reading of the failure in Sec.~\ref{sec:worked},
which is a physical explanation rather than a claim of this corpus.

The toolchain is pinned (Lean \texttt{v\leantoolchain}, Mathlib
\texttt{\mathlibrev}); the community library
dependency~\cite{Meiburg2025PhysLib} is pinned to a commit verified to
share the identical Mathlib pin, and the theorems that touch it are
verified by axiom audit not to depend on the one sorried declaration
present in that library at our pin.
