\title{Continuous Verification for Computational Physics Research:\\
A Three-Layer Pipeline with Worked Cases from Schwinger--Keldysh
Effective Field Theory, Algebraic General Relativity, and
Topological Quantum Field Theory}

\author{John Roehm}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The most informative outputs of a machine-checked physics programme are
its failures. We report three formalization episodes in which failure,
rather than success, carried the result. In two of them the work was
done by an automated prover's refusal: a returned counterexample
localized a defective hypothesis to the five field components its
specification never named, and a second refuted an expectation that a
condensate gap stays below its cutoff, in doing so yielding a
closed-form ceiling on the coupling range in which the model is valid
at all. In the third no prover was involved: decomposing a large
exclusion into structural sub-lemmas forced a physics assumption that
no assumption registry recorded into the open, where it was removed by
keeping the quantity a free parameter of every statement rather than
admitted as an axiom. A fourth result concerns the
verification apparatus itself: a citation can be metadata-valid, with
a resolving identifier, a cached primary source and correct
bibliographic data, while the claim the prose attributes to it is unsupported by
that source. A pilot of roughly ten spot-checked load-bearing
attributions found three wrong at that level, every one of them
passing all deterministic checks; the systematic sweep that alarm
motivated then read every cached source against every load-bearing
sentence in eighteen manuscripts and found six such fabrications in a
hundred and thirty-four pairs, so the pilot rate was an alarm and not
an estimate. Making such failures legible rather than silent
is, we argue, the real design problem. The apparatus that produced
these results is a three-layer pipeline integrating Python numerics,
Lean~4 proofs and an automated theorem prover, run as a staged
development cycle under explicit invariants; applied across
dissipative effective field theory, analog Hawking radiation, emergent
gravity, anomaly constraints on Standard Model particle content, dark
sector constraints and topological quantum computation, it has produced
\totaltheorems{} machine-checked theorems across \leanmodules{} Lean
modules with \sorrycount{} remaining gaps (\sorrypercent\%), plus
\aristotleproved{} theorems closed by automated proving. Two further
studies document the classical-general-relativity mathematics the
programme accumulated as a byproduct, and the discipline by which a
result is scoped around infrastructure the ambient library does not
contain. Nothing in the method is specific to this physics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Numerical computation in physics research is increasingly
sophisticated and increasingly opaque. A typical large
computational-physics program now combines several thousand lines of
Python or Julia, dozens of Jupyter notebooks, and a paper-trail of
PDF drafts whose numerical content is the output of code paths that
the published prose does not name. The standard publication workflow
provides no machine-checkable connection between the prose and the
code: reviewers cannot, and do not, run the computation. Five years
after publication, when the original code has bit-rotted and the
authors have rotated, no one can reconstruct which version of which
formula produced which figure.

This is not a hypothetical concern. We have personally observed
each of the following failure modes during a single research
program: (i) a numerical claim in a draft that traced to a code
path that no longer existed; (ii) a ``formally verified'' claim
whose underlying Lean theorem was a placeholder of type
\texttt{True}; (iii) a citation whose arXiv identifier resolved to
a paper from an unrelated field; (iv) a parameter whose
value was inherited from a prior draft and had drifted from the
primary source it cited. None of these were detected by the
authors before adversarial review. All of them shipped to draft
state.

The pipeline we describe makes verification \emph{continuous}, in
the sense of integrated into the daily research workflow rather
than retrofitted before submission. Antecedents on the
formal-verification side include landmark engineering verifications
(CompCert~\cite{compcert} for a C compiler; seL4~\cite{sel4} for an
operating-system kernel) and the more recent emergence of
Lean-based physics infrastructure (PhysLean, published under its
earlier name HepLean~\cite{TooBySmithHepLean}). Antecedents on the
proof-assistance side include large-language-model copilots for
Lean theorem proving~\cite{leancipilot} and the silver-medal
International Mathematical Olympiad result obtained by reinforcement
learning inside Lean~\cite{AlphaProof2025}.
Our pipeline has fourteen stages and seventeen invariants, integrates
three orthogonal verification layers, and produces
machine-checkable artifacts at each stage. We have applied it to
a research program spanning \papercount{}
papers covering Schwinger--Keldysh dissipative effective field
theory, analog Hawking radiation in Bose--Einstein condensates and
graphene, emergent gravity through black-hole thermodynamics,
anomaly constraints on Standard Model particle content, dark-sector
substrate constraints from causal-set theory, entropic gravity, and
Jacobson thermodynamic emergence, and topological quantum
computation foundations. The pipeline has produced
\totaltheorems{} machine-checked theorems across \leanmodules{}
Lean modules, with \sorrycount{} remaining unproved gaps
(\sorrypercent\%) and \aristotleproved{} theorems closed by the
Aristotle automated theorem prover. The pipeline, its process
documents, and the validation machinery described below are
publicly available; monospaced file paths throughout this paper
are paths from the root of the public repository (see the Code
Availability statement).

The development cycle described here runs from a substrate change
through formalization, mechanical validation, drafting and three
independent review passes, under a set of invariants that a change
must not violate. Two of those invariants carry most of the weight in
what follows: a declaration proved trivially may never be cited as a
result, and a new project-local axiom requires an explicit discharge
plan rather than a note of intent. The programme currently ships no
project-local axioms at all.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:architecture} describes the three-layer
verification architecture (Python numerics, Lean~4 formal proofs,
Aristotle automated proving). Sections~\ref{sec:case1},
\ref{sec:case2}, \ref{sec:case3} present three worked cases that
illustrate the pipeline catching errors that single-layer
verification cannot. Section~\ref{sec:pipeline} presents the
fourteen-stage workflow and its invariants in detail.
Section~\ref{sec:strengthening} documents the preemptive-strengthening
discipline.
Section~\ref{sec:provenance} describes sentence-level provenance and
the cross-paper consistency cluster mechanism that enables bundle
publication. Section~\ref{sec:three_layer_extended} returns to the
three-layer architecture in the context of automated theorem proving
integration. Section~\ref{sec:adversarial} describes the
fresh-context adversarial-reviewer pattern, the recorded-review
readiness semantics, and the limits of metadata-level citation
validation surfaced by a June~2026 external-review remediation.
Section~\ref{sec:phase6f_substrate} presents the
machine-checked layer of classical general relativity the programme
accumulated at component level. Section~\ref{sec:riemannian_sidebar}
presents the connection-level companion built above the upstream
covariant-derivative interface, up to Levi-Civita uniqueness.
Section~\ref{sec:structural_prop_scoping} describes how results are
scoped around infrastructure the ambient library does not yet
contain. Section~\ref{sec:lessons} discusses lessons
learned and outlines future pipeline extensions.

\section{The Three-Layer Verification Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The pipeline integrates three orthogonal verification layers, each
addressing a failure mode that the others cannot.

\textbf{Layer~1: Python numerics for fast iteration.} A formula is
implemented in a canonical \texttt{formulas.py} module, with a
docstring containing (i) the mathematical formula in plain text,
(ii) the Lean theorem name that formalizes it, (iii) the Aristotle
run identifier (or ``manual'' or ``pending''), and (iv) a citation
to the published source for derived formulas. Domain-specific
modules import from \texttt{formulas.py} rather than reimplementing.
Python tests verify computational correctness, edge cases, and
physical bounds. Layer~1 catches computation errors but not
formalization errors.

\textbf{Layer~2: Lean~4 formal proofs for mathematical certainty.}
Each formula is stated as a Lean theorem in the strongest form
possible. Statements are not weakened for proof convenience; if
the strongest form is not provable, the proof architecture is
wrong (Section~\ref{sec:strengthening}). Manual proofs are written
for theorems within reach. Layer~2 catches formalization errors
but not definition errors: a formally verified proof can still
be wrong if the definitions it operates on are wrong
(Section~\ref{sec:case3}).

\textbf{Layer~3: Aristotle automated theorem prover for sorry
gaps.} The entire Lean project is submitted with strategy hints
embedded in the docstrings of unproved theorems (the ``PROVIDED
SOLUTION'' convention). Aristotle uses tactic synthesis, term
search, and machine learning to find proofs. Results are
manually reviewed before integration (no blind acceptance)
to verify that the proof exercises the hypotheses, that
\texttt{lake build} passes after integration, and that no
heartbeat overrides have been introduced (Invariant~10).

The three layers interact in a discovery--formalization--automation
cycle. A typical cycle: a Python implementation of a new formula
exposes a numerical regularity; the regularity is stated as a Lean
theorem with sorry; the theorem is closed by Aristotle in a
priority batch; the closure feeds back into Python by setting the
Aristotle run identifier in the formula docstring, which is then
checked by the validation suite (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}). The gap
equation for tetrad condensation ran this cycle end to end. Its
critical coupling $G_c = 8\pi^2/(N_f \Lambda^2)$ was derived in Python
and proved in Lean within the project
(\texttt{criticalCoupling\_formula}, shown equal to the
emergent-gravity module's own critical coupling by
\texttt{criticalCoupling\_eq\_adw}), and the nine theorems fixing the
equation's solution structure around that coupling were closed by
Aristotle in run~\texttt{79e07d55}, whose identifier the Python
docstrings then carry for validation to check
(Section~\ref{sec:case2}).

The composition is asymmetric in a way that matters. Layer~1 alone
gives reproducibility but not correctness: notebooks can produce
the same wrong answer indefinitely. Layer~2 alone gives correctness
but not numerical scope: a formalization of an idealized model is
silent on the actual experimental parameters.
Layer~3 alone gives proof-search throughput but no verification of
what was proved: an automated proof can be vacuous if the
definitions it operates on are wrong (Section~\ref{sec:case3}). The
composition gives all three.

\section{Worked Case~1: The FirstOrderKMS Aristotle Counterexample}
\label{sec:case1}

An automated prover can return a refutation instead of a proof, and the
refutation is the more valuable of the two outcomes. This section works
through the case in which that happened here. A uniqueness theorem for
the first-order Schwinger--Keldysh action was submitted with a
hypothesis that had been written to encode the Kubo--Martin--Schwinger
(KMS) condition and did not encode it. Aristotle returned an explicit
nine-component coefficient vector satisfying the hypothesis and
violating the conclusion. The defect was in the hypothesis, not in the
physics being formalized and not in the prover: the informal sentence
the hypothesis was meant to capture (``KMS fixes the first-order
dissipative action up to two transport coefficients'') is true, and is
now the closed theorem
\texttt{SKEFTHawking.SKDoubling.firstOrder\_uniqueness}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/SKDoubling.lean}, lines~412--439, Aristotle
run~\texttt{270e77a0}). What was wrong was the formal rendering of one
of its three hypotheses, and nothing short of a machine attempting the
proof was going to say so.

\subsection{The formal setting}

Two distinct nine-fold objects appear in this case, and keeping them
apart is the whole of the bookkeeping. The first is the local field
content. \texttt{SKFields} (\texttt{SKDoubling.lean}, line~80) is a
record of nine real numbers: the retarded and advanced fields
$\psi_r, \psi_a$; their first derivatives
$\partial_t\psi_r, \partial_x\psi_r, \partial_t\psi_a, \partial_x\psi_a$;
and the second derivatives
$\partial_t^2\psi_r, \partial_t\partial_x\psi_r, \partial_x^2\psi_r$.
An \texttt{SKAction} is a map from this record to a pair
$(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{L}, \operatorname{Im}\mathcal{L})$ of reals,
so hermiticity of the Lagrangian is carried by the data type rather
than by an assumption.

The second nine-fold object is the coefficient space.
\texttt{FirstOrderCoeffs} (lines~292--301) enumerates nine candidate
monomials, six in the retarded-response sector and three in the noise
sector:
%
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{L} &=
  r_1\,\psi_a\partial_t^2\psi_r
+ r_2\,\psi_a\partial_x^2\psi_r
+ r_3\,\psi_a\partial_t\partial_x\psi_r
+ r_4\,\psi_a\partial_t\psi_r
+ r_5\,\psi_a\partial_x\psi_r
+ r_6\,\psi_a\psi_r , \\
\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{L} &=
  i_1\,\psi_a^2
+ i_2\,(\partial_t\psi_a)^2
+ i_3\,(\partial_x\psi_a)^2 ,
\end{align*}
%
assembled by \texttt{firstOrderAction} (lines~304--315). The target of
the uniqueness statement is the two-parameter family
\texttt{firstOrderDissipativeAction} (lines~251--261), built from
\texttt{DissipativeCoeffs} (\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/Basic.lean},
lines~152--158), which carries $\gamma_1$ (isotropic phonon damping,
related to bulk viscosity), $\gamma_2$ (anisotropic damping along the
superfluid flow), and the two non-negativity fields
\texttt{gamma\_1\_nonneg} and \texttt{gamma\_2\_nonneg}. Uniqueness
means: every point of the nine-dimensional coefficient space that
satisfies the constraints is the image of some $(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)$.

\subsection{The published axiomatic and its Lean rendering}

The constraints are not this program's invention. Crossley, Glorioso,
and Liu~\cite{CrossleyGloriosoLiu2017} impose on the closed-time-path
action, in their \S I.D, a normalization condition (their eq.~(1.28),
sharpened at the level of the action to eq.~(1.36): the action vanishes
when the two contour legs are identified, so every term carries at
least one factor of the advanced field), a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ reflection
condition on the action (their eq.~(1.29)), and, as item~5 of the same
list, the requirement that the imaginary part of the action be
non-negative. For a thermal density matrix (their eq.~(1.60)) the
generating functional obeys in addition the local KMS condition, a
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry (their \S I.F, eq.~(1.68)) whose quadratic-order
content is the familiar fluctuation--dissipation theorem
$\operatorname{Im}G_R = \tanh(\beta_0\omega/2)\,G_S$ (their eq.~(1.62)).
Glorioso, Crossley, and Liu~\cite{GloriosoCrossleyLiu2017} replace the
local KMS condition by the \emph{dynamical} KMS symmetry (their \S IV.A,
eq.~(4.1)), equivalent to it in the classical limit but applicable to
any state in local equilibrium rather than only to a thermal density
matrix perturbed by background fields. Glorioso and
Liu~\cite{GloriosoLiu2018} then derive the local second law
$\partial_\mu S^\mu \ge 0$ from that $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry together
with the classical remnant of quantum unitarity, which supplies both
$\operatorname{Im}I_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge 0$ and the normalization
condition (their \S II, eqs.~(2.5)--(2.6)); the second law is proved in
general and its local form perturbatively in the derivative expansion.
A pedagogical account of the same construction is given in the lectures
of Glorioso and Liu~\cite{GloriosoLiu2018TASI}. The realization of
dynamical KMS is not unique when the theory carries ultraviolet content
that the infrared symmetries do not fix: Jain and
Kovtun~\cite{JainKovtun2024} exhibit distinct Schwinger--Keldysh models
that share an effective action but realize the symmetry differently
(their Appendix~C, eqs.~(C.9)--(C.11)), which is why this program keeps
the strict and algebraic readings of the condition as separate
predicates rather than collapsing them.

Three of these conditions transcribe into Lean without difficulty.
Normalization is \texttt{satisfies\_normalization} (lines~126--129),
positivity of the imaginary part is \texttt{satisfies\_positivity}
(lines~140--141), and hermiticity is discharged by the
$\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$ codomain. The fourth condition is where the
case begins.

\subsection{The hypothesis as first submitted}

The KMS condition was first rendered at the level of the field
transform, as the structure \texttt{KMSSymmetry} (lines~156--167). It
bundles a map on \texttt{SKFields}, a specification of that map, and an
invariance clause. The specification is a conjunction of exactly four
equations, fixing $\psi_r$, $\partial_t\psi_r$ and $\partial_x\psi_r$
and shifting $\psi_a \mapsto \psi_a + \beta\,\partial_t\psi_r$. The
remaining five components of the record, namely
$\partial_t\psi_a$, $\partial_x\psi_a$ and the three second derivatives
of $\psi_r$, are named nowhere in the specification, so a witness may
transform them however it likes. That the specification pins exactly
four components is not a reading of the source but a theorem,
\texttt{GloriosoLiu.kmsSpec\_pins\_four\_components}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/GloriosoLiu/Phase1Reconciliation.lean},
lines~118--127), whose proof projects the four conjuncts out of the
structure field itself.

Set against the published condition, this rendering fails in two
independent directions at once. It is too permissive, in that it
constrains four of nine field components and is silent on the rest. It
is also too rigid: its \texttt{invariance} clause demands pointwise
equality of the Lagrangian under the transform, whereas the dynamical
KMS condition of ref.~\cite{GloriosoCrossleyLiu2017} is equality up to a
total derivative (their eq.~(5.3)), imposed on combinations that are
closed under the symmetry rather than on individually truncated orders
(their eq.~(5.9) and the sentence following it). The library records
this by keeping the transform-level reading as
\texttt{GloriosoLiu.hasDynamicalKMS\_strict}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/GloriosoLiu/Axioms.lean}, lines~91--92), a
sanity primitive used for degenerate witnesses, while the substantive
\texttt{dynamical\_KMS} field of the six-condition skeleton
\texttt{SKEFTAxioms} (lines~160--172) is
\texttt{hasDynamicalKMS\_algebraic} (lines~108--111). Neither reading is
a Lean \texttt{axiom} declaration: both are ordinary structures, and the
theorems below are proved from them.

One further declaration should be named here, because the reader is
otherwise entitled to think it carries more than it does. The bridge
from the skeleton back to the nine-coefficient setting is
\texttt{GloriosoLiu.FirstOrderProjection\_exists}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/GloriosoLiu/FirstOrderProjection.lean},
lines~48--52): under \texttt{SKEFTAxioms} there exist
\texttt{FirstOrderCoeffs} whose \texttt{firstOrderAction} reproduces the
action's Lagrangian on every field configuration. Its proof body is
the single term \texttt{A.local\_equilibrium}, because the
local-equilibrium field of the skeleton \emph{is} that existential. The
theorem is therefore a projection and not a derivation, and the
polynomial-form content it appears to establish is content the
local-equilibrium condition was already assumed to carry. We name it
because the contrast with \texttt{firstOrder\_uniqueness}, a
twenty-eight-line declaration closed by the prover, is the distinction a
reader needs in order to see which declaration in this section carries
the load.

\subsection{What came back}

Submitted with the transform-level hypothesis, the uniqueness statement
was refuted. The witness is the coefficient vector
%
\[
c = (r_1,\ldots,r_6,i_1,i_2,i_3) = (0,0,0,0,0,0,0,1,0),
\]
%
a single unit of noise in the $(\partial_t\psi_a)^2$ channel and nothing
else, now recorded as
\texttt{GloriosoLiu.aristotleCounterexample} (lines~139--140). It
defeats the statement for a reason the reader can check against
\texttt{firstOrderAction} directly. Its action is
$(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{L},\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{L}) =
(0,(\partial_t\psi_a)^2)$, so positivity holds. Matching the imaginary
part against \texttt{firstOrderDissipativeAction}, whose noise sector is
$(\gamma_1/\beta)\psi_a^2 + (\gamma_2/\beta)(\partial_t\psi_a)^2$, forces
$\gamma_1 = 0$ and $\gamma_2 = \beta > 0$; but then the retarded sector
of the target is $\beta\,\psi_a\partial_t^2\psi_r$, which does not
vanish identically, while the witness has no retarded part at all. No
pair $(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)$ reproduces it. The coefficient $i_2$ that the
witness excites multiplies $(\partial_t\psi_a)^2$, and
$\partial_t\psi_a$ is precisely one of the five components the transform
specification never mentions.

The witness survives in the library as a kernel-checked object rather
than as an anecdote. \texttt{GloriosoLiu.aristotle\_counterexample\_violates\_FirstOrderKMS}
(lines~150--158) proves
$\neg\,\texttt{FirstOrderKMS}\ c\ \beta$ for every $\beta > 0$, and
\texttt{GloriosoLiu.four\_of\_nine\_partition\_recovered}
(lines~189--204) combines that with the four-component projection into
one statement: any instance of the transform-level condition pins those
four components, and the witness is ruled out only by the algebraic
condition. A corollary,
\texttt{GloriosoLiu.four\_of\_nine\_partition\_under\_GLU}
(lines~231--239), derives the same conclusion from the six-condition
skeleton by extracting the coefficient witness from its
\texttt{dynamical\_KMS} field. We emphasize the scope: what is refuted
is a formalization, not the axiomatic of
refs.~\cite{CrossleyGloriosoLiu2017,GloriosoCrossleyLiu2017}.

\subsection{The corrected condition}

The repair is to impose the KMS condition on the coefficients rather
than on the transform. \texttt{FirstOrderKMS} (lines~367--379) is a
\texttt{Prop}-valued structure carrying seven relations: the four
lower-derivative retarded monomials vanish,
$r_3 = r_4 = r_5 = r_6 = 0$; two fluctuation--dissipation relations tie
the surviving noise coefficients to the retarded ones,
$i_1\beta = -r_2$ and $i_2\beta = r_1 + r_2$; and the spatial noise
channel is switched off, $i_3 = 0$. The two fluctuation--dissipation
relations are stated multiplicatively in $\beta$, which is equivalent to
the familiar $i_1 = -r_2/\beta$ and $i_2 = (r_1+r_2)/\beta$ at any
positive temperature and avoids a division in the hypothesis. Every one
of the nine coefficients appears in at least one of the seven relations,
and the solution set is two-dimensional, coordinatized by $(r_1,r_2)$.

Under this hypothesis the uniqueness theorem closes.
\texttt{firstOrder\_uniqueness} states that for every
\texttt{FirstOrderCoeffs} $c$ and every $\beta > 0$, if
\texttt{firstOrderAction}~$c$ satisfies positivity and $c$ satisfies
\texttt{FirstOrderKMS}~$c$~$\beta$, then there exist
\texttt{DissipativeCoeffs} whose action agrees with it on every field
configuration. The proof supplies the witness explicitly,
$\gamma_1 = -r_2$ and $\gamma_2 = r_1 + r_2$, and discharges the two
non-negativity obligations of \texttt{DissipativeCoeffs} from positivity
together with the two fluctuation--dissipation relations. Exactly one of
the seven relations is what excludes the counterexample: with
$r_1 = r_2 = 0$ and $i_2 = 1$, the relation $i_2\beta = r_1 + r_2$
collapses to $\beta = 0$.

\subsection{Probing the corrected condition on purpose}

A hypothesis that has just been caught being too weak invites the
question of whether it is now too strong, and the same instrument
answers it. Two further statements were submitted deliberately. The
first, \texttt{firstOrder\_KMS\_optimal} (lines~582--603), proves that
under \texttt{FirstOrderKMS} and $\beta > 0$ the positivity of
\texttt{firstOrderAction}~$c$ is \emph{equivalent} to
$i_1 \ge 0 \wedge i_2 \ge 0$, so positivity contributes exactly the two
sign conditions that \texttt{DissipativeCoeffs} already carries and no
further relation among the coefficients is hiding in it. The second
mis-states the condition on purpose. \texttt{FirstOrderKMS\_altSign}
(lines~610--618) flips one sign, replacing $i_1\beta = -r_2$ by
$i_1\beta = +r_2$, and
\texttt{firstOrder\_altSign\_uniqueness\_test} (lines~636--656) is the
kernel-checked \emph{negation} of the corresponding uniqueness claim,
witnessed by $c = (1,1,0,0,0,0,1,2,0)$ at $\beta = 1$: that vector
satisfies the flipped relations and positivity, yet forces
$\gamma_1 = -r_2 = -1$, contradicting \texttt{gamma\_1\_nonneg}. The
sign in the fluctuation--dissipation relation is therefore load-bearing
rather than conventional, and the library says so in a form a reader can
recompute rather than in a remark.

\subsection{What generalizes}

The case supports a narrow claim and not a broad one. A prover's failure
to close a statement is evidence about the statement, and this
particular failure localized the defect to a single hypothesis out of
three and, within that hypothesis, to the five field components its
specification never named. That is more information than a human review
of the same statement produced, because the informal sentence the
statement encodes is true; the error lived entirely in the gap between
the sentence and its transcription, which is the one place prose review
cannot look. Three conditions made the outcome usable. The statement was
posed over a finite-dimensional coefficient space, so a counterexample
was expressible as a tuple rather than as an obstruction argument. The
refuting witness was retained and formalized, so the negative result is
now checkable by the kernel instead of remembered. And the corrected
hypothesis was afterwards attacked on purpose, by flipping a sign and
proving the resulting claim false, so its non-triviality is measured
rather than assumed.

The limits are equally concrete. The refutation did not supply
\texttt{FirstOrderKMS}; it established only that the submitted
hypothesis admitted a vector the conclusion could not accommodate.
Choosing the seven algebraic relations required reading the derivation
in refs.~\cite{CrossleyGloriosoLiu2017,GloriosoCrossleyLiu2017} and
projecting it onto this coefficient space by hand. A prover localizes a
defect; it does not supply the physics that repairs it. Nor does a
refutation distinguish between the two ways a statement can be wrong. A
hypothesis can be too weak, as here, or the definitions underneath a
correct-looking statement can themselves be wrong, in which case a
successful proof is the dangerous outcome rather than the reassuring one
(Section~\ref{sec:case3}). Only the first failure mode announces itself
by refusing to close.

\section{Worked Case~2: The Gap-Solution-Bounded Conjecture Disproof}
\label{sec:case2}

Worked Case~1 turned on a prover's failure to close a theorem that was
true only under a stronger hypothesis. This case is the sharper
variant. The target statement was false, the prover said so with an
explicit witness, and the statement that replaced it is weaker in a way
that changes what the surrounding physics is entitled to assume. The
episode is the one that most directly answers the objection that formal
verification only re-certifies what the author already believes:
nothing in the informal derivation was going to produce this
counterexample, because the informal derivation never wrote down the
hypothesis the counterexample violates.


The emergent-gravity sector of the program formalizes a
Nambu--Jona-Lasinio-type self-consistency condition for tetrad
condensation, relating a condensate gap $\Delta$, a four-fermion
coupling $G$, a UV cutoff $\Lambda$, and a fermion flavour count $N_f$
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/TetradGapEquation.lean}). The gap equation
is the fixed-point condition
\begin{equation}
  \Delta \;=\; G\,N_f\,\Delta\,I(\Delta),
  \qquad
  I(\Delta) \;=\; \int_0^{\Lambda} \frac{c_4\,p^3}{p^2+\Delta^2}\,\mathrm{d}p
  \;=\; \frac{c_4}{2}\left[\Lambda^2 - \Delta^2
        \log\!\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^2}{\Delta^2}\right)\right],
  \label{eq:gapeq}
\end{equation}
with density-of-states coefficient $c_4 = 1/(4\pi^2)$ in $d=4$. The
right-hand evaluation is exact, and it is what the Lean development
takes as primitive: \texttt{gapIntegral} is defined as the closed form
rather than as a Bochner integral, which keeps the module inside real
analysis and away from measure theory. Two properties of $I$ carry the
whole case. It is strictly positive for every $\Delta \geq 0$ and every
$\Lambda > 0$ (\texttt{gapIntegral\_pos}), and it is strictly
decreasing in $\Delta$ (\texttt{gapIntegral\_strictAnti}). Neither
statement carries an upper restriction on $\Delta$, and neither
needs one: Eq.~(\ref{eq:gapeq}) is a convergent integral over a fixed
momentum range whatever the value of the gap.

The rest of the module is the standard phase-transition picture.
Writing $G_c = 1/(N_f\,I(0)) = 8\pi^2/(N_f\Lambda^2)$
(\texttt{criticalCoupling\_formula}), the trivial solution $\Delta = 0$
is the only solution below $G_c$
(\texttt{gap\_trivial\_unique\_subcritical}), a positive fixed point
exists above it (\texttt{gap\_nontrivial\_exists}, by the intermediate
value theorem applied to $g(\Delta) = G N_f I(\Delta) - 1$), and at
$G = G_c$ the reduced gap function still vanishes at $\Delta = 0$
(\texttt{bifurcation\_at\_Gc}), so the trivial branch persists through
the transition. That last statement is weaker than the word
bifurcation suggests, and weaker than the module's own summary comment
claims: no pitchfork is proved, and the exchange of stability is read
off the two theorems either side of it rather than established
directly. Nine theorems of this module closed in Aristotle
run~\texttt{79e07d55}, among them
\texttt{gap\_trivial\_unique\_subcritical} and
\texttt{gap\_nontrivial\_exists}. \texttt{criticalCoupling\_formula}
and \texttt{bifurcation\_at\_Gc} were not among them; both were proved
inside the project against a live goal state.

A tenth was submitted with them. Above the critical coupling the gap is
a low-energy parameter of an effective description valid up to
$\Lambda$, so a gap exceeding the cutoff is outside the regime in which
the equation was derived, and the expectation that a solution obeys
$\Delta < \Lambda$ is the natural one. It is worth being precise about
the status of that expectation, because it is the thing the formalization
tested. It is not a stated condition of the model. The standard reviews
impose the cutoff as a regularization prescription, not as a bound on the
condensate, and Buballa's analysis in fact exhibits a high-mass branch of
gap-equation solutions in which the dynamical mass is comparable to or
exceeds the cutoff, discarding that branch on phenomenological grounds --
an empirical bound on the quark condensate -- rather than by any
appeal to $\Delta < \Lambda$~\cite{Buballa2005}. The literature's own
numerics already visit the regime the expectation forbids, without ever
forbidding it. What the formalization did was write the expectation down
as a hypothesis list, at which point it became falsifiable. The formalization plan recorded
it as a target theorem, \texttt{gap\_solution\_bounded}, and stated it
the only way a Lean statement can be stated, with every hypothesis
enumerated: $0 < G$, $0 < N_f$, $0 < \Lambda$, $0 < \Delta$, and
$\Delta$ a fixed point of the gap operator, concluding $\Delta <
\Lambda$. That enumeration is the substantive difference from the
informal argument. On paper, ``the supercritical regime'' silently
carries a second condition, that $G$ is not too far above $G_c$,
because that is the regime anyone has in mind while writing. In Lean
the second condition either appears in the hypothesis list or it does
not exist, and here it did not.

Aristotle returned a witness rather than a proof: $N_f = 1$,
$\Lambda = 1$, $\Delta = 1$, and
$G = 1/\bigl[(c_4/2)(1-\log 2)\bigr]$. The check is two lines. For
$\Delta > 0$ the fixed-point condition of Eq.~(\ref{eq:gapeq}) reduces
to $G N_f I(\Delta) = 1$, and at $\Delta = \Lambda$ the closed form
gives $I(\Lambda) = (c_4\Lambda^2/2)(1-\log 2) = I(0)\,(1-\log 2)$, so
that value of $G$ makes $\Delta = \Lambda$ an exact solution. The
witness saturates the claimed strict inequality rather than violating
it by a margin, which is the shape a counterexample to a strict bound
usually takes and the shape a numerical scan is least likely to find.

The saturating witness is not a boundary artefact, and this is where
the formalization pays for itself a second time. Because $I$ is
strictly positive at every $\Delta$, the coupling
\begin{equation}
  G(\Delta) \;=\; \frac{1}{N_f\,I(\Delta)}
  \label{eq:invertgap}
\end{equation}
is well defined and positive for every $\Delta > 0$, and by
construction $\Delta$ is a fixed point of the gap operator at that
coupling. Because $I$ is strictly decreasing, $G(\Delta) > G(0) = G_c$,
so every member of this family lies inside the supercritical regime the
conjecture was about, and $G(\Delta)$ increases without bound as
$\Delta$ does. The fixed-point set is a curve in the $(G,\Delta)$ plane
that runs past $\Delta = \Lambda$ and never turns back. Strict
monotonicity of $I$ then converts the conjecture into an exact
threshold: a positive fixed point satisfies $\Delta < \Lambda$ if and
only if
\begin{equation}
  G \;<\; G^{\ast} \;=\; \frac{G_c}{1-\log 2},
  \label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
since $\Delta < \Lambda$ is equivalent to $I(\Delta) > I(\Lambda)$,
which is equivalent to $1/(G N_f) > 1/(G^{\ast} N_f)$. The conjecture
was therefore not merely wrong. It was missing exactly one hypothesis,
and the missing hypothesis is identifiable in closed form from
components that were already verified. Equations~(\ref{eq:invertgap})
and~(\ref{eq:threshold}) follow from \texttt{gapIntegral\_pos} and
\texttt{gapIntegral\_strictAnti} together with the closed form; the
library does not currently carry either as a declaration, and they are
presented here as consequences of verified components rather than as
verified statements.

The physical content of Eq.~(\ref{eq:threshold}) is worth stating in
its own right, because it is a result obtained from a failed proof. The
window of couplings in which this model condenses \emph{and} stays
inside its own regime of validity is the bounded interval
$G_c < G < G_c/(1-\log 2)$, a factor of $1/(1-\log 2)$ in coupling, not
the half-line $G > G_c$ that the folklore reading licenses. A
calculation that scans supercritical couplings without that ceiling
will at some point report a condensate the effective description cannot
support, and will do so without any internal signal that it has left
the model's domain.

What the library ships is the surviving statement,
\texttt{gap\_solution\_monotone}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/TetradGapEquation.lean}, lines~333--349,
Aristotle run~\texttt{79e07d55}): for couplings $G_1 < G_2$ with
positive fixed points $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_2$ respectively,
$\Delta_1 < \Delta_2$. Its shape records the refutation as much as its
content does. It quantifies over fixed points supplied as hypotheses,
not over a solution function $\Delta^{\ast}(G)$, so it assumes neither
that a branch has been selected nor that a selected branch stays
anywhere in particular; and it concludes an ordering rather than a
bound, so it says nothing that a large enough $G$ can falsify. The
disproved statement is retained in the module as a commented-out block
at lines~311--325, carrying the counterexample data and the reason the
statement fails, so that a reader of the module encounters the
refutation at the point where the missing theorem would otherwise look
like an oversight.

Downstream, the bound does not silently reappear. Where a later module
needs a condensate that stays below the cutoff, it takes that as an
explicit assumption rather than importing it from the gap equation. The
predicate \texttt{H\_ScalarChannelIsTetradBifurcationOutput}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/ScalarRungInterpretation.lean},
lines~170--172) states that a scalar channel arising from the
supercritical branch has condensate VEV bounded by the UV cutoff, and
it is consumed as a hypothesis by
\texttt{mexican\_hat\_vev\_under\_supercritical\_bridge}
(lines~179--184), whose conclusion is exactly as conditional as its
input. The predicate has content rather than being a convenience:
\texttt{bridge\_excludes\_super\_uv\_vev} (lines~190--196) proves the
contrapositive, that a channel whose VEV exceeds the cutoff cannot be
an output of the branch, so a super-cutoff channel refutes the bridge
instead of quietly satisfying it. The assumption is carried in the
program's \texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_REGISTRY} (\texttt{src/core/constants.py})
with an eliminability grade and a named discharge path, namely the
quantitative map from the scalar-channel parameters to the expansion
coefficients of the gap-equation bifurcation, on whose resolution the
predicate becomes a theorem of the gap-equation module. A referee can
see, without reading a proof, exactly which conclusions of the
emergent-gravity sector rest on the sub-cutoff assumption and what
would be required to remove it.

The general lesson is narrower than ``formal methods find bugs'' and
more useful. A pen-and-paper theorem inherits its hypotheses from the
context in which it is written, and the reader supplies the missing
ones without noticing. A mechanized theorem cannot: the hypothesis list
is finite, explicit, and adversarially searchable, and an automated
prover that cannot close the goal is searching precisely the gap
between the hypotheses that were written and the ones that were meant.
In this case the search terminated in a witness, the witness localized
the omission to a single missing inequality, and the inequality turned
out to have a closed form that bounds the model's regime of validity
from above. None of that required knowing in advance that the
conjecture was false.

The refutation has not yet propagated back to the numerics, and saying
so is part of the point. The project's own solver
\texttt{tetrad\_gap\_solution} still iterates to a fixed point that
does not satisfy Eq.~(\ref{eq:gapeq}) above the saturation coupling,
returning a value with a large positive residual rather than reporting
that no solution exists there. The formal layer knows the bound and the
Python layer does not, which is the drift the three-layer architecture
exists to prevent, in the direction it is weakest at catching: a
theorem constrains what may be claimed, not what a function returns.
The defect is filed against the substrate rather than repaired here,
because the repair belongs with the solver and its tests, not with the
manuscript that reports it.

\section{Worked Case~3: The Chirality-Wall Axiom Decomposition}
\label{sec:case3}

A proof goal can be out of reach because it is large rather than
because it is deep, and from inside a tactic loop the two failures are
indistinguishable: in both cases nothing closes. The distinction is
operational. A deep goal needs an idea. A large one needs bookkeeping,
and the bookkeeping is mechanical once someone insists on it. The third
worked case is a large goal. The decomposition that made it tractable
also did something no monolithic proof could have done: it forced into
the open a physics assumption that none of the project's assumption
registries recorded.

The theorem is a Standard-Model exclusion, formalized in
\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/EWBaryogenesisChiralityWall.lean}. The module
encodes viability of electroweak baryogenesis as a conjunction of two
necessary conditions,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ewbgviable}
\texttt{EWBGViable}(a, p, c) \;\equiv\;
\texttt{WallCracked}(a) \;\wedge\;
\mathsf{IsBaryogenesisViable}(p, c),
\end{equation}
where $a \in \mathbb{Z}_{16}$ is the anomaly of the fermion content,
$p$ is a record of finite-temperature effective-potential parameters
(\texttt{EWFiniteTParams} in
\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/EWPhaseTransition.lean}), and $c$ is a real
sphaleron-decoupling threshold. \texttt{WallCracked}$(a)$ is $a = 0$:
the anomaly cancels, so the chiral theory admits the gauging step and a
non-perturbative regulator exists. The second conjunct is the
phase-transition condition, $0 < E$ together with
$c\,\lambda\,T_c < E$. Here $\lambda$ is the quartic self-coupling,
$\mu^2$ the mass-squared parameter and $c_T$ the thermal-mass
coefficient of the finite-temperature effective potential, and $E$ is
its cubic thermal coefficient
\texttt{p.cubic\_coeff} and
$T_c = \sqrt{\mu^2 / c_T}$ is the critical temperature built from the
record's other fields.

The root theorem is
\texttt{sm\_no\_nu\_R\_ewbg\_doubly\_forbidden}. For every parameter
record $p$, every threshold $c$, and under the hypothesis that the
resummed Standard-Model transition is a crossover, it asserts
\emph{both}
$\neg\,\texttt{EWBGViable}(45, p, c)$ and
$\neg\,\texttt{EWBGViable}(48, p, c)$. The two conjuncts are statements
about two different fermion contents and they fail for two different
reasons. Three generations without right-handed neutrinos carry
$3 \times 15 = 45$ Weyl components and $45 \equiv 13 \not\equiv 0
\pmod{16}$, so the wall is intact and the exclusion is independent of
transition order. Adding three right-handed neutrinos gives $48 \equiv
0$, which cracks the wall, and the exclusion then rests entirely on the
crossover. Stating both is not a redundant bundle: neither conjunct
implies the other, and dropping either leaves a fermion content for
which the paper's headline claim is unsupported.

Stated monolithically, that goal is large. Unfolding
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ewbgviable} at both anomaly values expands the negated
conjunction into two decision problems in $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$, two copies
of a three-way real-arithmetic conjunction containing a square root,
and the four bundled positivity fields that \texttt{EWFiniteTParams}
carries with every instance ($0 < \mu^2$, $0 < \lambda$, $0 < c_T$,
$0 \leq E$). De~Morgan over the whole thing needs a case split on
$a = 0$ that the goal does not supply. The pipeline's own guidance
(\S\ref{sec:pipeline}) lists monolithic simplification over goals of
this size as an anti-pattern and sets the target for a submitted
sub-goal at roughly a dozen term-level constraints.

None of the pieces is deep. The $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ facts are decidable
and close by \texttt{decide}. The crossover exclusion is a two-line
contradiction against a strict inequality. The wall-intact verdict for
the Standard Model is a direct consumption of an upstream arithmetic
theorem. The size came from stating all of them at once, and it is the
only thing that made the goal hard.

biconditional discussed below; no other proof in the module runs past
seven. The root theorem's own proof is a two-element anonymous
constructor. Reading upward from it, the chain that discharges the
exclusion is:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{sm\_no\_nu\_R\_wall\_intact}: $45 \neq 0$ in
$\mathbb{Z}_{16}$, consuming
\texttt{Z16AnomalyComputation.three\_gen\_anomalous}.
\item \texttt{ewbg\_forbidden\_if\_wall\_intact}: an intact wall
refutes the first conjunct of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ewbgviable} for any $p$ and

\item \texttt{sm\_no\_nu\_R\_ewbg\_blocked}: the two above, composed at

\item \texttt{ewbg\_forbidden\_if\_transition\_crossover}: a crossover

\item \texttt{sm\_with\_3nu\_R\_ewbg\_forbidden\_under\_klrs}: the
previous item at $a = 48$, under the tracked crossover hypothesis.

\texttt{wall\_intact\_xor\_cracked} and

\texttt{sm\_no\_nu\_R\_anomaly\_eq\_neg\_three}, which identifies the

\texttt{sm\_single\_gen\_with\_nu\_R\_wall\_cracks} and

\texttt{sm\_with\_3nu\_R\_ewbg\_iff\_first\_order\_strong}, which
records that with the wall cracked the compound predicate collapses to
the phase-transition condition alone; and the quantitative anchor
\texttt{sm\_klrs\_overshoot\_ratio\_gt\_threshold}.

The payload of this case is not that the decomposition worked. It is
what the decomposition exposed. One of the sub-lemmas,
\texttt{ewbg\_forbidden\_iff\_wall\_intact\_or\_not\_viable}, is a
biconditional rather than an implication: it asserts that every failure
of \texttt{EWBGViable} is attributable to the wall, to the transition,
or to both, and that there is no third failure mode. Writing that
statement out is what raised the question the monolith never had to
answer. Exhaustivity holds only relative to what the compound predicate
records, and the sphaleron rate, which is the physical quantity the
whole decoupling criterion is about, appears in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ewbgviable} nowhere except as the free threshold
parameter $c$. Nothing in the predicate constrains the rate itself. The
biconditional is therefore true as stated only under a further
assumption about the sphaleron rate, and that assumption had no entry
in any of the project's assumption registries.

The audit closed it by proving it rather than by declaring it. The
module defines the dimensionless suppression factor
$\Sigma(v,T) = e^{-v/T}$ explicitly, as a structural stand-in whose
limits are the physically meaningful content ($\Sigma \to 0$ deep in
the broken phase, $\Sigma \to 1$ in the symmetric phase), and then
bounds it from both sides:
\texttt{sphaleronSuppression\_pos} gives $0 < \Sigma(v,T)$ at any
finite $(v,T)$, and
\texttt{sphaleronSuppression\_le\_one} gives $\Sigma(v,T) \leq 1$
whenever $v/T \geq 0$. A factor that is strictly positive and never
exceeds unity cannot open a failure channel of its own, so the rate
enters the exclusion only through the threshold, which is exactly what
the biconditional's exhaustivity requires. The threshold itself stays a
free real parameter in every statement in the module, including the
root theorem; its numerical value lives in the project's
experimental-parameter table and never inside a theorem. Consistent
with that resolution, no sphaleron-rate hypothesis appears today in
\texttt{AXIOM\_METADATA}, in \texttt{PLACEHOLDER\_THEOREMS}, in
\texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_REGISTRY} or in \texttt{BORDISM\_HYPOTHESES}: there
is nothing left to record, because no statement in the module depends
on the rate. The module ships zero new axioms.

One qualification belongs here, because it is the distinction this
paper is about. The two bounds are proved, but nothing in the project
consumes either: no declaration lists them among its dependencies.
They document why a strictly positive factor bounded by unity cannot
open a failure channel; they are not lemmas the root theorem invokes.
What actually removes the dependency is the threshold remaining a free
real parameter. A proved theorem that nothing consumes records an
argument rather than carrying one, and the audit's own value here was
in forcing the assumption into view, not in the two bounds it left
behind.

The general point is that an implicit dependency is invisible in
proportion to the size of the statement that hides it. A monolithic
proof of the root theorem would have been correct, would have passed
every mechanical gate the pipeline applies, and would have carried the
same unexamined assumption indefinitely, because no gate in the pipeline
can ask a question about an assumption nobody has named. Decomposition
is what turns an implicit assumption into a statement, and a statement
is something a reviewer can refuse.

What the root theorem does still assume, it assumes visibly. Exactly
one tracked hypothesis appears in its type: \texttt{H\_KLRS\_SM\_Crossover}~$p$,
the proposition that the fully resummed Standard-Model transition is a
crossover at the physical Higgs mass. This is an external lattice
result and is not derived here. It is recorded in
\texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_REGISTRY} (\texttt{src/core/constants.py}), graded as
an external-boundary input of hard eliminability and naming this
theorem among its dependent theorems, and its evidence is quantitative:
the four-dimensional lattice analysis of the SU(2)-Higgs model places
the endpoint of the first-order line at $m_H = 66.5 \pm 1.4$~GeV and
transforms it perturbatively to $m_H = 72.4 \pm 1.7$~GeV for the full
Standard Model, above which only a rapid crossover is
seen~\cite{CsikorFodorHeitger1999}; the earlier three-dimensional
analysis had bracketed the endpoint between $70$ and $95$~GeV, most
likely near $80$~GeV~\cite{KLRS1996}. All three lattice figures are
themselves under Invariant~8: they are registered in
\texttt{PARAMETER\_PROVENANCE} (\texttt{src/core/provenance.py}) as
\texttt{EW.M\_H\_ENDPOINT\_SU2HIGGS\_GEV},
\texttt{EW.M\_H\_ENDPOINT\_SM\_GEV} and
\texttt{EW.M\_H\_ENDPOINT\_KLRS\_RANGE\_UPPER\_GEV}, each carrying the
tier, the DOI, and the verbatim sentence of the abstract it was read
from. That registration is what makes the next paragraph's arithmetic
falsifiable rather than merely true: a revision to either published
endpoint surfaces as provenance drift instead of leaving a
still-checking \texttt{norm\_num} behind. Against the observed $m_H =
125.20$~GeV the margin is wide, and the module states it as a
falsifiable arithmetic claim rather than a remark:
\texttt{sm\_klrs\_overshoot\_ratio\_gt\_threshold} proves
$1.5 < 125.20 / 72.4$ by \texttt{norm\_num}.

Three registries carry the project's assumption inventory, and which
one an assumption lands in is itself a claim that has to be checked.
\texttt{AXIOM\_METADATA} holds project-local axioms and their retirement
record, graded by eliminability; \texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_REGISTRY} holds
tracked propositions that consumers take as explicit hypothesis
parameters; \texttt{PLACEHOLDER\_THEOREMS} holds declarations proved
\texttt{True := trivial}, which Invariant~9 forbids any proof, formula
or paper claim from leaning on; \texttt{BORDISM\_HYPOTHESES} holds
bordism-theoretic inputs deferred to future Mathlib infrastructure. The
grades are not static. The entry \texttt{gapped\_interface\_axiom} was a
live project-local axiom and is now graded \texttt{closed}, because the
axiom was replaced by a tracked proposition (\texttt{TPFConjecture} in
\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/SPTClassification.lean}) that every consumer
takes as an explicit hypothesis parameter. The substantive conjecture is
unchanged; what changed is that it is now visible in four type
signatures instead of silently present in every closure below the
axiom. That is the same move as the sphaleron case one step further
along: name the assumption, then discharge it if you can.

\section{What a Development Cycle Has to Guarantee Before Review Can Find Anything}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The deterministic layer described in this section does not substitute for
the review layer of Section~\ref{sec:adversarial}; it exists to make that
layer's findings possible. A reviewer can only ask whether a cited
declaration says what the prose claims if the declaration resolves, the
count is current and the figure matches its generator. Every mechanism
below buys one such precondition, and the three worked cases above turn on
preconditions that held: the counterexample of
Section~\ref{sec:case1} is informative because the coefficient space was
finite and explicit, and the hidden assumption of Section~\ref{sec:case3}
became visible because a decomposition had names to attach it to.

The cycle runs as sequential stages, each with a gate that must pass before
the next begins. Reordering them produces rework and lets errors propagate
downstream.

Interactive Lean development via the language-server protocol
replaces a ``write tactic, run \texttt{lake build}, parse error,
iterate'' loop, whose cycle time is set by the build, with one whose
cycle time is set by a language-server query. The
interactive loop reads the proof state at any line and column,
tests candidate tactic sequences without editing the file, and
returns ``no goals'' when the proof is complete. Per tactic-test cycle, the interactive
loop replaces a $\sim\!15$\,s lake-build round-trip with
millisecond-scale LSP state inspection. End-to-end
proof-construction time on a given theorem improves by a factor
determined by the number of tactic-test cycles the proof requires;
we have not measured this factor on a controlled benchmark set, and
quantitative speedup claims would require such a benchmark to be
load-bearing.

Stage~13, fresh-context adversarial review, runs a Lean-aware
adversarial reviewer agent in a fresh context window against each
paper or bundle near submission readiness. The agent works through
eight finding classes (citation integrity, parameter provenance,
formal-verification-claim substantiation, cross-paper
consistency, narrative grounding, assumption disclosure, numerical
freshness, and production-run health, the same eight classes
enumerated in detail in Section~\ref{sec:adversarial}) and emits
a structured markdown report.
Findings are auto-picked up as graph nodes; ``BLOCKER'' findings
flip the affected readiness gate to blocked. Stage~13 is meaningful
only on a codebase that already passes its own internal validation
(Stages~1--12); it backstops the failure classes that internal
validation cannot detect by construction.

adversarial-review findings for systemic patterns: failure classes
that recur across multiple papers, or findings that indicate a gap
in the pipeline itself. Such patterns are recorded in a quality-
improvement register with owners and target dates. Stage~14 is
advisory; it never blocks publication. Its purpose is feeding
pipeline improvements back into subsequent waves. The April~2026
external-adversarial-review round, which surfaced a thirteen-
dimensional problem space across the project, was the seed event
for the register.

work. Stage~1 establishes the canonical sources of truth: physical
constants in \texttt{constants.py}, formulas in
\texttt{formulas.py}, figure functions in \texttt{visualizations.py}.
Stage~2 implements formulas with provenance metadata. Stages~3--4
formalize and prove (interactive plus automated). Stage~5 verifies
the formal layer compiles cleanly. Stage~6 runs Python tests.
Stage~7 runs the automated cross-layer validation suite
(enumerated live by \texttt{validate.py --list}; the count is
deliberately not quoted here, because a census figure written into
prose is stale the moment a check is added: this sentence
read ``thirty-three'' until an audit found the live figure had
more than doubled)
spanning formula--theorem correspondence, parameter provenance,
numerical freshness, citation integrity, and bundle consistency
(Sections~\ref{sec:provenance} and~\ref{sec:adversarial}).
Stage~8 generates publication-
quality figures. Stage~9 runs a figure-reviewer agent. Stage~10
drafts the paper with full provenance for every claim. Stage~11
produces reproducible computational notebooks. Stage~12 syncs
documentation and runs counts regeneration. Each stage's gate must
pass before proceeding.

The pipeline's invariants must hold at all times, not just at stage
completion:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Canonical formulas.} \texttt{formulas.py} is the only
place physics formulas live.

\item \textbf{Canonical constants.} \texttt{constants.py} is the only
place experimental parameters and the theorem registry live.

\item \textbf{Canonical visualizations.} \texttt{visualizations.py}
is the only place figure functions live.

\item \textbf{Every formula has a Lean theorem.} Every Lean theorem
has a proof (zero sorry target).

\item \textbf{Every computed quantity has bounds.} Physical bounds
are tested and enforced by validation.

\item \textbf{Every paper claim traces to computation.} Numerical
claims match formula output within 0.5\%.

\item \textbf{Narrative derives from data.} Feasibility claims need
computed support.

\item \textbf{Every parameter has verified provenance.} Each value
traces to a published source via the
\texttt{PARAMETER\_PROVENANCE} table.

\item \textbf{Placeholder theorems are non-load-bearing.} Theorems
proved as \texttt{True := trivial} are documentation markers
only. Tracked in a \texttt{PLACEHOLDER\_THEOREMS} registry; not
counted as substantive.

\item \textbf{No heartbeat overrides in proof bodies.} A proof body
that hits the heartbeat limit is evidence that the proof
architecture is wrong; the fix is sub-lemma decomposition, not
raising the budget.

\item \textbf{Every external bibitem has a primary-source cache
file.} Each non-in-prep entry in the citation registry carries a
resolvable path to a cached copy of the cited primary source,
held in a per-phase literature cache maintained alongside the
repository, structurally preventing the hallucinated-citation
failure mode.

\item \textbf{Provenance DOIs resolve to the registry.} Every DOI
mentioned in any parameter-provenance source must resolve to a
registry bibkey; every cited bibkey must exist in the registry.

\item \textbf{Stage~14 quality-improvement register is auto-
regenerated and manually curated, never wiped.} Closed items
are preserved verbatim across regenerations.

\item \textbf{Every paper-shaped output lifts into a publication
bundle.} New drafts, section additions, and notebook companions
identify their target bundle at Stage~1 and record it in the
draft-mapping document before it is assembled, so every published
sentence has a recorded route from the source that produced it.

\item \textbf{Every new project-local axiom requires explicit
user sign-off.} No axiom ships on
recommendation-only authority; every new axiom carries either a
discharge plan (a scheduled wave producing a substantive
constructive proof) or a documented argument that no constructive
proof is feasible against the current upstream mathematics
library. The posture is that axioms are temporary scaffolding,
not permanent commitments: the project's last pre-existing
project-local axiom was retired into a tracked-hypothesis
\texttt{Prop} on 2026-05-19 (Section~\ref{sec:case3}), taking the
live project-local axiom count to zero.

\item \textbf{Every consumed tracked-hypothesis proposition is in the
single registry.} A \texttt{Prop}-valued definition encoding an
undischarged claim, consumed as a binder by any theorem, must appear
in \texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_REGISTRY} with its tier, statement, status,
source, risk and publication-facing gloss, or be listed as
non-load-bearing with a reason. This is the invariant that makes the
disclosures of Sections~\ref{sec:case2} and~\ref{sec:case3} obligatory
rather than optional: it is the reason a free parameter left in a
statement cannot quietly become an unrecorded assumption.

\item \textbf{Every provably-false settled no-go has a live,
kernel-pure, non-vacuous backing theorem.} The negative-front mirror
of the previous invariant. A route the project has settled as
\emph{false} is recorded with the false statement it rules out and
with backing theorems that must exist in the dependency export, use
only the ambient kernel axioms, and be non-vacuous, since a
self-discharging no-go blocks nothing. Routes closed by policy or
preference rather than by proof are explicitly out of its scope and
stay in prose.

\end{enumerate}

The placeholder invariant addresses an inflation problem that would
otherwise be invisible: \placeholdertheorems{} of \totaltheorems{}
declarations are proved trivially and serve as markers for where a future
proof attaches. They are registered as such and excluded from the
substantive count, which is \substantivetheorems{}. A theorem total that
silently included them would be the kind of number this paper argues no
reader should have to take on trust.

\subsection{Writing statements that can fail}
\label{sec:strengthening}

One precondition deserves its own treatment, because it is the one a
reviewer cannot supply after the fact. A theorem whose statement cannot
fail carries no information, and no amount of downstream review recovers
that: the declaration resolves, the proof checks, and the claim is
vacuous. The strengthening pass is an audit of each new theorem statement
against six recurring anti-patterns. Originally retroactive (the audit ran after
theorems were stated and proved, with retroactive cuts applied
where the audit identified a vacuous statement), the pass has
been moved to the prospective phase: each theorem statement is
checked against the same six anti-patterns \emph{before} the
statement is written. The reduction in audit cost is substantial, and
the record is per-wave rather than a trend line. The
pre-discipline baseline is the wave labelled 6c.3 (2026-04-27), in
which the pass still ran retroactively and cost twelve cuts; it is
recorded in that wave's roadmap as ``the baseline to beat''. Across
the fifteen subsequent waves for which the cost is logged, with the
pass prospective throughout, it never again reached twelve and never
exceeded six; the median is two, and it first reached zero at 6e.5 and
again at 6b.2. We give the series rather than its endpoints because it
is not monotone (the wave immediately after the first prospective
one cost more, not less), and a reader entitled to the claim is
entitled to see that.

The six anti-patterns are:

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{1. Bundle redundancy.} A theorem stating a
conjunction is redundant if dropping any conjunct produces a
statement of equal information content. The pattern occurs when
multiple conjuncts are algebraically equivalent: for instance,
$\mathrm{satisfiesAt}\ m\ \mathrm{WEP}\ \wedge\ \mathrm{satisfiesAt}\
m\ \mathrm{EEP}\ \wedge\ \mathrm{satisfiesAt}\ m\ \mathrm{SEP}$ when
$\mathrm{violationLevel}\ m = \mathrm{none}$ is one substantive
theorem dressed as three. The fix is to write the substantive form
($\mathrm{violationLevel}\ m = \mathrm{none}$) plus a separate
extraction lemma if downstream callers need the bundle shape.

\item \textbf{2. Multiplication-only triviality.} A theorem
whose substantive content is exhausted by an obvious arithmetic
identity ($0.34 = 2 \times 0.17$, for instance) is structural, not
physical. The fix is to replace with a load-bearing comparison
that separates a conformant case from a violator
($0.34 < 1.0$).

\item \textbf{3. Self-discharging axiom.} An axiom whose
statement reduces to truth via algebra alone is vacuous. The fix
is to either remove the axiom or write the substantive content
elsewhere.

\item \textbf{4. Falsifier as restated hypothesis.} A
``falsifier'' theorem whose body restates its hypotheses without
exercising any computation is structural-tautology repackaging.
The fix is to ensure the falsifier exercises the hypothesis.

\item \textbf{5. Cross-module bridge integrity.} If a
theorem's docstring references another module's theorem, the body
must \texttt{import} that module and \emph{call} the theorem,
not merely list it as a documentation pointer. The audit pattern
is: every cross-reference in a docstring must be backed by a Lean
call in the body. A bridge whose body never invokes the named
target is documentation-only and rots silently as the codebase
evolves.

\item \textbf{6. Defining the conclusion.} If a function is
defined as \texttt{:= <obvious target>}, a theorem that asserts
$0 \leq C/A$ for that function is trivially true. The fix is to
either push the substantive content elsewhere (a tracked-hypothesis
proposition, or a project-local infrastructure module) or commit to
a non-trivial concrete model.

\end{itemize}

The discipline is applied as a five-question prospective checklist
on each theorem statement before it is written. The reduction in
strengthening-pass cost is the primary metric, but a ruthless
post-wave review remains mandatory: empirically, the
prospective discipline catches the more obvious failure modes
($\exists$-absorption, biconditional-tautology in conditional
violators) but misses subtler patterns: identity-function wrappers
($\theta\ h_a = h_b$), within-own-$\pm 2\sigma$-band tautologies,
pairwise-distinctness on inductive constructors, and definitional-
unfolding-as-physics. Both passes are mandatory.

The discipline is not aesthetic. Each anti-pattern corresponds to
a specific failure of the theorem-statement-as-falsifiable-claim
property: a theorem that can be discharged by \texttt{rfl} or
\texttt{decide} or \texttt{not\_lt.mpr h\_disagree} or
$0 \leq C$ for any positive $C$ does not constitute a falsifiable
physical claim. The strengthening discipline ensures that what we
prove is what we claim.

\section{Sentence-Level Provenance and Cross-Paper Consistency Clusters}
\label{sec:provenance}

The provenance machinery described so far answers one question:
where did this number come from? A program that publishes more than
one paper from one codebase has to answer a second. What does this
sentence assert, and which other sentences in the program assert the
same thing? Sentence-level provenance records the first; cross-paper
consistency clusters record the second. The failure they exist to
prevent is not a wrong number. It is one fact stated two different
ways in two papers that a referee may well read side by side.

Every prose sentence of every draft carries a record in a per-paper
state file. The record fixes the sentence's claim type (one of nine:
numeric, theorem reference, citation, parameter, formal claim,
qualitative, methodology, transition, metaclaim); its chain of
backing links, each typed as a formula, a Lean theorem, an axiom, a
parameter, a citation, a hypothesis, an automated-prover run, or a
production run, and carrying a completeness verdict of full, partial
or none; the verdict of the most recent automated claims review; and
a content-hash identifier.

The hash is the leading eight hexadecimal digits of a digest of a
normalized form of the sentence: lowercased, with markup-only
commands replaced by their arguments, citation and cross-reference
commands deleted, quotation marks and dashes unified, whitespace
collapsed, and a trailing bibliographic bracket stripped.
Normalization is what makes the identifier worth having. A sentence
that is retypeset, recited or rewrapped keeps its identifier, so the
identifier names the assertion rather than its presentation. Any
change of wording yields a new identifier, which is the intended
behaviour rather than a defect: the record's job is to make a changed
claim visible, not to preserve continuity across one.

A single command-line interface,
\texttt{scripts/sentence\_state.py}, is the only path that mutates
that file or the append-only audit log beside it.
Each mutating operation takes an exclusive advisory lock on the
paper's state, appends its audit event \emph{before} computing the
new state, then installs the new state by writing a temporary file
and renaming it. A crash between those steps therefore loses no
history: the audit log is replay-canonical, and the same interface
replays it, reports any drift against the current state, and rewrites
the state on request. Human ratification is a separate field from the
agent verdict and takes one of four values, recorded with actor and
timestamp, so a reader of the record can always tell which of the two
a given sentence rests on.

Findings are pinned to sentence identifiers rather than to line
numbers, and that is what makes repeated review tractable. When a new
review run is ingested, every prior finding is re-checked. One that
reproduces keeps its first-observed date. One that does not is
auto-closed only when a deterministic recheck accounts for the change
(a registry lookup, a declaration-name resolution, a count, a pin
read); otherwise it becomes a supersession candidate that a human
must confirm, so no judgment-based finding closes silently. An
identifier present in the prior state and absent from the new run is
tombstoned with its own audit event, and a tombstoned sentence whose
normalized text is a near match for a current one is reported as a
rewrite candidate rather than as a deletion beside an unrelated
insertion.

Cross-paper consistency clusters are built from the same hashes.
\texttt{scripts/cluster\_detect.py} indexes every non-tombstoned
sentence in the program and groups them by normalized hash. An
exact-match cluster is a set of sentences that reached the same
digest through the same normalization the per-paper review used. A
normalized-match cluster is a set that agrees only under a more
aggressive normalization, which additionally folds Unicode and
discards punctuation, so that two sentences differing in hyphenation
or citation style still meet. A group counts as a cluster only if it
spans two or more papers; repetition inside one paper is redundancy,
not a consistency risk. The result is written to
\texttt{papers/claim\_clusters.json}, and
\texttt{scripts/bundle\_clusters.py} projects each cluster through
the draft-to-target assignment table into
\texttt{papers/cluster\_bundle\_index.json}, tagging every cluster
with the set of publication targets its members are destined for and
flagging those that span two or more. The overview manuscript is
excluded from that test: every draft contributes to it, so counting
it would mark every cluster cross-target and the flag would carry no
information.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/i1_fig3_sentence_state_clusters.png}
\caption{Sentence-state claim clusters (schematic). Three source

\texttt{validate.py --check bundle\_consistency} reads that index,
and its guarantee is narrower than the word consistency suggests. For
an exact-match cluster the members are identical after normalization
by construction; the check certifies that identity, and the residual
risk is a later direct edit of one member. For a normalized-match
cluster the members are known to differ, and the check does not
adjudicate the difference: it flags the cluster for the adversarial
review of Section~\ref{sec:adversarial}, which resolves it against
that target's anchor list. A cluster whose match kind the index does
not recognize fails the check outright, since that means the index is
stale with respect to the detector that wrote it. The companion check
\texttt{validate.py --check claim\_clusters\_fresh} regenerates the
cluster file whenever any sentence record is newer than it, so
staleness is a transient state rather than a silent one.

The mechanism's reach is lexical, and its blind spot follows
directly. Two papers asserting the same fact in different words share
no normalized hash and form no cluster. A falsification factor
written as a power of ten in a letter and as a count of orders of
magnitude in the deep paper that shares its subject is exactly the
discrepancy a reader notices and exactly the one the detector cannot
see. A semantic matching strategy over sentence embeddings is
specified in the detector and deliberately left unimplemented; until
it exists, agreement on a load-bearing claim across two manuscripts
is carried by the per-target anchor list of
Section~\ref{sec:adversarial}, which names the specific claims and
citations a target's review must verify, and the cluster index covers
the narrower case of verbatim reuse. We state the boundary rather
than describing the check as a consistency guarantee, because a
reader who believed the stronger claim would draw the wrong
conclusion from a green result.

Clusters mean something only because each source draft has a declared
destination. The program's publication targets are declared once, in
a registry (\texttt{scripts/bundle\_registry.py}) carrying each
target's code, tier, working title and journal target, with a
strategy document (\texttt{docs/PAPER\_STRATEGY.md}) as its
human-readable counterpart and a validation check that fails the
suite if the two disagree or if any consumer re-hardcodes the roster.
Each source draft is assigned to one or more targets in an
append-only table
(\texttt{docs/PAPER\_DRAFT\_MAPPING.md}) together with a lift action
recording whether its content becomes a section of one target, a
letter accompanied by a section of a deep paper, an experimental
companion, an overview summary, a retention in place, or a
retirement. That assignment propagates into every sentence record as
a destination, a section hint and a lift action, which is what allows
a cluster to be projected onto targets at all. Content arriving after
a target is drafted is absorbed through a documented protocol whose
branches are selected by the target's current review state, rather
than by redrafting.

Per-target state lives in a metadata file beside each manuscript: the
journal and page contract, the declared apex theorems, the status of
each review stage with the date and report path of the review that
produced it, a freshness flag against the source manifest, and the
open-finding counts the readiness semantics of
Section~\ref{sec:adversarial} consume. Taken together this is what
makes consolidation of many sources into few publications tractable
without losing per-claim provenance: the audit trail travels with the
prose, the cluster index preserves the agreement it can actually
certify, and the per-target metadata records which review produced
which verdict.

Two deposit-time standards extend the same principle to publication
mechanics: what a register can determine is derived, not decided.
The manuscript-level disclosure of machine assistance exists in
exactly two variants of one canonical text, differing only in a
conditional sentence crediting the automated theorem prover, and the
variant a given manuscript carries is computed from the run registry,
the per-paper declared Lean modules, the draft-to-target table and
the Lean dependency export, rather than chosen by an author. Deposit
sequencing is triggered by completion of the content-level
attribution sweep of Section~\ref{sec:adversarial}, and the
conversion of in-preparation self-citations into deposited
identifiers at that point is a single scripted pass that edits the
citation registry, regenerates each affected bibliography entry from
registry data rather than pattern-matching the heterogeneous prior
forms, and writes the primary-source cache stubs the corresponding
invariant requires, so the invariant holds across the transition
instead of being repaired after it.

\section{Aristotle Automated Theorem Prover Integration}
\label{sec:three_layer_extended}

Automated theorem proving is the fallback layer of this pipeline and
not its engine. Proof construction happens against a live goal state,
interactively, and the automated prover is reserved for goals that
resist that loop after they have been decomposed into sub-lemmas. The
project's cumulative record of automated closures is the Aristotle run
registry in \texttt{src/core/constants.py}, which holds
\aristotleproved{} entries against \aristotleruns{} distinct
identifiers. This section describes the registry, the submission and
re-incorporation protocol, and the operational asymmetry that keeps
the prover in the fallback position. The two episodes in which a
prover outcome carried the physics are worked through separately in
Sections~\ref{sec:case1} and~\ref{sec:case2};
Section~\ref{sec:case3}, which involved no prover run, is what the
fallback position looks like when the interactive loop suffices.

The division of labour was not the original one. In the earlier
arrangement the prover was the default proof-construction tool: a
theorem statement was registered with a strategy hint in its
docstring, the \emph{entire} Lean project was uploaded, and the
returned files were reviewed by diff and merged. Targeting flags
existed but narrowed only the prompt, never the upload, so every
submission shipped the whole substrate and the cycle time was about a
day. Interactive proof against the language server changed the
economics rather than the capability: a candidate tactic is tested
without editing the file and the resulting goal state is read back at
the latency of a language-server query rather than of a build.

Three situations still indicate the prover, and they are not
interchangeable. The first is a goal whose tactic search space is too
large to explore by candidate-at-a-time testing: long rewrite chains
over non-commutative algebras, multi-step decompositions that branch
at every step, and proofs needing an auxiliary construction that the
local goal state does not suggest. The second is breadth rather than
depth: a module carrying many small, independent obligations, where
one submission amortises the fixed cost of a round trip across all of
them. The third is the case where a \emph{refutation} is the useful
output. Failure to close a goal interactively is not evidence that the
goal is false, and no amount of it becomes evidence; a returned
counterexample settles the question in one step. That is the mechanism
behind both Section~\ref{sec:case1} and Section~\ref{sec:case2}, and
it is the only one of the three that cannot be replaced by more
patience at the keyboard. In every case decomposition precedes
submission. A monolithic goal is not a candidate for the prover, it is
a candidate for the bookkeeping described in
Section~\ref{sec:case3}.

A registry entry is a smaller object than it is often taken for, and
the difference matters for anyone auditing a provenance claim. The key
is a bare declaration name and the value is a run identifier, or a
marker recording that the theorem was closed by hand alongside an
automated batch. There is no module field, no priority field and no
date field. Three consequences follow. A key does not identify
a theorem on its own, so a claim of the form ``this theorem was closed
in this run'' has to be resolved against the module in which the
declaration actually lives before it is written down. Nine entries
carry the manual marker in place of a run identifier: they are
theorems closed by hand alongside an automated batch, several of them
because a machine run had certified a weaker statement that was
afterwards strengthened. The prover-closed figure
\aristotleproved{} quoted in the abstract and in
Section~\ref{sec:intro} excludes them by construction, because the
decider for ``the prover closed it'' is the presence of a run
identifier and not the presence of a key, so it is the number a machine
actually closed. The run count \aristotleruns{} does not yet make the
same distinction: it counts the marker itself as though it were a
run, so the number of genuine runs is one below it. Fourteen keys currently name no live
declaration, the residue of modules restructured after their batch was
integrated; the population is frozen as a ceiling that fails on any
new occurrence, and it is not reduced by deletion, because the
registry is append-only provenance for results already published.

The largest single batch by registered theorems carries sixty-three
entries across the fracton-hydrodynamics and
Wetterich--Nambu--Jona-Lasinio modules. The most instructive one is
smaller. Run \texttt{78dcc5f4} carries twenty-five
entries spanning seven unrelated modules in a single session:
S-matrix unitarity and Verlinde fusion coefficients at $k = 1$ and
$k = 2$ in \texttt{SU2kSMatrix} (ten entries); modularity of both
data in \texttt{RibbonCategory}; the determinant
(\texttt{e8\_det\_one}) and a leading principal minor of the $E_8$
Cartan matrix in \texttt{E8Lattice}; Rokhlin divisibility of the
signature in \texttt{SpinBordism} (\texttt{rokhlin\_from\_bordism});
the generator image of the small-quantum-group quotient in
\texttt{RestrictedUq} (\texttt{uqToSmallUq\_E}); two
integrated-autocorrelation-time bounds in \texttt{VerifiedJackknife}
(\texttt{intAutocorrTime\_ge\_half} among them); and seven
Hopf-algebra structure identities in \texttt{Uqsl2Hopf}
(\texttt{comul\_coassoc} and the two antipode identities among them).
Yield of that kind is a poor proxy for value. The two runs this paper
works through carry ten registered theorems between them, nine in run
\texttt{79e07d55} (Section~\ref{sec:case2}) and one in run
\texttt{270e77a0} (Section~\ref{sec:case1}), and both mattered for
what they refused to prove.

The current submission protocol is partial rather than whole-project,
and re-incorporation is verify-then-graft. A submission stages a
minimal Lean project consisting of the transitive import closure of
the target file, with only the target editable and the closure
supplied as context; a dependency that no module in the closure
imports is pruned from the staged copy, which is disposable and leaves
the repository untouched. Submission requires explicit authorisation
and is refused for a closure already submitted in an unchanged state.
Results are retrieved without being integrated. The graft applies only
to the target files and refuses outright if the returned tree touched
anything else in the closure, then runs a verification gauntlet: a
clean build with no new \texttt{sorry}, a fresh dependency export, an
axiom-closure audit of the grafted declarations against the project's
allowed axiom set, the validation suite, and the tests. The graft is
kept on a pass and reverted automatically on a failure, so a rejected
proof costs a submission and never the substrate. Only a kept graft is
registered, and registration is a fixed chain: the registry entry, the
pinned count assertion that guards it, the counts regeneration, and
the machine-assistance disclosure derived from the registry as
described in Section~\ref{sec:provenance}. The batches enumerated
above predate this protocol and were integrated under the earlier
whole-file arrangement with manual diff review and, where two runs
overlapped on one file, per-theorem cherry-picking. Every declaration
named in this section is live in the module named beside it.

The prover's sandbox runs a Lean toolchain four minor versions behind
the project's \texttt{\leantoolchain}, and the project does not
down-port its own sources to close that gap. A maintained
backward-compatible copy of the substrate would be a second source of
truth obliged to track the first, and it fails the moment a proof
genuinely needs a feature of the current toolchain. The mismatch is
instead handled at the two boundaries where it is cheap: a standing
note prepended to each submission tells the prover what its
environment contains, so that budget is not spent rediscovering it,
and the gauntlet rejects any returned proof that does not build and
kernel-verify against the project's own toolchain. What remains is a
per-submission overhead that is independent of how hard the goal is.
That overhead, and not any limitation of the prover, is why the
fallback stays a fallback: when the cost of the round trip exceeds the
cost of constructing the proof interactively, the interactive loop is
simply the better instrument, and for the overwhelming majority of
this project's theorems it is.

\section{The Fresh-Context Adversarial-Reviewer Pattern}
\label{sec:adversarial}

Every check described so far is deterministic, and every one of them
was written by the same hand that wrote the artifact it inspects.
That is a structural limit, not a resourcing one. A check can confirm
that a bibliographic key is registered; it cannot confirm that the
registered paper is the paper the sentence needs. A check can confirm
that a Lean declaration exists and compiles; it cannot confirm that
its statement is the proposition the prose claims. The failures that
survive an internal pipeline are exactly the failures whose detection
requires reading the artifact against something outside it.

An external adversarial review of the project's drafts in April 2026
measured the size of that blind spot, and the result was worse than
the pipeline's authors expected. Three citations carried identifiers
resolving to papers in unrelated fields: a lattice-gauge-theory claim
supported by an infrared imaging atlas of active galactic nuclei, a
polariton quasinormal-mode prediction supported by a machine-learning
paper on extreme multi-label classification, and a non-Abelian gauge
argument supported by a paper on magnetic-resonance coil design. One
background-field table disagreed with the current solver output on
every platform it tabulated, in one entry by more than a factor of
four, and its inline scattering length had been superseded by a later
measurement. Two theorems cited as verified had the trivially true
proposition as their stated conclusion, with the physically
meaningful hypotheses never used in the proof. A construction was
attributed throughout one draft to a physicist who had no part in it,
while the companion draft named the correct authors.

\subsection{Why the context must be fresh}

The reviewer is a language-model agent invoked against the manuscript
with no shared state: no memory of the author's earlier reasoning, no
access to the discussion in which a contested phrasing was settled,
no knowledge of which claims were already defended. That property is
the whole mechanism, and everything else in this section is
machinery around it.

A reviewer that shares the author's context has already accepted the
author's framing, and its findings are then bounded by what that
framing admits. The specific failure is not laziness but agreement:
the justification that made a sentence acceptable while writing it is
still available, still persuasive, and still absent from the page the
referee will read. A fresh-context reviewer has access to the
artifact and to the primary sources, and to nothing else. It is
therefore not persuadable by an argument that was never written down.

Three standing instructions protect the property from erosion. The
reviewer does not defer to author intent: prose implying a conclusion
the artifacts do not support is an overclaim whatever the author
meant. The reviewer does not pattern-match on previously observed
failures, because a reviewer optimising for last time's defect is a
reviewer that has acquired the memory the design removed; each pass
scans the manuscript in class order against the current state of the
knowledge graph. And the reviewer does not implement fixes. Its
output is findings; the author remediates and re-invokes. Separating
the two roles is the reason the agent exists, and an agent that
edits what it judges has resumed authorship of the thing it is
judging.

\subsection{The eight finding classes}

The reviewer works a fixed roster of eight classes, in order, one
class at a time, and does not batch findings across classes. Each
class maps to exactly one readiness gate, and that gate's pass
criteria define what correctness means for the class, so a finding is
never an aesthetic judgement. Table~\ref{tab:finding_classes} gives
the roster, the gate each class backstops, and the evidence the class
requires.

Three of the eleven gates have no finding class of their own.
Computation correctness is decided mechanically from the kind of
assertion each test makes about a formula, and a bounds-only test
cannot discharge it. Fix propagation is decided from the open or
closed state of the findings themselves. First-claim verification has
no mechanical evaluator at all, and the prior-art search inside class
five is the only thing that feeds it; a priority claim is verified by
a person or it is not verified.

Two protocol details in that roster carry more weight than their
length suggests. Citation verification consults a cache of previous
fetches, and the cache amortises cost without licensing a skip: any
entry whose recorded verification is stale, or whose bibliographic
text has changed since it was recorded, is fetched again. And a fetch
that a network policy refuses is recorded as a failed fetch, never as
a null result. Not being able to look and finding nothing are
opposite outcomes, and collapsing them is how a priority claim
acquires false support. The same rule governs every unknown the
reviewer meets: an unreadable file or an empty graph query is emitted
as a finding, because a silent skip is indistinguishable from a pass.

\subsection{From finding to gate}

Each invocation writes one report for one manuscript, with one
section per finding. A finding states its severity, the gate it
belongs to, the file and line it was observed at, what was observed,
the evidence, what was expected, and the mechanical step that would
remediate it. Severity is a declared field taking one of four values,
not a glyph to be read off a heading. The distinction is
load-bearing: severity determines the closure bar, and a severity
inferred from decoration can be changed without leaving a trace.
Declaration is enforced by
\texttt{validate.py --check review\_severity\_declared}.

Reports are parsed on the next knowledge-graph build into finding
nodes with edges to the manuscripts they flag, and a blocking finding
flips its gate to blocked. The aggregate verdict is recomputed by
\texttt{validate.py --check readiness\_submission\_gate}. A
submission-blocking finding is thereby a state of the graph rather
than a note in a document, which is what makes it survive the session
that produced it.

\subsection{A reviewer may not close its own findings}

Every finding is born open, at every severity, and the only thing
that can transition one is a record in the supersession ledger at
\texttt{docs/review\_finding\_supersessions.json}. Wording a heading
as resolved does nothing. This matters because the natural
alternative, letting a review document report which of the previous
findings are now fixed, gives the reviewing channel the power to
close the findings it raises, and that power was repeatedly exercised
by accident: a re-review that reconstructs an earlier report can mint
every finding in it as closed, including the ones its own body
describes as partially addressed.

The ledger is deliberately a narrow channel. A record names the
finding, an explicit closing status, the review or close-out document
that confirmed the fix, a written rationale, and a date. Closing a
blocking finding additionally requires that the rationale be
substantive rather than a restatement, and that the record carry a
commit or a date as its provenance anchor; a record meeting neither
condition leaves the finding open, whatever status it declares.
Accepting a blocking finding rather than fixing it is a decision that
must be argued in writing, which
\texttt{validate.py --check accepted\_findings\_carry\_rationale}
enforces. A separate guard checks that every ledger record names a
finding that actually exists, since a closure filed against a
nonexistent identifier closes nothing while appearing to close
something.

The author's assertion that a finding is fixed is not evidence for
the gate. The re-invocation is the evidence: the gate reopens only
when a fresh pass over the same class returns no blocking finding.

\subsection{Absence of findings is not evidence of review}

A readiness verdict computed only from open findings cannot
distinguish a manuscript that was reviewed and passed from one that
was never reviewed, because both have none. The verdict therefore
requires a recorded review as a third condition alongside zero
blocking findings and a bounded advisory count, and manuscripts with
no blocking findings and no recorded review occupy an explicit
unreviewed class of their own. Review-recordedness resolves against
the manuscript's own metadata, or else against a dated review
document on disk; a review scoped to one section does not satisfy a
whole-manuscript requirement.

The pattern extends from a single manuscript to a multi-source
manuscript without changing its semantics. A review brief names every
contributing draft; the reviewer walks each of them plus the
assembled manuscript, and carries a per-manuscript anchor list of the
load-bearing claims and citations that pass must verify. Manuscripts
of different kinds get different profiles: a methodology manuscript
is held to the standard that every worked case trace to a
reproducible run identifier or a pinned counterexample, which is the
standard the three cases of Sections~\ref{sec:case1}
to~\ref{sec:case3} are written against. Agreement between manuscripts
that share a claim is checked deterministically rather than by the
reviewer, as described in Section~\ref{sec:provenance}.

\subsection{What the pattern still does not catch}

The citation machinery described above validates citation
\emph{metadata}. A remediation pass in June 2026 identified a failure
class that metadata validation cannot reach by construction: a
citation can be entirely metadata-valid, with a resolving identifier,
a cached primary source and correct bibliographic data, while the
claim the prose attributes to it is fabricated. Three instances
turned up in roughly ten spot checks of load-bearing attributions. In
the first, a quantitative susceptibility range was attributed to a
cached source that is a unitarity proof in a lattice-gravity
framework and contains no such analysis. In the second, an
observational exclusion was attributed at the wrong observables, the
wrong significance and the wrong year relative to the cached paper.
In the third, the citation registry's own notes carried a fabricated
title and wrong journal coordinates for a real paper whose actual
framing is conditional rather than a no-go. All three passed every
metadata-level check, because the sources are real, cached and
correctly identified. Only the attributed content was wrong.

Part of the response is deterministic. One check scans every draft
for axiom-count claims and fails any present-tense claim that
disagrees with the machine-generated count. A second requires every
identifier-shaped reference in monospaced prose to resolve to a live
declaration in the Lean dependency export, with a per-instance waiver
mechanism for deliberate references to declarations that no longer
exist. A third covers declaration names that embed an author and a
year: when prose cites such a declaration, the citing manuscript's
bibliography must contain the matching entry, so that a reader can
follow the citation the name encodes.

Deterministic checks guard presence and consistency; only reading the
source against the sentence verifies support. That is a content-level
attribution sweep, and it is the pattern applied one level deeper.
For every load-bearing pairing of a citation with the claim it
supports, a fresh-context agent reads the cached primary source and
issues a dated verdict: supported; supported with a caveat, which the
manuscript must then carry; not supported, in which case prose and
registry are corrected and a ledger record is written; or source
missing, in which case the source is fetched and the verdict
deferred. Each visit pairs the content pass with a fresh-context
re-invocation of the reviewer, takes falsification-critical claims
first and the overview manuscript last so that it inherits its
companions' corrections.

The base rate that motivated the sweep, of order three fabricated
attributions per ten load-bearing pairs spot-checked, is exactly the
kind of number a verification programme has to be willing to publish
about itself. It says that the metadata layer this programme had been
treating as citation integrity is necessary and not sufficient, and
that the fresh-context property buys nothing unless the reviewer is
also required to open the source.

The sweep itself is the better-powered measurement, and it is a
weaker number, so we report it beside the pilot rather than in place
of it. It ran on 2026-06-10 across eighteen manuscripts, with
per-manuscript populations of load-bearing citation--claim pairs
ranging from three to twenty-three and totalling one hundred and
thirty-four where the record states a count. Six of those pairs were
fabrication-class: a method attributed to a paper that does not
describe it, and the same paper credited with a four-orders-of-magnitude
comparison it does not make; a log-coefficient given with the wrong
value; a lattice crossover temperature attributed to the wrong one of
two lattice papers; and an exact ancilla-free construction credited to
a magic-state distillation paper rather than to the textbook that
contains it. That is a rate near one in twenty, not near one in three.
Two readings are available and we cannot separate them: either the
pilot's ten were not a random sample (they were chosen because they
looked load-bearing, which is also what makes an error costly), or
the corrections the pilot triggered had already removed the densest
part of the population before the sweep ran. Either way the pilot was
right about the failure class and wrong about its size, and a
programme that publishes the alarm owes the reader the follow-up.
Neither figure covers this manuscript: I1 is listed in the sweep's
priority tranche and has no entry in its record.

\section{Mathematics the Pipeline Accumulated as a Byproduct}
\label{sec:phase6f_substrate}

A verification programme pointed at physics accumulates mathematics the
physics does not contain, and the accumulation is not incidental: it
happens precisely at the boundary of what the ambient library already
proves. Neither of the two layers described here was a goal. Each exists
because some physics result needed a statement the library did not have,
and the programme's rule is that a needed statement is proved rather
than assumed. Both build with no unproved gap and no project-local
axiom, and no declaration in either is registered as a placeholder.

What the library supplies, and where it stops, is checkable against the
pinned revision (\mathlibrev{}). It reaches smooth manifolds, tangent
and vector bundles, a covariant-derivative and torsion interface, and a
positive-definite Riemannian-metric class. It has no Riemann, Ricci or
scalar curvature of a connection anywhere in the manifold hierarchy; no
Bianchi identity; no indefinite-signature metric, the positive-definite
fibre inner product being structural rather than a parameter; a
tensoriality interface with constructors for one and two arguments, so
the three-argument curvature map has no bundled form to be constructed
into; and the Leibniz form of the Jacobi identity for the manifold Lie
bracket but not the cyclic form, which is the form the Bianchi
identities consume.

Two layers follow from that boundary. The first states the classical
identities over component arrays
$\mathrm{Fin}\,4 \to \cdots \to \mathbb{R}$, which is in any case the
form in which a physicist reads them, and is where the physics results
of this programme actually draw. The second asks whether the same
identities can be stated at connection level, where the objects are
sections of a vector bundle; it is the larger body of reusable
mathematics and carries no physics content of its own. A companion
manuscript in preparation treats both as its primary subject.

\subsection{Component scope: the classical identities}

\phaseSixfWonesixTotal{}~theorems across six modules. This subsection
records what each layer proves and which later result consumes it. The
connection-level layer is
Section~\ref{sec:riemannian_sidebar}.

Notation, since these modules borrow two letters twice over. In the
curvature and exact-solution layers $K$ is a sectional-curvature
constant and $g_{\mu\nu}$ the metric; in the slice-data layer
$K_{ij}$ is the extrinsic curvature of a spatial slice, $\mathrm{tr}\,K$
its trace, ${}^{(3)}\!R$ the intrinsic scalar curvature of that slice,
and $\rho$ the matter energy density. The two uses of $K$ are
unrelated and are kept apart by their index structure.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Curvature algebra} (\texttt{Curvature.lean},
\curvatureThms{}~theorems; \texttt{EinsteinTensor.lean},
\einsteinTensorThms{}): the three algebraic properties a Riemann
tensor inherits from its underlying connection are carried as
separate predicates, \texttt{AntisymLastTwo} (any linear
connection), \texttt{FirstBianchi} (torsion-free), and
\texttt{AntisymPair12} (metric-compatible, on the index-lowered
tensor), so that the derivation visibly uses each. The headline is
the pair-symmetry $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$
(\texttt{pair\_symmetry\_lowered}), through four cyclic Bianchi
instances, with Ricci symmetry as its corollary.
Non-vacuity is established from both sides: a constant-sectional
witness gives $\mathrm{Ric}_{\sigma\nu} = 3K g_{\sigma\nu}$ and
diagonal trace $12K$, exhibiting the dimension factors $n-1$ and
$n(n-1)$ at $n = 4$ (\texttt{constantSectional\_Ricci\_eq},
\texttt{constantSectional\_diag\_trace\_eq}), and an explicit
one-component tensor refutes the Bianchi predicate
(\texttt{nonBianchiTensor\_violates\_FirstBianchi}). On top of this
sits the Einstein tensor $G_{\mu\nu} := \mathrm{Ric}_{\mu\nu} -
\tfrac{1}{2}R\,g_{\mu\nu}$, with the dimension-4 trace identity
$G^{\mu}{}_{\mu} = -R$
(\texttt{einsteinTensor\_trace\_eq\_neg\_scalar}), the vacuum
characterization $G = 0 \iff \mathrm{Ric} = 0$
(\texttt{einsteinTensor\_zero\_iff\_ricci\_zero}), and the
$\Lambda$-vacuum biconditional $G + \Lambda g = 0 \iff \Lambda = 3K$
on the constant-$K$ witness
(\texttt{constantSectional\_lambda\_vacuum\_iff}), whose forward
direction needs one non-degenerate metric component and uses it.

\item \textbf{Energy conditions} (\texttt{EnergyConditions.lean},
\energyConditionsThms{}~theorems): the null, weak, dominant and
strong conditions as predicates on a stress-energy bilinear form in
signature $(-,+,+,+)$, with the causal-vector restrictions
load-bearing rather than decorative (dropping the timelike
restriction from the weak condition would turn it into
positive-semidefiniteness, false in any nontrivial Lorentzian
theory). The implication chain from dominant to weak to null ships
with its continuity hypothesis exposed in the statement rather than
assumed silently, since null vectors are limits of timelike ones
(\texttt{WEC\_implies\_NEC\_under\_continuity}). The separations are
witnessed concretely: a cosmological-constant fluid satisfies the
weak and null conditions and violates the strong one for $\Lambda
> 0$ (\texttt{cosmologicalLambda\_violates\_SEC}); a stiff fluid at
$\rho = 1$, $p = 2$ violates the dominant condition on the explicit
pair $v = (1, 9/10, 0, 0)$, $w = (1, -9/10, 0, 0)$
(\texttt{stiff\_fluid\_violates\_DEC}); a ghost scalar violates the
null condition (\texttt{ghostScalar\_violates\_NEC}).

\item \textbf{Exact solutions} (\texttt{ExactSolutions.lean},
\exactSolutionsThms{}~theorems, over \texttt{KerrSchild.lean}): four
canonical solutions as named consumers of the two layers above.
Minkowski is the unique $\Lambda = 0$ member of the constant-$K$
family (\texttt{minkowski\_lambda\_zero\_iff\_K\_zero}); the
$\Lambda$-vacuum biconditional specialized to the Minkowski carrier
gives $\Lambda = 3K$ in the sign-agnostic form shared by de Sitter
and anti-de Sitter (\texttt{deSitter\_lambda\_vacuum\_iff}), with
$\Lambda = -3/\ell^2$ as the anti-de Sitter reading
(\texttt{ads\_lambda\_eq\_neg\_three\_over\_ell\_sq}). Schwarzschild
enters in Kerr--Schild form $g = \eta + (2M/r)\,l \otimes l$: the
horizon is characterized as the unique radius at which the scalar
reaches unity (\texttt{schwarzschild\_horizon\_iff}), and three
theorems fix the sign of $g_{tt}$ outside, on, and inside it, the
last of which
(\texttt{schwarzschild\_g\_tt\_inside\_horizon\_pos}) is the
character flip of the $t$-direction. The Kerr--Schild inverse is
worth one sentence of method: the naive rank-one form
$g^{-1} = \eta - \varphi\, l \otimes l$ is false, and what ships is
the Sherman--Morrison form with the index raised through $\eta$
(\texttt{raiseIndex}, \texttt{ks\_inverse\_formula}).

\item \textbf{The $3+1$ split and the tetrad correspondence}
(\texttt{ADMFormalism.lean}, \admFormalismThms{}~theorems;
\texttt{TetradFormalism.lean}, \tetradFormalismThms{}): the
Arnowitt--Deser--Misner constraints are carried as algebraic
functions of the slice data. In units $G = 1$ the Hamiltonian
constraint $H = {}^{(3)}\!R + (\mathrm{tr}\,K)^2 - K_{ij}K^{ij} -
16\pi\rho$ has a vacuum biconditional
(\texttt{hamiltonianConstraint\_vacuum\_iff}) and, at a moment of
time symmetry, collapses to ${}^{(3)}\!R = 16\pi\rho$
(\texttt{hamiltonianConstraint\_moment\_of\_time\_symmetry\_iff}),
which is the algebraic statement behind Yamabe positivity of the
slice at non-negative density; the momentum constraint's vacuum form
says the trace-free extrinsic curvature is divergence-free
(\texttt{momentumConstraint\_vacuum\_iff}). Flat-slicing de Sitter
data, $K_{ij} = -H\gamma_{ij}$ and ${}^{(3)}\!R = 0$ against
$\rho_\Lambda = \Lambda/8\pi$, recovers $\Lambda = 3H^2$ from the
constraint alone
(\texttt{deSitter\_flat\_slicing\_hamiltonian\_iff}), which is the
same identity the exact-solution layer obtained from the field
equations. On the tetrad side, the induced metric $g_{\mu\nu} =
\eta_{ab}e^a{}_\mu e^b{}_\nu$ (\texttt{tetradInducedMetric}) is
symmetric by construction
(\texttt{tetradInducedMetric\_symm}), the Kronecker tetrad returns
$\eta$ exactly
(\texttt{minkowskiTetrad\_induces\_minkowski\_metric}), and the
reduction of the tetrad formulation to the metric one is stated as
a biconditional in the Einstein--Cartan parameter,
vanishing residual if and only if $\alpha_{\mathrm{EC}} = 1$
(\texttt{tetrad\_levi\_civita\_iff\_alpha\_unity}).
\end{itemize}

The layering is real rather than nominal, and it is checkable by
following the calls. Within the layer, the Einstein tensor is
built from the curvature module's Ricci and scalar operators, the
exact solutions specialize the Einstein-tensor biconditionals to the
Minkowski carrier, and the slice-data module reaches back into the
exact solutions to identify the Arnowitt--Deser--Misner mass with
half the horizon radius
(\texttt{schwarzschild\_adm\_mass\_eq\_half\_horizon\_radius}).
Outside it, the horizon-area monotonicity result rewrites through
the closed form $A = 16\pi M^2$ proved here
(\texttt{schwarzschild\_area\_eq\_16pi\_M\_sq} consumed by
\texttt{schwarzschild\_area\_monotone}); the black-hole uniqueness
module reads its horizon radius and area from the same definitions
(\texttt{kerr\_family\_schwarzschild\_horizon\_radius},
\texttt{kerr\_family\_schwarzschild\_area}); and the singularity
work discharges the statement that a positive cosmological constant
evades the Hawking--Penrose hypothesis by applying this layer's
strong-energy counterexample to the hypothesis bundle, rather than
re-deriving the algebra
(\texttt{cosmologicalLambda\_violates\_HP\_hypothesis} calling
\texttt{cosmologicalLambda\_violates\_SEC}).

Three limits are worth stating, because each one is a place where a
statement could have been manufactured and was not. The vacuum-Ricci
verification for Schwarzschild is absent: it needs a Lorentzian
metric and curvature built from a connection, and a tracked
hypothesis asserting it would have been discharged trivially at this
level, so the statement waits for the infrastructure rather than
appearing as a proposition with nothing behind it. The contracted
second Bianchi identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ is absent for the
same reason, a covariant divergence not being modelled here. The
Cartan structure equations are absent because manifold-level
differential forms are, so the tetrad module's torsion enters as an
external amplitude and its physical content is carried by the
Einstein--Cartan biconditional instead. Relatedly, the Schwarzschild
thermodynamic identities in this layer, $\kappa \cdot 4M = 1$, $2\pi
T_H = \kappa$, $A = 16\pi M^2$, and $S_{\mathrm{BH}} = 4\pi M^2$,
fix the closed forms of named quantities and their mutual
consistency; they do not derive the Hawking temperature from a field
calculation, and the de Sitter analogue
(\texttt{deSitter\_T\_H\_eq\_kappa\_over\_2pi}) has exactly the same
standing.

\subsection{Connection scope: curvature above the covariant-derivative interface}
\label{sec:riemannian_sidebar}

The connection-level layer closes the gap named above at two scopes: a
coordinate scope on Christoffel arrays, and a bundle scope built
directly on the upstream covariant-derivative predicate.

\subsubsection{What the upstream interface supplies}

The connection itself is upstream. The module
\texttt{Mathlib.Geometry.Manifold.VectorBundle.CovariantDerivative}
supplies the predicate
\texttt{IsCovariantDerivativeOn}, which asks that a map from sections of
a bundle $V \to M$ to sections of $\mathrm{Hom}(TM, V)$ be additive and
satisfy the Leibniz rule at points where the sections are
differentiable, together with a bundled covariant derivative and
a torsion tensor with its antisymmetry and vanishing-diagonal lemmas.
A companion file in the same directory supplies metric compatibility,
but only for bundles carrying a positive-definite fibre inner product.
The Riemannian typeclass it consumes
(\texttt{Mathlib.Geometry.Manifold.VectorBundle.Riemannian}) is fixed to
a real inner-product space on each fibre. The manifold Lie bracket lives
in \texttt{Mathlib.Geometry.Manifold.VectorField.LieBracket}.

Four things are absent at that revision, and each is checkable by a
referee against the pinned commit. There is no Riemann, Ricci or scalar
curvature of a connection anywhere in the manifold hierarchy. There is
no indefinite-signature metric, because the positive-definite fibre
inner product is structural rather than a parameter. The tensoriality
interface provides constructors for one and two arguments only, so the
three-argument curvature map has no bundled form available to be
constructed into. And the Lie-bracket file carries the Leibniz form of
the Jacobi identity but not the cyclic form, which is the form the
Bianchi identities consume.

\subsubsection{Coordinate scope}

Two modules work on Christoffel arrays
$\Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu} : \mathrm{Fin}\,4 \to \mathrm{Fin}\,4 \to
\mathrm{Fin}\,4 \to \mathbb{R}$ with the metric partials passed as
explicit input data. \texttt{RiemannCoordinate.lean} carries the full
coordinate Riemann tensor as the sum of its linear and quadratic pieces,
\begin{equation}
R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}
  = \partial_{\mu}\Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\nu\sigma}
  - \partial_{\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\mu\sigma}
  + \Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}{}_{\nu\sigma}
  - \Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\sigma},
\label{eq:riem-coord}
\end{equation}
with antisymmetry in the last index pair and the algebraic first Bianchi
identity. That identity is not an unconditional fact about
\eqref{eq:riem-coord}: \texttt{riemannCoord\_FirstBianchi} consumes one
torsion-freeness hypothesis on $\Gamma$ and a second on its partial
array, and the quadratic half alone
(\texttt{riemannQuadraticTerm\_AntisymLastTwo} and its Bianchi
companion) already requires the first of the two. Lowering the tensor
and combining with the pair symmetry of the algebraic substrate yields
$R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ through
\texttt{riemannCoord\_pair\_symmetry\_lowered}, which calls
\texttt{firstBianchi\_lift} and \texttt{pair\_symmetry\_lowered} in the
substrate module rather than restating them. The Koszul closed form for
the Levi-Civita Christoffel symbols ships as \texttt{christoffelKoszul},
and \texttt{christoffelKoszul\_isTorsionFree} proves it symmetric in its
lower pair under symmetry of the metric partials. That hypothesis is
load-bearing rather than decorative: dropping it leaves a connection
with torsion.

\texttt{RiemannDifferentialBianchi.lean} adds the second-derivative
carrier with an explicit Schwarz-symmetry predicate and the covariant
derivative of a generic $(1,3)$-tensor. Two pieces of the differential
second Bianchi identity are discharged there. The linear piece cancels
under Schwarz symmetry alone
(\texttt{riemannLinearTerm\_partial\_DifferentialBianchi}), and the
lower-index correction terms cancel under torsion-freeness together with
antisymmetry of the tensor in its last pair
(\texttt{covRiemann\_groupCD\_cyclic\_zero}). The bilinear and cubic
pieces are not discharged at this scope. They are tractable and
uninteresting: several hundred normalised monomials, against three lines
at bundle scope once the cyclic Jacobi identity is available.

\subsubsection{Indefinite signature}

\texttt{LorentzianMetric.lean} supplies the signature layer the upstream
Riemannian typeclass cannot host. \texttt{IsLorentzianSignature} pins the
canonical diagonal form $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$, and
\texttt{IsLorentzianMetric} bundles it with symmetry; the Minkowski
metric is the witness, discharged by calling the existing component
lemmas of the linearised-field-equation module rather than recomputing
them. The scope limit is worth stating plainly. This is the
canonical-frame predicate, not a signature theory: a classification of
general indefinite forms would need Sylvester's law of inertia at
bilinear-form level, which is not available upstream. What the predicate
does support is a falsifier,
\texttt{riemannian\_not\_lorentzian\_signature}, which derives a
contradiction from a metric asserted to have both signatures, and a
fibrewise family predicate shaped to mirror the upstream Riemannian
bundle typeclass.

\subsubsection{Bundle scope}

\texttt{BundleRiemannAux.lean} defines the curvature operator on a vector
bundle over a manifold over a nontrivially normed field, in the textbook
form
\begin{equation}
R(X, Y)Z = \nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z - \nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z
           - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z,
\label{eq:bundle-riem}
\end{equation}
unrolled into the upstream argument order. It is a bare function rather
than a bundled fibrewise-trilinear map, and that is a consequence of the
missing three-argument tensoriality constructor noted above, not a design
preference. Its structural lemmas need less than a connection.
\texttt{bundleRiemannAux\_self} and
\texttt{bundleRiemannAux\_swap\_apply} follow from bracket
antisymmetry and from continuous linearity of the direction slot alone,
with no covariant-derivative hypothesis anywhere in their statements;
\texttt{bundleRiemannAux\_commuting\_brackets} reduces
\eqref{eq:bundle-riem} to the pure connection commutator when the
bracket vanishes at the point, which is the case a coordinate
calculation actually meets. The degeneracies in the section slot and in
the $X$ slot are where \texttt{IsCovariantDerivativeOn} first enters.

The cyclic Jacobi identity for the manifold Lie bracket ships as
\texttt{mlieBracket\_cyclic\_jacobi\_apply}, derived from the upstream
Leibniz form by one outer antisymmetry rewrite and one negation
distribution, the latter shipped alongside as
\texttt{mlieBracket\_neg\_right\_apply}. On top of it,
\texttt{bundleRiemannAux\_first\_bianchi\_torsionfree\_apply} proves the
algebraic first Bianchi identity on the tangent bundle from
torsion-freeness in the upstream sense plus additivity of the connection
in its section argument. A second form of the same theorem removes the
abstract additivity hypothesis by consuming
\texttt{IsCovariantDerivativeOn} directly, at the cost of six pointwise
differentiability hypotheses on the section pairs the proof forms. Two
root-namespace helpers written in upstream style,
\texttt{IsCovariantDerivativeOn.neg} and
\texttt{IsCovariantDerivativeOn.sub}, carry that discharge.

\subsubsection{Levi-Civita uniqueness, and the hypothesis it carries}

\texttt{LeviCivita.lean} states the classical uniqueness theorem for the
torsion-free metric-compatible connection at bundle scope, which is the
fundamental theorem of Riemannian geometry, in the form given by
Wald~\cite[Theorem 3.1.1]{Wald1984} and, in the pure-mathematics
setting, by Kobayashi and Nomizu~\cite[Theorem IV.2.2]{KobayashiNomizu1963}. The predicate
\texttt{IsLeviCivita} has four fields: the connection is a covariant
derivative in the upstream sense, it is torsion-free, the bilinear form
is symmetric, and the connection annihilates that form. The fourth field
carries its own differentiability side conditions inline, so the
predicate does not depend on a particular smoothness scaffolding.
From it, \texttt{koszul\_identity\_of\_isLeviCivita} derives
\begin{align}
2\,g(\nabla_{X}Y, Z) ={}& X g(Y,Z) + Y g(Z,X) - Z g(X,Y) \nonumber \\
 & + g([X,Y], Z) - g([X,Z], Y) - g([Y,Z], X),
\label{eq:koszul}
\end{align}
whose right-hand side does not mention the connection. The algebraic
kernel \texttt{leviCivita\_pointwise\_unique\_of\_koszul} converts that
into pointwise equality of two connections, using non-degeneracy of the
form at the point to realise an arbitrary
tangent vector as the value of some section. Their composition,
\texttt{leviCivita\_unique\_of\_isLeviCivita}, closes the argument under
$2 \neq 0$ in the base field, which the derivation genuinely needs: the
factor of two in \eqref{eq:koszul} is what the cancellation removes.

One feature of the composed statement should be read directly rather
than inferred, and we state it because a reader assessing what has been
proved needs it. Because the kernel needs \eqref{eq:koszul} for every
triple of test sections, the composition quantifies its differentiability
hypothesis over every triple as well, in a single blanket conjunction.
Sections here are arbitrary dependent functions carrying no smoothness
constraint, so on a manifold admitting one section that is not
differentiable at the point, that conjunction is false and the composed
statement holds vacuously. The per-triple form in
\texttt{koszul\_identity\_of\_isLeviCivita} does not have this shape, and
it, together with the algebraic kernel, is where the mathematical content
of the derivation sits. The two statements should be weighed separately.

\subsubsection{What is claimed}

These modules build with no \texttt{sorry} and introduce no new
project-local axiom. Together they carry \phaseSixfWsevenTotal{}
theorem-level declarations at the signature and coordinate-connection
layer, and \phaseSixfWeightTotal{} across the coordinate Riemann,
differential Bianchi, bundle curvature, bundle Bianchi and Levi-Civita
modules (\riemannCoordinateThms{}, \riemannDifferentialBianchiThms{},
\bundleRiemannAuxThms{}, \bundleRiemannThms{} and \leviCivitaThms{}
respectively). A companion manuscript in preparation treats the
construction as its primary subject; the account here is sized to the
methodological point.

The novelty claim is deliberately narrow. At the pinned revision, no
counterpart to any of these identities exists in the upstream library,
and that is a statement a referee can check. A survey of the Isabelle
Archive of Formal Proofs found none there either: its manifold entry
stops at partitions of unity and the tangent and cotangent bundles, and
its relativity entries are order-theoretic axiomatisations carrying no
metric tensor. We make no claim about proof assistants beyond those two.
A systematic search of the other major libraries was not carried out,
and an unsearched library is not an empty one. That is the
distinction this manuscript spends Section~\ref{sec:adversarial}
insisting on, and we decline to violate it here.
The modules follow upstream file, naming and section conventions with an
eventual contribution in view. That is an intention about future work and
not a claim about what has been accepted anywhere.

The methodological point is the one this paper can support on its own
evidence. A verification pipeline pointed at physics accumulates
mathematics that the physics does not contain, and the accumulation is
not incidental: it happens precisely at the boundary of what the upstream
library already proves. The discipline that keeps the accumulation honest
is visible in the statements above. Each identity names the hypothesis it
consumes, torsion-freeness, Schwarz symmetry, non-degeneracy, or
invertibility of two, rather than importing it silently from the physical
setting in which the identity is usually met.

\section{Worked Case~4: Scoping a Result Around Absent Library Infrastructure}
\label{sec:structural_prop_scoping}


Formalization in an applied domain runs ahead of its library. A
proof assistant offers three responses when a target theorem rests
on mathematics the ambient library does not contain, and only one
of them preserves what makes a formal development worth reading.
The first response is to rebuild the missing theory inside the
project. The second is to admit the gap and continue. The third is
to name the missing statement as an explicit proposition, prove
everything the project can prove relative to that name, and record
the name as an upstream target. We take the third. The property
being protected is that the status of every claim can be read off
its statement, by a reader who does not trust the surrounding
prose.

Four states are available to a claim in our substrate, and the
manuscript keeps them apart by name.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Proved outright:} the statement is closed against
    the kernel with no project-supplied stand-in. Hypotheses may
    appear, but each one constrains the data the statement is about.
  \item \textbf{Proved modulo an explicit gating proposition:} the
    statement is closed, but one of its hypotheses is a named
    proposition that the project does not prove and does not intend
    to prove locally. The theorem is conditional, and is reported as
    conditional wherever it is used.
  \item \textbf{Declared without content:} a predicate has been given
    a name and a docstring, but its definition is the trivially true
    proposition. Any theorem concluding it is an architectural
    marker. It fixes the point at which a future proof attaches and
    carries no mathematical weight.
  \item \textbf{Admitted:} the proof is stubbed. The build rejects
    this, and the state does not occur in the substrate described
    here.
\end{itemize}

The line between the first two states is not the presence of
hypotheses. A regularity assumption on a function is part of what
the theorem says; a gating proposition is a different object,
standing in for a theorem someone else will prove. Conflating them
is what lets a conditional result be quoted as an unconditional one.

The one-dimensional wave equation is the worked case
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/WaveEquation1D.lean}). The target is
$\partial_t^2 u = c^2\, \partial_x^2 u$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, encoded by
the predicate \texttt{IsClassicalWaveSolution} as an equality of
iterated single-variable derivatives at every point, which agrees
with the classical equation on twice continuously differentiable
fields. Three results are proved outright. Two exhibit explicit
solutions, the constant field
(\texttt{const\_isClassicalWaveSolution}) and the linear travelling
wave $u(t,x) = a(x - ct) + b$
(\texttt{linearTraveling\_isClassicalWaveSolution}), so the predicate
is inhabited and is inhabited non-constantly. The third is linearity
(\texttt{add\_isClassicalWaveSolution}): the sum of two solutions is
a solution, given that each summand is differentiable in each
variable separately and that each of its first partial derivatives
is differentiable in the corresponding variable, at every point.
Those eight hypotheses are conditions on the data, so the theorem
belongs to the first state above, not the second.

What the same module does not prove is equally on the record. The
reduction of the Einstein vacuum equations in harmonic gauge to a
quasilinear hyperbolic system, whose principal part is the wave
operator~\cite{FouresBruhat1952,FriedrichRendall2000}, motivates the
choice of target; it is not itself a formalized step, and no declaration
in the substrate asserts it. The
predicate \texttt{IsHarmonicGauge}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/CauchyProblem.lean}) reserves a name for
the gauge condition, and as currently written every chart satisfies
it. \texttt{IsLocallyWellPosed}, in the same module, is defined to be
the trivially true proposition. Consequently
\texttt{waveSolution\_implies\_locally\_well\_posed} is an
architectural marker in the third state: it records where the
eventual well-posedness argument attaches to the concrete solution
theory, and it proves nothing about the Einstein equations. No
theorem in the substrate consumes \texttt{IsLocallyWellPosed} as a
hypothesis, which is the honest reading of a predicate that is
currently vacuous. The apex content of this module is the solution
theory, and the scoping decision is that the missing partial
differential equation machinery, local existence for quasilinear
hyperbolic systems and the energy estimates it rests on, is named
rather than rebuilt.

The lifecycle of a gating proposition is visible in the curve-level
focusing results. The comparison bound
$\theta(\lambda) \le \theta_0 / (1 + \theta_0\lambda/3)$, in which
$\theta$ is the expansion of a null congruence and $\lambda$ its affine
parameter (unrelated to the quartic coupling of
Section~\ref{sec:case3}), first entered
the development as a field of the hypothesis bundle
\texttt{IsCurveTheoreticPenroseHypothesis}, that is, in the second
state, with the intended route recorded as the library's ordinary
differential equation comparison interface. It was later proved
outright as \texttt{riccati\_comparison\_on\_focal\_window}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/RiccatiComparison.lean}) from the
Raychaudhuri hypothesis bundle \texttt{IsRaychaudhuriCurve}, by a
different route: the reciprocal of the expansion has derivative at
least $1/3$ on the focal window, and the mean-value inequality on a
convex domain, already present in the library, converts that into the
comparison. The bridge
\texttt{isRaychaudhuriCurve\_toCurveTheoreticPenroseHypothesis} then
discharges the former gating field, leaving the composition
conditional only on data hypotheses. Nothing had to be unpicked when
the route was found, because no partial reimplementation had been
built underneath the name. The area-evolution result
\texttt{horizon\_area\_curve\_monotone}
(\texttt{lean/SKEFTHawking/AreaTheoremCurveTheoretic.lean}) is proved
outright by the same economy: the evolution equation
$A' = \theta A$ and non-negativity of $\theta$ and $A$ give a
non-negative derivative, and monotonicity follows from the library's
monotonicity-from-derivative lemma.

The discipline's cost is that a name can look like a result. That
risk is not hypothetical in this development. The conclusion
\texttt{IsNullGeodesicallyIncomplete} carried by the curve-level
composition is defined as positivity of the focal parameter, so the
mathematical weight of that chain sits in the comparison bound and in
the strict negativity of the expansion on the focal window, not in
the word incompleteness. Three controls are meant to bound the risk: a
predicate whose definition is the trivially true proposition is
registered in \texttt{PLACEHOLDER\_THEOREMS}
(\texttt{src/core/constants.py}) with the claim it stands for and what
a real proof would require; a registered name is excluded from theorem
totals; and a registered name is not cited in a paper as a result.
The first of those is a hand-maintained registry rather than a
derivation, and on inspection it does not have the coverage the other
two controls assume. Seven definitions in the substrate unfold to the
trivially true proposition: \texttt{IsLocallyWellPosed} and
\texttt{IsBelRobinsonEnergyFinite} in the Cauchy-problem module,
\texttt{IsStationaryAxisymmetricVacuumBH}, and two pairs named
\texttt{hasCTPStructure} and \texttt{hasHermiticity} in the
Schwinger--Keldysh axiom modules. None of the seven is registered, and
the registry's entries are a different family of names. The seventh
was missed on a first pass because its definition splits \texttt{:
Prop :=} and \texttt{True} across two lines, which a line-oriented
scan does not see. That is the same shape of measurement error this
section is about. Two
consequences follow directly. Markers concluding with these predicates
sit inside the substantive theorem count rather than outside it, and
the check meant to enforce the third control reads the registry, so it
cannot see them either. The last pair is the one worth flagging: they
are fields of the six-condition skeleton \texttt{SKEFTAxioms} that
Section~\ref{sec:case1} works through, so two of that structure's six
conditions constrain nothing. We state the gap rather than the intent,
because a control assumed complete is worth less than one whose
coverage is known; closing it means deriving the registry from the
definitions instead of maintaining it by hand.
Against
those costs, local reimplementation carries an unbounded one. A
partial reimplementation that downstream theorems already consume
rots asymmetrically once the library ships the real thing, because
the downstream statements are then phrased against definitions that
nobody else uses and that nobody will maintain.

For a referee the practical consequence is a checkable one. Each
claim in this section can be classified without reading the prose
that surrounds it: open the named declaration, read its hypotheses,
and unfold any predicate that appears in its conclusion. A claim
proved outright survives that procedure with its hypotheses
constraining data. A conditional claim exposes the proposition it is
conditional on. A marker unfolds to the trivially true proposition
in one step. We take the availability of that procedure, rather than
the size of the theorem count, to be the measurable output of the
discipline.

\section{Lessons and Future Work}
\label{sec:lessons}

We close with the lesson that generalizes least controversially, drawn from running the
pipeline at the scale described.

\textbf{Continuous verification is cheaper than retrofitted
verification, by a margin large enough to be a workflow choice,
not a methodology preference.} Each of the four failure modes we
opened with (a numerical claim that traces to a non-existent
code path, a ``formally verified'' claim whose theorem is a
placeholder, a citation that resolves to the wrong paper, a
parameter that has drifted from its primary source) did ship to
draft state in this project, and none was caught by the continuous
checks. Each was found at adversarial review, the last stage before
submission, which is late but still upstream of citation. That is the
honest version of the claim, and it is the one that sets the agenda:
the checks a stage runs continuously are cheaper than the review that
caught these, so the work is to move findings of this kind earlier.
We give the one side of that comparison we have measured and decline
the other. A full run of the Stage~7 validation suite over the whole
Lean, Python and paper corpus took 485 seconds on the authors'
workstation; the run is archived under
\texttt{docs/validation/reports/} with the timestamp 2026-08-15T03:30:20Z,
so the denominator it covered is recoverable rather than asserted. The
full pre-merge certification, which additionally rebuilds and re-runs
the test suites, took thirty-three minutes on 2026-08-13. Minutes,
then, not seconds, and not hours. The cost of
finding and fixing a propagated error after publication we have not
measured and do not quote; we know only that in this project it has
never been paid, because no such error has yet reached print.

The pipeline is open-source, domain-agnostic, and immediately
applicable in any research domain where the connection between
computation and published claims must be trustworthy. We
specifically encourage adoption in any setting where the cost of a
correctness failure post-publication exceeds the cost of running
continuous verification: a condition that holds in
pharmaceutical software (FDA SaMD algorithmic vigilance),
verified-risk financial modeling, climate modeling, and any other
research domain where the audit trail from code to claim is itself
the deliverable.
