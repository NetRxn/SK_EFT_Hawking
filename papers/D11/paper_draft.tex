\title{Kernel-Verified Topological Band Theory and Metamaterial Substrate}

\author{John Roehm}

\begin{abstract}
We report a machine-checked formalization, in the \Lean{} proof assistant against Mathlib, of five
layers of band theory and effective-medium physics: (i) a genuine
\emph{integer-valued} lattice Chern number on a discretized Brillouin torus, built via the
Fukui--Hatsugai--Suzuki plaquette construction, with unconditional integrality and gauge invariance;
(ii) a two-band diatomic acoustic Bloch operator and a band-gap existence theorem whose gap edges are
\emph{attained}, certified down to a rational enclosure with no floating-point arithmetic;
(iii) non-Hermitian Bloch Hamiltonians with a sharp biconditional $\mathcal{PT}$ transition and a
genuinely defective order-two exceptional point, certified without constructing a Jordan normal form;
(iv) algebraic effective-medium theory --- Maxwell--Garnett permittivity inside its constituent
bounds, and the \emph{exact} Voigt--Reuss gap; and (v) graphene electronic structure, culminating in
a concrete Haldane-model Chern witness certifying $C = -1$ on a $4\times4$ torus. Every headline
theorem is kernel-pure (axiom profile exactly $\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},
\,\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$), and the bundle carries \emph{no} project-local axioms and \emph{no}
tracked-hypothesis propositions: every result is unconditional given its explicit mathematical
hypotheses. Two of our results are refutations. We show that the zero set of the honeycomb structure
factor is \emph{not} invariant under a general $GL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ chart change, and that a
multi-band Hamiltonian is \emph{not} determined by its spectrum --- the latter caught a wrong bilayer
model that had passed every determinant-level gate we had, and forced an eigenvector-level invariant
into our standing methodology. The through-line is that discrete, kernel-checkable invariants and
rational enclosures reach genuine condensed-matter conclusions \emph{without} the continuum
differential-geometry infrastructure the field ordinarily assumes is prerequisite --- infrastructure
which, as we document, Mathlib does not have at the pinned revision, and which we are aware of in no
proof assistant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Topological band theory rests on a small number of structural results --- the identification of the
quantized Hall conductance with a Chern number~\cite{TKNN1982}, the geometric phase that underlies
it~\cite{Berry1984}, and the demonstration that a nonzero Chern number survives at zero net magnetic
flux~\cite{Haldane1988} --- that are derived once and then relied upon across an enormous downstream
literature in condensed matter, photonics and acoustics. A formally verified statement of each,
machine-checked to the logical kernel, would provide a reusable and unambiguous reference layer.

The obstacle is that the textbook route to a Chern number runs through Chern--Weil theory: one
integrates a curvature two-form over a closed manifold and appeals to de~Rham cohomology to see that
the result is an integer. In Mathlib that route is not merely unformalized; it is
\emph{structurally unavailable}. As of Mathlib revision \texttt{81a5d257} (toolchain
\texttt{v4.32.0}), Mathlib has differential forms on normed spaces --- with the manifold version an
explicit in-source TODO --- and the Poincar\'e lemma only for $1$-forms on convex sets. It has no
integration of forms over manifolds, no de~Rham cohomology, and no characteristic classes. Every one
of the four ingredients the Chern--Weil argument needs is missing.

Our response is to take the other route. The Fukui--Hatsugai--Suzuki (FHS)
construction~\cite{FukuiHatsugaiSuzuki2005} computes a Chern number on a \emph{discretized}
Brillouin zone from link variables and plaquette phases, and produces an integer by construction
rather than by an integrality theorem. Formalized, it needs no manifolds, no forms and no cohomology
--- only the arithmetic of principal branches on a finite torus. Section~\ref{sec:lattice} shows that
this is enough to obtain both integrality and gauge invariance unconditionally, and
Section~\ref{sec:graphene} shows it is enough to certify a nontrivial Chern number for a named
physical model.

The same discipline --- replace an analytic argument by a discrete or algebraic one that a kernel can
check --- organizes the rest of the bundle. A band gap becomes a pair of rational inequalities
(Section~\ref{sec:phononic}). A $\mathcal{PT}$ transition becomes a biconditional on a secular
determinant, and an exceptional point becomes a comparison of two explicitly computed eigenspaces
rather than a Jordan decomposition (Section~\ref{sec:nonhermitian}). Effective-medium theory becomes
a division-free identity plus an ordering (Section~\ref{sec:effmedium}).

\paragraph{Two-layer honesty.} Throughout, the algebra and the inequalities are verified in the
kernel; the identification of a model with a specific material, device or measurement is a
literature-cited hypothesis carried explicitly by the consumer, and is never smuggled into a theorem
statement. We are equally explicit about what we did \emph{not} prove: Section~\ref{sec:scope}
records that Berry curvature and the bulk--boundary correspondence are \emph{deferred}, not shipped
in conditional form, and that our claimed novelty carries four mandatory carve-outs.

\section{The lattice-invariant core}
\label{sec:lattice}

\subsection{Principal branches and a finite torus}

The FHS construction assigns to each plaquette of a discretized Brillouin zone the argument of a
product of link variables, and recovers an integer by counting how many times that argument wraps.
Everything therefore rests on a fixed convention for the principal branch. We define
\thm{principal} as \texttt{toIocMod} on $(-\pi,\pi]$ and \thm{branchIndex} as the corresponding
\texttt{toIocDiv}, and freeze the sign once; \thm{principal\_add\_branch} and
\thm{principal\_mem\_Ioc} are the two facts everything downstream uses.
\thm{arg\_plaquette\_eq\_principal\_rawCurl} then gives the exact four-fold plaquette shape,
expressing the argument of a plaquette product as the principal branch of a signed sum of link
arguments.

The Brillouin zone is \thm{Torus}, the product $\mathbb{Z}/N_1 \times \mathbb{Z}/N_2$, a finite
type. Its single load-bearing property is telescoping: \thm{sum\_forwardDiff\_eq\_zero} states that a
discrete forward difference sums to zero around the torus, which is what makes the raw curl sum
vanish.

\subsection{Integrality and gauge invariance, unconditionally}

With \thm{latticeChern} defined as minus the sum of \thm{plaquetteBranch} over the torus --- a sum of
integers, hence an integer \emph{by construction} --- the substantive theorem is that this integer
carries the total plaquette phase,
\begin{equation}
\sum_k \thm{plaquetteArg}(k) \;=\; 2\pi \cdot \thm{latticeChern},
\end{equation}
formalized as \thm{sum\_plaquetteArg\_eq\_two\_pi\_mul\_latticeChern}. It carries \emph{no} spectral,
gap or continuum hypothesis whatsoever; the proof is \thm{sum\_rawCurl\_eq\_zero} plus the branch
decomposition. Gauge invariance is \thm{latticeChern\_gaugeInvariant}, via
\thm{plaquette\_gaugeTransform}.

This is deliberately a statement about link \emph{data}, not about a physical band. The bridge is
\thm{AdmissibleBandFrame}, a structure carrying a normalized state field and --- as an explicit
field --- the requirement that nearest-neighbour overlaps do not vanish. We stress that this is a
genuine hypothesis and not a consequence of a spectral gap: a gapped band can still have a vanishing
overlap between adjacent sampled frames, which is exactly the situation in which the discrete
construction fails to track the continuum object. Given the structure,
\thm{blochLatticeChern\_integrality} and \thm{blochLatticeChern\_rephase} inherit both properties in
one or two lines each.

For non-vacuity, \thm{latticeChern\_Uwit} exhibits a link field on a $2\times2$ torus whose lattice
Chern number is $1$, against the negative fixture \thm{latticeChern\_trivial}, which is $0$ for the
constant field. The complementary vanishing criterion
\thm{blochLatticeChern\_eq\_zero\_of\_narrow\_D} says that a frame with no phase frustration --- all
nearest-neighbour overlap arguments below $\pi/4$ in absolute value --- cannot wind.

\subsection{What connects this to a Hamiltonian}

The spectral input enters through \thm{blochProj}, the lower-band projector of the two-band
$d\cdot\sigma$ Hamiltonian \thm{blochPauli}, whose idempotence \thm{blochProj\_idem} follows from the
Pauli identity \thm{blochPauli\_sq}. The band structure itself is \thm{blochPauli\_band\_secular}
(the two energies are genuine eigenvalues, via the secular determinant) and
\thm{blochPauli\_gap\_pos\_iff}: the model is gapped \emph{if and only if} $d \neq 0$, so the
band-touching point the invariant is defined around is characterized rather than merely excluded.
The forward direction is \thm{blochPauli\_gap\_pos} and the converse follows from
\thm{blochPauli\_gap\_zero\_of\_eq\_zero}.

Turning a $d$-field into an admissible frame is \thm{blochFrameOfD}, built on the explicit
lower-band eigenvector \thm{lbVec} with its eigenvector law \thm{blochPauli\_mulVec\_lbVec}. It
carries two hypotheses, both real: a north-pole positivity condition, which fails on the negative
$d_3$ axis where the gauge choice genuinely degenerates, and the overlap condition above.

\section{Phononic band gaps}
\label{sec:phononic}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/d11_fig1_phononic_band_gap.png}
\caption{The certified phononic band gap of the diatomic chain
$(m_1,m_2,\kappa,a) = (1,2,1,1)$. Both branches are plotted across the Brillouin zone; the shaded
band contains no state. Both edges are \emph{attained} at $k = \pi$
(\thm{branchMinus\_at\_pi}, \thm{branchPlus\_at\_pi}) and, by evenness of the branches in $k$
(\thm{branchMinus\_neg}, \thm{branchPlus\_neg}), also at $-\pi$
(\thm{branchMinus\_at\_neg\_pi}, \thm{branchPlus\_at\_neg\_pi}), so the bound is tight rather
than loose. Both endpoints are theorem-backed; neither is asserted by symmetry alone.}
\label{fig:phononic}
\end{figure*}

PhysLib's \texttt{TightBindingChain} is a one-dimensional, single-band, gapless model: its dispersion
is a single cosine and its Brillouin zone is a real interval. A gap cannot exist in a single band, so
a two-band object had to be built. \thm{DiatomicChain} is a structure carrying two masses, a spring
constant and a lattice spacing with their positivity proofs, and \thm{blochMatrix} is the
mass-normalized $2\times2$ Bloch dynamical matrix.

The spectral content is stronger than an eigenvalue exhibition.
\thm{acousticBloch\_charpoly\_factor} factors the secular determinant into the two branch factors for
\emph{every} $\lambda$, from which \thm{acousticBloch\_eigenvalue\_iff} gives spectral
\emph{completeness}: the determinant vanishes if and only if $\lambda$ is one of the two branches.
\thm{acousticBloch\_spectrum} packages Hermiticity with the branch ordering.

On the concrete crystal $(m_1,m_2,\kappa,a) = (1,2,1,1)$ we obtain
$\omega^2_\pm = \tfrac{3}{2} \pm \sqrt{\tfrac{5}{4} + \cos k}$, and hence
\thm{phononic\_band\_gap\_exists}: there exist $\mathrm{lo} < \mathrm{hi}$ bracketing the two
branches for all $k$, witnessed at $\mathrm{lo} = 1$ and $\mathrm{hi} = 2$. Both edges are attained
--- \thm{branchMinus\_at\_pi} and \thm{branchPlus\_at\_pi} evaluate to exactly $1$ and $2$ at
$k = \pi$ --- so the gap is the true one and not a loose bracket; see Fig.~\ref{fig:phononic}.

The existence statement has a strictly stronger companion. \thm{band\_gap\_falsifier} says that
\emph{any} real $\omega^2$ annihilating the secular determinant strictly inside the gap yields a
contradiction: the gap is stated as a refutation, not merely as an unmet bound. (The gap $(1,2)$
above is in $\omega^2$; the frequency gap below is in $\omega$.) Finally,
\thm{band\_gap\_rational\_enclosure} certifies the frequency gap by a rational inner bracket,
discharged by \texttt{norm\_num} with no floating-point arithmetic anywhere
(\thm{sqrt\_two\_enclosure}).

\section{Non-Hermitian exceptional points}
\label{sec:nonhermitian}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/d11_fig2_pt_exceptional_point.png}
\caption{The $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric two-level model. Eigenvalues are real in the unbroken phase and
purely imaginary beyond it; the transition is a sharp biconditional
(\thm{pt\_symmetric\_real\_spectrum\_iff}) and the critical point is a genuinely defective order-two
exceptional point (\thm{exceptional\_point\_defective}).}
\label{fig:pt}
\end{figure*}

For the standard $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric two-level model \thm{ptBloch}, the secular determinant is
computed by \thm{ptBloch\_secular\_det}, so the eigenvalues are $\pm\sqrt{1-g^2}$. The transition is
stated as a sharp biconditional, \thm{pt\_symmetric\_real\_spectrum\_iff}: a real eigenvalue exists
\emph{if and only if} $g^2 \le 1$. Both directions carry content.

At the critical point the interesting claim is not that the splitting vanishes but that the matrix is
\emph{defective}. Establishing that ordinarily invokes a Jordan normal form, which Mathlib does not
have. We avoid it: \thm{ptBlochEP\_nilpotent} gives a vanishing square, hence algebraic multiplicity
two; \thm{ptBlochEP\_eigenvector} exhibits an eigenvector and \thm{ptBlochEP\_kernel\_line} shows
every null vector lies on the line it spans, hence geometric multiplicity one; and
\thm{ptBlochEP\_not\_eigenvector} confirms a second candidate is not an eigenvector. The conjunction
is \thm{exceptional\_point\_defective}, and \thm{ep\_order\_two} records the characteristic
polynomial there. Computing the two eigenspaces explicitly is arguably a stronger certificate than a
Jordan decomposition would be, since it exhibits the defect rather than asserting it.

Approach to the exceptional point is quantified by \thm{ep\_proximity\_enclosure}, which bounds
\thm{eigenvalueSplitting} on a neighbourhood, with \thm{ep\_splitting\_at\_ep} giving exact vanishing
at the critical point.

We note a scope limitation plainly: despite the module name \texttt{NonHermitianWinding}, the
non-Hermitian winding number and skin effect are \emph{deferred}. What that module contains is the
eigenvalue-splitting analysis above.

\section{Algebraic effective-medium bounds}
\label{sec:effmedium}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/d11_fig4_effective_medium.png}
\caption{Left: Maxwell--Garnett effective permittivity inside its constituent bounds, with the
certified point $(\varepsilon_h,\varepsilon_i,f) = (1,4,\tfrac12)$ giving exactly $2$, strictly
inside $[1,4]$ (\thm{effectiveMedium\_worked\_point\_strict}). Right: the Voigt and Reuss
elastic \emph{averages} --- not bounds on a physical $M_{\rm eff}$, see \S\ref{sec:effmedium} ---
with their \emph{exact} arithmetic-minus-harmonic gap (\thm{voigt\_sub\_reuss\_eq}).}
\label{fig:effmedium}
\end{figure*}

The Maxwell--Garnett mixing rule~\cite{MaxwellGarnett1904} is formalized as \thm{maxwellGarnett}, and
\thm{maxwellGarnett\_clausius\_mossotti} verifies that this closed form solves the Clausius--Mossotti
relation in a cross-multiplied, division-free formulation, so the identity holds without a
side-condition beyond nonvanishing of the denominator. \thm{maxwellGarnett\_host\_recovery} is the
zero-fill sanity check.

The bound we actually prove is the elementary \emph{constituent} bracket
$\varepsilon_h \le \varepsilon_{\rm eff} \le \varepsilon_i$
(\thm{effectiveMedium\_constituent\_bounds}), for a physical composite
$0 < \varepsilon_h \le \varepsilon_i$ at fill fraction $0 \le f \le 1$ --- and the ordering
hypothesis is load-bearing rather than a relabeling, since Maxwell--Garnett is not symmetric under
exchanging host and inclusion. Away from the degenerate endpoints the bracket is \emph{strict}:
\thm{effectiveMedium\_constituent\_bounds\_strict} gives
$\varepsilon_h < \varepsilon_{\rm eff} < \varepsilon_i$ whenever
$\varepsilon_h < \varepsilon_i$ and $0 < f < 1$, so neither constituent bound is attained on a
genuinely mixed two-phase composite. The worked instance
\thm{effectiveMedium\_hashinShtrikman\_enclosure} at
$(\varepsilon_h, \varepsilon_i, f) = (1, 4, \tfrac12)$ gives exactly $2$, and
\thm{effectiveMedium\_worked\_point\_strict} places it strictly inside $[1,4]$ by instantiating the
general theorem rather than by re-deriving order facts from the value.

The naming needs care, because two distinct sharper families sit above this one. The
Wiener bounds~\cite{Milton2002} are the harmonic and arithmetic means of the phases, and the
Hashin--Shtrikman bounds~\cite{HashinShtrikman1962} are tighter still. At the worked point the
constituent bracket is $[1,4]$ while the Wiener bracket, computed from the definition as the
harmonic and arithmetic means at $(1, 4, \tfrac12)$, is $[1.6, 2.5]$, so our lower bound sits
strictly below Wiener's and the theorem entails neither family. Neither is formalized here, and the
declaration's historical name should not be read as a claim to have proved the Hashin--Shtrikman
bound. The Lean module states this scope explicitly in its header and in the theorem's own
docstring.

For elastic moduli we ship \thm{voigtModulus}~\cite{Voigt1889} and
\thm{reussModulus}~\cite{Reuss1929} with the ordering chain
\thm{effectiveModuli\_enclosure}, $M_1 \le M_{\rm R} \le M_{\rm V} \le M_2$. This is an
ordering of the two \emph{averages} together with constituent bounds; no effective modulus of a
composite appears in the statement. The classical Voigt--Reuss bracket $M_{\rm R} \le M_{\rm eff}
\le M_{\rm V}$ is a claim about a physical $M_{\rm eff}$ and is \emph{not} formalized here ---
the same distinction, and for the same reason, as the constituent-versus-Hashin--Shtrikman one
above. The Hashin--Shtrikman elastic bounds~\cite{HashinShtrikman1963} refine that physical
bracket, not this ordering. The sharpest statement here is
\thm{voigt\_sub\_reuss\_eq}, which gives the arithmetic-minus-harmonic gap \emph{exactly},
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm V} - M_{\rm R} \;=\; \frac{f(1-f)(M_1-M_2)^2}{(1-f)M_2 + fM_1},
\end{equation}
rather than only as the inequality $M_{\rm V} \ge M_{\rm R}$, which was all that was formalized
at the time the module header already asserted the exact identity.

\paragraph{Route disclosure.} This section is deliberately \emph{algebraic}. The analytic route ---
two-scale convergence and periodic homogenization --- was not attempted. Mathlib carries Sobolev
inequalities but no two-scale convergence, and PhysLib's optics directory is by its own docstring a
placeholder. Two-scale convergence is, to our knowledge, formalized in \emph{no} proof assistant, so
this is a choice between two equally unformalized routes in which the algebraic one was tractable ---
not the avoidance of an existing formalization. The
nearest substrate an analytic route would need is the Lax--Milgram theorem, for which a detailed
proof prepared for Coq formalization is available~\cite{BoldoLaxMilgram2016} and a Coq development
followed.

\section{Graphene and a concrete Chern witness}
\label{sec:graphene}

\subsection{Honeycomb structure and the chart obligation}

The honeycomb structure factor is \thm{structureFactor}, entering the $d\cdot\sigma$ frame through
\thm{honeycombD}. Band energies are the two signed norms as genuine eigenvalues
(\thm{honeycomb\_energy\_eq}, \thm{honeycomb\_band\_secular}), and
\thm{structureFactor\_eq\_zero\_iff} characterizes the Dirac points in a quotient-free form. The two
cones are inequivalent: \thm{diracK'\_ne\_recip\_translate\_diracK} shows one is not a
reciprocal-lattice translate of the other.

The generality question --- which coordinate charts are admissible --- is answered by
\thm{isHoneycombChart\_of\_neighbours}: the honeycomb's own neighbour geometry \emph{forces} the
chart, with \thm{chart\_len\_sq\_of\_neighbours} fixing the primitive-vector length in terms of the
carbon--carbon distance. Section~\ref{sec:refutations} explains why this is stated as a forcing
result rather than an invariance result.

\subsection{Dirac expansion with a global remainder}

The linearization about a Dirac point is controlled by a \emph{global} inequality:
\thm{norm\_exp\_mul\_I\_sub\_one\_sub\_id\_le} bounds the second-order remainder of
the complex exponential for \emph{every} real argument, with constant $1$ and no validity ball.
Consequently \thm{structureFactor\_linear\_expansion\_global} bounds the difference between the exact
and linearized band norms globally.

Isotropy is a theorem, and a chart-specific one: \thm{quadForm\_of\_chart} identifies the quadratic
form with an isotropic one only for an admissible honeycomb chart. The slope is
\thm{dispersion\_slope\_of\_neighbours}, and \thm{dispersion\_slope\_eq\_hbar\_fermiVelocity}
delivers the linear dispersion with \thm{fermiVelocity} left parametrized rather than carrying a
baked-in unit convention. We also record where linearity \emph{fails}:
\thm{dispersion\_linear\_not\_exact} exhibits a point at which the linear form and the exact
structure factor differ.

Adding a Semenoff mass gives \thm{gappedHoneycombD}, with the Dirac-point gap exactly twice the
absolute mass (\thm{gapped\_dirac\_gap\_eq}), shown to be the model minimum by
\thm{gapped\_gap\_ge\_dirac} with its exact minimizer set \thm{gapped\_gap\_eq\_dirac\_iff}.

\subsection{The Haldane witness}

The Haldane model~\cite{Haldane1988} adds a next-nearest-neighbour complex hopping, formalized via
\thm{haldaneNNN} and bridged to the physical neighbour sum by \thm{haldaneNNN\_eq\_nnnSum}. Because
\thm{haldaneNNN\_diracK} and \thm{haldaneNNN\_diracK'} take opposite values, the two cones acquire
\emph{opposite} masses (\thm{haldaneD\_diracK}, \thm{haldaneD\_diracK'}) --- the entire mechanism ---
with gaps \thm{haldane\_gap\_diracK} and the inversion criterion
\thm{haldane\_mass\_inversion\_iff}.

Sampling on a $4\times4$ torus and feeding the result through the machinery of
Section~\ref{sec:lattice} yields the bundle's headline result: the lattice Chern number of the
Haldane frame is $-1$, formalized as \thm{haldaneFrame\_latticeChern\_eq\_neg\_one} at parameters
$t = t_2 = 1$, $\varphi = \pi/2$, $m = 1$. The sign follows from our orientation convention
(\thm{latticeChern} is defined with a leading minus sign) and flips under $\varphi \mapsto -\varphi$;
we state the parameter point explicitly because the result is a certificate at that point, not a
general classification. That the value is a property of the sampled band rather than of our
particular gauge representative is \thm{haldaneFrame\_latticeChern\_gauge\_independent}, which is a
direct call to \thm{blochLatticeChern\_rephase}.

Nothing in the adapter layer was rebuilt for this: \thm{blochFrameOfD} constructs the frame,
\thm{linkOfFrame} and \thm{blochLatticeChern} turn it into link data, and the argument-sector lemmas
(\thm{arg\_cell\_A} and its companions) place each raw curl in its window by radical-free real and
imaginary comparisons. The admissibility obligations are discharged explicitly and are not
formalities: \thm{haldane1\_pos} checks the north-pole condition at all sixteen vertices, and
\thm{haldane1\_link\_ne} checks that all thirty-two nearest-neighbour overlaps are nonzero.

The companion values are as important as the headline.
\thm{haldane\_trivial\_phase\_chern\_zero} gives $C = 0$ at $m = 6$, outside the analytic window. And
\thm{haldane\_massInversion\_not\_sufficient\_at\_N4} gives $C = 0$ at $m = 5$ --- which is
\emph{inside} the window $|m| < 3\sqrt{3} \approx 5.196$ (\thm{haldane\_window\_bounds}). At this
grid size the invariant flips \emph{numerically} near $|m| \approx 3.3177$ --- a bisection on a
Python port of the same FHS construction (\texttt{haldane\_lattice\_chern},
\texttt{haldane\_flip\_location} in \texttt{formulas.py}, pinned against the three Lean-certified
masses by regression test), with no Lean backing for the crossing itself --- so on roughly a third of
the analytic window
the masses have inverted while the $4\times4$ invariant still reads zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:haldane}).
Section~\ref{sec:refutations} records what we did about that.

Independently, the Berry phase around a cone is exact rather than enclosed:
\thm{coneBerryPhase\_pi} gives $\pi$ for the diamond loop, with \thm{coneWinding\_two\_pi} recording
increments summing to $2\pi$, against the contrast pair \thm{flatLoopBerryPhase\_zero} and
\thm{flatWinding\_zero} for a loop enclosing no degeneracy. The honest limitation, stated in the
source: the increments are certified non-marginal --- each keeps a margin from the branch cut
(\thm{coneWinding\_increments\_off\_branch\_cut}) --- but not certified alias-free.

\subsection{Bilayer graphene}

The Bernal-stacked bilayer required Mathlib-grade infrastructure: \thm{det\_fin\_four}, since Mathlib
ships determinant expansions only through \texttt{Matrix.det\_fin\_three}. With it, \thm{bernal\_det\_eq}
collapses the $4\times4$ secular determinant to a single real quadratic, and
\thm{bernal\_eigenvector\_iff} confirms its roots are genuine Bloch eigenvectors. The chirality
relation \thm{bernal\_chirality\_two} is exact; the low-energy behaviour is bracketed globally by
\thm{bernal\_lowRoot\_enclosure}; the field-induced gap is a genuine least element
(\thm{bernal\_halfGapSq\_isLeast}) matching the standard form (\thm{bernal\_fullGapSq\_eq}); and
\thm{bernal\_mexicanHat} certifies that the band minimum sits at finite momentum, with the gap
strictly below the applied bias.

\section{Two refutations}
\label{sec:refutations}

Two of this bundle's results are negative, and both are registered as machine-enforced no-gos in the
project's kernel no-go registry, each backed by a refutation theorem rather than by prose.

\paragraph{The zero set is not $GL_2(\mathbb{Z})$-invariant.} It is tempting to state honeycomb
results for an arbitrary primitive-vector pair, since any two primitive pairs differ by an element of
$GL_2(\mathbb{Z})$. That is false at the level we needed:
\thm{structureFactor\_zero\_set\_not\_shear\_invariant} exhibits a shear under which the zero set of
the structure factor is not preserved, and \thm{not\_isHoneycombChart\_shear} confirms the sheared
chart is inadmissible. This is why Section~\ref{sec:graphene} states a \emph{forcing} result rather
than an invariance result: the correct generality is that the physical neighbour geometry determines
the chart, not that any chart will do.

\paragraph{The spectrum does not determine a multi-band model.} Our first bilayer Hamiltonian
carried a conjugated off-diagonal block --- the wrong model. It passed every gate we had: the
determinant identity, the gap theorem, the enclosure, kernel purity, the \texttt{sorry} count and the
full validation suite. What exposed it was an eigenvector-level quantity.
\thm{bernal\_chirality\_two} gives the exact chirality relation for the correct model, while
\thm{bernalSwapped\_chirality\_zero} shows the conjugated variant is phase-blind. The standalone
refutation \thm{bernal\_spectrum\_not\_determine\_model} then certifies, as a four-way conjunction,
that the swapped model is Hermitian at all parameters, that the two secular determinants agree at
all parameters, that the two Hamiltonians are nonetheless distinct at $f = i$, and that
$i^2 \neq i\,\bar{\imath}$ --- the last being the arithmetic fact that separates the chirality
coefficients $f^2$ and $f\bar{f}$ which the spectrum cannot see.

We regard this as the bundle's most transferable result, and it has changed our standing practice:
for any multi-band Hamiltonian, ship at least one eigenvector-level invariant --- a winding number, a
Berry phase, a chirality relation --- verified against the primary source. A determinant-level gate
cannot certify that a model has been correctly identified, and ours did not.

\paragraph{A retracted classification.} We previously shipped a theorem asserting that the lattice
Chern number is nonzero exactly where the Dirac masses invert. It was deleted, for two independent
reasons. It is false at fixed grid size, as \thm{haldane\_massInversion\_not\_sufficient\_at\_N4} now
shows directly; and, as stated, it carried no content beyond its own hypotheses. We record the
standing rule it produced: no statement in this corpus may be phrased as holding ``exactly where the
masses invert'' at a fixed grid size.

\section{Scope, deferrals and prior art}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{What is deferred, and not shipped conditionally.} Berry curvature is not formalized here:
neither a Berry connection nor a Berry curvature occurs anywhere in this development. The
band-theoretic bulk--boundary correspondence is likewise absent from this bundle --- not shipped
conditionally on a disclosed hypothesis, but simply not undertaken. (A carve-out symmetric with the
\texttt{ChernBridge} one below: this group's tree does contain categorical bulk--boundary objects in
its SymTFT modules. They are a different construct --- an algebraic condensation statement, not a
band-theoretic edge-mode count --- and we name them rather than let the absence claim stand
unqualified against our own tree.) The continuum invariant is deferred on the infrastructure
grounds set out in Section~\ref{sec:intro}. Our earlier planning documents described this thread as
including Berry curvature and a conditional bulk--boundary result; that description did not match the
tree and has been corrected. What we claim is the finite-lattice FHS invariant and its gauge
invariance, and the source itself disclaims equality to any continuum first Chern class.

A further discretization caveat: grids whose size is divisible by three are inadmissible, because
they sample the Dirac points where the $d$-vector is purely longitudinal and the gauge representative
degenerates. This is a real constraint on which grids may be used, not a cosmetic one.

\paragraph{Novelty, with carve-outs.} We are aware of no \emph{prior} formalization, in any proof
assistant, of a Chern number or a Chern class \emph{of a vector bundle} --- the topological
invariant --- nor of an acoustic or phononic band structure, an exceptional point, a
$\mathcal{PT}$ transition, a Maxwell--Garnett constituent enclosure, a Wiener or
Hashin--Shtrikman-type effective-medium bound, a honeycomb band structure, or the Haldane model.
(Stated in the past tense deliberately: the present paper formalizes six of these
eight --- the acoustic band structure, the exceptional point, the $\mathcal{PT}$ transition, the
Maxwell--Garnett enclosure, the honeycomb band structure and the Haldane model --- so a present-tense
universal negative would be self-refuting. Two of the eight
are \emph{not} delivered here and are listed for the absence claim only: the vector-bundle Chern class
(Section~\ref{sec:lattice}'s invariant is the Fukui--Hatsugai--Suzuki lattice quantity, not proved
equal to it) and the Wiener/Hashin--Shtrikman families, which Section~\ref{sec:effmedium} states
explicitly are not formalized in this development.) One carve-out is our own: this group's \texttt{ChernBridge} module defines a categorical
Chebyshev \emph{marker} that a companion bundle describes in Chern language. It is a different
object from the Bloch-band invariant reported here --- an algebraic marker, not an integral of
curvature over a bundle --- and we flag it rather than let the absence claim stand unqualified
against our own tree. The Mathlib statements are pinned to revision \texttt{81a5d257} and were
verified by direct inspection of that checkout rather than from documentation; every occurrence of
the string \texttt{Chern} in Mathlib is in fact \texttt{Chernoff}. Every PhysLib claim in this paper is
likewise pinned, to revision \texttt{c4843367}, and verified against that checkout. Four carve-outs
are mandatory.

First, PhysLib does formalize a tight-binding chain --- but a one-dimensional, single-band, gapless
one whose Brillouin zone is a real interval, with no multi-band Bloch matrix, no gap, no Berry
quantity and no topological invariant. Second, Cubical Agda has independently formalized synthetic
cohomology including cup products, the Gysin sequence, and the cohomology rings of the spheres, the
torus, the Klein bottle, the real and complex projective \emph{planes}, and infinite real projective
space~\cite{LjungstromMortberg2024}. The Gysin sequence is the machinery from which Euler and Chern
classes are built, so our claim is that no assistant \emph{defines} the classes, not that the
surrounding machinery is absent. (Corrected 2026-07-31: three earlier drafts of this sentence named
``the cohomology ring of $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$'', which that development does not contain --- its
infinite projective space is the \emph{real} one, whose ring is the Stiefel--Whitney substrate
$H^*(\mathbb{RP}^\infty;\mathbb{Z}/2)$, not the $\mathbb{Z}[c_1]$ from which Chern classes are
defined. The substitution made the carve-out concede more than the source supports.) Third, Isabelle/HOL formalizes winding
numbers, Cauchy indices and the argument principle~\cite{LiPaulson2018}, which is the nearest
machinery to our non-Hermitian winding content and to any index-theoretic reading of bulk--boundary.
Fourth, the Lax--Milgram theorem --- the backbone of elliptic-PDE well-posedness and so of any
analytic homogenization route --- has a detailed proof prepared for Coq
formalization~\cite{BoldoLaxMilgram2016}, with a Coq development following it. We also note the formal-proof energy-benchmarking work of Ugwuanyi
\emph{et al.}~\cite{LeanLJ2025} as the nearest ``periodic lattice in Lean'' precedent, though it is
not band theory.

Two coverage limits bound these claims, and we state them rather than let a referee find them. The
Isabelle Archive of Formal Proofs was not directly searchable during our survey, so our negative
results for index theory, for defective-eigenvalue infrastructure, and for the
exceptional-point / $\mathcal{PT}$ / non-Hermitian items of the list above should be read as probable
rather than established. And for HOL4 and PVS our coverage is materially weaker than for Lean, Agda
and Coq; for those two systems we say only that we are not aware of relevant work.

\section{Conclusion}

The five layers reported here share a method rather than a subject. In each, a construction that is
ordinarily analytic --- an integral of curvature, a spectral gap, a Jordan decomposition, a
homogenization limit --- was replaced by a discrete or algebraic certificate that a kernel can check
outright, and in each case the replacement was strong enough to reach the physical conclusion. The
lattice Chern number is an integer by construction rather than by a theorem; the band gap is a pair
of rational inequalities with attained edges and a general falsifier; the exceptional point is a
comparison of two explicitly computed eigenspaces; the effective-medium bounds are an ordering plus
an exact gap.

What this buys, beyond the individual results, is that the certificates compose. The Haldane witness
of Section~\ref{sec:graphene} is not a separate development but an instantiation of
Section~\ref{sec:lattice}'s adapter at a named physical model, and it inherits gauge invariance by a
one-line call. It also buys a discipline: the corpus contains no project-local axioms and no tracked
hypotheses, so every statement here is unconditional given its explicit mathematical hypotheses, and
the two results we could not obtain are recorded as deferred rather than dressed as conditional.

The most useful thing we learned was negative. A wrong bilayer Hamiltonian passed every
determinant-level gate we had, and was caught only by an eigenvector-level invariant. Formal
verification certifies that a proof is correct; it does not certify that the object proved about is
the intended one. Closing that second gap needs invariants that see more than the spectrum, and we
now require one for every multi-band model we ship.
