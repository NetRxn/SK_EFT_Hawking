\title{Kernel-Verified Foundations of Computational Quantum Chemistry and Open-System Dynamics}

\author{John Roehm}

\begin{abstract}
We report a machine-checked formalization, in the \Lean{} proof assistant with Mathlib and the
PhysLib physics library, of three pillars of the electronic-structure and open-system stack of
computational quantum physics: (i) non-equilibrium Green's-function (NEGF) quantum transport: the
retarded/advanced Green's functions, the fundamental spectral relation $A = G^{R}\Gamma G^{A}$, the
Landauer--B\"uttiker/Meir--Wingreen conductance, and a conductance-quantization certificate with an
explicit falsifier; (ii) density-functional-theory (DFT) foundations: a Kato--Rellich reduction establishing
\emph{unconditional} essential self-adjointness of the molecular many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian (the two formerly
disclosed analytic inputs, kinetic essential self-adjointness and the Coulomb relative bound $a<1$, are
now discharged as proven kernel-pure theorems, the latter via an in-tree Fourier--Kato development and a
core-to-closure relative-boundedness extension), the Hohenberg--Kohn
uniqueness and variational theorems, and the Levy--Lieb constrained-search functional;
and (iii) open-system dynamics: the Gorini--Kossakowski--Sudarshan--Lindblad (GKSL) generator with
complete positivity of its dissipator, the GKSL structure theorem, and the Markovian dynamical
semigroup $e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ realized as a matrix exponential of the non-Hermitian Liouvillian. Every
headline theorem is kernel-pure (axiom profile exactly $\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},
\,\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$) with no project-local axioms. To our knowledge these are the first
formalizations of NEGF/Landauer \emph{conductance} transport, of the Hohenberg--Kohn/Levy--Lieb DFT
\emph{statements} (see the scope note in \S\ref{sec:dft-scope}: those are proved over abstract data
structures carrying the Rayleigh--Ritz inequalities as fields, not over the molecular Hamiltonian of
\S4.1), and of the Lindblad \emph{generator and dynamical semigroup} in any proof assistant; we are careful to
distinguish the last from the static quantum-channel/CPTP machinery that already exists in \Lean{},
Coq, and Isabelle. Throughout we adopt a two-layer honesty discipline: the algebraic and analytic
\emph{formulas} are verified in the kernel, while the identification of a model with a specific
device, material, or bath remains literature-cited.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Computational quantum chemistry and open-system physics rest on a small number of structural
theorems: the Landauer picture of coherent conductance, the Hohenberg--Kohn theorems underpinning
density-functional theory, and the GKSL form of Markovian master equations. These are derived once
and then relied upon by an enormous downstream simulation literature. These foundations are
mathematically nontrivial: they involve unbounded operators on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
(the molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian), variational principles over admissible densities, and complete
positivity of superoperators. A formally verified statement of each, machine-checked down to the
logical kernel, provides a reusable and unambiguous reference layer.

We present such a layer, built in \Lean{}~4~\cite{lean4} on top of Mathlib~\cite{mathlib} and the
PhysLib physics library, spanning three subjects:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{NEGF quantum transport} (Sec.~\ref{sec:negf}): the retarded/advanced Green's functions
on a Hermitian device Hamiltonian, the fundamental NEGF spectral relation
$A = G^{R}\Gamma G^{A}$~\cite{datta1995}, the Landauer--B\"uttiker conductance
$G = G_{0}\!\int T(E)(-\partial_{E}f)\,dE$~\cite{landauer1957,buttiker1986} with the
Meir--Wingreen transmission~\cite{meirWingreen1992}, and a conductance-quantization certificate.
\item \textbf{DFT foundations} (Sec.~\ref{sec:dft}): essential self-adjointness of the molecular
many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian via an in-tree Kato--Rellich development~\cite{kato1995,reedSimon2},
the Hohenberg--Kohn uniqueness~\cite{hohenbergKohn1964} and variational principles, and the
Levy--Lieb constrained-search functional~\cite{levy1979,lieb1983}.
\item \textbf{Open-system dynamics} (Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}): the GKSL generator~\cite{gksl1976,
lindblad1976}, complete positivity of its dissipator via the Choi route, the trace-preservation and
canonical-form structure theorem, and the Markovian semigroup $e^{t\mathcal{L}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Novelty, stated honestly.} A prior-art reconnaissance across the interactive-theorem-prover
(ITP) ecosystem (\Lean{}/Mathlib, Coq/Rocq, Isabelle/HOL including the Archive of Formal Proofs, Agda,
HOL Light, Mizar; see Ref.~\cite{survey2021} and the records archived with this work) found no existing
formalization of any of the three results. To our knowledge this is the first NEGF Green's-function and
Landauer-\emph{conductance} transport, the first Hohenberg--Kohn/Levy--Lieb DFT \emph{statements}
(qualified exactly as in \S\ref{sec:dft-scope} and \S1: proved over abstract data structures
carrying the Rayleigh--Ritz inequalities as fields, \emph{not} over the molecular Hamiltonian of
\S4.1), and the first Lindblad
\emph{generator and Markovian dynamical semigroup} in any proof assistant. Two carve-outs are essential
and we make them explicitly. First, quantum \emph{channels} (completely-positive trace-preserving
(CPTP) maps, Choi matrices, superoperators) already exist in \Lean{}'s PhysLib quantum-information
layer~\cite{leanQI2025}, in Coq's CoqQ~\cite{coqq2022}, and in Isabelle/AFP~\cite{isabelleCBO}; our
contribution in Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad} is the \emph{generator} $\mathcal{L} = -i[H,\cdot] + \mathcal{D}$,
the complete positivity of the dissipator $\mathcal{D}$, and the one-parameter semigroup $e^{t\mathcal{L}}$
built on that channel substrate, not the static channel theory. Second, Mathlib carries a substantial
unbounded-operator and perturbation-theory development; our self-adjointness claim is for the
\emph{molecular many-body Coulomb} Hamiltonian specifically, established via a Kato--Rellich argument we
build in-tree, rather than a claim about abstract perturbation theory.

\paragraph{Two-layer honesty.} Following the program's standing discipline, every claim separates a
\emph{verified formula layer} from a \emph{cited identification layer}. The kernel verifies the algebra
and analysis: that the spectral function is positive semidefinite, that the Coulomb Hamiltonian is
essentially self-adjoint, that the dissipator is completely positive. It does \emph{not} certify that a
given matrix is the Hamiltonian of a particular nanowire, that a given potential describes a particular
molecule, or that a given jump operator realizes a particular bath; those identifications remain the
province of the cited physics literature and are flagged at each point of use.

\section{Verification framework}
\label{sec:framework}

All results are \Lean{}~4 theorems checked against Mathlib (toolchain \texttt{leanprover/lean4:v\leantoolchain})
and PhysLib, the latter supplying the unbounded-operator resolvent and spectral API used in
Secs.~\ref{sec:negf}--\ref{sec:dft} and the CPTP/Choi quantum-information layer used in
Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}. We hold every headline theorem to a \emph{kernel-purity} bar: its transitive
axiom dependency, as reported by \Lean{}'s \texttt{\#print axioms}, is exactly
$\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},\,\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$ (the three standard
classical axioms underlying Mathlib), with \emph{no} project-local axioms, no \texttt{sorry}, no
\texttt{native\_decide}, and no proof that relies on raising the elaborator's heartbeat limit. Several
theorems carry \emph{disclosed tracked hypotheses} (\Lean{} \texttt{Prop}s, not axioms), which scope
conditional content explicitly. The DFT self-adjointness apex of Sec.~\ref{sec:dft} is now
\emph{unconditional}: its two formerly disclosed inputs, kinetic essential self-adjointness and the
Coulomb relative bound $a<1$, are discharged as proven kernel-pure theorems, and the potential's symmetry
is likewise discharged, so \texttt{molecularHamiltonian\_essSelfAdjoint} takes no analytic-hypothesis
argument (the variational and Levy--Lieb theorems retain semibounded-functional inputs). The open-system
contractivity of Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad} is likewise now unconditional: its formerly disclosed CPTP
realization of the time-$t$ propagator is discharged (complete positivity and trace preservation are
proven, so a Kraus realization is extracted rather than assumed), and
\texttt{traceDist\_lindblad\_monotone} takes no such argument. The remaining disclosed inputs (the
variational/Levy--Lieb semibounded-functional inputs) are enumerated, with their discharge plans, in
Sec.~\ref{sec:synthesis}.

Quantitative claims are backed by rational \emph{enclosures} rather than floating-point evaluation:
where a transcendental such as $e^{-\gamma t}$ appears, we bracket it by rational endpoints
(Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}), so the bound is itself kernel-checkable.

\section{Non-equilibrium Green's-function transport}
\label{sec:negf}

\subsection{Green's functions and the spectral relation}
For a Hermitian device Hamiltonian $H$ and lead self-energy with broadening $\Gamma$, the retarded
Green's function is $G^{R}(E) = (E + i\eta - H - \Sigma)^{-1}$ and the advanced function its conjugate
transpose. We verify that the retarded argument is invertible for $\eta>0$
(\thm{retardedArg\_isUnit}), that $G^{A} = (G^{R})^{\dagger}$ (\thm{gAdvanced\_eq\_conjTranspose}), and
the fundamental NEGF identity
\begin{equation}
A(E) \;=\; G^{R}(E)\,\Gamma\,G^{A}(E),
\label{eq:negf-spectral}
\end{equation}
the spectral function as the Green's-function-dressed broadening (\thm{spectralFn\_eq\_gR\_broadening\_gA}).
Crucially, the spectral function is positive semidefinite (\thm{spectralFn\_posSemidef}), the
operator-level statement of a non-negative density of states.

\subsection{Landauer--B\"uttiker conductance}
The Meir--Wingreen transmission $T(E) = \mathrm{tr}(\Gamma_{L} G^{R} \Gamma_{R} G^{A})$ is verified to
be real for Hermitian lead broadenings (\thm{transmission\_isReal}) and non-negative for
positive-semidefinite broadenings (\thm{transmission\_nonneg}), i.e.\ a genuine transmission
probability, established without invoking a dual-norm characterization Mathlib lacks. The linear-response
conductance
\begin{equation}
G \;=\; G_{0}\!\int T(E)\,\bigl(-\partial_{E} f(E)\bigr)\,dE ,
\qquad G_{0} = \tfrac{2e^{2}}{h},
\label{eq:landauer}
\end{equation}
is defined (\thm{landauer\_conductance\_def}); the Fermi window integrates to unity for $\beta>0$
(\thm{fermiWindow\_integral\_eq\_one}), and the wide-band (energy-independent transmission) limit
$G = G_{0} T_{0}$ is obtained unconditionally for positive temperature
(\thm{landauerConductance\_const\_transmission'}).

\subsection{Conductance quantization certificate}
For $n$ channels with transmissions $\tau_{i}\in[0,1]$, the channel conductance obeys the quantum
bound $G \le n\,G_{0}$ (\thm{channelConductance\_le\_quantum}), with equality $G = n\,G_{0}$ when every
channel is fully open (\thm{conductance\_quantization}). The bound is falsifiable: a conductance
strictly exceeding $n\,G_{0}$ is impossible (\thm{conductance\_quantization\_falsifier}), witnessed
concretely by a \texttt{norm\_num}-backed two-channel point contact that cannot carry three conductance
quanta (\thm{two\_channel\_no\_three\_quanta}). The certificate ties back to the integral picture of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:landauer} through \thm{channelConductance\_eq\_landauer}. To our knowledge this is the
first machine-checked NEGF/Landauer conductance transport in any proof assistant.

\section{Density-functional-theory foundations}
\label{sec:dft}

\subsection{The molecular Hamiltonian and self-adjointness}
\label{subsec:molecular}
The $N$-electron molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian, with kinetic term $T$ and the electron--nucleus plus
electron--electron Coulomb potential $V$, is an unbounded operator whose self-adjointness is the
precondition for a real spectrum and unitary dynamics. We formalize the Kato--Rellich theorem in-tree
(Mathlib does not supply unbounded-operator essential self-adjointness in the form required) as a
relative-boundedness criterion: if $A$ is self-adjoint and $B$ is symmetric with $A$-relative bound
$a<1$, then $A+B$ is self-adjoint. The molecular apex \thm{molecularHamiltonian\_essSelfAdjoint}
instantiates this for the closure of the kinetic operator plus the Coulomb potential, and is now
\emph{fully unconditional}: all three load-bearing hypotheses are discharged as proven kernel-pure
theorems. (i) The potential's symmetry reduces to measurability of the Coulomb sum
(\thm{molecularPotentialOperator\_isSymmetric}). (ii) Kinetic essential self-adjointness
(\thm{kineticOperator\_isSelfAdjoint\_closure}) follows from the momentum-square operator's self-adjoint
closure via the dense-range criterion. (iii) The Coulomb relative bound $a<1$ (\thm{coulomb\_isRelBounded})
is proven by an in-tree Fourier--Kato development: a single-electron relative bound (the momentum-space
$L^\infty$--$L^2$ estimate with an $\varepsilon$-dilation trick) is lifted to each molecular
electron--nucleus and electron--electron term by a measure-preserving coordinate split and a Tonelli/lower-integral
Minkowski reassembly, summed over the finitely many terms with $\varepsilon$ chosen so the total bound is
$<\tfrac12$, established on the Schwartz core and then extended to the kinetic operator's closure by a
graph-limit relative-boundedness lemma (\thm{IsRelBounded.extend\_to\_closure}, absent from Mathlib and
built in-tree). The former conditional apex is retained as the superseded
\thm{molecularHamiltonian\_essSelfAdjoint\_of\_hpot\_hrel}. To our knowledge this is the
first formalization of \emph{unconditional} self-adjointness of a molecular many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian.

\subsection{Hohenberg--Kohn theorems}
The first Hohenberg--Kohn theorem (the ground-state density determines the external potential up to a
constant) is formalized as a \emph{constructive reductio} (\thm{hohenberg\_kohn\_uniqueness}), not as
an axiom: two potentials sharing a ground-state density lead, through the Rayleigh--Ritz variational
inequalities, to a contradiction. The contrapositive (\thm{hohenberg\_kohn\_density\_injective}) gives
density injectivity directly. The second Hohenberg--Kohn theorem, the density variational principle
\begin{equation}
E_{v}[n] \ge E_{0}, \qquad E_{v}[n] = E_{0} \iff n = n_{0},
\label{eq:hk2}
\end{equation}
is formalized on the conditionally-complete-lattice substrate of $\mathbb{R}$ as
\thm{hohenberg\_kohn\_variational}: the lower bound is the extremal property of an infimum, and the
equality characterization is the (load-bearing) non-degeneracy of the ground state.

\subsection{Levy--Lieb constrained search}
The Levy--Lieb functional $F_{\mathrm{LL}}[n] = \inf_{\psi \mapsto n}\langle\psi|T+V_{ee}|\psi\rangle$
makes the Hohenberg--Kohn universal functional constructive on every $N$-representable density. We
verify that it is a lower bound for the kinetic-plus-interaction energy of any state with the given
density (\thm{levyLieb\_functional}) and that it agrees with the Hohenberg--Kohn functional on
$v$-representable densities (\thm{levyLieb\_eq\_HK\_on\_vrep}), the constrained-search infimum attained
at the ground state.

\subsection{Scope of the DFT formalization}
\label{sec:dft-scope}

We state plainly what these four theorems do and do not establish, because the
distinction is easy to lose. \thm{hohenberg\_kohn\_uniqueness},
\thm{hohenberg\_kohn\_variational}, \thm{levyLieb\_functional} and
\thm{levyLieb\_eq\_HK\_on\_vrep} are proved over the abstract structures
\thm{GroundStateData}, \thm{DensityVariational} and \thm{LevyLiebData}. Those
structures carry the strict Rayleigh--Ritz inequalities, the non-degeneracy of the
ground state, and the constrained-search decomposition \emph{as fields}: they are
hypotheses of the statements, not conclusions of them. The theorems are therefore
faithful formal renderings of the DFT \emph{arguments} (each derives its
conclusion from those inputs by exactly the reasoning the textbook proof uses)
and they are kernel-pure. They are not derivations of the inequalities from a
Hamiltonian.

Two consequences follow, and we state both. First, no inhabitant of these three
structures is constructed anywhere in this development, so the theorems are not
yet instantiated at any concrete system. Second, nothing connects them to
\S\ref{subsec:molecular}'s \thm{molecularHamiltonian\_essSelfAdjoint}; the Lean
modules say so at
\thm{HohenbergKohnUniqueness.lean:19-21} and
\thm{HohenbergKohnVariational.lean:26-27}, and this paper should have said so too.
Supplying an inhabitant from the molecular Hamiltonian, which requires the
Rayleigh--Ritz inequalities as \emph{theorems} about that operator, is the
substantive next step, and it is not claimed here.

By contrast \S\ref{subsec:molecular}'s unconditional self-adjointness result takes no
analytic hypothesis of this kind: it is a theorem about the operator itself.

\section{Open-system dynamics}
\label{sec:lindblad}

\subsection{The GKSL generator and dissipator complete positivity}
The Markovian master equation $\dot\rho = \mathcal{L}(\rho)$ has the GKSL generator
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\rho) = -i[H,\rho] + \sum_{k}\Bigl(L_{k}\rho L_{k}^{\dagger}
- \tfrac{1}{2}\{L_{k}^{\dagger}L_{k},\,\rho\}\Bigr),
\label{eq:gksl}
\end{equation}
formalized as three linear pieces on $\mathrm{Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C})$ (\thm{lindbladGenerator}), built from
\texttt{LinearMap} combinators so that linearity is automatic. The dissipative jump map
$\rho \mapsto \sum_{k} L_{k}\rho L_{k}^{\dagger}$ is the completely-positive engine of the dynamics:
\thm{lindblad\_generator\_CP} certifies its complete positivity through the PhysLib Choi route
(\texttt{MatrixMap.of\_kraus\_CP}, whose proof exhibits the Choi matrix as a sum of rank-one
positive-semidefinite blocks). We are explicit that complete positivity is asserted for the
\emph{dissipator}; the full generator $\mathcal{L}$ is only conditionally completely positive.

\subsection{Structure theorem}
The canonical form \thm{gksl\_canonical\_form} assembles the explicit pointwise action of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gksl}, and trace preservation \thm{gksl\_trace\_preserving} establishes
$\mathrm{tr}\,\mathcal{L}(\rho) = 0$ for every $\rho$: the unitary part is traceless by cyclicity, and
the dissipative gain $\sum_{k}\mathrm{tr}(L_{k}^{\dagger}L_{k}\rho)$ exactly cancels the anticommutator
loss. Trace preservation is what makes the propagator map density matrices to density matrices.

\subsection{Markovian semigroup}
Because $\mathcal{L}$ is a superoperator and the Liouvillian is non-Hermitian, the propagator
$\Lambda_{t} = e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ is taken as the matrix exponential of the \emph{vectorized}
Liouvillian (\thm{lindbladLiouvillian}, via \texttt{LinearMap.toMatrix}), using Mathlib's
\texttt{Matrix.exp} machinery, \emph{not} a Hermitian functional calculus, which does not apply to
the non-Hermitian generator. We verify $\Lambda_{0}=\mathbf{1}$ (\thm{lindblad\_propagator\_zero}) and
the semigroup law
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{t}\,\Lambda_{s} = \Lambda_{t+s}
\label{eq:semigroup}
\end{equation}
(\thm{lindblad\_semigroup}), from \texttt{Matrix.exp\_add\_of\_commute} applied to the commuting scalar
multiples $t\mathcal{L}$, $s\mathcal{L}$. Trace-distance contractivity (data processing),
$D(\Lambda_{t}\rho,\Lambda_{t}\sigma) \le D(\rho,\sigma)$ (\thm{traceDist\_lindblad\_monotone}), follows
from the project's CPTP data-processing inequality once the propagator is realized by a Kraus channel;
that CPTP realization, the content of the GKSL completely-positive-semigroup theorem, is now
\emph{discharged} rather than disclosed: the propagator is proven completely positive and trace
preserving, so a Kraus realization is extracted, and \thm{traceDist\_lindblad\_monotone} takes no
disclosed CPTP-realization argument. To our
knowledge this is the first Lindblad generator and Markovian dynamical semigroup in any proof
assistant, as distinct from the static CPTP/channel formalizations already present in \Lean{}, Coq, and
Isabelle.

\subsection{A certified damped two-level model}
A concrete amplitude-damping model ($d=2$, $H=0$, single jump $L=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\sigma_{-}$) instantiates
the generator. The excited-state population obeys $\dot p_{e} = -\gamma p_{e}$, verified directly from
the canonical form as $(\mathcal{L}\rho)_{11} = -\gamma\,\rho_{11}$
(\thm{dampedTwoLevel\_generator\_decay\_rate}); the factor $p_{0}e^{-\gamma t}$ is verified to solve this
rate equation (\thm{dampedTwoLevel\_population\_solves\_rate}); and the decay factor admits the kernel-only
rational enclosure $1-\gamma t \le e^{-\gamma t} \le 1/(1+\gamma t)$
(\thm{dampedTwoLevel\_decay\_envelope}), with a \texttt{norm\_num} operating point at $\gamma t = \tfrac12$.

\section{Synthesis, scope, and limitations}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The three layers share one verified substrate (PhysLib's unbounded-operator resolvent and spectral
theory underpins both the NEGF Green's functions of Sec.~\ref{sec:negf} and the molecular
self-adjointness of Sec.~\ref{sec:dft}, while PhysLib's CPTP/Choi quantum-information layer underpins
the open-system dynamics of Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}) and one methodological posture, the two-layer
separation of verified formula from cited identification. Together they constitute a kernel-checked
reference layer for the structural theorems that the electronic-structure and open-system simulation
literatures rely on.

We are explicit about what is \emph{not} claimed. The DFT self-adjointness apex is now unconditional:
its formerly disclosed analytic inputs (kinetic essential self-adjointness; the Coulomb relative bound,
proven here by an in-tree Fourier--Kato development rather than assumed) are discharged as kernel-pure
theorems, as is the open-system contractivity's formerly disclosed CPTP-realization hypothesis. The only
disclosed inputs that remain are the semibounded-functional inputs of the variational and Levy--Lieb
theorems; none is an axiom, each is a tracked \Lean{} \texttt{Prop}. No
result asserts a numerical magnitude beyond what a rational enclosure certifies, and no model is claimed
to be the dynamics of a specific physical device beyond the cited identification.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have formalized, kernel-pure and without project-local axioms, the foundational theorems of three
pillars of computational quantum physics (NEGF/Landauer transport, Hohenberg--Kohn/Levy--Lieb DFT,
and the Lindblad/GKSL open-system formalism) in a single verified stack on \Lean{}/Mathlib/PhysLib.
The molecular self-adjointness apex and the open-system contractivity are now fully unconditional, their
formerly disclosed analytic inputs discharged as kernel-pure theorems (the Coulomb relative bound via an
in-tree Fourier--Kato development with a core-to-closure relative-boundedness extension; the CPTP
realization of the Markovian propagator via complete positivity and trace preservation). The natural next
steps are to discharge the remaining semibounded-functional inputs of the variational/Levy--Lieb
theorems and to connect the transport and open-system layers through a verified treatment of Markovian
quantum transport.
