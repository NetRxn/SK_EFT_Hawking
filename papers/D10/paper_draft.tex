\title{Kernel-Verified Foundations of Quantum Transport and Open-System Dynamics}

\author{John Roehm}

\begin{abstract}
Structural theorems are the load-bearing layer of computational quantum physics: they are derived
once, in the review literature, and then relied upon by simulation codes that never revisit them.
We report a machine-checked formalization, in the \Lean{} proof assistant with Mathlib and the
PhysLib physics library, of three such layers, and we are equally explicit about a fourth that we
attempted and did \emph{not} achieve. Verified: (i) non-equilibrium Green's-function (NEGF) quantum
transport --- the retarded and advanced Green's functions, the spectral relation
$A = G^{R}\Gamma G^{A}$, positive semidefiniteness of the spectral function, the
Meir--Wingreen transmission, the Landauer--B\"uttiker linear-response conductance, and a
conductance-quantization bound stated as a refutation; (ii) \emph{unconditional} essential
self-adjointness of the molecular many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian, via a Kato--Rellich development
built in-tree --- both of its formerly disclosed analytic inputs, kinetic essential self-adjointness
and the Coulomb relative bound $a<1$, are discharged as proven kernel-pure theorems, the latter
through a Fourier--Kato argument and a core-to-closure relative-boundedness extension that Mathlib
does not supply; and (iii) open-system dynamics --- the
Gorini--Kossakowski--Sudarshan--Lindblad generator, complete positivity of its dissipator, the
structure theorem, the Markovian semigroup $e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ as a matrix exponential of the
vectorized non-Hermitian Liouvillian, and unconditional trace-distance contractivity. Not achieved:
our Hohenberg--Kohn and Levy--Lieb declarations are order-theoretic renderings of the \emph{shape} of
those statements over abstract data structures that carry the variational content as hypotheses; no
inhabitant of those structures is constructed, nothing connects them to the molecular Hamiltonian of
(ii), and we therefore make no density-functional-theory novelty claim. Section~\ref{sec:dft-scope}
states this in full. Every theorem reported as verified is kernel-pure --- axiom profile exactly
$\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},\,\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$ --- with no project-local
axioms, no \texttt{sorry}, and no floating-point arithmetic anywhere.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A simulation code that computes a transport spectrum, a molecular ground state, or a dissipative
trajectory does not re-derive the theorem that licenses it. It inherits it. The Landauer picture of
coherent conductance, the essential self-adjointness that makes a molecular Hamiltonian's spectrum
real, and the GKSL form of Markovian master equations are each proved once in a review or a
monograph, and are thereafter used as infrastructure by literatures far larger than the derivations
themselves. That is an efficient division of labour, and it has one structural weakness: the
inherited layer is exactly the layer nobody re-checks.

These foundations are not soft targets. They involve unbounded operators on infinite-dimensional
Hilbert spaces, where the distinction between symmetric and self-adjoint decides whether the
dynamics is unitary at all; variational principles over admissible densities, where the
existence of the infimum is itself a theorem; and complete positivity of superoperators, where the
difference between positivity and complete positivity is the difference between a physical channel
and an artifact. A formally verified statement of each, machine-checked to the logical kernel, gives
the downstream literature a reference layer that is unambiguous by construction: the statement is
the statement, the hypotheses are visible in the signature, and nothing is left to the reader's
reconstruction of what an author meant.

\subsection{What this paper contains}

We present such a layer, built in \Lean{}~4~\cite{lean4} on Mathlib~\cite{mathlib} and the PhysLib
physics library. It spans \bundleDecls{} declarations across \bundleModules{} \Lean{} modules,
reached from this paper's declared apex theorems.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{NEGF quantum transport} (Sec.~\ref{sec:negf}): the retarded and advanced Green's
functions on a Hermitian device Hamiltonian, the NEGF spectral relation
$A = G^{R}\Gamma G^{A}$~\cite{datta1995}, positive semidefiniteness of the spectral function, the
Meir--Wingreen transmission~\cite{meirWingreen1992}, the Landauer--B\"uttiker conductance
$G = G_{0}\!\int T(E)(-\partial_{E}f)\,dE$~\cite{landauer1957,buttiker1986}, and a
conductance-quantization certificate.
\item \textbf{The molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian} (Sec.~\ref{sec:molecular}): unconditional essential
self-adjointness, via an in-tree Kato--Rellich development~\cite{kato1995,reedSimon2}, with the
Coulomb relative bound established rather than assumed.
\item \textbf{Open-system dynamics} (Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}): the GKSL
generator~\cite{gksl1976,lindblad1976}, complete positivity of its dissipator through the Choi
route, trace preservation, the canonical form, the Markovian semigroup, and unconditional
trace-distance contractivity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What this paper does not contain, stated up front}
\label{sec:not-contained}

This development also contains \Lean{} declarations named for the Hohenberg--Kohn and Levy--Lieb
theorems of density-functional theory. They are not what their names suggest, and we
have placed our account of them in Sec.~\ref{sec:dft-scope} rather than among the results, because
they belong to the paper's limitations and not to its contributions. In summary: they are proved
over abstract data structures that carry the Rayleigh--Ritz inequalities, the non-degeneracy of the
ground state, and the constrained-search decomposition \emph{as fields}; two of the three conjuncts
of our Hohenberg--Kohn variational statement are those fields returned unchanged; the Levy--Lieb
declarations reduce to the fact that an infimum is a lower bound; no inhabitant of any of the three
structures is constructed anywhere in the development; and nothing connects them to the molecular
Hamiltonian of Sec.~\ref{sec:molecular}. We make no DFT novelty claim, and a reader looking for
verified density-functional theory should read Sec.~\ref{sec:dft-scope} and then look elsewhere.

We state this in the abstract and again here, before the results, because the failure mode we are
most concerned to avoid is the one where a scope caveat sits in a late subsection that a reader
reaches after having already formed a belief from the title.

\subsection{Novelty, stated honestly}

A prior-art reconnaissance across the interactive-theorem-prover (ITP) ecosystem
(\Lean{}/Mathlib, Coq/Rocq, Isabelle/HOL including the Archive of Formal Proofs, Agda, HOL Light,
Mizar; see Ref.~\cite{survey2021} and the records archived with this work) found no existing
formalization of the transport or open-system results. To our knowledge this is the first
machine-checked NEGF Green's-function and Landauer-\emph{conductance} transport, the first
\emph{unconditional} essential self-adjointness of a molecular many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian, and
the first Lindblad \emph{generator and Markovian dynamical semigroup}, in any proof assistant. We
claim nothing of the kind for density-functional theory (Sec.~\ref{sec:not-contained}).

Two carve-outs are essential to the third claim and we make them explicitly. First, quantum
\emph{channels} --- completely-positive trace-preserving (CPTP) maps, Choi matrices, superoperators
--- already exist in \Lean{}'s PhysLib quantum-information layer~\cite{leanQI2025}, in Coq's
CoqQ~\cite{coqq2022}, and in Isabelle/AFP~\cite{isabelleCBO}. Our contribution in
Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad} is the \emph{generator} $\mathcal{L} = -i[H,\cdot] + \mathcal{D}$, the
complete positivity of the dissipator $\mathcal{D}$, and the one-parameter semigroup $e^{t\mathcal{L}}$
built on that channel substrate --- not the static channel theory, which we use. Second, Mathlib
carries a substantial unbounded-operator and perturbation-theory development; our self-adjointness
claim is for the molecular many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian specifically, established through a
Kato--Rellich argument we build in-tree because Mathlib does not supply essential self-adjointness
in the form required, and it is not a claim about abstract perturbation theory.

\subsection{Two-layer honesty}

Every claim separates a \emph{verified formula layer} from a \emph{cited identification layer}. The
kernel verifies the algebra and the analysis: that the spectral function is positive semidefinite,
that the Coulomb Hamiltonian is essentially self-adjoint, that the dissipator is completely
positive. It does \emph{not} certify that a given matrix is the Hamiltonian of a particular
nanowire, that a given potential describes a particular molecule, or that a given jump operator
realizes a particular bath. Those identifications remain the province of the cited physics
literature, and we flag them at each point of use rather than in a single disclaimer.

\section{Verification framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{The kernel-purity bar}

All results are \Lean{}~4 theorems checked against Mathlib (toolchain
\texttt{leanprover/lean4:v\leantoolchain}) and PhysLib, the latter supplying the unbounded-operator
resolvent and spectral API used in Secs.~\ref{sec:negf}--\ref{sec:molecular} and the CPTP/Choi
quantum-information layer used in Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}.

We hold every theorem reported here to a \emph{kernel-purity} bar: its transitive axiom dependency,
as reported by \Lean{}'s \texttt{\#print axioms}, is exactly
$\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},\,\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$ --- the three standard
classical axioms underlying Mathlib --- with \emph{no} project-local axioms, no \texttt{sorry}, no
\texttt{native\_decide}, and no proof that relies on raising the elaborator's heartbeat limit. The
last of these is a proof-architecture constraint rather than a logical one: a proof that needs the
heartbeat limit raised is a proof that has not been decomposed, and we treat it as a defect.

\subsection{What a discharged hypothesis means here}

Several theorems in this development were first shipped with \emph{disclosed tracked hypotheses} ---
\Lean{} \texttt{Prop}s appearing as arguments, never axioms --- which scoped their conditional
content explicitly. Two have since been discharged, and the distinction matters enough to state
mechanically rather than rhetorically.

A hypothesis is discharged when the theorem's \emph{signature no longer contains it}. This is
checkable by reading the signature, and it is the only sense in which we use the word. The molecular
self-adjointness apex \thm{molecularHamiltonian\_essSelfAdjoint} takes the electron count $N$, the
mass $m$, a positivity proof $0<m$, and the nuclei --- and nothing else. The open-system
contractivity result \thm{traceDist\_lindblad\_monotone} takes a Hermitian Hamiltonian, the jump
operators, $0 \le t$, and two Hermitian states --- and nothing else. Neither takes an
analytic-hypothesis argument, and the earlier conditional forms are retained in the tree under their
own names so that the supersession is auditable rather than asserted.

The converse case is Sec.~\ref{sec:dft-scope}'s, and it is why we insist on the signature test: a
statement can be kernel-pure, sorry-free, and unconditional in its axiom profile while still carrying
its entire physical content in the fields of a structure passed as an argument. Kernel purity is a
statement about what a proof depends on, not about what a theorem says.

\subsection{Rational enclosures instead of floating point}

Quantitative claims are backed by rational \emph{enclosures} rather than floating-point evaluation.
Where a transcendental such as $e^{-\gamma t}$ appears in a bound, we bracket it between rational
endpoints (Sec.~\ref{sec:damped}), so that the bound is itself kernel-checkable by
\texttt{norm\_num} and no floating-point arithmetic enters the verified layer at any point.

\section{Non-equilibrium Green's-function transport}
\label{sec:negf}

\subsection{Green's functions and the spectral relation}
\label{sec:negf-green}

For a Hermitian device Hamiltonian $H$ on a finite site basis, we define the retarded and advanced
resolvent arguments
\begin{equation}
(G^{R})^{-1} = (E + i\eta)\mathbf{1} - H,
\qquad
(G^{A})^{-1} = (E - i\eta)\mathbf{1} - H ,
\label{eq:negf-args}
\end{equation}
and verify that the retarded argument is invertible for every $\eta>0$
(\thm{retardedArg\_isUnit}), which is what makes $G^{R}(E) = \bigl((E+i\eta)\mathbf{1}-H\bigr)^{-1}$
well defined rather than formal. The proof is by exhibiting
$(G^{R})^{-1}(G^{A})^{-1} = \eta^{2}\mathbf{1} + (H - E\mathbf{1})^{2}$, a sum of a positive scalar
and a square of a Hermitian matrix, hence invertible. We then verify
$G^{A} = (G^{R})^{\dagger}$ for Hermitian $H$ (\thm{gAdvanced\_eq\_conjTranspose}), so the advanced
function is not a second definition but a consequence of the first.

The spectral function $A = i(G^{R} - G^{A})$ satisfies the NEGF relation
\begin{equation}
A(E) \;=\; G^{R}(E)\,\Gamma\,G^{A}(E)
\label{eq:negf-spectral}
\end{equation}
(\thm{spectralFn\_eq\_gR\_broadening\_gA}), and is positive semidefinite
(\thm{spectralFn\_posSemidef}) --- the operator-level statement that the density of states is
non-negative. The positivity proof is structural rather than spectral: $A$ reduces to
$2\eta\,G^{R}(G^{R})^{\dagger}$, which is a non-negative scalar times a matrix of the manifestly
positive-semidefinite form $MM^{\dagger}$.

\paragraph{Scope of Eq.~\eqref{eq:negf-spectral}: the self-energy is structureless.}
We state precisely what is verified here, because the notation invites a stronger reading than the
theorem supports. In this development the self-energy is not a free parameter. It is
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(\eta) = -i\eta\,\mathbf{1},
\qquad\text{whence}\qquad
\Gamma = i(\Sigma - \Sigma^{\dagger}) = 2\eta\,\mathbf{1},
\label{eq:negf-selfenergy}
\end{equation}
a scalar multiple of the identity. The Dyson-form identity
$(G^{R})^{-1} = E\mathbf{1} - H - \Sigma$ is proved for exactly this $\Sigma$
(\thm{retardedArg\_eq\_sub\_selfEnergy}), and Eq.~\eqref{eq:negf-spectral} is therefore
established in the structureless, energy-independent broadening limit --- the wide-band limit with
identical coupling on every site --- and \emph{not} for a general, site- and energy-dependent lead
self-energy. A referee should read Eq.~\eqref{eq:negf-spectral} with that restriction attached.
Extending the Green's-function layer to a general Hermitian-decomposable $\Sigma$ is a well-defined
next step and it is not done here.

The transmission layer of Sec.~\ref{sec:negf-landauer} carries no such restriction: its broadening
matrices are arbitrary.

\subsection{Meir--Wingreen transmission and the Landauer conductance}
\label{sec:negf-landauer}

The Meir--Wingreen transmission is
\begin{equation}
T(E) = \mathrm{tr}\bigl(\Gamma_{L}\,G^{R}\,\Gamma_{R}\,G^{A}\bigr),
\label{eq:meir-wingreen}
\end{equation}
defined for arbitrary matrices $\Gamma_{L}, \Gamma_{R}$ and an arbitrary $G^{R}$. Two properties
make it a transmission probability rather than merely a trace.

First, $T(E)$ is real whenever the lead broadenings are Hermitian
(\thm{transmission\_isReal}): the statement verified is that complex conjugation fixes it,
$\overline{T} = T$, proved by moving the conjugation through the trace as a conjugate-transpose and
using Hermiticity of $\Gamma_{L}$ and $\Gamma_{R}$. No assumption is placed on $G^{R}$.

Second, $T(E) \ge 0$ whenever the broadenings are positive semidefinite
(\thm{transmission\_nonneg}). The proof factors $\Gamma_{R} = BB^{\dagger}$ and rewrites the trace as
$\mathrm{tr}\bigl((G^{R}B)^{\dagger}\,\Gamma_{L}\,(G^{R}B)\bigr)$, a congruence of a
positive-semidefinite matrix, which is non-negative. The factorization step is itself in-tree: we
build the symmetric spectral square root $B = U\,\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda})\,U^{\dagger}$ by hand
from Mathlib's \texttt{IsHermitian.spectral\_theorem} (\thm{posSemidef\_exists\_mul\_conjTranspose}),
because the continuous-functional-calculus square root is unavailable at this type without a
C$^{\star}$-operator-norm instance that conflicts with the entrywise matrix norms. This is the
"dual-norm characterization Mathlib lacks" that the route avoids.

The linear-response conductance is
\begin{equation}
G \;=\; G_{0}\!\int T(E)\,\bigl(-\partial_{E} f(E)\bigr)\,dE ,
\qquad G_{0} = \frac{2e^{2}}{h},
\label{eq:landauer}
\end{equation}
with $f$ the Fermi--Dirac distribution. Equation~\eqref{eq:landauer} is our \emph{definition} of
\texttt{landauerConductance}, not a theorem; we make the distinction because the substantive content
sits in the two results that follow it.

The Fermi window $-\partial_{E}f$ integrates to unity for every $\beta>0$
(\thm{fermiWindow\_integral\_eq\_one}). This is the improper fundamental theorem of calculus applied
to $f$, using $f(-\infty)=1$ and $f(+\infty)=0$, and it is the only genuinely analytic step in the
transport layer. With it, the wide-band limit follows unconditionally: for an energy-independent
transmission $T_{0}$,
\begin{equation}
G = G_{0}\,T_{0}
\qquad (\beta > 0),
\label{eq:wideband}
\end{equation}
with no normalization hypothesis left in the signature
(\thm{landauerConductance\_const\_transmission'}). The conditional form that takes the
normalization as an argument is retained separately, so the discharge is visible in the tree.

\subsection{The conductance-quantization certificate}
\label{sec:negf-quantization}

For $n$ channels with transmissions $\tau_{i}$, the channel conductance is
$G = G_{0}\sum_{i}\tau_{i}$, and
\begin{equation}
G \;\le\; n\,G_{0}
\label{eq:quantum-bound}
\end{equation}
(\thm{channelConductance\_le\_quantum}), with equality when every channel is fully open
(\thm{conductance\_quantization}). We record one respect in which the verified statement is
\emph{stronger} than the physical framing usually given: the theorem assumes only $\tau_{i}\le 1$
and $G_{0}\ge 0$. It does \emph{not} require $\tau_{i}\ge 0$. The bound is therefore a statement
about the sum of quantities capped at unity, and it holds on a strictly larger domain than the
physical range $\tau_{i}\in[0,1]$ would give it.

Equation~\eqref{eq:quantum-bound} is also stated as a refutation: a conductance strictly exceeding
$n\,G_{0}$ is impossible (\thm{conductance\_quantization\_falsifier}), with a concrete
\texttt{norm\_num}-backed witness --- a two-channel point contact cannot carry three conductance
quanta (\thm{two\_channel\_no\_three\_quanta}). We should be plain that the falsifier is the
contrapositive of Eq.~\eqref{eq:quantum-bound} and carries no content the bound does not already
have; its value is that it fixes the direction in which the claim is refutable, and the two-channel
instance makes that refutation concrete and arithmetically checkable rather than schematic. The
certificate connects back to the integral picture of Eq.~\eqref{eq:landauer} through
\thm{channelConductance\_eq\_landauer}.

\section{The molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:molecular}

This section contains the paper's strongest result, and the one whose route required the most
infrastructure that did not previously exist.

\subsection{Why self-adjointness is the precondition}

The $N$-electron molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian $H = T + V$, with kinetic term $T$ and the
electron--nucleus plus electron--electron Coulomb potential $V$, is an unbounded operator. For such
an operator, symmetry is cheap and self-adjointness is not, and the gap between them is physical: a
symmetric operator that is not essentially self-adjoint has no unique self-adjoint extension, hence
no well-defined unitary dynamics $e^{-iHt}$ and no guarantee of a real spectrum. Every statement
about molecular energy levels presupposes that the gap has been closed.

\subsection{Kato--Rellich, built in-tree}

Mathlib does not supply unbounded-operator essential self-adjointness in the form required, so we
build the Kato--Rellich criterion in-tree: if $A$ is self-adjoint and $B$ is symmetric with
$A$-relative bound $a<1$, meaning
\begin{equation}
\|Bu\| \;\le\; a\,\|Au\| + b\,\|u\|
\qquad\text{for all } u \in \mathcal{D}(A),
\label{eq:relbound}
\end{equation}
then $A+B$ is self-adjoint. The apex theorem
\begin{equation}
\thm{molecularHamiltonian\_essSelfAdjoint} : \quad
\mathrm{IsSelfAdjoint}\bigl(\overline{T} + V\bigr)
\label{eq:apex}
\end{equation}
instantiates this for the closure of the kinetic operator plus the Coulomb potential. Its signature
takes the electron count $N$, the mass $m$, a proof that $0<m$, and the nuclei --- and nothing else.
There is no analytic-hypothesis argument and no tracked \texttt{Prop}. This is what we mean by
unconditional, and it is checkable by reading the signature.

Reaching it required discharging three inputs, each now a proven kernel-pure theorem.

\subsection{Input (i): symmetry of the potential}

The potential operator is multiplication by a real-valued function, so its symmetry reduces to
measurability of the Coulomb sum (\thm{molecularPotentialOperator\_isSymmetric}). This is the
easiest of the three and we note it for completeness.

\subsection{Input (ii): the kinetic operator's self-adjoint closure}

PhysLib's kinetic operator is a real scalar multiple of the momentum-square operator, so essential
self-adjointness reduces, by a scaling argument on closures, to the momentum-square operator's
self-adjoint closure (\thm{kineticOperator\_isSelfAdjoint\_closure}). The route runs through a
dense-range criterion --- a densely defined symmetric $S$ whose $S \pm i\mu$ have dense range has
self-adjoint closure --- which we established separately, and which avoids needing a
Plancherel-unitary bridge that would have been considerably more expensive.

\subsection{Input (iii): the Coulomb relative bound $a<1$}
\label{sec:coulomb-bound}

This is the substantive one. The statement verified is existential:
\begin{equation}
\exists\, a, b : \quad 0 \le a \;\wedge\; a < 1 \;\wedge\; 0 \le b \;\wedge\;
\mathrm{IsRelBounded}\,\bigl(\overline{T},\, V,\, a,\, b\bigr)
\label{eq:coulomb-relbound}
\end{equation}
(\thm{coulomb\_isRelBounded}). The existential quantifier is the point: the constants are
\emph{produced} by the proof, not supplied as parameters by the caller. A version that took $a$ and
$b$ as hypotheses would have been a considerably weaker theorem wearing the same name.

The argument is a Fourier--Kato development rather than the from-scratch Hardy inequality one might
expect. A single-electron relative bound is obtained from the momentum-space
$L^{\infty}$--$L^{2}$ estimate with an $\varepsilon$-dilation, then lifted to each molecular
electron--nucleus and electron--electron term by a measure-preserving coordinate split and a
Tonelli/lower-integral Minkowski reassembly, and summed over the finitely many terms with
$\varepsilon$ chosen so that the total bound falls below $\tfrac12$. Establishing it below
$\tfrac12$ rather than merely below $1$ costs nothing extra in the argument and leaves margin.

That bound is established on the Schwartz core. Transferring it to the kinetic operator's closure ---
which is what Eq.~\eqref{eq:relbound} must hold on for Kato--Rellich to apply --- is a separate
theorem, and Mathlib does not have it.

\subsection{The core-to-closure extension}
\label{sec:extend-closure}

We prove that relative boundedness on a core extends to the operator's closure
(\thm{IsRelBounded.extend\_to\_closure}), given that the dominating operator is closable and the
dominated one is closed. The proof is a graph-limit argument: a vector in the closure's domain is a
limit of core vectors along which both the vectors and their images converge, the bound passes to
the limit by continuity of the norm, and closedness of the dominated operator identifies the limit
of the images as the image of the limit.

We flag this as the piece of the development most likely to be reusable outside this paper. It is
stated for a general pair of unbounded operators, it makes no reference to Coulomb potentials or to
molecular systems, and any Kato--Rellich application that establishes its bound on a convenient core
--- which is the normal situation --- needs exactly this step to reach the closure. It is
Mathlib-grade infrastructure that Mathlib happens not to carry, and we built it because the molecular
result could not be reached without it.

\subsection{The supersession trail}

The earlier conditional apex, which disclosed the potential-symmetry and relative-bound hypotheses as
arguments, is retained in the tree as
\thm{molecularHamiltonian\_essSelfAdjoint\_of\_hpot\_hrel}. We keep superseded forms rather than
deleting them so that a reader can check the discharge by comparing signatures, instead of taking
our word that a hypothesis was removed.

To our knowledge Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex} is the first formalization of \emph{unconditional} essential
self-adjointness of a molecular many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian in any proof assistant.
