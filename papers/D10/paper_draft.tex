\title{Kernel Purity Is Not Aboutness: Unconditional Self-Adjointness of the
Molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian, and Three Formal Layers Measured by Inhabitance}

\author{John Roehm}

\begin{abstract}
We prove, machine-checked to the logical kernel and without hypotheses, that the $N$-electron
molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian is essentially self-adjoint. The route is a Kato--Rellich development
built from scratch in the \Lean{} proof assistant: a single-electron momentum-space estimate with a
dilation parameter, lifted to every electron--nucleus and electron--electron term by a
measure-preserving coordinate split and a lower-integral reassembly, summed with the dilation chosen
so the relative bound falls below $\tfrac12$, then transferred from the Schwartz core to the kinetic
operator's closure by a graph-limit extension lemma that Mathlib does not contain and that we state
for arbitrary unbounded operators. To our knowledge this is the first unconditional formalization of
essential self-adjointness of a molecular many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian in any proof assistant. We
report it alongside two further formalized layers ---
non-equilibrium Green's-function transport and Lindblad open-system dynamics --- and one we could not
complete, the Hohenberg--Kohn and Levy--Lieb variational theorems of density-functional theory. The
comparison is the paper's second contribution. All four layers are kernel-pure, with axiom profile
exactly $\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},\,\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$ and no
\texttt{sorry}; three terminate in a constructed witness and one does not. We argue that this last
property, not the axiom profile, distinguishes a formalization that is about a physical system from
one that is about the shape of a statement, and that the layer failing the test is the most
physically celebrated of the four for an identifiable and general reason: variational inequalities
are cheap as hypotheses and expensive as theorems about an operator. Our self-adjointness result is
the expensive direction, and is exactly what the abstract variational layer does not sit on.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The $N$-electron molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
H \;=\; \mathcal{T} + \mathcal{V},
\qquad
\mathcal{V}(x) = -\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{p}\frac{Z_{p}}{|x_{i}-R_{p}|}
\;+\; \sum_{i<j}\frac{1}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|},
\label{eq:molecular-H}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{T}$ the kinetic operator, $x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ the electron coordinates and
$(R_{p},Z_{p})$ the nuclear positions and charges, is an unbounded operator; and for unbounded
operators symmetry is cheap while self-adjointness is not. The gap between them is physical rather
than technical. A symmetric operator that is not essentially self-adjoint admits no unique
self-adjoint extension, hence no well-defined unitary dynamics $e^{-iHt}$ and no guarantee that its
spectrum is real. Every statement about molecular energy levels presupposes that the gap has been
closed, and the closing is Kato's theorem.

Our first result is a machine-checked proof of that closure, carrying no hypotheses:
\begin{equation}
\thm{molecularHamiltonian\_essSelfAdjoint} :
\quad \mathrm{IsSelfAdjoint}\bigl(\overline{\mathcal{T}} + \mathcal{V}\bigr),
\label{eq:apex}
\end{equation}
whose signature takes the electron count $N$, the mass $m$, a proof that $0<m$, and the nuclei ---
and nothing else. There is no analytic-hypothesis argument, no tracked proposition, and no
project-local axiom. To our knowledge it is the first unconditional formalization of essential
self-adjointness of a molecular many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian in any proof assistant.
Section~\ref{sec:molecular} is its proof, and it is the longest section here because reaching
Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex} required building infrastructure that did not exist.

\subsection{The second contribution: what kernel purity does not measure}
\label{sec:inhabitance-thesis}

Formalizing physics has two costs, and they are routinely conflated. One is proving the theorem. The
other is connecting the theorem to the object it is supposed to be about. The second is the binding
cost, and it is invisible in the report that formalization projects habitually publish.

A \Lean{} declaration can be kernel-pure --- depending on nothing beyond the three standard classical
axioms, containing no \texttt{sorry}, passing every mechanical check a verification pipeline applies
--- and still establish nothing whatever about any physical system. The mechanism is mundane. One
defines a structure whose fields are the physical content, states a theorem over that structure, and
derives the conclusion from the fields. The derivation is honest and the kernel is satisfied. But if
nothing ever inhabits the structure, the theorem has been proved about a shape, not about a system.

The diagnostic that separates the two cases is not the axiom profile. It is whether anything inhabits
the structure: whether the development, somewhere, constructs a witness and thereby closes the loop
from the abstract statement back to a concrete object about which something falsifiable can be said.

This paper is in an unusually good position to make that argument, because it contains both outcomes,
built to one standard by one author in one development. Three of its layers close the loop. One does
not. And the one that does not is the most physically celebrated of them.

\subsection{The four layers}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian} (Sec.~\ref{sec:molecular}): unconditional essential
self-adjointness, Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex}, via an in-tree Kato--Rellich development. A theorem about the
operator itself, with no abstract carrier between statement and system.
\item \textbf{The DFT variational layer} (Sec.~\ref{sec:dft-scope}): Hohenberg--Kohn uniqueness and
the variational principle, and the Levy--Lieb constrained-search functional --- proved over abstract
structures carrying the Rayleigh--Ritz inequalities, the non-degeneracy of the ground state and the
constrained-search decomposition \emph{as fields}. \textbf{Nothing inhabits those structures, and
nothing connects them to Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex}.} This is the negative case, and we make no
density-functional-theory novelty claim of any kind.
\item \textbf{NEGF quantum transport} (Sec.~\ref{sec:negf}): a chain from resolvent invertibility to
a \texttt{norm\_num}-checked refutation that a two-channel point contact can carry three conductance
quanta. The loop closes at a falsifier.
\item \textbf{Open-system dynamics} (Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}): a chain from the
Gorini--Kossakowski--Sudarshan--Lindblad generator to a constructed amplitude-damping instance with a
rational decay enclosure. The loop closes at an instance.
\end{enumerate}

The development spans \bundleDecls{} declarations across \bundleModules{} \Lean{} modules, reached
from this paper's declared apex theorems.

\subsection{Two-layer honesty}

Every claim separates a \emph{verified formula layer} from a \emph{cited identification layer}. The
kernel verifies algebra and analysis: that the Coulomb Hamiltonian is essentially self-adjoint, that
the spectral function is positive semidefinite, that the dissipator is completely positive. It does
not certify that a given matrix is the Hamiltonian of a particular nanowire, that a given potential
describes a particular molecule, or that a given jump operator realizes a particular bath. Those
identifications remain with the cited physics literature, and we flag them where used rather than in
a single disclaimer.

\section{What a kernel-checked claim asserts}
\label{sec:framework}

This section fixes the measuring instrument before any measurement is taken.

\subsection{The purity bar}

All results are \Lean{}~4 theorems checked against Mathlib (toolchain
\texttt{leanprover/lean4:v\leantoolchain}) and the PhysLib physics library, the latter supplying the
unbounded-operator resolvent and spectral API used in Secs.~\ref{sec:molecular} and~\ref{sec:negf}
and the completely-positive trace-preserving (CPTP) and Choi-matrix layer used in
Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}.

Every theorem reported here has transitive axiom dependency, as given by \Lean{}'s
\texttt{\#print axioms}, exactly $\{\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Classical.choice},\,
\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$ --- the three standard classical axioms underlying Mathlib --- with no
project-local axioms, no \texttt{sorry}, no \texttt{native\_decide}, and no proof relying on a raised
elaborator heartbeat limit. The last is a proof-architecture constraint rather than a logical one: a
proof that needs the limit raised is a proof that has not been decomposed.

\subsection{Discharge is a property of the signature}

A hypothesis is discharged when the theorem's \emph{signature no longer contains it}. That is the
only sense in which we use the word, and it is checkable by reading the signature rather than by
trusting a narrative. Where this development supersedes an earlier conditional form, the conditional
form is retained in the tree under its own name, so a reader may compare signatures directly.

\subsection{Inhabitance}
\label{sec:inhabitance-def}

Purity and discharge are both properties of a proof. Neither is a property of the \emph{statement},
and neither detects the failure mode of Sec.~\ref{sec:inhabitance-thesis}.

We therefore report a third property. A layer is \emph{inhabited} when the development constructs a
witness of the data the layer quantifies over --- when some concrete object is exhibited satisfying
the layer's hypotheses, so that its conclusions apply to something. A layer that is pure, discharged
and uninhabited has proved a conditional whose antecedent is not known to be satisfiable by anything
in the development, and whose conclusions are therefore about a shape.

\subsection{Rational enclosures instead of floating point}

Quantitative claims are backed by rational \emph{enclosures}, never floating-point evaluation. Where
a transcendental such as $e^{-\gamma t}$ enters a bound, we bracket it between rational endpoints
(Sec.~\ref{sec:damped}), so the bound is decided by exact rational arithmetic. No floating-point
number enters the verified layer at any point.

\section{The molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:molecular}

\subsection{The criterion and what it demands}

The Kato--Rellich criterion states that if $A$ is self-adjoint and $B$ symmetric with $A$-relative
bound $a<1$ --- meaning that for some $b\ge0$
\begin{equation}
\|Bu\| \;\le\; a\,\|Au\| \;+\; b\,\|u\|
\qquad \text{for all } u\in\mathcal{D}(A)
\label{eq:relbound}
\end{equation}
--- then $A+B$ is self-adjoint. Mathlib does not supply essential self-adjointness in this form, so
we build the criterion in-tree.

Applying it to Eq.~\eqref{eq:molecular-H} requires three inputs, each now a proven kernel-pure
theorem rather than a hypothesis: that $\mathcal{V}$ is symmetric; that $\overline{\mathcal{T}}$ is
self-adjoint; and that Eq.~\eqref{eq:relbound} holds for $A=\overline{\mathcal{T}}$,
$B=\mathcal{V}$ with some $a<1$. The third is the substantial one, and the bar over $\mathcal{T}$ in
it is the reason Sec.~\ref{sec:extend-closure} exists.

\subsection{Symmetry of the potential}

$\mathcal{V}$ is multiplication by a real-valued function, so its symmetry reduces to measurability
of the Coulomb sum (\thm{molecularPotentialOperator\_isSymmetric}). We record it and move on.

\subsection{Self-adjointness of the kinetic closure}

PhysLib's kinetic operator is a nonzero real scalar multiple of the momentum-square operator, so by a
scaling argument on closures the question reduces to that operator
(\thm{kineticOperator\_isSelfAdjoint\_closure}). We establish it through a dense-range criterion: a
densely defined symmetric $S$ for which $S\pm i\mu$ have dense range has self-adjoint closure. The
route matters for cost --- it avoids needing a Plancherel-unitary bridge, which would have been
considerably more expensive than the dense-range argument.

\subsection{The single-electron seed estimate}
\label{sec:seed}

The chain begins with one electron and one Coulomb centre. For every dilation parameter
$\varepsilon>0$ there exists $C\ge0$ such that, for every Schwartz function
$v\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})$ and every centre $R\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl\|\,|y-R|^{-1}v\,\bigr\|_{2}
\;\le\;
\varepsilon\,\bigl\|\Delta v\bigr\|_{2}
\;+\;
C\,\|v\|_{2}
\label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
(\thm{exists\_coulomb\_relbound\_center}), where $\Delta$ is written in the development as the
momentum-square sum $\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}$. This is the momentum-space $L^{\infty}$--$L^{2}$ estimate
with an $\varepsilon$-dilation: the Coulomb singularity is split into a near and a far part, the near
part controlled in $L^{2}$ and the far part bounded, with $\varepsilon$ tuning the split. The crucial
feature for what follows is that $\varepsilon$ is \emph{arbitrary} and $C$ depends on it. We will
spend $\varepsilon$ later to buy a bound below $\tfrac12$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:seed} is uniform in the centre $R$, which is what lets it apply to every nucleus
at once. The uniformity comes from translation: we build a translated Schwartz function
(\thm{translateSchwartz}), verify translation is measure preserving
(\thm{measurePreserving\_translate}), and check it leaves each of the three integrals invariant
(\thm{integral\_coulombSq\_translate}, \thm{integral\_kineticSq\_translateSchwartz},
\thm{integral\_normSq\_translateSchwartz}). The centred estimate then transports to every $R$ without
re-proof.

\subsection{From one electron to $3N$ dimensions: the fibre split}
\label{sec:fibre}

Equation~\eqref{eq:seed} lives on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$. Each term of $\mathcal{V}$ is singular in exactly
one electron coordinate, or in one difference of two, while the state $u$ lives on $\mathbb{R}^{3N}$.
Bridging that gap is the technical heart of the development, and it is where the formalization
diverges most sharply from the textbook argument, which performs the reduction in a sentence.

We construct, for each electron index, a measure-preserving linear change of coordinates splitting
$\mathbb{R}^{3N}$ into the singular $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ fibre and the spectator directions, and verify
that it carries Schwartz functions to Schwartz functions. This requires showing the map has temperate
growth (\thm{hasTemperateGrowth\_fiber}), is an isometry (\thm{isometry\_fiber}) and is antilipschitz
(\thm{antilipschitz\_fiber}) --- the three conditions under which the induced composition operator is
well defined on $\mathcal{S}$ (\thm{fiberSchwartz}). None of this is difficult mathematics and all of
it is load-bearing: without it there is no object to which Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed} can be applied.

Reassembly is a Tonelli argument carried out in the extended non-negative reals. We work with lower
integrals rather than Bochner integrals through the middle of the chain
(\thm{molecular\_normSq\_lintegral}, \thm{molecular\_kineticSq\_lintegral},
\thm{lintegral\_z\_fiberKineticSq\_le}) because integrability of the fibre integrand is not available
until after the bound is established, whereas $[0,\infty]$-valued integrals need no integrability
side-condition to be manipulated. Measurability of the fibre integral
(\thm{measurable\_fiber\_lintegral}) licenses the Tonelli exchange, and Minkowski's inequality in
$L^{2}$ form recombines the fibres (\thm{coulombTerm\_relbound\_eLpNorm}). We return to Bochner
integrals only at the end, once integrability of the squared Coulomb integrand has itself been proved
(\thm{integrable\_coulombSq}) by the same near/far disjoint-support split used for
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed}.

\subsection{Bounding the block kinetic energy by the full kinetic energy}
\label{sec:cross-terms}

There is a step here the textbook argument does not have to take, and it is worth isolating.
Equation~\eqref{eq:seed} applied on a fibre bounds the Coulomb term by the kinetic energy \emph{of
that fibre's three directions}. Equation~\eqref{eq:relbound} requires a bound by the kinetic energy
of all $3N$.

These are not the same quantity and the inequality between them is not free: expanding the full
kinetic form produces cross terms between the block and the remaining directions
(\thm{fullKinetic\_eq\_block\_add\_rest}). We show the cross terms vanish, using self-adjointness of
each momentum component on $\mathcal{S}$ (\thm{momentumCLM\_self\_adjoint}) and commutativity of the
components (\thm{momentumCLM\_comm}) to rewrite the cross term as an inner product that cancels
(\thm{momentum\_cross\_term\_eq}, \thm{integral\_cross\_kinetic\_eq}). The block kinetic energy is
therefore genuinely dominated by the full kinetic energy (\thm{integral\_block\_kineticSq\_le}), and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed} may be summed.

\subsection{Summation, and spending $\varepsilon$}
\label{sec:summation}

With the fibre estimate available termwise, the electron--nucleus sum
(\thm{coulomb\_relbound\_nuclear}) and the electron--electron sum (\thm{coulomb\_relbound\_ee}) are
assembled and combined (\thm{coulomb\_relbound\_schwartz}). Both are finite, and each inherits the
same $(\varepsilon,C)$ pair from Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed}, so the combined bound carries the prefactor
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{tr}} \;=\; N\sum_{p}|Z_{p}| \;+\; \binom{N}{2},
\label{eq:ctr}
\end{equation}
the number of Coulomb terms weighted by nuclear charge.

Now $\varepsilon$ is spent. The development chooses
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon \;=\; \frac{1}{4m\,(C_{\mathrm{tr}}+1)},
\label{eq:eps-choice}
\end{equation}
which after the $\tfrac{1}{2m}$ of the kinetic operator gives relative bound
\begin{equation}
a \;=\; 2m\,C_{\mathrm{tr}}\,\varepsilon
\;=\; \frac{C_{\mathrm{tr}}}{2\,(C_{\mathrm{tr}}+1)}
\;<\; \frac{1}{2},
\label{eq:a-value}
\end{equation}
with $b=C_{\mathrm{tr}}C$ (\thm{coulomb\_isRelBounded\_core}). Landing below $\tfrac12$ rather than
merely below $1$ costs nothing --- the $4$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps-choice} could have been a $2$ --- and
leaves margin, worth having in a formalized bound where a later refactor might otherwise silently
consume it.

We emphasise the logical form of the packaged statement:
\begin{equation}
\exists\, a,b:\;\; 0\le a \,\wedge\, a<1 \,\wedge\, 0\le b \,\wedge\,
\mathrm{IsRelBounded}\bigl(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\,\mathcal{V},\,a,\,b\bigr)
\label{eq:coulomb-relbound}
\end{equation}
(\thm{coulomb\_isRelBounded}). The quantifier is existential: the constants are \emph{produced} by
the proof. A version taking $a$ and $b$ as parameters would be a strictly weaker theorem wearing the
same name, and that distinction is exactly what Sec.~\ref{sec:inhabitance-def} is about.

\subsection{From the core to the closure}
\label{sec:extend-closure}

Everything above is established on the Schwartz core. Equation~\eqref{eq:relbound} must hold on the
domain of $\overline{\mathcal{T}}$, which is larger. Bridging that is a separate theorem, and Mathlib
does not have it:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&A \text{ closable},\;\; B \text{ closed},\;\; a,b\ge0,\;\;
  \mathrm{IsRelBounded}(A,B,a,b)\\
&\qquad \Longrightarrow\;\; \mathrm{IsRelBounded}(\overline{A},B,a,b)
\end{split}
\label{eq:extend}
\end{equation}
(\thm{IsRelBounded.extend\_to\_closure}).

The proof is a graph-limit argument. A vector $x\in\mathcal{D}(\overline{A})$ has
$(x,\overline{A}x)$ in the closure of $A$'s graph, so there is a sequence $\varphi_{n}$ in
$\mathcal{D}(A)$ with $\varphi_{n}\to x$ and $A\varphi_{n}\to\overline{A}x$. Applying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:relbound} to differences $\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{k}$ shows $(B\varphi_{n})$ is Cauchy,
hence convergent; closedness of $B$ identifies its limit as $Bx$ and places $x$ in $\mathcal{D}(B)$;
and the bound passes to the limit by continuity of the norm. Each hypothesis is used exactly once and
neither can be dropped: without closability the graph limit need not be a function, and without
closedness of $B$ the limit of the images need not be an image.

For Eq.~\eqref{eq:molecular-H} both hold. $\mathcal{T}$ is closable, being a scalar multiple of the
symmetric densely-defined momentum-square operator, and $\mathcal{V}$ is closed, being self-adjoint
as multiplication by a real function.

We flag Eq.~\eqref{eq:extend} as the piece of this development most likely to be reused outside this
paper. It is stated for an arbitrary pair of unbounded operators on a complex Hilbert space, makes no
reference to Coulomb potentials or molecular systems, and is needed by \emph{any} Kato--Rellich
application that establishes its bound on a convenient core --- which is the normal situation. It is
Mathlib-grade infrastructure that Mathlib happens not to carry.

\subsection{The apex}

Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:coulomb-relbound} through Eq.~\eqref{eq:extend} with the symmetry and
kinetic-closure inputs gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex}: the molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian is essentially
self-adjoint, unconditionally. The signature carries $N$, $m$, $0<m$ and the nuclei. Nothing else.

\section{The variational layer above it}
\label{sec:dft-scope}

Density-functional theory sits on top of Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex} in the physics literature. This
development contains \Lean{} declarations named for its foundational theorems. They do not sit on top
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex}, they do not sit on top of anything, and this section says so precisely. It is
the negative case of Sec.~\ref{sec:inhabitance-thesis}, presented as evidence rather than as an
apology.

\subsection{The objects}

Three structures carry the layer. \thm{GroundStateData} holds a density $n$, an energy $E$, a
universal-functional value $F$, and the decomposition $E=F+\int v\,n$ as a field.
\thm{DensityVariational} holds an energy functional $E_{v}[\cdot]$ over densities, a distinguished
density $n_{0}$, and three further fields: that $E_{v}$ is bounded below, that
$E_{v}[n_{0}]=\inf_{n}E_{v}[n]$, and that the infimum is attained only at $n_{0}$.
\thm{LevyLiebData} holds a kinetic-plus-interaction energy on states, a density map, and the field
that each density fibre is bounded below in energy.

\subsection{What the theorems then say}

Hohenberg--Kohn~I is a genuine reductio (\thm{hohenberg\_kohn\_uniqueness}): two potentials with
non-degenerate ground states sharing a density, each satisfying the strict Rayleigh--Ritz inequality
against the other, yield a contradiction. The two strict inequalities are hypotheses. Once the shared
density makes the $\int$ terms coincide, the residual content is that $F_{1}<F_{2}$ and
$F_{2}<F_{1}$ cannot both hold. It is correct, and it is asymmetry of $<$.

Hohenberg--Kohn~II (\thm{hohenberg\_kohn\_variational}) concludes $E_{0}\le E_{v}[n]$ together with
$E_{v}[n]=E_{0}\iff n=n_{0}$, where $E_{0}$ is \emph{defined} as $\inf_{n}E_{v}[n]$. Of the three
conjuncts, the bound is one application of the Mathlib lemma \texttt{ciInf\_le}; the forward
implication of the equivalence is the non-degeneracy field returned unchanged; and the reverse is the
ground-state field returned unchanged. Two of three conjuncts are hypotheses handed back.

Levy--Lieb is thinner still. $F_{\mathrm{LL}}[n]$ is \emph{defined} as the infimum of the
kinetic-plus-interaction energy over the density fibre, so \thm{levyLieb\_functional} --- that
$F_{\mathrm{LL}}[n]$ is a lower bound for states in that fibre --- is one \texttt{ciInf\_le} and
contains no density-functional content whatever. \thm{levyLieb\_eq\_HK\_on\_vrep} is an antisymmetry
argument concluding $F_{\mathrm{LL}}[n]=(T{+}V_{ee})[\psi_{0}]$ from the hypothesis that $\psi_{0}$
minimises the fibre. We note that no Hohenberg--Kohn functional occurs anywhere in that statement,
despite the name; the only equality asserted is to the value at $\psi_{0}$.

\subsection{Nothing inhabits them}

The three structures are referenced in no other module of the development. No inhabitant of any of
them is constructed anywhere. Nothing connects them to Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex}, and the \Lean{} modules
say so in their own headers (\src{HohenbergKohnUniqueness.lean:19--21},
\src{HohenbergKohnVariational.lean:26--27}).

Supplying an inhabitant from the molecular Hamiltonian is the substantive missing step. It requires
the Rayleigh--Ritz inequalities as \emph{theorems} about that operator --- hence a ground state, hence
spectral theory on the self-adjoint $\overline{\mathcal{T}}+\mathcal{V}$ that Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex}
provides but that we have not developed further. That is the honest statement of the gap, and it is
why this section makes no claim.

\subsection{Why this layer and not another}

The failure is not an accident of effort, and this is the part we think generalises. The structures'
fields are variational inequalities. As hypotheses they cost nothing: one writes them into a
structure and they are available. As theorems about a specific operator they are expensive --- they
are essentially the existence and characterisation of a ground state. The gap between the cheap and
the expensive direction is exactly the gap Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex} spent this paper's longest section
crossing, for a strictly weaker statement about the same operator.

So the layer failing the inhabitance test is the one whose physical content is most concentrated in
inequalities that are cheap to assume. That is a general hazard in formalizing physics, and it is
invisible to every mechanical check we apply.

\section{NEGF transport: a chain that closes at a falsifier}
\label{sec:negf}

\subsection{Green's functions and the spectral relation}

For a Hermitian device Hamiltonian $H$ on a finite site basis, an energy $E$ and a positive regulator
$\eta$, the retarded and advanced resolvent arguments are
\begin{equation}
(G^{R})^{-1} = (E + i\eta)\mathbf{1} - H,
\qquad
(G^{A})^{-1} = (E - i\eta)\mathbf{1} - H .
\label{eq:negf-args}
\end{equation}
The retarded argument is invertible for every $\eta>0$ (\thm{retardedArg\_isUnit}), which makes
$G^{R}(E)$ well defined rather than formal; the proof exhibits
$(G^{R})^{-1}(G^{A})^{-1}=\eta^{2}\mathbf{1}+(H-E\mathbf{1})^{2}$, a positive scalar plus the square
of a Hermitian matrix. The advanced function is then not a second definition but a consequence:
$G^{A}=(G^{R})^{\dagger}$ (\thm{gAdvanced\_eq\_conjTranspose}).

The spectral function is $A=i(G^{R}-G^{A})$, and the broadening of a self-energy $\Sigma$ is
$\Gamma(\Sigma)=i(\Sigma-\Sigma^{\dagger})$. We verify the NEGF relation
\begin{equation}
A(E) \;=\; G^{R}(E)\,\Gamma\,G^{A}(E)
\label{eq:negf-spectral}
\end{equation}
(\thm{spectralFn\_eq\_gR\_broadening\_gA}) and positive semidefiniteness of $A$
(\thm{spectralFn\_posSemidef}), the operator-level statement that the density of states is
non-negative. The positivity proof is structural rather than spectral: $A$ reduces to
$2\eta\,G^{R}(G^{R})^{\dagger}$, a non-negative scalar times a matrix of the manifestly
positive-semidefinite form $MM^{\dagger}$.

\paragraph{Scope of Eq.~\eqref{eq:negf-spectral}.}
The self-energy in this development is not a free parameter. It is
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(\eta) = -i\eta\,\mathbf{1},
\qquad\text{whence}\qquad
\Gamma = 2\eta\,\mathbf{1},
\label{eq:negf-selfenergy}
\end{equation}
a scalar multiple of the identity, and the Dyson form $(G^{R})^{-1}=E\mathbf{1}-H-\Sigma$ is proved
for exactly this $\Sigma$ (\thm{retardedArg\_eq\_sub\_selfEnergy}). Equation~\eqref{eq:negf-spectral}
is therefore established in the structureless limit --- energy-independent broadening, identical on
every site --- and not for a general site- and energy-dependent lead self-energy. A referee should
read Eq.~\eqref{eq:negf-spectral} with that restriction attached. Extending this layer to a general
Hermitian-decomposable $\Sigma$ is well defined and is not done here. The transmission layer below
carries no such restriction.

\subsection{Transmission}

The Meir--Wingreen transmission is
\begin{equation}
T(E) = \mathrm{tr}\bigl(\Gamma_{L}\,G^{R}\,\Gamma_{R}\,G^{A}\bigr),
\label{eq:meir-wingreen}
\end{equation}
defined for arbitrary $\Gamma_{L},\Gamma_{R}$ and arbitrary $G^{R}$. Two properties make it a
transmission probability rather than merely a trace.

It is real whenever the broadenings are Hermitian (\thm{transmission\_isReal}); the verified
statement is that complex conjugation fixes it, proved by moving conjugation through the trace as a
conjugate-transpose. No assumption is placed on $G^{R}$.

It is non-negative whenever the broadenings are positive semidefinite
(\thm{transmission\_nonneg}). The proof factors $\Gamma_{R}=BB^{\dagger}$ and rewrites
Eq.~\eqref{eq:meir-wingreen} as $\mathrm{tr}\bigl((G^{R}B)^{\dagger}\Gamma_{L}(G^{R}B)\bigr)$, a
congruence of a positive-semidefinite matrix. The factorization is itself in-tree
(\thm{posSemidef\_exists\_mul\_conjTranspose}): we build the symmetric spectral square root
$B=U\,\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda})\,U^{\dagger}$ by hand from Mathlib's
\texttt{IsHermitian.spectral\_theorem}, because the continuous-functional-calculus square root is
unavailable at this type without a C$^{\star}$-operator-norm instance that conflicts with the
entrywise matrix norms.

\subsection{Conductance}

With $f(E)=\bigl(1+e^{\beta(E-\mu)}\bigr)^{-1}$ the Fermi--Dirac distribution at inverse temperature
$\beta$ and chemical potential $\mu$, we \emph{define} the linear-response conductance as
\begin{equation}
G \;=\; G_{0}\!\int T(E)\,\bigl(-\partial_{E}f(E)\bigr)\,dE ,
\qquad G_{0} = \frac{2e^{2}}{h}.
\label{eq:landauer}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:landauer} is a definition in this development, not a theorem, and we say so
plainly because the substantive content lies in the two results that give it force.

The Fermi window $-\partial_{E}f$ integrates to unity for every $\beta>0$
(\thm{fermiWindow\_integral\_eq\_one}). This is the improper fundamental theorem of calculus applied
to $f$, using $f(-\infty)=1$ and $f(+\infty)=0$, and it is the only genuinely analytic step in the
transport layer. From it the wide-band limit follows with no normalization hypothesis left in the
signature: for energy-independent transmission $T_{0}$,
\begin{equation}
G = G_{0}\,T_{0} \qquad (\beta>0)
\label{eq:wideband}
\end{equation}
(\thm{landauerConductance\_const\_transmission'}).

\subsection{The falsifier}

For $n$ channels with transmissions $\tau_{i}$ the channel conductance is $G_{0}\sum_{i}\tau_{i}$,
and
\begin{equation}
G \;\le\; n\,G_{0}
\label{eq:quantum-bound}
\end{equation}
(\thm{channelConductance\_le\_quantum}), with equality when every channel is fully open
(\thm{conductance\_quantization}). We record that the verified statement is \emph{stronger} than the
usual physical framing: it assumes only $\tau_{i}\le1$ and $G_{0}\ge0$, not $\tau_{i}\ge0$, so it
holds on a strictly larger domain than $\tau_{i}\in[0,1]$ would give it.

Equation~\eqref{eq:quantum-bound} is stated as a refutation --- a conductance strictly exceeding
$n\,G_{0}$ is impossible (\thm{conductance\_quantization\_falsifier}) --- and instantiated: a
two-channel point contact cannot carry three conductance quanta
(\thm{two\_channel\_no\_three\_quanta}), decided by \texttt{norm\_num}. The falsifier is the
contrapositive of the bound and carries no content the bound lacks; its value is that it fixes the
direction of refutation, and the two-channel instance makes that refutation concrete rather than
schematic. That instance is this layer's witness in Table~\ref{tab:inhabitance}: the chain closes on
a specific system about which a specific false statement has been ruled out.

\section{Open-system dynamics: a chain that closes at an instance}
\label{sec:lindblad}

\subsection{Generator and dissipator}

The Markovian master equation $\dot\rho=\mathcal{L}(\rho)$ has GKSL generator
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\rho) = -i[H,\rho] + \sum_{k}\Bigl(L_{k}\rho L_{k}^{\dagger}
- \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl\{L_{k}^{\dagger}L_{k},\rho\bigr\}\Bigr),
\label{eq:gksl}
\end{equation}
formalized as three \texttt{LinearMap} pieces on $\mathrm{Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C})$ so that linearity is
automatic rather than a lemma.

Complete positivity of the jump map is \thm{lindblad\_generator\_CP}. We are precise about
provenance: we \emph{define} the jump map as PhysLib's Kraus-form map, and the theorem applies
PhysLib's \texttt{MatrixMap.of\_kraus\_CP}, whose own proof exhibits the Choi matrix as a sum of
rank-one positive-semidefinite blocks. This is correct use of the library, not a new proof of
complete positivity, and a reader should not credit the Choi argument to us. We are equally explicit
that complete positivity is asserted for the \emph{dissipator}; the full generator is only
conditionally completely positive.

\subsection{Structure and trace preservation}

The canonical form (\thm{gksl\_canonical\_form}) assembles the pointwise action of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gksl} from the three pieces; it is a definitional rearrangement, and its role is to put
the generator in the form the trace computation needs.

That computation is the substantive result here:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}\,\mathcal{L}(\rho) = 0 \quad\text{for every }\rho
\label{eq:trace-preserving}
\end{equation}
(\thm{gksl\_trace\_preserving}). The unitary part is traceless by cyclicity; the dissipative gain
$\sum_{k}\mathrm{tr}(L_{k}\rho L_{k}^{\dagger})$ is rewritten by cyclicity as
$\sum_{k}\mathrm{tr}(L_{k}^{\dagger}L_{k}\rho)$, cancelling the anticommutator loss exactly. Without
Eq.~\eqref{eq:trace-preserving} the propagator would not carry density matrices to density matrices
and the semigroup would not act on states at all.

\subsection{Semigroup and contractivity}

The Liouvillian is non-Hermitian, so a Hermitian functional calculus does not apply; the propagator
$\Lambda_{t}=e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ is the matrix exponential of the \emph{vectorized} Liouvillian, via
Mathlib's \texttt{Matrix.exp}. We flag this because it is the design decision a reader is most likely
to assume we got wrong.

We verify $\Lambda_{0}=\mathbf{1}$ (\thm{lindblad\_propagator\_zero}) and
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{t}\Lambda_{s} = \Lambda_{t+s}
\label{eq:semigroup}
\end{equation}
(\thm{lindblad\_semigroup}), from \texttt{Matrix.exp\_add\_of\_commute} on the commuting scalar
multiples. Equation~\eqref{eq:semigroup} holds for all real $t,s$ including negative: it is the
algebraic identity for the exponential and does not by itself express irreversibility.

Irreversibility is contractivity. With $D(\cdot,\cdot)$ the trace distance,
\begin{equation}
D\bigl(\Lambda_{t}\rho,\,\Lambda_{t}\sigma\bigr) \;\le\; D(\rho,\sigma)
\qquad (t\ge0)
\label{eq:contractivity}
\end{equation}
(\thm{traceDist\_lindblad\_monotone}), the data-processing inequality for this dynamics. Its
signature takes a Hermitian $H$, the jump operators, $0\le t$ and two Hermitian states, and nothing
else: the propagator's action is proven completely positive for $t\ge0$, and a Kraus family is
\emph{extracted} from that proof, re-indexed to a finite type, and fed to the project's CPTP
data-processing inequality.

\subsection{A certified damped two-level model}
\label{sec:damped}

An abstract semigroup that is never instantiated is precisely the failure mode of
Sec.~\ref{sec:dft-scope}, so this layer closes on a constructed instance: amplitude damping at $d=2$
with $H=0$ and a single jump operator $L=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\sigma_{-}$.

The excited-state population obeys $\dot p_{e}=-\gamma p_{e}$, read off the formalized generator as
$(\mathcal{L}\rho)_{11}=-\gamma\rho_{11}$ (\thm{dampedTwoLevel\_generator\_decay\_rate}) rather than
posited, and $p_{0}e^{-\gamma t}$ is verified to solve it
(\thm{dampedTwoLevel\_population\_solves\_rate}). The decay factor admits a kernel-only rational
enclosure
\begin{equation}
1-\gamma t \;\le\; e^{-\gamma t} \;\le\; \frac{1}{1+\gamma t}
\qquad (\gamma t\ge0)
\label{eq:decay-envelope}
\end{equation}
(\thm{dampedTwoLevel\_decay\_envelope}), with a \texttt{norm\_num} operating point at
$\gamma t=\tfrac12$. Both endpoints are rational functions, so a transcendental enters a
kernel-checked bound with no floating-point evaluation anywhere. This instance is the layer's witness
in Table~\ref{tab:inhabitance}.

\section{Related work and priority}
\label{sec:related}

A prior-art reconnaissance across the interactive-theorem-prover ecosystem --- \Lean{}/Mathlib,
Coq/Rocq, Isabelle/HOL including the Archive of Formal Proofs, Agda, HOL Light and Mizar; see
Ref.~\cite{survey2021} and the records archived with this work --- found no existing formalization of
the molecular self-adjointness, transport or open-system results reported here.

We make three priority claims and no others.

\emph{First,} Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex}: unconditional essential self-adjointness of a molecular many-body
Coulomb Hamiltonian. The carve-out is that Mathlib carries a substantial unbounded-operator and
perturbation-theory development; our claim is for this specific operator via a Kato--Rellich argument
built in-tree, and is not a claim about abstract perturbation theory.

\emph{Second,} the transport chain of Sec.~\ref{sec:negf}, stated in terms of what is proved rather
than what is named: the Fermi-window normalization as an improper-FTC computation, the wide-band
limit obtained unconditionally from it, transmission non-negativity established without the dual-norm
characterization Mathlib lacks, and the quantization bound instantiated at a \texttt{norm\_num}
witness. Equation~\eqref{eq:landauer} itself is a definition and we claim no priority for writing it
down.

\emph{Third,} the Lindblad \emph{generator and Markovian dynamical semigroup} of
Sec.~\ref{sec:lindblad}. The carve-out here is essential: quantum \emph{channels} --- CPTP maps, Choi
matrices, superoperators --- already exist in \Lean{}'s PhysLib quantum-information
layer~\cite{leanQI2025}, in Coq's CoqQ~\cite{coqq2022} and in Isabelle/AFP~\cite{isabelleCBO}, and we
build on that substrate rather than replacing it. Our contribution is the generator
$\mathcal{L}=-i[H,\cdot]+\mathcal{D}$, complete positivity of $\mathcal{D}$, and the one-parameter
semigroup above them.

We claim nothing for density-functional theory (Sec.~\ref{sec:dft-scope}).

The physics of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:molecular-H}--\eqref{eq:decay-envelope} is standard, due to
Kato~\cite{kato1995} and Reed--Simon~\cite{reedSimon2} for the self-adjointness theory,
Hohenberg--Kohn~\cite{hohenbergKohn1964}, Levy~\cite{levy1979} and Lieb~\cite{lieb1983} for the
variational layer, Landauer~\cite{landauer1957}, B\"uttiker~\cite{buttiker1986},
Meir--Wingreen~\cite{meirWingreen1992} and Datta~\cite{datta1995} for transport, and
Ref.~\cite{gksl1976} with Lindblad~\cite{lindblad1976} for the open-system generator. Our
contribution is the machine-checked formalization, not the physics.

\section{What inhabitance measures}
\label{sec:synthesis}

Table~\ref{tab:inhabitance} separates four layers built by one author, in one development, to one
standard, using a property that no mechanical check in our pipeline reports.

Every layer has the same axiom profile. Every layer is \texttt{sorry}-free. Two have had every
disclosed hypothesis discharged from their signatures. On the measurements a formalization project
usually publishes, the four are indistinguishable.

They are not indistinguishable. Two terminate in a constructed object --- a two-channel point
contact, a damped two-level atom --- about which a specific false statement has been ruled out by
exact arithmetic. One is a theorem about the operator itself, needing no carrier at all, which is the
strongest position of the four. And one quantifies over structures that nothing in the development
inhabits, so that its conclusions, while correctly derived, apply to no object we have exhibited.

Three consequences seem general rather than local to this paper.

\emph{Kernel purity is a property of a proof, not of a statement.} It bounds what a proof may assume.
It says nothing about whether the theorem's antecedent is satisfiable by anything, and a project
reporting only purity is reporting the easier half.

\emph{The hazard concentrates where the physics is inequalities.} A structure field costs nothing to
assume and can be very expensive to prove about a specific operator. Layers whose content is
variational --- exactly the celebrated ones --- are therefore the most exposed, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:dft-scope} is the instance.

\emph{Inhabitance is mechanically checkable, and we did not have it.} Whether a structure is
constructed anywhere in a development is a query over the dependency graph, not a matter of
judgement. That no such check exists in our pipeline is why Sec.~\ref{sec:dft-scope}'s status had to
be established by reading proofs. Building it is the obvious next piece of infrastructure.

\subsection{Limitations}

Beyond the DFT gap, three limitations bound this work. Equation~\eqref{eq:negf-spectral} holds for the
structureless self-energy of Eq.~\eqref{eq:negf-selfenergy}, not a general lead self-energy. The
transport layer is finite-dimensional throughout, so it does not meet the unbounded-operator
machinery of Sec.~\ref{sec:molecular} even though both sit on the same substrate. And no numerical
transport or dynamical run backs any figure here; the quantitative claims are the rational enclosures
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:decay-envelope} and the \texttt{norm\_num} witnesses, and nothing else.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have proved, machine-checked and unconditionally, that the $N$-electron molecular Coulomb
Hamiltonian is essentially self-adjoint, by a Kato--Rellich development built in-tree whose reusable
component --- relative boundedness transferring from a core to an operator's closure --- is stated for
arbitrary unbounded operators and is absent from Mathlib. Alongside it we have formalized NEGF
transport through a refutable conductance bound and Lindblad dynamics through an instantiated
amplitude-damping model, and we have reported, as a result rather than a caveat, that our
density-functional-theory layer is uninhabited.

The natural next steps are the two Table~\ref{tab:inhabitance} makes visible. One is to construct an
inhabitant of the variational structures from Eq.~\eqref{eq:apex}, which needs the Rayleigh--Ritz
inequalities as theorems about the self-adjoint operator and would close the only open row in the
table. The other is to make inhabitance a mechanical check rather than a reading, so that the next
development finds out before publication which of its layers are about something.
