\title{Analog Hawking Radiation in a Bilayer-Graphene Electronic de~Laval Nozzle:\\
A Falsifiable Current-Noise Spectrum and What It Costs to Predict}

\author{J.~Roehm}
\affiliation{Independent Researcher}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
A sonic horizon in the bilayer-graphene electron fluid emits analog Hawking
radiation as excess current noise
$\Delta S_I(\omega) = 2\hbar\omega\,\sigma_Q\,\Gamma(\omega)\,n_H(\omega)$,
a low-frequency plateau of height $2 k_B T_H \sigma_Q$ rolling off above
$\omega_H = k_B T_H/\hbar$. We evaluate this spectrum with a
Schwinger--Keldysh effective field theory at the parameters of the
electronic de~Laval nozzle of Geurs \emph{et al.}, and we report what the
prediction actually rests on. For an assumed horizon velocity gradient
$\kappa = \SI{2e12}{\per\second}$ the plateau is
$\SI{1.2e-26}{\ampere\squared/\hertz}$ against a
$\SI{1.3e-24}{\ampere\squared/\hertz}$ Johnson--Nyquist background,
separated by shape rather than amplitude. The two leading corrections are
comparable and opposite in sign, a dispersive
$\delta_{\rm disp} = -c_1 D^2$ with $D \approx 0.23$ against a viscous
$\delta_{\rm diss} \approx +6.5\%$, which makes graphene the one analog
platform where the dissipative sector is measurable rather than formal.
The sign of the net shift is not yet a prediction: it flips between the
values of the electron-electron length reported for this device and the
one the framework derives, and it depends on the $O(1)$ profile
coefficient $c_1$. We state the gradient scale, the transport coefficients,
and the coefficient $c_1$ as the assumptions they are, and identify the
three measurements that would convert each into a datum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A fluid that crosses from subsonic to supersonic flow carries a sonic
horizon, and a sonic horizon radiates at a temperature fixed by the
velocity gradient there~\cite{Unruh1981,Visser1998,Bilic1999}. Geurs
\emph{et al.}~\cite{Geurs2025} have now realized the transonic transition
in an electronic fluid: current driven through an etched constriction in
hBN-encapsulated bilayer graphene accelerates the electron liquid past its
own speed of sound and relaxes it at a stationary shock, imaged directly by
Kelvin-probe force microscopy. The speed of sound they report is the
Fermi-liquid value $v_{FL}(n) = \hbar\sqrt{\pi n}/(\sqrt{2}\,m^*)$,
equal to \SI{440}{\kilo\meter/\second} at carrier density
$n = \SI{e12}{\per\centi\meter\squared}$~[\citenum{Geurs2025}, p.~3]. That
is three orders of magnitude above the sound speed of an atomic
Bose--Einstein condensate, and it is the entire reason this platform is
interesting for analog gravity.

Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025} closes by noting the correspondence, attributing the
de~Laval-nozzle-as-acoustic-black-hole analogy to Unruh, and estimating
$T_H \sim \SI{500}{\milli\kelvin}$ for the electronic fluid. What is
missing, and what this Letter supplies, is the observable: the spectrum,
its normalization in a measurable quantity, the two leading corrections to
it, and an honest account of which inputs are measured and which are
assumed.

The Hawking temperature is universal,
\begin{equation}
T_H = \frac{\hbar \kappa}{2\pi k_B},
\label{eq:T_H}
\end{equation}
with $\kappa$ the velocity-gradient surface gravity at the horizon. Only
$\kappa$ distinguishes one platform from another. We take the
Schwinger--Keldysh dissipative effective field
theory~\cite{CGL2017a,CGL2017b} as the organizing framework because it
treats the dispersive and dissipative corrections to Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H} in
one expansion rather than as unrelated refinements, and because graphene
is the platform where that distinction is quantitative.

Two transport inputs enter. Majumdar \emph{et al.}~\cite{Majumdar2025}
report, for ultra-clean monolayer graphene near charge neutrality, a
quantum-critical conductivity $\sigma_Q \simeq (4 \pm 1)\,e^2/h$, a
Wiedemann--Franz violation exceeding $200\times$, and a shear-viscosity-to-
entropy ratio approaching the holographic limit
$\hbar/(4\pi k_B)$~\cite{KSS2005,Romatschke2010} to within a factor of four
[\citenum{Majumdar2025}, abstract]. We adopt both, and we state plainly
below the two respects in which the adoption goes beyond what that
experiment measured.

\section{Acoustic metric and quasi-1D reduction}

The $2{+}1$D acoustic metric for flow along $x$ in a two-dimensional
electron fluid is~\cite{Bilic1999}
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu} = \Omega^2
\begin{pmatrix}
-(c_s^2 - v^2)/v_F^2 & -v/v_F^2 & 0 \\
-v/v_F^2 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
With translational symmetry along $y$, the $3\times3$ form
block-diagonalizes: the $y$ row has vanishing off-diagonal entries and the
$yy$ entry reduces to the conformal factor. Both statements are machine-checked
in \texttt{DiracFluidMetric.lean}, as
\texttt{diracFluidMetric\_y\_offdiag\_zero}
($\forall\, v, v_F, \Omega^2,\ j \neq 2:\ G_{2j} = 0$) and
\texttt{diracFluidMetric\_gyy} ($\forall\, v, v_F, \Omega^2:\ G_{22} = \Omega^2$).
These are algebraic identities about the matrix of Eq.~\eqref{eq:metric},
and they are what they look like; the physical content is that the $(t,x)$
block is then isomorphic to the $1{+}1$D acoustic metric, so every
$1{+}1$D result (WKB connection formulae, Bogoliubov coefficients,
dispersive corrections) transfers to it unchanged.

The transverse-channel cutoff is the only genuinely $2{+}1$D effect. For a
channel of width $W$ with Dirichlet boundaries the transverse momenta are
$k_n = (n{+}1)\pi/W$ and the channel cutoffs are $\omega_\perp(n) = c_s k_n$.
Taking $W = \SI{1}{\micro\meter}$ and $c_s = \SI{4.4e5}{\meter/\second}$
gives $\omega_\perp(0) = \SI{1.38e12}{\per\second}$, which exceeds
$4\,\omega_H = \SI{1.27e12}{\per\second}$ and so leaves the whole
$[0, 4\omega_H]$ integration window inside the single-channel regime.

That inequality is checked in
\texttt{DiracFluidWKB.dean\_lowest\_channel\_above\_four\_omega\_H}, and
its exact content is
$(4.4\!\times\!10^5)\pi/10^{-6} > 4 \times 3.14\!\times\!10^{11}$: a
comparison between two numerals, with the identification of the left side
as $\omega_\perp(0)$ and of the right as $4\omega_H$ carried in prose here
rather than in the theorem. The margin is $10\%$, not a factor of four; the
factor of four is the separation between $\omega_\perp(0)$ and $\omega_H$
itself, which is $4.4$. Two caveats follow. Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025} does not
state the throat width numerically, remarking only that a
momentum-relaxation length of $\sim\SI{2}{\micro\meter}$ is ``comparable to
the throat width'' [\citenum{Geurs2025}, p.~6]; $W = \SI{1}{\micro\meter}$
sits at the low end of what that supports, and at $W = \SI{2}{\micro\meter}$
the lowest channel falls to $2.2\,\omega_H$ and the single-channel
guarantee over $[0,4\omega_H]$ fails. A measured throat width is therefore
a precondition for the quasi-1D treatment, not a refinement of it.

Finite-width corrections to the greybody factor decompose into a
surface-gravity term bounded by $(l_{\rm ee}/W)^2$ and an evanescent-leakage
term bounded by $(\omega/\omega_\perp)^2 e^{-2\pi L/W}$, summing to
$\approx 1.8\%$ at $\omega_H$ for $l_{\rm ee} = \SI{51}{\nano\meter}$,
$W = \SI{1}{\micro\meter}$ and $L = \SI{200}{\nano\meter}$.
\texttt{QuasiOneDReduction.quasi1D\_validity\_bound} combines the two by
triangle inequality and \emph{takes each component bound as a hypothesis};
the partial-differential-equation content behind them, that transverse
averaging of a Poiseuille profile and sub-cutoff Helmholtz decay produce
these forms, is not proved there. The $1.8\%$ figure is an arithmetic
consequence of the two assumed bounds at the stated geometry, and is
reported here as such.

\section{The horizon gradient scale}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig102_graphene_hawking_T_sweep.png}
\caption{Hawking temperature $T_H = \hbar\kappa/2\pi k_B$ versus the
horizon velocity-gradient length $L$, with $\kappa = c_s/L$, for the
bilayer sound speed \SI{440}{\kilo\meter/\second} (dashed) and the
monolayer \SI{710}{\kilo\meter/\second} (solid). The bilayer point plotted
at $L = \SI{200}{\nano\meter}$ is the assumed gradient of this Letter, not
a measured device dimension. The monolayer points show $T_H$ only and are
not viable operating points (Fig.~\ref{fig:diss_window}). Horizontal lines:
the high-bias electron temperature $T_{\rm e} = \SI{150}{\kelvin}$
[\citenum{Geurs2025}, p.~3] and the disorder scale
$T_{\rm imp} \approx \SI{80}{\kelvin}$.}
\label{fig:T_sweep}
\end{figure}

Everything quantitative below is proportional to $\kappa$, so $\kappa$
deserves to be stated before it is used. It is not measured in
Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025}. We take $\kappa = \SI{2.0e12}{\per\second}$, which
is the value carried by this work's parameter registry and which
corresponds, through the order-of-magnitude identification
$\kappa \approx c_s/L$, to a velocity-gradient length
$L \approx \SI{200}{\nano\meter}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:T_H} then gives
\begin{equation}
T_H = \SI{2.43}{\kelvin}, \qquad
\omega_H \equiv \frac{k_B T_H}{\hbar} = \SI{3.18e11}{\per\second},
\label{eq:T_H_dean}
\end{equation}
that is $\nu_H = \omega_H/2\pi = \SI{50.7}{\giga\hertz}$ as an ordinary
frequency. This is nine orders of magnitude above the atomic-BEC analog
temperature of $\SI{0.35}{\nano\kelvin}$~\cite{Steinhauer2016,deNova2019},
and it sits inside the \SIrange{1}{10}{\kelvin} window where noise
spectroscopy is routine. That is the platform's decisive advantage and it
survives every caveat below.

The value of $L$ does not survive them. Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025} contains no
nozzle length of \SI{200}{\nano\meter}. The two occurrences of that scale
in it are a high-bias momentum-relaxation mean free path
$\ell_{\rm mr} \sim \SI{200}{\nano\meter}$ and the width of the
shock feature resolved by Kelvin-probe microscopy
[\citenum{Geurs2025}, p.~6], neither of which is the velocity-gradient
length at the sonic point. Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025}'s own estimate
$T_H \sim \SI{500}{\milli\kelvin}$ corresponds, through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H}, to $\kappa \approx \SI{4.1e11}{\per\second}$ and a
gradient length $\approx \SI{1}{\micro\meter}$, which is the throat-width
scale rather than a sub-throat one. Our $\SI{2.43}{\kelvin}$ and their
$\SI{500}{\milli\kelvin}$ are therefore not in conflict about the physics;
they are two choices of gradient length differing by a factor of five, and
the entire spectrum scales with that choice. We quote
Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H_dean} throughout because it is what the stated $\kappa$
gives, and we treat the gradient length as the single most consequential
quantity the experiment could return.

\section{The two leading corrections}

The dispersive correction is governed by the adiabaticity parameter
$D = \kappa\, l_{\rm ee}/c_s$, with $l_{\rm ee}$ the electron-electron
scattering length at which hydrodynamics gives out:
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\rm disp} = - c_1 D^2 , \qquad c_1 = O(1),
\label{eq:disp}
\end{equation}
following Coutant and Parentani and Finazzi and
Parentani~\cite{Finazzi2012}. The coefficient $c_1$ depends on the horizon
profile and is not universal. This work's positivity result,
\texttt{GrapheneHawking.graphene\_T\_eff\_positive}, states
$\forall\, T_H, D:\ 0 < T_H \wedge D^2 < 6/\pi \Rightarrow
0 < T_H(1 - \tfrac{\pi}{6}D^2)$, that is, it is proved for the particular
choice $c_1 = \pi/6$ and both hypotheses are used. Our numerical pipeline
uses the same $\pi/6$; a separate general-purpose routine in the same
codebase uses $c_1 = 1$. We therefore quote $\delta_{\rm disp}$ with $c_1$
displayed rather than absorbed, and note that the two choices differ by a
factor $1.9$.

At $T_{\rm e} = \SI{150}{\kelvin}$, which is the electron temperature
Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025} estimates the device reaches under high bias while
the lattice bath sits near \SI{10}{\kelvin} [\citenum{Geurs2025}, p.~3],
the framework's own $l_{\rm ee} = \hbar v_F/(k_B T)$ evaluates to
\SI{51}{\nano\meter} and gives
\begin{equation}
D = 0.23, \qquad
\delta_{\rm disp} = -2.8\%\ (c_1 = \pi/6),
\label{eq:D_dean}
\end{equation}
comfortably inside the perturbative regime. Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025}, however,
reports $\ell_{\rm ee} \sim \SI{100}{\nano\meter}$ for this device, as the
length scale setting the width of the hydraulic jump
[\citenum{Geurs2025}, p.~6]. At that value $D = 0.45$ and
$\delta_{\rm disp} = -11\%$. The discrepancy traces to the Fermi velocity
used in $\hbar v_F/(k_B T)$: the value \SI{e6}{\meter/\second} is the
monolayer Dirac velocity, whereas the bilayer band is quadratic and the
velocity consistent with the measured $c_s$ is
$\sqrt{2}\,c_s = \SI{6.2e5}{\meter/\second}$. We report both, because the
choice changes the sign of the net correction below.

The dissipative correction is of comparable size and opposite sign. What
the Schwinger--Keldysh damping kernel requires is a momentum-diffusion
constant with units of $\si{\meter\squared\per\second}$, not the relaxation
time $\eta/(sT)$:
\begin{equation}
\nu = \frac{\eta\,v_F^2}{w} = 2\,\frac{\eta}{s\,T}\,c_s^2 ,
\label{eq:nu}
\end{equation}
the first equality because the relativistic momentum density is $w/v_F^2$,
the second for a fluid with $c_s^2 = v_F^2/2$. The velocity squared in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:nu} is not decorative: omitting it turns a time into a
dimensionless number and misstates $\delta_{\rm diss}$ by eleven orders of
magnitude. Because the bilayer band touching is quadratic and admits no
emergent light cone, $\nu$ must be written in the \emph{measured} $c_s$
rather than in a nominal Fermi velocity. With
$\eta/s = 4 \times \hbar/(4\pi k_B)$ and $T = \SI{150}{\kelvin}$ this gives
$\nu = \SI{6.3e-3}{\meter\squared\per\second}$ and, at the horizon
wavenumber $k_H = \kappa/c_s$,
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_H = \nu \left(\frac{\kappa}{c_s}\right)^2
= \SI{1.3e11}{\per\second}, \quad
\delta_{\rm diss} = \frac{\Gamma_H}{\kappa} = +6.5\%.
\label{eq:diss}
\end{equation}

Taking Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D_dean} and~\eqref{eq:diss} together, the two
channels partially cancel and leave $T_{\rm eff}/T_H - 1 = +3.7\%$, a net
shift \emph{above} $T_H$ rather than below it. This is the observable the
Schwinger--Keldysh organization buys, and it is the point of the platform:
at $\SI{2.4}{\kelvin}$ and a few percent each, both corrections sit inside
the derivative expansion's domain, so their relative size is a prediction
rather than an extrapolation to the edge of validity. For comparison, the
smooth-horizon polariton configuration of Falque \emph{et al.} has
$\delta_{\rm disp} \approx -19\%$ against $\delta_{\rm diss} \approx 14\%$
at the same $c_1 = \pi/6$; the corrections there are comparable too, but
neither is small.

The sign of the net shift, however, is not yet a prediction. It is $+3.7\%$
with $l_{\rm ee} = \SI{51}{\nano\meter}$ and $c_1 = \pi/6$; it is
$-4.3\%$ with Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025}'s $\SI{100}{\nano\meter}$ at the same
$c_1$, and $+1.1\%$ with $c_1 = 1$ at $\SI{51}{\nano\meter}$. A measured
$l_{\rm ee}$ and a profile-specific $c_1$ are what convert the sign into a
falsifiable statement. The magnitude, a few percent, is robust across all
four combinations.

Two scope conditions attach to the transport inputs. Ref.~\cite{Majumdar2025}
observes $\sigma_Q \to 4e^2/h$ only for
$|n| \lesssim \SI{5e10}{\per\centi\meter\squared}$ and reports $\sigma_Q$
below that value at larger density [\citenum{Majumdar2025}, p.~5], while
the nozzle of Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025} is quoted at
$n = \SI{e12}{\per\centi\meter\squared}$, twenty times higher; and the
factor-of-four approach to $\hbar/(4\pi k_B)$ is reported for the cleanest
device close to room temperature, whereas $\eta_{\rm th}/s_{\rm th}$ varies
as $1/T^3$ below $T_{\rm imp} \sim \SI{100}{\kelvin}$
[\citenum{Majumdar2025}, p.~7]. Adopting both values at
$n = \SI{e12}{\per\centi\meter\squared}$ and $T = \SI{150}{\kelvin}$ is a
modeling assumption, and it is one that Ref.~\cite{Majumdar2025}'s own
density and temperature dependence argues against rather than for. It is
additionally a transfer from monolayer to bilayer. We use it because no
bilayer measurement of either quantity exists; a measurement of $\sigma_Q$
on the nozzle device itself would replace the largest single uncertainty in
the prefactor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise_psd}.

\section{The current-noise observable}

The Hawking flux at an electronic horizon is a current, so it is visible as
excess current noise. Two routes give the same closed form. The
Callen--Welton fluctuation-dissipation theorem~\cite{CallenWelton1951} for
a hydrodynamic conductor yields the symmetrized current noise; at a sonic
horizon, pair creation replaces the thermal $\coth$ kernel by the
mode-resolved distribution $F_u(\omega) = 1 + 2\Gamma(\omega) n_H(\omega)$
with $\Gamma(\omega) \leq 1$ the greybody transmission factor. Separately,
the B\"uttiker multichannel noise formula~\cite{Blanter2000}, generalized
to Bogoliubov-mixing channels on the structural template of Anantram and
Datta~\cite{AnantramDatta1996}, applied to a two-dimensional conductor with
$N_{\rm eff} = \sigma_Q h/(2e^2)$ effective channels, gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Delta S_I(\omega) = 2\hbar\omega\,\sigma_Q\,\Gamma(\omega)\,n_H(\omega)\;}
\label{eq:noise_psd}
\end{equation}
with $n_H(\omega) = [e^{\hbar\omega/k_B T_H} - 1]^{-1}$. The dimensions
check: $[\hbar\omega]\times[\sigma_Q] = \mathrm{J}\cdot\mathrm{S}
= \mathrm{A}^2\mathrm{s} = \mathrm{A}^2/\mathrm{Hz}$.

The agreement of the two routes is established here at the level of
matching prefactors and channel content, and it is not what the machine
checking covers. \texttt{GrapheneNoiseFormula.fdt\_consistency} states
$\forall\, \hbar\omega, \sigma_Q, n_H:\
\texttt{hawkingNoisePSD}(\hbar\omega, \sigma_Q, 1, n_H)
= 2\hbar\omega\,\sigma_Q\,n_H$, which is the substitution
$\Gamma = 1$ into the definition of the closed form, discharged by
unfolding and ring normalization. It certifies the algebraic identity of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise_psd} at unit greybody factor. It does not certify
either derivation, and it does not certify their equivalence. The name
notwithstanding, no fluctuation-dissipation content enters it.

Two features of Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise_psd} matter for detection. Because
$\omega\, n_H(\omega) = (k_B T_H/\hbar)\,x/(e^x - 1)$ with
$x = \hbar\omega/k_B T_H$, the spectrum is a plateau at low frequency and
falls monotonically: it does \emph{not} peak at $\omega_H$. The plateau
height is
\begin{equation}
\Delta S_I(\omega \to 0) = 2 k_B T_H\, \sigma_Q\, \Gamma_0
= \SI{1.2e-26}{\ampere\squared/\hertz},
\label{eq:plateau}
\end{equation}
and the signal falls to half of it at $\omega = 1.6\,\omega_H$. Second, the
plateau is independent of $\omega$ and therefore of the detector's tuning,
which is why the discriminant against the background is spectral shape.

The greybody factor encodes the horizon's departure from a perfect thermal
emitter. For a $1{+}1$D acoustic horizon with a smooth velocity profile the
zero-frequency limit is profile-independent,
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_0 = \frac{4\,c_R\,v}{(c_R + v)^2},
\label{eq:greybody}
\end{equation}
% TODO: Eq. (7) and its profile-independence are attributed to
% Anderson, Balbinot, Fabbri and Parentani, PRD 87, 124018 (2013), which is
% NOT held in Lit-Search/Phase-*/primary-sources/. Not read at page level.
% Verify the closed form and the profile-independence claim against the
% primary before submission.
giving $\Gamma_0 = 0.99995$ at $v = 0.985\,c_s$ and $c_R \approx c_s$. That
$\Gamma_0$ is within $10^{-4}$ of unity is a statement about the near-sonic
asymptotic flow, and it is the reason the prefactor of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise_psd} is effectively $\sigma_Q$ alone. In the adiabatic
regime the frequency dependence is
$\Gamma(\omega) \approx \Gamma_0[1 - (\omega/\omega_{\max})^2]$ with
$\omega_{\max} = \sqrt{\kappa c_s / l_{\rm ee}} \approx 7.8\,\omega_H$, so
$\Gamma$ stays within a few percent of $\Gamma_0$ across the detection
band. The frequency dependence of $\Gamma$ is the largest device-specific
unknown in the spectrum's shape, which is the quantity the shape-based
discriminant is built on.

\section{Detection and the falsifiable window}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig104_graphene_noise_spectrum.png}
\caption{Hawking excess current noise $\Delta S_I(\omega)$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise_psd}) at the bilayer-nozzle parameters: a
\SI{1.2e-26}{\ampere\squared/\hertz} low-frequency plateau falling to half
height at $1.6\,\omega_H$. The white Johnson--Nyquist background
$4k_B T_{\rm e}\sigma_Q = \SI{1.3e-24}{\ampere\squared/\hertz}$ at
$T_{\rm e} = \SI{150}{\kelvin}$ lies two orders of magnitude above it, and
is separated from it by shape, not amplitude.}
\label{fig:noise}
\end{figure}

The excess sits a factor $\sim 100$ below the Johnson--Nyquist floor
$4 k_B T_{\rm e} \sigma_Q = \SI{1.3e-24}{\ampere\squared/\hertz}$, so
detection is a shape measurement. The per-bin ratio is
$\Delta S_I/S_{\rm JN} = \hbar\omega\,\Gamma\, n_H/(2 k_B T_{\rm e})$,
which peaks at $9.6\times10^{-3}$ at the low-frequency end and falls with
the plateau. Integrating the squared ratio across the band,
$t = [\int (\Delta S_I/S_{\rm JN})^2 \, d\nu]^{-1}$, gives a
statistics-limited integration time of \SI{1.3e-7}{\second} over the full
\SI{0.5}{\giga\hertz} to \SI{507}{\giga\hertz} window, and
\SI{6.4e-6}{\second} in the single best bin. Those figures assume a
background whose gain and temperature are stable to better than $1\%$ over
the measurement, which no real cryogenic amplifier chain provides; the
measurement is systematics-limited by many orders of magnitude, and the
statistical time is reported to show that photon shot noise is not the
obstacle.

The window over which the prediction is cleanest is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\omega/\omega_H \in [0.5, 4]\;}
\label{eq:falsifiable_window}
\end{equation}
Below it the plateau carries no shape information and the measurement
degenerates into an amplitude comparison against an uncalibrated
background; above it the greybody roll-off and the transverse-channel edge
both enter. Inside it, $D = 0.23$ keeps the dispersive expansion
perturbative, $\Gamma$ stays within a few percent of $\Gamma_0$, and the
lowest transverse channel remains closed at the assumed $W$.

A measured $\Delta S_I(\omega)$ in this band determines $T_H$ from the
Planckian shape and $\sigma_Q$ from the plateau height independently, so the
framework is tested twice by one spectrum. The prediction is falsified if
the measured profile is Planckian at a temperature inconsistent with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H} at the measured velocity gradient, or if the plateau
height is inconsistent with $2 k_B T_H \sigma_Q$ at the measured $\sigma_Q$.
It is not falsified by a disagreement in $T_H$ alone while $\kappa$ remains
unmeasured, and we do not claim otherwise.

Three measurements would convert the three assumptions of this Letter into
data, in descending order of leverage. First, the velocity-gradient length
at the sonic point, from the Kelvin-probe velocity profile already
demonstrated in Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025}: this sets $\kappa$ and with it every
frequency and temperature quoted here. Second, $\sigma_Q$ and
$\eta/s$ on the nozzle device at its operating density, which would replace
a cross-device, cross-density, cross-material transfer with a measurement.
Third, the throat width and the electron-electron length at operating bias,
which together decide whether the quasi-1D reduction holds and which sign
the net temperature shift takes.

An independent cross-check of the same critical scaling runs through the
Kibble--Zurek--Unruh correspondence, and a demarcation result localizes the
classically-simulable regime for this device; both are developed, with the
scope caveat on the categorical marker they use, in the companion
paper~\cite{Roehm2026GrapheneDeep}.

\section{Comparison across platforms}

The temperature advantage is worth stating against a like-for-like
normalization, because the analog-gravity literature does not use one
uniformly. Falque \emph{et al.}~\cite{Falque2025} report an exciton-polariton
horizon with measured steepness $\kappa = 0.07$ to
$\SI{0.11}{\per\pico\second}$ and quote a Hawking temperature of
\SI{3}{\kelvin}. That number is not $\hbar\kappa/2\pi k_B$: their Eq.~(9)
writes the emitted flux as $[e^{\omega/2\pi\kappa} - 1]^{-1}$, an exponent
smaller than the standard $2\pi\omega/\kappa$ by $(2\pi)^2$, and it is that
exponent, not the label attached to it, which reproduces their quoted
fluxes exactly (their $I^{\rm HR} = 3$ and $5$ at $20$~GHz detuning follow
from $\kappa = 0.07$ and $\SI{0.11}{\per\pico\second}$ to within rounding).
Under the normalization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H}, their measured $\kappa$
corresponds to \SIrange{0.085}{0.13}{\kelvin}. The bilayer-graphene value
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_H_dean} is therefore roughly twenty times above the
polariton scale, not comparable to it; the apparent near-agreement in the
literature is a convention artifact.

We take the polariton comparison only at the smooth-horizon
configurations. Ref.~\cite{Falque2025} states that the spectrum of their
steep horizons is dominated by nonlinear frequency dispersion and is
therefore not thermal [\citenum{Falque2025}, p.~6], and we make no thermal
claim about those configurations.

\section{Conclusion}

The bilayer-graphene electronic de~Laval nozzle is the first platform on
which an analog Hawking spectrum is both thermodynamically accessible and
electrically readable. At the assumed horizon gradient
$\kappa = \SI{2e12}{\per\second}$ it radiates at
$T_H = \SI{2.43}{\kelvin}$, nine orders of magnitude above atomic BEC and
roughly twenty times above the polariton platform on a common
normalization, and the signal is a
\SI{1.2e-26}{\ampere\squared/\hertz} Planckian excess separated from a
white background by shape over $\omega/\omega_H \in [0.5,4]$. The
Schwinger--Keldysh organization of the corrections is what distinguishes
this platform: the dispersive and dissipative channels are comparable, of
opposite sign, and both small, so their ratio is a quantitative prediction
rather than an extrapolation.

That prediction is currently gated on three inputs the experiment has not
yet supplied: the velocity gradient at the sonic point, the transport
coefficients at the operating density, and the electron-electron length at
operating bias. The first fixes the whole frequency scale, and our value
differs from Ref.~\cite{Geurs2025}'s own estimate by a factor of five for
exactly that reason. The third fixes the sign of the net temperature shift,
which flips between the two values in circulation. We report the magnitudes
as predictions and the gradient scale as an assumption because that is what
they are, and the machine-checked chain
(\bundleTheorems{}~theorems / \bundleModules{}~modules;
\texttt{lake build} clean, v\leantoolchain, Mathlib \texttt{\mathlibrev})
supplies the algebraic backbone rather than the physical inputs.
