## 1. Complete algebraic coefficient representation

For every actual anchor and each chiral SU(2) factor, write

  U(q)=q0 I + i(q1 sigma1+q2 sigma2+q3 sigma3),  q real,
  q0^2+q1^2+q2^2+q3^2=1,  Udagger=q0 I-i sum_k qk sigmak.          (2)

Spin(4)=SU(2)+ times SU(2)- here; its two centers must not be identified. This is a coordinate substitution for the actual link representation; it does not change anchored labels or multiply matrix words in a different order. Each retained or integrated Spin(4) anchor has two independent S3 coordinates. Matrix multiplication remains ordered until individual scalar entries are expanded. Conjugation conjugates scalar coefficients and fixes real q.

The polynomial function algebra on these product spheres is the polynomial ring modulo the ideal generated by one relation (2) per chiral anchor. A concrete exact normal form repeatedly replaces q0^2 by 1-q1^2-q2^2-q3^2 in each factor. The distinct leading monomials q0,a^2 are relatively prime, so these relations form a Groebner basis in a block order prioritizing each q0; the remainder has q0 degree at most one per factor. Completeness can also be seen directly: for one sphere write the remainder A(q1,q2,q3)+q0 B(q1,q2,q3). Its values at both q0=+sqrt(1-|q|^2) and -sqrt(1-|q|^2) force A=B=0 on the open unit ball if it vanishes on S3; hence both polynomials vanish identically. Apply this argument successively for all factors. This also handles complex coefficients by real/imaginary parts.

For an integrated SU(2) factor the exact normalized moment is zero if any exponent is odd; otherwise, writing a0+...+a3=A,

  integral_S3 product_(k=0..3) qk^(2ak)
    = product_k (2ak-1)!! / [4*6*...*(2A+2)],                     (3)

with empty denominator 1 and (-1)!!=1. Derivation: write an isotropic four-dimensional standard Gaussian as r q, with independent radius and uniform q; the numerator is its independent-coordinate even moment and E r^(2A)=4*6*...*(2A+2). Haar equals uniform S3 because left/right unit-quaternion multiplication is orthogonal and preserves normalized surface measure. Product Haar factors only across distinct actual anchor/chirality variables AFTER shared occurrences have been collected. Equations (2)-(3) include unitarity, determinant-one, pseudoreality, center-parity and all higher polynomial Haar identities without imposing independent formal edge letters. They do not license separate averaging of factors sharing an anchor.

Apply (3) first to the sixteen original temporal anchors in the two independent edges, then to the 24 sewn spatial anchors in b. Reduce the remaining 24 retained-anchor polynomials by (2). Let {m_mu} be the finite union of normal-form monomials occurring in all b_ij (zero and repeated features may remain). Write

  b_ij=sum_mu beta_(mu,ij) m_mu,
  r_(mu,ab)=sum_PQR h_(PQ;ab) beta_(mu,(10,QR),(01,RP)),

Then T(H)=r is EXACTLY Phi(H)=R for every retained u and every a,b. Include every coefficient row in this finite union; a degree cutoff chosen before proving completeness would alter the problem. Fixed exact couplings and gamma conventions belong to the coefficient field; no rationality of arbitrary real couplings is assumed. Formal symbolic coupling equality is stronger than equality at the prescribed coupling point.

## 3. A useful closedness result specific to this Gram map

General SDP images need not be closed; the present fully retained Gram map has extra structure. The following manual argument is a proposed supporting result, not an evaluated L1 certificate.

Set X(u,w)_(ia,b)=x_i(u,w) delta_ab, so Phi(H)=integral dw Xdagger H X. Define

  F=integral du dw X Xdagger,    ell(Q)=integral du Tr Q(u).
  ell(Phi(H))=Tr(H F).                                             (6)

F is a fixed PSD matrix. If v lies in ker F, then integral ||Xdagger v||^2=0, so Xdagger v=0 almost everywhere and, by continuity/full support, everywhere. Thus Phi(H)=Phi(P H P), where P projects onto range F; compression loses no feasible output. It removes globally zero linear feature combinations only, not boundary configurations, physical observables or negative output directions. On that finite-dimensional range, F is positive definite, unless the whole map is zero. For compressed H PSD,

  lambda_min(F|range F) Tr H <= ell(Phi(H)).                        (7)

Consequently if Phi(H_n) converges in its finite polynomial coefficient space, the right side is bounded, H_n has bounded trace/norm, and a subsequence converges to H PSD with the prescribed limit output. The Gram output cone is therefore closed. The zero-map case is trivially closed. No numerical lower eigenvalue bound or practical complexity estimate follows from this argument.

It follows by finite-dimensional closed-cone separation that an infeasible Hermitian R admits a real y of the type in section 2, after representing Phi by the complete real coefficient map. This is a mathematical existence statement for this map, not a computed witness or blanket SDP strong-duality assumption. It relies on retaining the full output matrix, its integrated trace functional, the complete coefficient representation and global feature-kernel compression. Dropping output rows/entries or using an unproved truncated moment system can destroy that argument. If all data are real algebraic, real-closed-field transfer also permits algebraic certificate entries; rational entries are not guaranteed. For arbitrary real couplings, no effective exact coefficient oracle is supplied here.

For a concrete route, first determine the exact linear feature kernel through F, compress, and solve the affine PSD feasibility or its dual.
