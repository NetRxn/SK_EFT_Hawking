## 1. Exact Hermitian-square / Gram identity: strongest compact-certificate template

J. W. Helton, “Positive” Non-Commutative Polynomials are Sums of Squares, Theorem 1.1, pp. 1–2: https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~helton/osiris/NONCOMMINEQ/posPoly.pdf . For a symmetric free polynomial over real noncommuting generators and their transposes, positivity under **every finite-dimensional real matrix substitution of every size** is equivalent to a sum of transpose squares. The easy square-to-positivity direction can be checked directly on bounded operators; the converse does not say that an element positive at this one concrete representation has a free SOS, much less a small one. Complex adjoints require an explicit legitimate complexification or direct Hilbert-space verification, not an unstated application of the real theorem.

Prospective inference: if a *separately proved exact source identity* has the form `c I + sum_i z_i G_i = sum_a Q_a^* Q_a`, or includes individually proven positive constraint terms, it establishes positivity on the entire original carrier, including the infinite complement. The target must remain in the frozen linear span; Qs may be auxiliary concrete bounded operators but their products are NOT assigned moments unless independently related to frozen rows. Scalar c must be nonnegative as the complement demands. A contradictory original assigned value is `c + sum_i z_i n_i/Z < 0`, checked with the actual complex denominator and exact reality/sign arguments. This is a prospective negative certificate, not a discovered one. In particular the ordered one-kernel pair C_AB is not presumed to be C_A* C_B or any loop product.

Discovery cost is unknown and need not be small. Certificate size is the encoded Q circuits, coefficient/adjoint/source identities and scalar proof, not 233 alone. Verification could avoid full support assembly only if each source-kernel operator identity is checked by a separately bounded sparse exact algebra/integration argument; expansion may grow exponentially and equality on a sampled subspace does not suffice. Moment evaluation is independent: provide the exact original numerators, the same Z, all signs, and an exact strict inequality, with complete bit/time bounds.

## 2. Explicit exact Gram matrices and boundary caveat

K. Cafuta, I. Klep, J. Povh, “Rational sums of Hermitian squares of free noncommutative polynomials,” Proposition 2.1 p. 4 and Theorems 3.2–3.3 pp. 7–8: https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/3984.pdf . Proposition 2.1 uses the word vector through degree d: a symmetric degree-at-most-2d free polynomial is SOS iff it has a PSD Gram matrix in that word basis. Section 3's rounding/projection result requires the stated strict quantitative eigenvalue-versus-residual condition; its facial-reduction equivalence concerns the paper's specified reduction. The authors also explicitly show a rational polynomial cyclically equivalent to a real SOS but not rationally so (p. 7): rational exactification is not automatic at a boundary.

Use only as a *prospective certificate representation*: an exact small PSD `D` (e.g. exact LDL* with nonnegative diagonal) and exact kernel identity `Y = V* D V` verify a witness without diagonalizing the astronomical support matrix, provided `V` consists of bounded concrete operators and the equality is proven in the original representation. A nonnegative rational-weighted square factorization also handles PSD D when rational square roots are absent. Do not substitute cyclic equivalence: it proves a trace statement under suitable tracial conditions, not operator positivity. The proposed positive comparison functional is not assumed tracial, and graded original trace is not an ordinary positive trace.

Discovery is an SDP or algebraic search of unbounded, separately priced cost, not licensed here; even a tiny PSD matrix may hide a huge `V` or proof of identity. Verification includes exact Gram PSD, full source identity, auxiliary boundedness, exact scalar block and original moment evaluation. No proof that such a small Gram vector exists for the rows follows from the paper.

## 3. Sparse nc optimization: guarded technique, and an explicit reason not to assume completeness

I. Klep, V. Magron, J. Povh, “Sparse Noncommutative Polynomial Optimization,” Assumptions 2.3–2.4 pp. 5–6, Theorem 3.3 p. 7, and Example 5.2 pp. 16–17: https://igorklep.github.io/files/ncsparse.pdf . Theorem 3.3 requires the displayed local boundedness constraints and running-intersection structure; it assumes strict positivity for all operator tuples in the specified infinite-dimensional domain and then yields a sparse quadratic-module representation. Example 5.2 explicitly gives a globally SOS sparse polynomial with no SOS decomposition confined to the prescribed sparse variable blocks. Its convergence statements carry additional hypotheses (e.g. Corollary 5.9).

Potential use: select a few frozen labels and build *only after authorization* an exact candidate identity with small, source-justified operator-generator blocks; if found, independently check it as in item 1. This is a restricted sufficient witness scheme, not a complete characterization of this concrete system, and failure has no exclusionary force. There is no established running-intersection graph, local boundedness certificate, universal positivity hypothesis, or finite coefficient bound for this source. Theorem 3.3 supplies neither a small certificate nor a computational bound for these rows. Source-identity proof may dominate sparse Gram checking; original moment cost is unchanged.

## 4. Trace-only certificates: useful exclusion boundary, not an operator witness

I. Klep and M. Schweighofer, “Sums of Hermitian Squares and the BMV Conjecture,” Proposition 2.3 and Theorem 2.5, pp. 2–3: https://igorklep.github.io/files/bmv-31jul08.pdf . The paper explains cyclic equivalence through ordinary finite-matrix trace cyclicity, and distinguishes sums of Hermitian squares from sums of squares modulo commutators. Its trace-positivity construction is not a claim that the underlying operator is positive. For the present full-carrier necessary condition, a commutator remainder cannot be discarded simply because an auxiliary ordinary trace kills it: a positive comparison state is not assumed tracial; original graded trace/Z has its own ordering, parity, denominator and domain. If a particular commutator is proven to vanish as an operator, or its assigned expectation is independently established with correct source identities, one can reassess that separate statement. Do not infer a positive witness from trace-only SOS.
