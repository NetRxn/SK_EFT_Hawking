## Correction to a claim made during the 2026-08-07 pass

⚠️ **The readiness formula was described as having no unreviewed-guard. It has one.**
`bundle_readiness.py:370-391` evaluates `not review_recorded → YELLOW (unreviewed)` before
reaching GREEN, and withholds GREEN when a P1 gate is blocked or could not be computed.

The actual defect is narrower and should be stated that way: **`review_recorded` does not
discriminate review KIND or SCOPE.** Any document named by `stage13_review_doc` satisfies it, so
a 16-anchor attribution sweep counts the same as a full adversarial pass. That is how D9 reached
GREEN with Stage 10 never run — not an absent guard, but a guard that cannot tell two very
different reviews apart.

## Correction — the chain-link figure

`chain_backing_targets_resolve` was built 2026-08-07 to gate what §5b of the remediation plan
identified as measured-but-ungated. It initially carried **its own resolver** and reported **156**
unresolvable links against §5b's **121** — a second resolver beside a working one, disagreeing
with it, which is the duplication §6a's approval sequence exists to prevent.

**Reconciled.** The local resolver is deleted; the check now calls
`chain_canonicalize.canonicalize_link` over `_iter_links` and reports **121 unresolvable
theorem/axiom/lemma links, 60 distinct targets, 25 papers** — matching `--report` exactly. An
AST test asserts no resolution helper can be reintroduced into the check.

§5b's caveat carries forward unchanged and is now recorded in the check itself: the count is an
**upper bound**, because ambiguous short names are counted `theorem-absent`.

## A5. The canonical AI-disclosure template asserts a process that is not backed — NEW, corpus-wide

**`docs/DISCLOSURE_TEXT.md:27-29` and `:42-43`** — the canonical block installed per bundle before
deposit — reads:

> *"Manuscript prose was AI-assisted and human-reviewed; all numerical claims trace to the
> repository's computation pipeline through its automated validation checks."*

**Eleven drafts carry it**, including D1, D2, D3, D4, D5, D9, E1, L2.

**The second clause is false for the bundles that carry it, and the project's own checks say so:**

- `numerical_literals` reports **116 inline literals** across the corpus — D1 13, D3 13, D5 7,
  E1 4, D4 3 — i.e. numbers typed into prose rather than routed through the pipeline. `count_literals`
  reports **107 hard-coded count literals across 64 papers**.
- **D9 has no `claims_review.json` at all**, so no sentence in it has a chain of backing.
- The per-bundle audit records theorem-count overclaims in the same drafts: **D1-03 states 26,077
  machine-verified theorems for a chain of 114 (~230×)**; E1-03 the same figure for five results;
  D5-01/-02 ~500×. These arise because `\substantivetheorems{}` is **project-scoped and used as if
  paper-scoped**, and it *auto-inflates* as the library grows.

**Why this is the sharpest item in the corpus.** The mechanism built to make numbers trustworthy —
a generated macro — is the mechanism producing the largest numerical overclaim in the portfolio,
inside manuscripts whose disclosure section asserts that every number traces through automated
validation. A referee who follows that sentence into the public repository finds checks that
report the opposite.

**Scope:** template-level, not per-bundle. Fixing D9 alone does not close it, and every future
bundle inherits it.

**Required end state:** the disclosure states only what is true of the bundle carrying it — that
proofs are kernel-checked, and which review stages actually ran. The numerical-traceability clause
is removed, or made conditional on `claims_review.json` existing and the literal checks being
clean for that bundle. **Size: `small` per bundle, `trivial` for the template — but it is a
claim-integrity item, not a copy-edit.**
