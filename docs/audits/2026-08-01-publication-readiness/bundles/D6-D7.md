## Per-bundle findings

### D7

**This bundle is not publishable, and it is not a paper.** At 3 compiled pages and 1,737 words it is roughly 7 % of a PRX-class article of its stated scope. Four of its seven sections — §2 Belief propagation, §3 LDP-controlled BP convergence, §4 Chebyshev Chern bridge, §5 The demarcation theorem (the *headline*) — contain no prose at all: each is a single bracketed editorial note listing Lean theorem names and telling a future editor to expand it. The two sections that do contain prose (Introduction, Discussion) are largely the same sentences restated. This is an **extended abstract with a bibliography**, and the section that should carry the paper's entire contribution is a placeholder.

**What this manuscript currently is:** a title, an abstract, an introduction that previews four contributions, four stub sections where those contributions should be, a scope-caveat section, a discussion that repeats the introduction's caveats, and six references of which three are never cited.

**Strongest asset:** the `loopCorrectionRate` construction in `BPLDPSimulability.lean` (672 lines) is real, non-trivial Lean work — a genuine Cramér/Legendre supremum with a proven closed form `−log(1−p)`, strict positivity, strict monotonicity, a finite-*n* Cramér identity, and a Kullback–Leibler bridge. It is the one thing in this bundle that would survive a referee's scrutiny *as mathematics*. It is not, however, a simulability criterion (see D7-02).

#### The headline claim, tested

The manuscript's central object is named, in the abstract (line 42) and in the Introduction (line 127), as the biconditional `analog_hawking_quantum_advantage_demarcation`.

**That theorem does not exist.** Grepping every `.lean` file under `lean/` returns zero declarations of that name. The same is true of `bp_convergence_iff_ldp_below_threshold`, also listed in `bundle_metadata.json:46` under `headline_theorems`. Two of the bundle's four declared headline theorems are phantoms. The actual theorem is `analog_hawking_fourCycleFree_demarcation` (`AnalogHawkingDemarcation.lean:141`) — a different name asserting a materially weaker claim.

**Is the "iff" genuine, or is a direction trivial?** The biconditional is *true* and both directions are proven, but its content is far weaker than the prose asserts, on three counts:

1. **The theorem's left-hand side is defined to be the right-hand side's preimage.** `IsAnalogHawkingFourCycleFreeSimulable G w c1 := IsFourCycleFreeNonnegWeighted G w ∧ c1 = 0` (line 118). The headline proof is two lines: `unfold` then `rw [fourCycleFree_nonneg_iff_ldp_rate_zero, crystalline_eq_quasicrystalline_iff_c1_zero]`. The theorem contributes only `A↔A′, B↔B′ ⊢ A∧B ↔ A′∧B′` — associativity of conjunction under two rewrites. This is the project's own "defining-the-conclusion" anti-pattern (CLAUDE.md preemptive-strengthening check #5).

2. **The topological axis is a linear polynomial, not topology.** `categoricalChernExpansion c0 c1 := ⟨[c0, c1]⟩` and `evalChebyshev ⟨[c0,c1]⟩ x = c0 + c1*x` (`ChebyshevTN.lean:166`). So the "crystalline limit" is `c0+c1`, the "quasicrystalline limit" is `c0−c1`, and the headline `crystalline_eq_quasicrystalline_iff_c1_zero` is `c0+c1 = c0−c1 ↔ c1 = 0`, discharged by `linarith` in both directions. There is no Chern class, no Berry curvature, no Brillouin zone, no bundle — `c1` is an unconstrained real number and the "real-space Chern marker" is the function `x ↦ c0 + c1·x`. The second of the abstract's two "load-bearing axes" is therefore **vacuous as physics**: it asserts `c1 = 0 ⟺ c1 = 0` with intermediate renaming.

3. **The LDP axis is a monotone reparameterisation of a 4-cycle count.** `loopDensity G := |fourCycles G| / |ν×ν×α×α|` (`BPLDPSimulability.lean:185`) and `loopCorrectionRate G = −log(1 − loopDensity G)`. Since `−log(1−p)` is strictly monotone and vanishes only at `p=0`, `rate = 0 ⟺ fourCycles G = ∅`. The elaborate rate-function apparatus adds no discriminating power over the predicate "the 4-cycle set is empty." The abstract concedes the connection to actual loop corrections (Chertkov–Chernyak loop series over BP message space) "remains future work" (line 122) — so the rate is attached to a combinatorial count with no established relation to BP error.

Stripped of naming, the headline is `(4-cycle-free ∧ w≥0 ∧ c1=0) ↔ (4-cycle-free ∧ w≥0) ∧ (c1=0)`.

**Most damning: the Lean source itself refutes the manuscript's reading.** `AnalogHawkingDemarcation.lean:31–39` carries an explicit block titled "**Honesty scope (remediation B-04, 2026-07-17)**":

> "The structural condition is *four-cycle-freeness* (+ non-negative weights + trivial Chern), NOT genuine acyclicity/tree-ness and NOT a proof of classical simulability or a quantum-advantage lower bound. … The 'classically simulable' / 'quantum advantage' reading is the physical interpretation of this screen, carried in the surrounding prose (papers / README), **not established by the Lean statements here**."

The manuscript's abstract (lines 34–42) nevertheless states that a system "is classically simulable in the combined BP / Chebyshev-TN regime **if and only if**" the two conditions hold. The Lean author wrote down, in the source file, that this is exactly the claim the theorem does not support — and the paper makes it anyway, in the abstract, as the title claim. This is the single worst finding in the pair.

#### Positioning against the literature

Three cited works. The classical-simulability boundary is one of the most contested areas in quantum information, with a large literature the manuscript does not touch: Gottesman–Knill and stabilizer/magic-state simulation, Markov–Shi treewidth contraction, Bravyi–Gosset stabilizer-rank methods, Aaronson–Chen and the sampling-hardness line, Zhou–Stoudenmire–Waintal and the Google-supremacy TN rebuttals, Pan–Zhang, Begušić–Chan on Kicked-Ising, Napp *et al.* on shallow 2D circuits, and Oh–Jiang–Fefferman on Gaussian boson sampling. A demarcation paper that cites none of these has not entered the conversation it claims to settle. A PRX referee would demand, at minimum: (a) where this criterion sits relative to treewidth and stabilizer-rank criteria; (b) whether it is *necessary*, *sufficient*, or neither, for any established notion of efficient simulation; (c) a worked comparison on at least one instance family where the classical/quantum boundary is known; (d) why belief propagation on a factor graph is the right lens when the cited Tindall–Sels result concerns BP-contracted PEPS for dynamics.

**This bundle is not submittable in its current form, though unlike D7 it is a real manuscript.** D6 is 12 compiled pages against a ~40 pp target, with zero figures and zero tables. It is the one section that reads like a physics result rather than a Lean inventory.

#### The "end-to-end" claim, tested specifically

The abstract's contribution sentence is: "*Together, these results form, to our knowledge, the first end-to-end kernel-verified 'substrate' (code → measurement → compiler → universal logic) for the FT-QC frontier*" (lines 49–52).

**The chain has a hole where the compiler link should be.** §2 (`sec:sk`), the compilation section, contains no technical content at all. It is 18 lines stating that the Solovay–Kitaev primitive's "canonical home … is the sibling bundle **D8**", that "D6 *consumes* D8's quantitative Solovay–Kitaev headline", and that the Phase 6t content "*previously held as a bundle-orphan stub*" now lives elsewhere (lines 136–146). There is no theorem statement, no bound, no ε-scaling, no word-length result — and **no `\cite` to D8 anywhere in the manuscript** (all 9 cite keys are external works; D8 appears only as bold prose text). So the compiler element of "code → measurement → compiler → universal logic" is neither proven here nor citable from here. A referee cannot follow the chain, and the end-to-end claim — which the abstract identifies as the whole contribution — is unverifiable from this document.

This directly answers the dependency question: **D6 does not remain coherent without D8.** Its headline synthesis requires a link that exists only as a forward-reference to an unpublished sibling. Either D8 must be cited as a preprint with an arXiv number, or the SK bound must be restated in D6 with its own proof.

**Are the results woven, or five disconnected sections?** They are five sections with a synthesis sentence on top. The interconnection prose that does exist is thin and in one case circular: §5.3 says the SK substrate "is consumed elsewhere in the bundle (§6 on the W-state QFT)" (lines 415–417), while §6.3 says the W-state decomposition is *exact* and needs no approximation, invoking SK only hypothetically "*when the target gate set is finite*" (lines 493–502). So the one place SK is claimed to be consumed states that it is not needed. No section's result is used as an input to another section's theorem. There is no composite theorem anywhere in the manuscript or the Lean that chains code → measurement → compilation → logic. The synthesis is asserted in the abstract and never constructed.

Note also that the abstract enumerates **four** results and omits §5 (Shor T-gate counts) entirely (lines 38–48), as does the Introduction's "four interlocking substrate-level questions" (lines 73–83) — while `PAPER_STRATEGY.md:130` lists **five** (adding "(v) the Google/Caltech Shor T-gate counts") and the Introduction's own "Bundle architecture" paragraph names a *third* four-element set that includes Shor but drops SK (lines 122–124). Three different rosters in one document.
