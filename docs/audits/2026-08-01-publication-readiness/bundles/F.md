## Per-bundle findings

### F

**This manuscript is not publishable as a Tier-0 review article, and the gap is not an editing gap.**

**What this manuscript currently is:** a 21-page, two-column executive summary of the SK-EFT
Hawking *project*, organised as one ~2-page section per internal publication bundle, written for
a reader who already knows what "Bundle D3", "Phase 6m Track B", and "Stage-13" mean. It contains
no figures, no tables, no labelled equations, and 44 external literature citations. Its §12
Conclusions restate §1 nearly verbatim, and its §11 describes the project's own submission
roll-out schedule. A *Reviews of Modern Physics* editor would read the first two pages — which
open with "The SK–EFT Hawking program asks a structural question" rather than with the field —
and desk-reject on genre and length before reaching any physics.

**Strongest asset:** the hedging discipline in the physics prose is genuinely good and unusual.
`paper_draft.tex:974-977` volunteers that "The factor-6000 figure on the emergent Newton constant
ratio is an informal extrapolation from this ≥3-orders-of-magnitude coupling deficit and is not
separately Lean-verified at the Newton-constant level"; `:985-988` flags the naturalness window
$\chi_{\rm vest}\in[0.1,10]$ as "a modeling choice of this project rather than a published
derivation"; `:1804-1805` repeats it. The 2026-05-11 first-claim removal pass (8 priority claims
deleted, per `change_log.md`) has held — a grep for "the first machine-checked / first in any proof
assistant" class returns nothing in the body. That epistemic posture is the thing worth keeping,
and it is rarer than the length.

### 2. Is there an index of results?

**No.** The closest artifact, §X ("Substrate predictive register: the boundary partition", `:1727-1946`, 3 compiled pp), is a set of five bulleted lists totalling 31 entries. For each entry a reader gets:

- a **statement** — yes, usually one sentence, and usually with the quantitative value (this part is genuinely decent);
- a **pointer to the deep paper** — only as an internal section number of an unpublished draft ("D3 \S 7", "D5 \S\ref{sec:dark-sector}"). Since D1–D5 are cited as *in preparation*, these pointers are unresolvable by any reader today, and they will not survive the deep papers' own section renumbering;
- a **pointer to the machine-checked artifact** — for **5 of 31 entries**. No module path, no line, no version, no repository (F-10). The remaining 26 entries name no theorem at all.

There is no table, no sort order, no completeness claim, and no way to look up a result by name. `:2149` explicitly tells the reader "\S\ref{sec:substrate-register} ships the consolidated table" — it does not. **For a review whose stated core value is the index, this is the single most consequential gap in the bundle**, and it is `new-work`: the table has to be generated from the Lean dependency graph and the registries, and nothing in the repo currently emits it in publication form.

### 3. Does it read as a review of a field, or as a summary of one group's repo?

**A summary of one group's repo, unambiguously.** Four independent indicators:

1. **No introduction to the field** (F-13). The document has no section that reviews prior work by anyone else. Unruh 1981 is not cited. Steinhauer 2016 and de Nova 2019 are cited once each, in a single parenthesis at `:582-583`, as background to the project's own module.

Roughly 3 of 21 body pages are about the project's own paper-production, not about physics.
3. **The reference list leans on self-citation to unpublished internal work.** 9 of 53 bibitems are `J. G. Roehm, "… (Bundle Dx)," in preparation (2026)`, and these 9 carry most of the load-bearing cross-references in §§IV–X — every "D3 §7" pointer resolves to one of them. 44 works by anyone else, total, for a survey of a field.

### 4. Did the `ARCHITECTURE_SCOPE.md` lift into §10 (delivered as §II) happen honestly?

**No — it is a stale snapshot, and it contradicts the source on the one point the source added specifically to stop that contradiction.**

What was lifted (faithfully): the three layers, Layer-3 SM+GR in-scope, dark-sector out-of-scope under tested mechanisms, "What this does not claim", the Phase 6m three-track closure, the post-6m recalibration, and the architectural implications. All of this matches `ARCHITECTURE_SCOPE.md:22-180` closely, and the "sharpening of scope, not a retreat" framing at `:349-353` is a fair reproduction of the source's `:55-96`. Credit where due: this part is honest, and the four-category boundary partition is a good idea well executed.

What was **not** lifted, all of it added to the source *after* F's last lift (2026-06-10):

- **`ARCHITECTURE_SCOPE.md:238-262` — "Formalization depth vs predictive scope (Phases 6n–6z)".** The source states the QC arc (D6/D7/D8) "is *orthogonal* to 'what fluid substrates predict about fundamental physics'" and that "A reader assessing the program's physics claims should weigh it as evidence of method rigor, not as new physics scope." F says the opposite in its own voice at `:123-127`: D6/D7/D8 are "the verified quantum-computation frontier **connected through the program's FKLW/horizon-MTC line**". The change log at 2026-06-10 shows this framing was a deliberate choice made to justify counting them among the siblings while avoiding a hard "sixteen faces" coupling claim — i.e. the project knew the coupling was not verifiable and softened the *count* while keeping the *connection* language. The source document's later correction was not propagated.
- **`ARCHITECTURE_SCOPE.md:266-336` — the 6C\* and 6E\* arcs (D11, D12), added 2026-07-30.** Absent from F entirely (F-08). This includes the source's own note that it "previously carried zero mentions of either, which made a substantial body of verified work invisible to anyone reading the scope boundary" — F now reproduces exactly that invisibility.
