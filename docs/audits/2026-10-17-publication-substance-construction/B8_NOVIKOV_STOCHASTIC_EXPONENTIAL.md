## 1. Actual probability objects and the theorem

Fix a finite deterministic horizon T>=0, a complete right-continuous filtration (F_t) on a probability space (Omega,F,P), and a real standard Brownian motion W relative to it. Thus W is continuous/adapted, W0=0, and W_t-W_s is independent of F_s with centered Gaussian law of variance t-s for s<=t. These are actual stochastic-process assumptions, not a continuous deterministic function named W.

Let theta be real predictable for the sigma algebra generated by (s,t] times A, A in F_s, with the time-zero sets irrelevant for dt. Put

V_t=integral_0^t theta_s² ds,    C=E exp(V_T/2)<infinity.             (1)

We construct the Brownian L² integral M_t=integral_0^t theta_s dW_s, then construct

Z_t=exp(M_t-V_t/2),    Z0=1.                                       (2)

We prove Z is a positive uniformly-integrable martingale on [0,T], closed by Z_T, with E Z_T=1. In particular Q(A)=E[1_A Z_T] is an actual probability equivalent to P on F_T. This is the exact coefficient1/2 Novikov criterion, with no extra (1+epsilon) exponential moment. T=0 gives V=M=0 and Z=1 directly. Below T>0. Since y<=2exp(y/2) for y>=0, (1) implies E V_T<infinity, and V_T is finite almost surely.

The proof constructs the integral and the exponential local martingale before proving true-martingale mass. It does not assume the desired exponential martingale as a field, or invoke the currently unconstructed project Itô predicate as a change-of-variables theorem. Classical elementary probability facts used below are Gaussian conditional integration, L² completion, conditional expectation, the martingale maximal inequality and bounded stopping; their use is mathematical, with no claim of new native API verification.

## 2. Elementary integral, actual isometry and completion

A bounded elementary predictable h has a finite deterministic partition 0=t0<...<tm=T and bounded F_ti-measurable real coefficients h_i, with h_s=h_i on (t_i,t_(i+1)]. Define the actual continuous adapted process

J_t(h)=sum_i h_i[W_(min(t,t_(i+1)))-W_(min(t,t_i))].                (3)

Conditional independence and Gaussian mean0 give the martingale property. To compute a terminal second moment, expand the square. For i<j the i-th weighted increment and h_j are F_tj-measurable, and the j-th future increment has conditional mean0, so every cross expectation vanishes. The diagonal conditional variance is h_i²(t_(i+1)-t_i). Refining partitions gives, for all t,

E J_t(h)²=E integral_0^t h_s² ds,
E[J_t(h)-J_t(k)]²=E integral_0^t(h_s-k_s)² ds.                     (4)

The same expansion conditionally from any deterministic s proves J_t(h)²-integral_0^t h² is a martingale, including within a partition cell (condition at s, where the active coefficient is already known).

These elementary functions are dense in L² of the predictable sigma algebra under P times dt. Here is the measure-theoretic reason, not a definition of predictability as the desired closure. Indicators of generating rectangles (s,t] times A are elementary. Their finite intersections are of the same type: use the later left endpoint, with both events measurable there; the constant function on(0,T] is included. The closed L² linear span of their indicators contains indicators of the generated sigma algebra by the monotone-class theorem: finite unions/complements remain in the algebra, and bounded monotone indicator limits converge in L² on this finite measure space. Simple predictable functions and then arbitrary L² predictable functions follow by truncation/simple approximation. Finite linear combinations of rectangles can be represented on one common finite partition, and their coefficients at each left endpoint are bounded and measurable there. This proves the asserted elementary density for theta under (1).

Choose bounded elementary h^k->theta in L²(P dt). For a square-integrable martingale N, the usual maximal inequality is

E sup_(t<=T)|N_t|²<=4 E|N_T|².                                    (5)

For context, it follows first at finite deterministic grids from the stopping-level inequality lambda P(max|N|>lambda)<=E[|N_T|1_{max|N|>lambda}], integration in lambda and Cauchy--Schwarz; refine grids and use continuity. Thus (4),(5) make J(h^k) Cauchy in L²(Omega;sup norm on C[0,T]). Its limit M has a continuous version, is adapted by completeness (take a uniformly almost-sure convergent subsequence) and is a true square-integrable martingale: at each s,t its L² limit preserves conditional expectation. Its value is independent of the elementary approximation by (4),(5), and (4) extends to theta. In particular

E M_t²=E V_t,    E sup_(t<=T)|M_t|²<=4 E V_T.                     (6)

The process V is adapted continuous nondecreasing, hence predictable. It is finite on[0,T] almost surely. Also

E sup_t |integral_0^t[(h^k)²-theta²]ds|
 <=||h^k-theta||_(L²(Pdt)) ||h^k+theta||_(L²(Pdt)) ->0.             (7)

By Cauchy--Schwarz, (5) and uniform L² bounds on M/J, J(h^k)²->M² in L¹ of the uniform norm. Passing the elementary conditional identity through this L¹ limit using (7) proves M²-V is a true martingale. Thus V is the actual bracket of M: it is a continuous predictable increasing compensator of M², with V0=0. Uniqueness does not hide an Itô assumption: a continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant. To see that assertion, stop both variation and size at finite bounds, expand square increments of the resulting bounded martingale over refining grids, and use orthogonality. The sum of squared increments is at most maximal increment times total variation, tending to0 by continuity and dominated convergence. Its expectation equals the terminal squared displacement, so that displacement is0; localization and rational times finish the assertion. Consequently two such continuous compensators coincide.

## 3. Genuine elementary stochastic exponentials

For any bounded elementary h and any real q, define

E_t(qh)=exp(q J_t(h)-q² integral_0^t h² ds/2).                      (8)

On one partition interval, conditioning on its left endpoint turns the coefficient into a known value. The actual Gaussian identity E exp(u DeltaW-u²Deltat/2)=1 holds also for that random known coefficient, by conditional Gaussian integration. Iterating the tower property across the finite partition, and first conditioning at an intermediate time if needed, proves (8) is a positive true martingale with expectation1 at EVERY time. This is not an inference from a continuity predicate.

If |h|<=B, then for p>1 the identity

E_t(h)^p=E_t(ph) exp[(p²-p)integral_0^t h² ds/2]

and the already proved unit mean of E(ph) give E E_t(h)^p<=exp[(p²-p)B²T/2]. In fact this martingale is closed by its integrable terminal value, as the finite conditional calculation gives E[E_T(qh)|F_t]=E_t(qh). It is therefore uniformly integrable on the finite horizon. Bounded optional stopping follows by finite-grid stopping and passage to the limit using continuity and this closed-martingale integrability; no unbounded stopping time is used here. The same facts apply for every fixed q and every h^k separately, although their bounds B_k may grow.

## 4. Construct local martingale (2) by controlled energy stopping

Set J^k=J(h^k), V^k=integral(h^k)², Z^k=exp(J^k-V^k/2). By (5),(7), choose one subsequence with J^k->M and V^k->V uniformly on[0,T] almost surely (take summably fast norm convergence and the elementary Markov/Borel--Cantelli argument). We use this subsequence throughout. Then Z^k->Z uniformly almost surely by continuity of the exponential on each path's compact range. This alone does NOT imply Z is a local martingale; the uniform-integrability control after appropriate stopping is supplied next.

For K>0 define the bounded stopping times

 tau_K=inf{t in[0,T]:V_t>=K}, capped atT,
 sigma_K^k=inf{t in[0,T]:V_t^k>=K}, capped atT.                     (9)

We must not assume hitting times continuous under uniform convergence at every level. For a continuous nondecreasing path V, bad levels consist of V_T and the values of its nontrivial constant intervals. The latter are countable (assign a rational pair in each constant interval; disjoint plateau interiors contain distinct rational witnesses). For every other K>0, uniform V^k->V implies sigma_K^k->tau_K: before the first hit, V is strictly below K, and after it, strictly above K; if V_T<K the whole sequence eventually stays below K. At a good crossing these strict inequalities at tau_K±epsilon sandwich every approximate first hit. The excluded case V_T=K covers a terminal plateau/touch.

This good-level choice can be made DETERMINISTIC, without assuming every random path shares its plateaus. The set of pairs(omega,K) with a nontrivial plateau atK is measurable, expressed by some rationals r<s with V_r=V_s=K, together with the measurable endpoint graph K=V_T. Each path's section has Lebesgue measure0. Tonelli therefore shows that for Lebesgue-almost every deterministic K the probability of a bad path atK is0. Choose a deterministic increasing sequence K_j->infinity from this full-Lebesgue-measure set. Intersect the countably many probability-one events; for every j, sigma_(K_j)^k->tau_(K_j) almost surely. The almost-sure uniform approximation event is also retained.

Continuity of M,V now gives, for fixed good K, uniform almost-sure convergence on t in[0,T]:

Z^k_(t capped at sigma_K^k) -> Z_(t capped at tau_K).                 (10)

For instance, a stopped J^k differs from stopped M by its uniform approximation error plus the modulus of continuity of M at the distance between stopping times. That distance tends to0. The same argument works for V and then its exponential. Continuity of V^k ensures V^k_(T capped at sigma_K^k)<=K; there is no jump overshoot.

Fix p>1. At the SAME bounded sigma_K^k, the elementary identity (8), optional stopping for E(ph^k), and nonnegative energy give

E[(Z^k_(T capped at sigma_K^k))^p]
 <=exp[(p²-p)K/2] E[E_(T capped at sigma_K^k)(p h^k)]
 =exp[(p²-p)K/2].                                                  (11)

The stopped Z^k processes are true martingales. The p-maximal inequality gives a uniform bound on E sup_t(Z^k_(t capped at sigma_K^k))^p by (p/(p-1))^p times (11). It follows in particular that their values at each time are uniformly integrable. Combining (10) with uniform integrability gives L¹ convergence at each time. Conditional expectation against any bounded F_s-measurable test variable passes to the limit, so Z stopped at tau_K is a true martingale. Its initial value is1.

For the selected K_j, tau_(K_j) increases toT, and indeed equalsT eventually on every path with finite V_T. Thus (2) is an ACTUAL positive continuous local martingale on the specified finite horizon, constructed from Brownian Gaussian increments and controlled approximations. This argument does not invoke an assumed Itô change-of-variables identity or unproved closure of arbitrary uniformly convergent martingales.

A positive local martingale starting at1 is a supermartingale with E Z_rho<=1 for every stopping time rho<=T. Here this follows directly by conditional Fatou from the above true stopped martingales and bounded optional stopping. It does not yet give equality of mass atT. The same entire construction applies to a theta for any finite real a, since its L² norm is finite, producing

Z_t(a)=exp(a M_t-a² V_t/2).                                        (12)

Linearity of the L² construction identifies its stochastic integral as a M, rather than postulating a different process. Hence Z(a) is a positive local martingale and the same supermartingale mass bound applies.

## 5. Exact Novikov coefficient via scaled exponentials

Fix 0<a<1. Since a(2-a)=1-(1-a)²<1, select

1<p<=1/[a(2-a)].                                                   (13)

Then p a<1 and

 p a(1-a)/(2(1-p a)) <=1/2.                                        (14)

For ANY stopping time rho<=T, the following pathwise identity is exact:

Z_rho(a)^p=Z_rho(1)^(p a) exp[p a(1-a)V_rho/2].                    (15)

Apply Holder with conjugate exponents1/(p a) and1/(1-p a). The first factor's expectation is at most1 by the already constructed nonnegative local-martingale supermass bound. The second uses (14), V_rho<=V_T and (1):

E Z_rho(a)^p
 <=(E Z_rho(1))^(p a)
    (E exp[p a(1-a)V_rho/(2(1-p a))])^(1-p a)
 <=C^(1-p a)<infinity.                                             (16)

This is uniform in rho. No moment E exp((1+epsilon)V_T/2) is needed. Apply (16) to an actual localizing sequence for Z(a); its stopped terminal values have expectation1 and a uniform p-moment bound. They converge almost surely to Z_T(a), hence in L¹ by uniform integrability. Consequently E Z_T(a)=1, and the analogous conditional passage shows Z_t(a)=E[Z_T(a)|F_t]. Thus every SCALED 0<a<1 exponential is a true uniformly-integrable martingale. Nothing has yet assumed the same for a=1.

AtT another exact identity is

Z_T(a)=Z_T(1)^a exp[a(1-a)V_T/2].                                  (17)

Taking expectations and Holder exponents1/a and1/(1-a) gives

1=E Z_T(a)
 <=(E Z_T(1))^a (E exp[a V_T/2])^(1-a)
 <=(E Z_T(1))^a C^(1-a).                                           (18)

Since C>=1 and finite, (18) implies E Z_T(1)>=C^(-(1-a)/a). Let a increase to1. The lower bound tends to1; the supermartingale bound is <=1. Therefore

E Z_T=1.                                                         (19)

This is the critical endpoint argument; proving only all a<1 by itself would not prove (19). Formula(18) provides the missing lower-mass estimate. There is no unjustified assertion that pointwise convergence of normalized densities preserves their integrals.

## 6. True/UI martingale and actual measure change

For s<=t<=T, nonnegative local-martingale superconditioning gives E[Z_t|F_s]<=Z_s. Also 1=E Z_T<=E Z_t<=1, so equality holds in expectations. The nonnegative gap must vanish almost surely. Hence Z is a true martingale and Z_t=E[Z_T|F_t]. It is closed by one integrable terminal variable. The corresponding family at bounded stopping times is uniformly integrable: for Y_rho=E[Z_T|F_rho] and any A,K>0,

E[Y_rho 1_{Y_rho>K}]
 <=E[Z_T 1_{Z_T>A}]+A P(Y_rho>K)
 <=E[Z_T 1_{Z_T>A}]+A/K.                                         (20)

Choosing A=sqrt(K) proves uniform integrability independently of rho. Standard finite-grid approximation for bounded stopping times, using continuity and this closed-family estimate, identifies Y_rho=Z_rho; equivalently this is the bounded optional-sampling form of a closed continuous martingale. This is classD on[0,T], not merely deterministic-time integrability.

Define Q on F_T by Q(A)=E[1_A Z_T]. Countable additivity is monotone convergence; (19) gives Q(Omega)=1. Positivity and finiteness of exp(M_T-V_T/2) almost surely show Q and P have the same null sets. This establishes a genuine normalized probability change. It does NOT by itself prove that W-integral theta dt is Brownian under Q; that additional Girsanov drift identification requires its own martingale/increment proof and is not silently claimed here.

## 7. Nontrivial controls and exact source distinction

Theta=0: M=V=0, Z=1, C=1. A real probability/filtration remains necessary to interpret expectation, even though this case is deterministic.

Constant theta=c!=0: M=cW, V=c²t, C=exp(c²T/2). Conditional Gaussian integration directly verifies E[exp(c(W_t-W_s)-c²(t-s)/2)|F_s]=1, so (2) has expectation1. Itô isometry gives E M_t²=c²t. This is a nonzero stochastic integral, beyond the source's allzero witness.

Genuinely random predictable control: fix 0<s<T and c!=0; A={W_s>0} in F_s, and theta_t=c 1_A 1_(s,T](t). This is a predictable elementary random integrand, generally discontinuous in time. Its integral is0 up to s and c1_A(W_t-W_s) afters; V_t=c²1_A(t-s) for t>=s. Thus C=(1-P(A))+P(A)exp[c²(T-s)/2]<infinity. Direct conditional Gaussian integration agrees with the general theorem. Time-continuity of a deterministic theta:R->R is neither the definition of a predictable random integrand nor the actual Novikov exponential-integrability assertion.

Energy-stopping control: even for the present integrable theta, un-stopped elementary approximations need not have uniform exponential moments because their bounds B_k can diverge. The good-level construction plus the explicit uniform (11) is what justifies the local-martingale limit. Neither vague convergence nor uniform path convergence alone suffices. Excluding arbitrary hitting-time discontinuity is essential; the deterministic good-level selection is proved rather than assumed.

Critical-moment control: for a in(0,1), any p satisfying (13) exists, tends down to1 as a increases to1, and (14) uses exactly coefficient1/2. No fixed p>1 is promised at a=1. The final unscaled exponential is UI by mass (18),(19), not by a fictitious unscaled Lp estimate.
