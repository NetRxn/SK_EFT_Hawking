## 1. Statement and convention

Let i:B→A be a ring homomorphism, M a RIGHT B-module and N a RIGHT A-module. Restriction means n·b=n·i(b). We construct an actual right A-module T(M)=M⊗_B A and a natural additive-group isomorphism

    Hom_A(M⊗_B A,N) ≅ Hom_B(M,Res N).                      (1)

If A is flat as a LEFT B-module, this induces, in every homological degree n≥0,

    Ext^n_A(M⊗_B A,N) ≅ Ext^n_B(M,Res N).                 (2)

Here Ext is ordinary derived Ext in right modules, calculated from a full projective resolution. We prove (2), including a constructive sufficient hypothesis: an explicitly supplied left B-basis of A. We do not infer flatness from (1). An injective ring-extension counterexample in §9 shows that the omitted hypothesis would make (2) false.

## 2. The balanced quotient exists independently of its desired Hom property

Let F be the free abelian group on the set M×A: its elements are finite integer linear combinations of symbols [m,a]. Let H be the subgroup generated by all elements of these three forms:

    [m+m',a] − [m,a] − [m',a],
    [m,a+a'] − [m,a] − [m,a'],
    [m·b,a] − [m,i(b)a].                                  (3)

Define T(M)=F/H as an abelian group and write m⊗a for the class of [m,a]. The additive relations imply 0⊗a=m⊗0=0 and the expected negative and integer-multiple identities. The last relation is the actual balance relation; it uses left multiplication by i(b) on A.

For c∈A the additive map on F taking [m,a] to [m,ac] preserves H. Each of the first two relations is sent to a relation of the same type; the balance generator maps to [mb,ac]−[m,i(b)(ac)], which is again a balance relation since (i(b)a)c=i(b)(ac). It therefore induces a map x↦xc on T(M). On pure tensors,

    (m⊗a)c=m⊗ac,
    ((m⊗a)c)d=m⊗a(cd),       (m⊗a)1=m⊗a.

The relation in the second coordinate gives x(c+c')=xc+xc'; additivity on F gives (x+x')c=xc+x'c. Pure tensors generate the quotient, so these identities prove that T(M) is a right A-module. No right action on a quotient has been assumed without checking its defining relations.

For any abelian group U, a biadditive B-balanced map β:M×A→U extends uniquely to an additive map F→U by the free-abelian-group property. Its value on every generator (3) is zero, so it factors uniquely through T(M). Conversely every additive map out of T(M) restricts to such a β. If U is a right A-module and β(m,ac)=β(m,a)c, the factor is A-linear: this identity holds on every pure tensor and hence on every finite sum. This is the module-valued universal property, proved from the quotient.

For a right B-linear f:M→M', put T(f)(m⊗a)=f(m)⊗a. Biadditivity, balance and A-linearity follow from those of f and the quotient. Agreement on pure tensors proves T(id)=id and T(gf)=T(g)T(f); thus T is a functor. The map η_M(m)=m⊗1 is right B-linear into Res T(M), because mb⊗1=m⊗i(b).

## 3. Natural Hom isomorphism with explicit inverses

For an A-linear g:T(M)→N define Φ(g)(m)=g(m⊗1). It is additive, and

    Φ(g)(mb)=g(mb⊗1)=g(m⊗i(b))=g(m⊗1)i(b).

It is therefore B-linear. For a B-linear f:M→Res N define

    Ψ(f)(m⊗a)=f(m)a.                                     (4)

The proposed β is biadditive. Its balance follows from

    f(mb)a=(f(m)i(b))a=f(m)(i(b)a),

and β(m,ac)=β(m,a)c, so §2 gives a unique A-linear Ψ(f). Direct evaluation gives ΦΨ(f)=f. Also ΨΦ(g)(m⊗a)=g(m⊗1)a=g(m⊗a), proving ΨΦ(g)=g on the generating pure tensors. Both assignments preserve addition, proving (1) as an additive-group isomorphism, rather than only equality of cardinalities or dimensions.

For a B-map u:M'→M and an A-map v:N→N', the explicit formulas give

    Φ(g∘T(u))=Φ(g)∘u,        Φ(v∘g)=Res(v)∘Φ(g).

These prove naturality in both variables; the inverse is natural as well. The counit Res(N)⊗_B A→N is n⊗a↦na. Its two triangle identities are respectively (m⊗1)a=m⊗a and n1=n, so the construction really gives induction left adjoint to restriction. It does not substitute restriction's right adjoint/coinduction. If B,A and the modules carry compatible central actions of a common commutative ring k, Φ and Ψ are k-linear. In general there is no claimed A-module structure on these Hom groups.

## 4. Free modules and coefficient order

Write B^(I) for the free right B-module with basis e_j. Define

    Θ_I:B^(I)⊗_B A → A^(I),
    Θ_I(x⊗a)_j=i(x_j)a,
    Λ_I(y)=Σ_j e_j⊗y_j.                                  (5)

The formula for Θ is biadditive, balanced since i(x_jb)a=i(x_j)i(b)a, and right A-linear. Λ has a finite sum because y has finite support and is A-linear. On y, ΘΛ(y)=y. On x⊗a,

    ΛΘ(x⊗a)=Σ_j e_j⊗i(x_j)a
             =Σ_j(e_j x_j)⊗a=x⊗a.

Thus (5) is a genuine free-module isomorphism, also for infinite I.

For a map d:B^(J)→B^(I), write d(e_j)=Σ_i e_i b_ij; each fixed column has finite support. Right linearity gives

    d(x)_i=Σ_j b_ij x_j.

Under (5), T(d)(y)_i=Σ_j i(b_ij)y_j. If c:B^(K)→B^(J) has coefficients c_jk, d∘c has coefficients Σ_j b_ij c_jk, in that order. Hence every chain identity is preserved, without commuting any coefficients.

An A-linear f:A^(I)→N is determined by n_i=f(e_i) and f(y)=Σ_i n_i y_i. Precomposition with the induced d therefore has coordinates

    (δ n)_j=Σ_i n_i i(b_ij).                              (6)

On the B-side the same n_i are used and B acts through i. This proves the Hom comparison commutes with actual differentials, not only that both Hom spaces have rank |I|.

A noncommuting control is B=A=M₂(F₂), b=E₁₂ and c=E₂₁. The right-linear maps d_b(x)=bx and d_c(x)=cx compose to d_b d_c(x)=bcx=E₁₁x, whereas reversing the product yields E₂₂x. Their Hom coboundaries are n↦nb and then n↦nc, with composite n↦nbc. Taking n=1 distinguishes this from ncb. Also d_b(cx)=bcx differs from c d_b(x)=cbx at x=1, so these right-module maps are not automatically left B-linear. This is a manual matrix-unit identity, not an executed software control.

## 5. Right exactness and projectivity: what does not need flatness

For u:L→M put Q=coker u. There is a natural A-linear isomorphism

    T(Q) ≅ T(M)/im T(u).                                 (7)

The map induced by m⊗a↦[m]⊗a kills im T(u). For its inverse define ([m],a)↦class(m⊗a). A different representative m+u(l) changes this by T(u)(l⊗a), so the value is independent of representative. The balanced relations and A-linearity descend, and the two maps are inverse on pure tensors. This proves (7), including its actual kernel statement. A surjection q:M→Q induces a surjection T(q): every q-value in a finite sum of pure tensors can be lifted term by term. Consequently T is right exact. It need not preserve injections.

T preserves arbitrary direct sums: the map (⊕_j M_j)⊗_B A→⊕_j T(M_j) sends m⊗a to its finitely many component tensors; its inverse sends each component tensor to the tensor of its inclusion. Balance and mutual inverses are checked componentwise.

T also preserves projectives without a flatness assumption. Given projective P_B, a surjection q:E→F of right A-modules, and g:T(P)→F, Φ(g):P→Res F lifts to h:P→Res E because restriction preserves this underlying surjection. Ψ(h) is a lift of g: naturality of Φ and its injectivity imply qΨ(h)=g. Thus T(P) is projective. Exactness of a whole induced resolution, however, is a separate requirement.

## 6. A constructive sufficient exactness hypothesis, on the correct side

Suppose we are actually supplied elements u_λ∈A such that each a∈A has a UNIQUE finite expression

    a=Σ_λ i(b_λ(a))u_λ.                                 (8)

This means A is free as a LEFT B-module, not as a right B-module. Uniqueness gives additive coordinate maps b_λ, finite support, and

    b_λ(i(b)a)=b b_λ(a).

Define additive maps, natural in the right B-module M,

    χ_M:T(M)→⊕_λ M,      χ_M(m⊗a)_λ=m b_λ(a),
    ρ_M((x_λ))=Σ_λ x_λ⊗u_λ.                             (9)

The balance check is (mb)b_λ(a)=m b_λ(i(b)a); the other relations are additive. Finite support makes both maps legitimate. The basis coordinate identity b_μ(u_λ)=δ_μλ proves χρ=id, and (8) plus balance gives ρχ(m⊗a)=Σ m b_λ(a)⊗u_λ=m⊗a. For f:M→M', χ_M' T(f)=(⊕_λ f)χ_M. The right side is an isomorphism of underlying additive groups; we do not assert a coordinatewise right A-action on ⊕M.

For an exact sequence L→M→Q, the corresponding direct-sum sequence is exact: if a finitely supported vector lies in the kernel, each of its finitely many coordinates has a preimage in L, and these can be chosen with zero outside its finite support. This gives an element of ⊕L with that vector as image. Injections and surjections are proved coordinatewise as well. By (9), T preserves that exact sequence. Equality of underlying kernels and images is equality of the corresponding A-submodules. Thus (8) proves exactness on ALL right B-module sequences, constructively from a basis, and on every degree of every exact complex. This supplies the precise one-sided freeness theorem needed for (2).

More generally we may require A to be flat as a left B-module, meaning M↦M⊗_B A preserves injections; (7) then proves it preserves all short exact sequences. Applying these to kernel/image short exact sequences gives exactness of an arbitrary complex. A supplied proof of exactness for one particular induced augmented complex is also sufficient for that particular Ext comparison. None of these conditions follows just from the existence of (1), free induced terms, dimensions, or finite low-degree basis counts.

## 7. Full projective resolutions and their comparison

Every right module K has a concrete free cover F_B(K): take one basis symbol e_k for every underlying element k∈K and evaluate Σ e_k b_k↦Σ k b_k. It is onto since e_k maps to k. Starting with such a cover of M, and then taking a free cover of each actual kernel, gives an infinite augmented free resolution P_*→M. Explicitly, P₀=F_B(M), K₀=ker(P₀→M), P₁=F_B(K₀) with map evaluation followed by inclusion, and recursively K_n=ker(P_n→P_{n−1}), P_{n+1}=F_B(K_n). At every degree image equals the specified kernel by surjectivity of the cover. This constructs the missing infinite tail; it does not call a six-term prefix an infinite resolution.

For any full projective resolution P_*→M, §5 makes every T(P_n) projective. If the left-flat or actual exactness hypothesis of §6 holds, the induced augmented complex T(P_*)→T(M) is exact. Therefore it is a projective resolution of T(M).

For completeness, the comparison machinery used by ordinary derived Ext can be justified here. For a map M→M', and projective resolutions P→M, Q→M', lift P₀→M' through Q₀→M'. If f_{n−1} is already constructed, f_{n−1}d_P,n maps into ker d_Q,n−1 (or the augmentation kernel at n=1). Exactness makes Q_n→that kernel surjective, and projectivity of P_n supplies f_n. This constructs a chain lift.

Two lifts f,g of the same map are chain homotopic. Put h_{−1}=0. At n=0, f₀−g₀ lands in the augmentation kernel, so lift through Q₁→ker ε_Q. Inductively, after h₀,…,h_{n−1} have been chosen, the map

    r_n=f_n−g_n−h_{n−1}d_P,n

is a cycle: applying d_Q,n, using the chain-map identities, the previous homotopy identity, and d_P,n−1 d_P,n=0 gives zero. Lift it through d_Q,n+1 using projectivity of P_n, obtaining h_n. Thus

    f_n−g_n=d_Q,n+1 h_n+h_{n−1}d_P,n.

Precomposition makes homotopic chain lifts induce equal cohomology maps. Lifts of identities/composites are homotopic to the corresponding identities/composites, by this same uniqueness argument. It follows that the cohomology construction is independent of chosen resolution and has the usual contravariant functoriality in M. It is covariantly functorial in N by postcomposition. This is the ordinary projective-resolution construction of derived Ext; no native `ProjectiveResolution.isoExt` theorem has been invoked or executed.

## 8. Actual cohomology comparison and the change-of-rings theorem

For an augmented projective resolution P→M set

    C_B^n=Hom_B(P_n,Res N),
    δ_B^n(f)=f∘d_P,n+1,
    C_A^n=Hom_A(T(P_n),N),
    δ_A^n(g)=g∘T(d_P,n+1).

Because consecutive differentials compose to zero, δ^{n+1}δ^n=0. Naturality from §3 gives degreewise isomorphisms Φ_n with

    Φ_{n+1}(δ_A^n g)=δ_B^n(Φ_n g).                        (10)

Their inverses obey the inverse equation. Hence Φ maps ker δ_A^n bijectively onto ker δ_B^n, and im δ_A^{n−1} bijectively onto im δ_B^{n−1}. It induces a well-defined isomorphism of the actual subquotients ker/im in every degree, with the inverse induced by Ψ. This is a cohomology isomorphism, not an equality of ranks.

If T(P) is a projective resolution as established in §7, these two cohomology groups are the Ext groups in (2). Equation (10) gives the asserted isomorphism. It is canonical: for different resolutions, comparison lifts commute with (10) by naturality in each P_n, and homotopic lifts have the same cohomology maps by §7. The same reasoning proves naturality in M; postcomposition proves naturality in N. With compatible central k-actions the maps are k-linear. This proves the genuine conditional noncommutative change-of-rings theorem.

A graded version requires graded algebras, inclusion, homogeneous resolutions and appropriate graded free/flat witnesses. Give m⊗a degree deg m+deg a. Balance respects degree. Hom of internal degree t corresponds by evaluation at the degree-zero unit and by f(m)a, so the comparison respects internal degree t as well as homological degree n. The grading hypotheses must be supplied for the actual application; the ungraded theorem alone does not identify an Adams bidegree or stem.

## 9. An injective nonflat extension where the unconditional Ext claim fails

Let B=ℤ and A=ℤ⊕F₂ with square-zero multiplication

    (n,t)(m,u)=(nm, n u+m t),

where the second component is computed in F₂. Addition is componentwise. Distributivity is immediate. The second component of a triple product is nm v+nℓ u+mℓ t in either association, so multiplication is associative; (1,0) is the unit. The map ℤ→A, n↦(n,0), is an injective unital homomorphism. Put ε=(0,1). Then ε≠0, ε²=0, and 2ε=0.

The exact right ℤ-sequence 0→ℤ --2→ ℤ→ℤ/2→0 becomes A --2→ A→A/2A→0. Its left map is not injective: its kernel is J={0,ε}. Thus the induction functor for this injective extension is not left exact, while the Hom adjunction of §3 still holds.

We can also show actual failure of the unconditional Ext isomorphism, not merely failure of that proof method. Let M=ℤ/2 and let N=F₂ be the right A-module defined by (n,t)↦n mod2. We have T(M)≅A/2A by (7). A free augmented resolution begins

    P₀=A → A/2A,
    d₁:P₁=A→P₀,          d₁(a)=2a,
    d₂:P₂=A→P₁,          d₂(a)=εa,
    d₃:P₃=A²→P₂,         d₃(a,b)=2a+εb.

Here ker(P₀→A/2A)=2A=im d₁. Also ker d₁=J=im d₂. Since ε(n,t)=(0,n mod2), ker d₂ consists exactly of pairs (2k,t); these form 2A+εA=im d₃. Extend this prefix by the free-cover construction on ker d₃ and subsequent kernels. It is a full free resolution, not a claim that the displayed prefix terminates.

On N both 2 and ε act as zero. Therefore the three Hom coboundaries C⁰→C¹→C²→C³ arising from d₁,d₂,d₃ all vanish. In particular C²=N and its incoming image and outgoing kernel give

    Ext²_A(A/2A,N)≅F₂.

In contrast the length-one free ℤ-resolution above gives Ext²_ℤ(ℤ/2,F₂)=0. Thus the unrestricted version of (2) is false even for an injective unital extension. No computation package or generated golden is used in this counterexample.

## 12. Original left-module H2, grading and topology are separate obligations

The original H2 is written for LEFT modules as

    Ext^n_A(A⊗_B k,k) ≅ Ext^n_B(k,k).

For a left B-module L, construct A⊗_B L by the free-abelian-group quotient with relation [a i(b),l]=[a,b l] and left A-action c[a,l]=[ca,l]. The evaluation/inverse maps are g↦(l↦g(1⊗l)) and f↦(a⊗l↦a f(l)). The left version requires A free/flat as a RIGHT B-module. Equivalently, it follows by applying the right theorem to Bᵒᵖ→Aᵒᵖ. For the ACTUAL right-module P, the correspondence is

    (Aᵒᵖ⊗_{Bᵒᵖ} P_left) → (P⊗_B A)_left-op,
    aᵒᵖ⊗m ↦ m⊗a.

Its balance reads m b⊗a=m⊗i(b)a because aᵒᵖ i(b)ᵒᵖ=(i(b)a)ᵒᵖ. Its inverse sends m⊗a to aᵒᵖ⊗m; the relation check reverses the same equality. This does not by itself identify left Aᵒᵖ Ext with the original LEFT A Ext.

One explicit sufficient conversion is a supplied pair of bijective anti-automorphisms χ_B,χ_A satisfying χ_A i=iχ_B and compatible augmentations εχ=ε. Twist a right module M to the left module M^χ by b·m=mχ_B(b). This is a genuine exact equivalence, with inverse given by χ_B^{-1}; it preserves projectives and Ext by the resolution comparison of §7. There is a natural left A-isomorphism

    (M⊗_B A)^χ ≅ A⊗_B M^χ,
    m⊗a ↦ χ_A^{-1}(a)⊗m,
    x⊗m ↦ m⊗χ_A(x).                                    (14)

For balance, the first image of mb⊗a equals χ_A^{-1}(a)i(χ_B^{-1}(b))⊗m, which is the image of m⊗i(b)a. Left A-linearity follows from χ_A^{-1}(aχ_A(c))=cχ_A^{-1}(a). The inverse is balanced because χ_A(x i(b))=i(χ_B(b))χ_A(x), and the formulas compose to identity. Trivial coefficients remain trivial by εχ=ε. These explicit data would convert the full right result to the original left H2. A source comment saying “via the antipode” is not the construction of those actual compatible bijections. Alternatively one may construct a genuine left resolution and the right-B free/flat witness directly.
